Kids’ corner – Activity 9: Water flows low

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Goes with: Convergence 1 – Week 9: Letting go of self

Length: 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. For: children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

Small goal: play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions value humility: not always needing to be first. For a child, water that flows to low places and still feeds the plants is an easy picture.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson Convergence 1 – Week 9: Letting go of self.

2. What science finds

  • [Expert judgement] There is no research synthesis on this game for children aged 6–10. It rests on teaching experience, not on specific scientific evidence.

3. What you need

A tray, something to tilt it slightly, a small cup of water or a dropper, a cloth.

4. How to play

  1. Tilt the tray slightly. The child drips a few drops of water at the high end.
  2. Watch together: where does the water go? (To the low end.)
  3. The adult says: "Water helps plants grow and never shows off. Water is happy to go to low places."
  4. Together think of one "water-like" thing for today: let a younger sibling choose a toy first, let a friend speak first.

Things the adult might say:

  • "Does water need to be first?"
  • "Where would you like to be like water today?"

5. Adjusting for age

  • Age 6–7: just watch the water and choose one thing the adult suggests.
  • Age 8–10: change the tilt and predict where the water will go; the child thinks of their own "water-like" act.
  • A sibling under 6: they drip the water while the adult holds the cup.

6. Cautions and when to stop

  • Do not call the child "grabby" or "selfish". Humility does not mean letting others push you around: if treated unfairly, the child should still speak up or find an adult. Wipe the tray together afterwards.
  • If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
  • If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See Get help now.

7. On daynentang

Daynentang has the lesson Nói thật, lòng nhẹ (telling the truth, feeling light) for families with children aged 2–8, currently in Vietnamese only. Your browser’s translate option can help.

8. Notes after playing

This activity has not been trial-taught yet. If you play it with your child, note three things for next time: did the child enjoy it (bored / OK / enjoyed); which steps worked and which were hard; what you would change.
