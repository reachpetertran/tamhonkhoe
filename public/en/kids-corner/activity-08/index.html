Kids’ corner – Activity 8: Making up

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Goes with: Convergence 1 – Week 8: Forgiveness

Length: 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. For: children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

Small goal: play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions teach letting go of resentment and mending relationships, in different ways. For a child, the first step is putting feelings into words and fixing small things together.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson Convergence 1 – Week 8: Forgiveness.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] Meta-analysis of 20 studies, 1,472 children and adolescents (mean age about 12) in 10 countries: forgiveness education programmes increased forgiveness (g = 0.54) and reduced anger (g = 0.29). These were structured school programmes for young people who had been treated unfairly; "Making up" is only for small quarrels and has not been studied as such. (Rapp, Wang Xu & Enright 2022)
  • Limits: no study has measured this particular game. The research shows a general direction only; it does not promise a child will change after a few rounds.

3. What you need

Nothing needed. Use only after a small quarrel, for example two siblings fighting over a toy.

4. How to play

  1. Wait until both children are calm. The adult sits with both of them.
  2. Each child says one sentence: "I felt ___." The adult repeats each child’s sentence back.
  3. Together they think of a fix: take turns, play together, swap to another toy.
  4. The adult asks: "How do you feel now?"

Things the adult might say:

  • "I heard you say you felt cross because your brother took your car. Is that right?"
  • "What could we try so you’re both happy?"

5. Adjusting for age

  • Age 6–7: the adult offers two possible fixes to choose from.
  • Age 8–10: the children come up with the fix; it can be used with a playmate, with an adult nearby.
  • A sibling under 6: the adult names the younger child’s feeling for them ("he’s sad because…").

6. Cautions and when to stop

  • Do not make a child say "sorry" or "I forgive you" before they are ready. Never use this game for bullying, or when someone has hurt or frightened the child: then an adult must step in and protect the child.
  • If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
  • If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See Get help now.

8. Notes after playing

This activity has not been trial-taught yet. If you play it with your child, note three things for next time: did the child enjoy it (bored / OK / enjoyed); which steps worked and which were hard; what you would change.
