Kids’ corner – Activity 6: A kind deed at home

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Goes with: Convergence 0 – Week 6: Service

Length: 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. For: children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

Small goal: play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions teach helping others without expecting anything back. For a child, service starts at home, with small things.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson Convergence 0 – Week 6: Service.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] An experiment with children under 2: they looked happier giving treats to others than receiving them, and happier still when giving away their own. It is one small study of very young children, measured once; it says nothing about the long-term effect of a helping habit. (Aknin, Hamlin & Dunn 2012)
  • Limits: no study has measured this particular game. The research shows a general direction only; it does not promise a child will change after a few rounds.

3. What you need

Nothing needed.

4. How to play

  1. Adult and child think together: who at home might need help today? Grandparents, parents, siblings.
  2. The child picks a small thing to do for that person: line up their shoes, bring a glass of water, tidy shared toys, hand them a towel.
  3. The child does it "in secret", without announcing it.
  4. In the evening the adult asks: "How did it feel doing that?"

Things the adult might say:

  • "Who do you think is most tired at home today?"
  • "A small thing is still a kind thing."

5. Adjusting for age

  • Age 6–7: the adult suggests two things for the child to choose from.
  • Age 8–10: the child observes and thinks of the task; it can be for a neighbour, with an adult along.
  • A sibling under 6: the two children do one task together.

6. Cautions and when to stop

  • Do not force the child, and do not call their choice too small. No food or money rewards. The task must be safe: nothing heavy, no stove, no sockets.
  • If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
  • If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See Get help now.

7. On daynentang

Daynentang has the lesson Việc tốt bí mật (secret good deeds) for families with children aged 2–8, currently in Vietnamese only. Your browser’s translate option can help.

8. Notes after playing

This activity has not been trial-taught yet. If you play it with your child, note three things for next time: did the child enjoy it (bored / OK / enjoyed); which steps worked and which were hard; what you would change.
