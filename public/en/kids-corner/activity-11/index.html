Kids’ corner – Activity 11: Listening to the bell fade

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Goes with: Convergence 1 – Week 11: Silence

Length: 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. For: children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

Small goal: play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions make room for silence, each understanding it in its own way. For a child, silence is a short listening game.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson Convergence 1 – Week 11: Silence.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] Meta-analysis of 33 randomised trials, 3,666 children and adolescents: mindfulness programmes improved attention, anxiety/stress and depression by a small amount (d from 0.16 to 0.30). Against active control groups, benefits remained only for mindfulness, depression and anxiety/stress. These were multi-session school programmes, not a 5-minute game at home. (Dunning et al. 2019)
  • Limits: no study has measured this particular game. The research shows a general direction only; it does not promise a child will change after a few rounds.

3. What you need

A small bell, or a spoon and a sturdy glass (held by the adult).

4. How to play

  1. The adult gently strikes the bell, or taps the spoon on the glass.
  2. The child closes their eyes (or looks down) and listens as the sound gets quieter.
  3. When they can no longer hear anything, the child raises a hand.
  4. Play three times. Then ask: "In the quiet, what other sounds did you hear?"

Things the adult might say:

  • "Where did the sound go?"
  • "After the bell, what could you hear?"

5. Adjusting for age

  • Age 6–7: three rounds are enough; accept an early hand.
  • Age 8–10: after the bell, silently count how many other sounds they hear (birds, traffic, a fan).
  • A sibling under 6: they get to ring the bell for the older child.

6. Cautions and when to stop

  • Each silence lasts only a few dozen seconds. Do not make the child sit still for long. If they do not want to close their eyes, let them keep them open. If using a glass, the adult holds it.
  • If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
  • If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See Get help now.

7. On daynentang

Daynentang has the lesson Đi chậm, ăn chậm (slow walking, slow eating) for families with children aged 2–8, currently in Vietnamese only. Your browser’s translate option can help.

8. Notes after playing

This activity has not been trial-taught yet. If you play it with your child, note three things for next time: did the child enjoy it (bored / OK / enjoyed); which steps worked and which were hard; what you would change.
