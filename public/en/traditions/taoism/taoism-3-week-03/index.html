Taoism 3 – Week 3: Transformation

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Before you start: this Level 3 course deals with death and loss. If you are newly bereaved, seriously ill, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, please save the course for another time and see Get help now straight away.

This week: read the story of the four friends in chapter VI of the Zhuangzi slowly. Sit for 35 minutes each day, moving through the body and noticing how the sensations in each place come and go. Each evening, note one small change in your body that does not need fixing.

Measurable goal: sit for 35 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days (30 minutes is fine if you feel unsteady); note one small change in the body each evening, on at least 5 of 7 evenings.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter VI of the Zhuangzi is called "The Great and Most Honoured Master". In it, four friends accept each other as friends because they all see life and death as parts of one body. Later, Tzu Yu falls seriously ill and his back is bent double. He drags himself to a well to look at his reflection, and his friend Tzu Ssu asks whether he is afraid.

Reading it in context

  • [Expert judgement] In the last sentence Giles writes "God". The Chinese has 天 (Heaven) in this sentence, and 造物者 (the maker of things) in earlier lines of the story. The Vietnamese follows the Chinese and says "Trời" (Heaven); the course does not put the concept of God of the monotheistic religions into the character's mouth. This is the writer's judgement; a reviewer should check it.
  • [Expert judgement] "Hanging in the air" (縣) is an image in the Chinese: a person clinging to life and death is like someone suspended; "being cut down" (縣解) means no longer being bound by that fear. The image is about fear, not about leaving life by one's own choice.
  • [Inference] Tzu Yu is deliberately funny: a crowing cock, a cross-bow for duck, buttocks for wheels. The Zhuangzi uses humour to loosen fear, not to make light of sick people. "Joy and sorrow touch me not" is the character's ideal; the lesson does not ask you not to feel sad.
  • The story continues with the third friend, Tzu Lai, when he is near death (Week 5 reads one passage). The course does not quote Tzu Lai's line about "dying quickly", to avoid misreading.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that directly evaluates reading this story or the "small change in the body" exercise.
  • [Verified] Mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount (47 trials, 3,515 people), no more than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)
  • [Verified] About 8% of meditators had an adverse experience, most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020)

Limits: the body scan is not for diagnosis. If you notice an unusual change in your body (new pain, a lump, bleeding, unexplained weight loss), see a doctor; do not "accept" it through meditation.

3. Practice for adults

About 35 minutes a day, plus 2 minutes of notes.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read both passages. Pause at "I obtained life because it was my time".
  2. Sit for 30 minutes, scanning the body.
    • Move slowly from the top of the head to the soles of the feet, about a minute for each area: head, face, neck, shoulders, arms, chest, belly, back, hips, legs, feet.
    • In each area, notice what sensation is there (warm, cool, itchy, heavy, nothing at all), then notice how it changes during that minute.
    • For the rest of the time: sit with the whole body, noticing sensations come and go like weather.
  3. Back to the ground (2 minutes). Open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, look around, name three things before you stand up.
  4. Evening note (2 minutes). One small change in the body that needs no fixing: hair growing longer, a callus on your hand, a hoarse voice in the morning, tired legs after a day of walking. Write one sentence.
  5. Tick it off. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

4. Kids' corner 6+

"Clay changes shape" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Give the child a lump of modelling clay (or homemade dough from flour, salt and water).
  2. Together, shape it into a bird. Squash it, shape it into a boat. Squash it, shape it into a cup.
  3. Ask: "Where did the bird go? Did the clay disappear?"
  4. Finish with: "The clay is still clay. Only the shape has changed."

Wash hands after playing. Do not let young children put the clay in their mouths.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
  • A feeling of being detached from your body, or of your body seeming strange, stays after you stand up. If it lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
  • Thoughts about your own death keep coming back during the day and are hard to let go of.
  • The body scan makes you worry more about illness. Then scan only your hands and feet, or stop this week.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

If you feel unsteady while sitting, daynentang has a lesson that brings your attention back to your body and surroundings: Neo lại (in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Look at your hand for one minute. Find one mark of time on it, and let it be.
