Taoism 3 – Week 2: When Zhuangzi's wife died

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Before you start: this Level 3 course deals with death and loss. If you are newly bereaved, seriously ill, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, please save the course for another time and see Get help now straight away.

This week in particular: the reading tells of the death of a wife. If you have lost someone close to you in the past year, skip this week, or read it only with a trusted friend sitting with you.

This week: read the story of the death of Zhuangzi's wife in chapter XVIII slowly. Sit for 30 minutes each day. Each day, look at and note one seasonal or cyclical change in nature (leaves, flowers, the moon, rain and sun). The exercise is not for thinking about the death of someone you love.

Measurable goal: sit for 30 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; note one change in nature each day, on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter XVIII of the Zhuangzi is called "Perfect Happiness". In it is the story of Hui Tzu, Zhuangzi's friend and frequent debating partner, coming to offer condolences when Zhuangzi's wife dies.

Reading it in context

  • [Inference] Zhuangzi does not say that grief is wrong. The first thing he says is "I could not help being affected by her death". The story tells how he went on living after the pain, by seeing death as a passage, like one season giving way to another. You do not have to stop crying to learn from this lesson.
  • Nor does the story tell readers to sing or drum on a bowl at a funeral. In Vietnam and many other places, funerals follow the customs of each family and each tradition; the course does not discuss funeral customs.
  • [Expert judgement] "Lying asleep in Eternity" is Giles's wording; the Chinese is 偃然寢於巨室, usually understood as "lying at rest in the great chamber" (that is, heaven and earth). "Spirit" is Giles's word for 氣 (qi). This is the writer's judgement; a reviewer should check it.
  • Chapter XVIII also has the story of a skull by the roadside that speaks of death as a joy. The course does not quote that story. The course reads the Zhuangzi to learn a way of seeing change, not to look for reasons to leave life. If you are having thoughts of dying, please see Get help now straight away.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that evaluates reading the Zhuangzi with bereaved people.
  • [Verified] A synthesis of 14 studies of bereaved adults (non-violent loss) found that about 9.8% were at risk of prolonged grief disorder; methods varied widely. (Lundorff et al. 2017)
  • [Inference] This means most bereaved people gradually find a way to go on, but about one in ten need more help. If the pain after a loss is not easing with time, that is the moment to see a professional, not to read more scripture on your own.
  • [Verified] Mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount, no more than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day, plus 2 minutes of notes.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read the story once. The second time, pause at "I could not help being affected". The third time, pause at "like the sequence of spring, summer, autumn, and winter".
  2. Sit for 25 minutes. If you can, sit near a window or on a veranda.
    • First 10 minutes: follow the breath.
    • Middle 10 minutes: open your eyes and look at something in nature (a tree, the sky, a pot plant). Notice what stage it is at: young leaves or old, morning light or evening light.
    • Last 5 minutes: close your eyes and repeat slowly "spring, summer, autumn, winter" with the breath.
  3. Back to the ground (2 minutes). Open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, name three things before you stand up.
  4. Note one change in nature (2 minutes). For example: "The almond tree in front of the house has dropped more leaves", "The moon is thinner tonight than last night". Write only what you see; it needs no meaning.
  5. Tick it off. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

If, while sitting, you remember someone who has died, let the memory be there, breathe slowly, then open your eyes. Do not try to "accept" it quickly.

4. Kids' corner 6+

"One tree, four seasons" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. With the child, choose a familiar tree (in front of the house, at school, in the park).
  2. Fold a sheet of paper into four boxes. The child draws the same tree four times: new leaves, deep green leaves, leaves changing colour or falling, bare branches or the rainy season (where there are only a dry and a rainy season, draw two seasons).
  3. Ask: "Is it a different tree? Why does it look different?"
  4. Finish with: "It is still the same tree. Only the season has changed."

Talk only about seasons and trees. Do not talk about death, and do not say "the tree is dying" when the leaves fall.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
  • The reading brings back pain about someone who has died, strongly, and it does not ease after you stand up. Then stop this week and call someone you trust.
  • Pain after a loss lasts many months, takes up most of the day, or makes it hard to work or look after yourself: please see a mental health professional.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

When you miss someone who has died, don't stay alone with the sadness. daynentang has a lesson to help you remember who can be there for you: Kết nối, người mình có thể nhờ (in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Look outside for one minute and find one sign that the season is changing.
