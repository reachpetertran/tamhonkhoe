Taoism 2 – Week 4: Less each day

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read chapter 48 of the Tao Te Ching (paragraphs 1–2) slowly, sit for 25 minutes each day while gradually dropping instructions, and during the day drop one unnecessary thing.

Measurable goal: sit for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; each day drop one unnecessary thing and write it down, on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 48 sets two paths side by side: learning adds something each day, following the Tao takes something away each day. Diminishing and diminishing again, one arrives at non-action; and having arrived there, there is nothing that is not done.

Reading it in context

  • [Inference] The chapter does not look down on learning. It compares two different directions: one of gathering, one of letting go. The course does not tell you to stop learning or stop working.
  • The words in brackets are Legge's additions. "(On purpose)" shows that Legge understood "doing nothing" as not acting out of deliberate striving, not as lying still.
  • The course does not quote paragraph 3, because it is about "getting all under heaven", a matter of government.
  • [Expert judgement] The line "non-action, yet nothing is left undone" (無為而無不為) is one of the most discussed sentences of the Tao Te Ching. The course does not present it as a secret of success; it only practises the "diminishing" part in small things.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any review that evaluates "doing less" as in this week's exercise.
  • [Verified] On longer sitting: a 2015 meta-analysis of 29 studies with 2,668 healthy adults found that mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) reduced stress, anxiety and depression by a moderate amount; results varied widely between studies. (Khoury et al. 2015)
  • [Verified] A 2018 critical review points out that "mindfulness" has no agreed definition, and that many studies are methodologically weak and rely heavily on self-report. (Van Dam et al. 2018)

Limits: dropping tasks is a way of organising yourself, not advice about work or money.

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day.

  1. Slow reading (2 minutes). Read the two paragraphs. Pause at "He diminishes it and again diminishes it".
  2. Sit, dropping instructions (25 minutes). Days 1–2: sit with the breath and labels as in Week 1. Days 3–4: stop noting labels; just attend to the breath. Days 5–7: let go of attending to the breath too; just sit, hear and see what is here. If you drift too far, go back to the previous step. For the first two days you can sit for 20 minutes.
  3. Drop one thing during the day. Choose one unnecessary thing and leave it out today: one session of scrolling, one notification, one thing you don't need to buy, one small commitment you haven't yet taken on. Do not drop things that keep you safe and well: medicines as prescribed, meals, sleep, care for young children or others who depend on you, work duties.
  4. Write one line (2 minutes). What did you drop? Where did that time go?
  5. Tick the day. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "Put one thing away" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Before playtime, look together at a table or play corner with lots of things on it.
  2. Invite the child to choose one thing to put back in its place. Then one more if the child wants.
  3. Play together with what is left. Ask: "How do we play with fewer things?"
  4. To finish: "When we take a little away, there's more room to play."

The child chooses what to put away; do not force them to give up toys, give them away or throw out the child's things.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • When you drop instructions, you feel empty, numb or disoriented, and this does not ease when you open your eyes and put your feet on the floor. Then go back to sitting with the breath, and shorten the sitting.
  • "Dropping things" starts spreading to skipping meals, sleep, time with people close to you or tasks you need to do.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
