Taoism 4 – Week 6: Review

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read the two opening paragraphs of chapter III of the Zhuangzi and chapter 9 of the Tao Te Ching slowly. Leave "the vessel a little unfilled": drop or postpone one unnecessary commitment. At the end of the week, review the whole course.

Measurable goal: drop or postpone one unnecessary commitment in the week; rate the course’s 6 habits (0–3), choose 2 to keep, and write a 4-week plan using "if… then…" sentences.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter III of the Zhuangzi, the chapter with the story of Cook Ding, opens with two short paragraphs on the limits of a human life. Chapter 9 of the Tao Te Ching says something similar in everyday images: a vessel filled too full is hard to carry, and a point sharpened again and again soon goes blunt.

Reading it in context

  • [Expert judgement] Giles translates the second paragraph of chapter III quite freely. The Chinese is usually read as: in doing good, keep away from fame; in doing wrong, do not go as far as punishment; and take the middle path as your rule. The course does not use this passage as permission to do wrong "as long as it is minor"; it keeps only the ideas of "the middle course" and "avoiding fame".
  • Giles titles the chapter "Nourishment of the Soul" and adds notes reflecting his own reading of "soul". The course does not follow that interpretation.
  • "Withdrawing into obscurity" in chapter 9 does not mean quitting your job or refusing credit you have earned. The text is about not clinging to an achievement once the work is done.
  • "Dropping a commitment" does not include medication, eating, sleep, or caring for people who depend on you.
  • [Inference] The two passages gather up the whole course: practising small parts rather than chasing everything (Weeks 1–2), knowing enough and knowing when to stop (Week 3), stepping back when the work is done (Week 4), doing things for others without seeking fame (Week 5).

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] Plans of the form "if… then…" help people reach their goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (94 tests, more than 8,000 people). (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
  • [Verified] Self-control training programmes have a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias (33 studies). (Friese et al. 2017)
  • [Inference] That is why the review plan keeps only 2 habits and spells out the time and place. Keeping a few things steadily is more realistic than keeping many.

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, and one 15-minute review at the end of the week.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read both passages. The second time, pause at "It is better to leave a vessel unfilled".
  2. Leave the vessel a little unfilled (once in the week, 10 minutes). Look at this week’s calendar. Choose one unnecessary commitment to drop, postpone or hand on: an unimportant appointment, an extra task you took on because you found it hard to say no, a group chat you do not need to follow. Let the people involved know, politely.
  3. One earlier habit each day (5 minutes). Repeat one of the five habits from Weeks 1–5, a different one each day.
  4. End-of-week review (15 minutes, once). Rate the course’s six habits from 0 (not done) to 3 (become a habit): practising one small part of a skill; catching things while they are small; stopping at "that’s enough"; putting the stake down and being careful at the end; acts at home without keeping score; leaving the vessel a little unfilled. Choose 2 habits to keep for the next 4 weeks, and write one sentence for each: "If it is [time, place], then I will [specific action]."

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "The little-by-little wall" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Ask the child: "What is something you couldn’t do before, but can do now?" For example: tying shoelaces, riding a bike, writing your name, pouring your own water.
  2. The child draws it on a sheet of paper.
  3. Then ask: "How did you practise?" The adult writes the child’s words under the drawing.
  4. Stick the sheet on a corner of the wall. Next time the child can do something new, add another sheet.

No comparing with brothers, sisters or friends. If the child cannot think of anything, the adult tells of one thing they have seen the child get better at.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The review makes you blame yourself for "not having done anything". Then write down only what you did manage, and choose 1 habit instead of 2.
  • If the signs are serious, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

When you find yourself chasing too many things and worrying without end, the skill has its home on daynentang: Worry and going round in circles (in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Look at tomorrow’s calendar and leave one fifteen-minute slot empty.
