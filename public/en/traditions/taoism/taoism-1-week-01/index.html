Taoism 1 – Week 1: Like water

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read the whole of chapter 8 of the Tao Te Ching slowly, sit still for 5 minutes, and each day choose one small matter in which to yield instead of contending.

Measurable goal: yield instead of contending once a day, on at least 5 of 7 days.

About this course

The course reads six passages from two foundational Taoist texts: the Tao Te Ching (traditionally attributed to Laozi) and the Zhuangzi. The English is James Legge’s translation (1891) and Herbert Giles’s (1889); words in brackets are Legge’s own additions for clarity. Taoism is also a religion, with temples, rituals and religious orders. This course only reads the texts; it does not teach Taoist ritual or cultivation methods.

About 10–15 minutes a day: slow reading, a short time sitting still, then carrying one idea from the lesson into your day.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Water is an image that returns again and again in the Tao Te Ching. It is soft, flows to the low places and nourishes everything without claiming a share. Chapter 8 takes water as the model for a person who lives by the Tao: benefiting others and not striving.

Reading it in context

  • "Not striving" is a major theme of the Tao Te Ching, repeated in many chapters. [Inference] Here it means not grabbing the better share, not arguing to win at all costs; it does not mean keeping silent in the face of wrong.
  • Paragraph 2 lists the "excellence" of many things, including government. [Expert judgement] Much of the Tao Te Ching is addressed to rulers; this course reads it as advice for one person’s life.
  • Yielding does not mean accepting harm. Safety, health and the legitimate interests of yourself or others are not part of this exercise.
  • Water in Taoism is an image, not a promise: living "like water" does not guarantee that others will treat you well in return.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that evaluates practising "not striving" as taught in the Tao Te Ching.
  • [Verified] The closest evidence is on humility. A 2025 meta-analysis of 65 studies found that more humble people had slightly better health, life satisfaction and sense of meaning, and slightly less depression and anxiety. All the associations were small. This is a correlation: we do not know whether humility makes people healthier, or healthier people find humility easier. (Lee et al. 2025)
  • [Inference] Yielding in one small matter is close to, but not the same as, humility as a personality trait, so this result is only background.

Limits: this exercise is self-observation, not therapy.

3. Practice for adults

About 12 minutes a day, plus one moment of yielding during the day.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read chapter 8 once, then paragraph 1 again. Pause on the phrase "the low place which all men dislike".
  2. Sit still (5 minutes). Sit upright but at ease. Put a glass of water in front of you if you like. Listen to the breath going in and out; when a thought pulls you away, gently come back.
  3. Choose one place to yield (1 minute). Write down one small matter in which you will yield today instead of contending. For example: - let the other person finish their sentence before you speak; - give way on the road, or give up a parking space; - not argue to win over something unimportant; - let a colleague take the credit for shared work.
  4. Look back in the evening (3 minutes). Write one line: did you yield, what did you feel in your body at the time, what happened afterwards?
  5. Tick the day. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "Water flows low": drip water onto a tilted tray, watch it run to the low end, then choose together one "water-like" thing to do (letting a friend go first, helping without needing praise). See the full activity at Kids’ corner – Activity 9.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The exercise makes you feel you must always give in, or you are in a relationship where yielding is a way to avoid being shouted at, threatened or hit. Then drop the yielding exercise and talk to someone you trust.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

7. Practice for today

Next time you are about to add one more line to win an argument, ask yourself silently: "What would water do here?"
