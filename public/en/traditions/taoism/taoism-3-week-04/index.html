Taoism 3 – Week 4: The fire passes on

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Before you start: this Level 3 course deals with death and loss. If you are newly bereaved, seriously ill, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, please save the course for another time and see Get help now straight away.

This week: read the end of chapter III of the Zhuangzi slowly. Each day do one 40-minute session (sitting, or sitting then slow walking; splitting it into two sessions is fine). Each day, do one small thing that carries on something you learned from someone: a grandparent, parent, teacher or friend, living or no longer living.

Measurable goal: one 40-minute session (40 minutes sitting, or 30 minutes sitting and 10 minutes slow walking) on at least 5 of 7 days; one "passing on the fire" act each day, on at least 5 of 7 days.

A reminder: this is the longest week of the course. If you felt unsteady while sitting in earlier weeks, keep to 30 minutes. When choosing a person for "passing on the fire", do not choose someone who died in the past year or someone who has hurt you.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter III of the Zhuangzi, "Nourishing the Lord of Life", is about caring for what governs life. It closes with the death of Lao Tzu. His friend Ch'in Shih comes to mourn, gives three cries and leaves. A disciple asks whether that is enough to show grief. Ch'in Shih replies that when he went in, he saw old people weeping as if for their children and young people as if for their mothers, and he judged that they had forgotten the source of their own lives. Reading it in context

  • [Inference] Ch'in Shih criticises those who weep too much. The course does not take this as advice for bereaved people: in Week 2, Zhuangzi himself says he "could not help being affected". This lesson reads the two sentences above as a way of seeing life passing on, not as a rule about how much one is allowed to grieve.
  • Between the two quoted sentences, Ch'in Shih also says that for those who understand life and death this way there is no place for lamentation, and that the ancients called death a release from being suspended. The course retells this in its own words without quoting it, and leaves out the sentence where Giles renders 帝 (Di) as "God".
  • [Expert judgement] The last sentence in the Chinese is 指窮於為薪，火傳也，不知其盡也. Some read it as speaking of something that goes beyond the death of the body; Giles's note takes it as the immortality of the soul. The course does not follow that note and does not choose an interpretation: the exercise only uses the image of "passing on the fire" for what one person learns from another.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that evaluates this week's "passing on the fire" exercise.
  • [Verified] Longer sittings can bring difficult experiences: a review of 83 studies found that about 8% of meditators had adverse experiences (about 4% in experimental studies). (Farias et al. 2020)
  • [Verified] An interview study of Western meditators recorded many factors that influence whether a difficult experience becomes more or less severe, including factors in the practitioner, in the practice, in relationships and in health habits. (Lindahl et al. 2017)
  • [Verified] Mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount (47 trials, 3,515 people), no more than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)

Limits: a 40-minute session is practice, not treatment. Sitting longer does not mean practising better.

3. Practice for adults

About 45 minutes a day.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read the two quoted sentences. The second time, read only "The fuel is consumed, but the fire may be transmitted".
  2. A 40-minute session. Choose one of two ways; you may split it into two 20-minute sessions:
    • Sit for 40 minutes: 10 minutes following the breath; 15 minutes scanning the body as in Week 3; 15 minutes just sitting, letting every sensation, sound and thought come and go.
    • Sit for 30 minutes, walk slowly for 10: walk very slowly, feeling your feet touch the ground, turning at the end of the path.
    • If your legs go numb or your back aches, shift position slowly; do not force yourself to stay still.
  3. Back to the ground (2 minutes). Open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, name three things before you stand up.
  4. One act of "passing on the fire". Choose one good thing you learned from someone, and do it today, or teach it to someone else. For example: cook a dish your grandmother taught you, use a saying your teacher often used, show your child how to tie shoelaces the way your father showed you.
  5. Write one line. What did I pass on today, and from whom?
  6. Tick it off. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

4. Kids' corner 6+

"Ice becomes water" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Put an ice cube in a bowl, somewhere sunny or warm.
  2. Sit and watch with the child for 5 minutes. Every minute ask: "What does the ice look like now?"
  3. When the ice has almost melted, ask: "Where did the ice go?" Let the child touch the water in the bowl.
  4. Finish with: "The ice did not disappear. It became water. Water left in the sun for a long time rises into the air as vapour."

Use ice from the freezer; no hot water or flames. The adult holds the bowl.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, a feeling that you or the world are "not real" that lasts, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
  • A feeling of being detached from your body, or of everything seeming strange, stays after you stand up; if it lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
  • Missing someone who has died comes back strongly and does not ease. Then choose a different person for "passing on the fire", or stop this week and call someone you trust.
  • You start skipping meals, sleep or caring for family in order to sit longer.
  • You have a lot of pain in your legs, back or joints. Then sit on a chair, or choose 30 minutes of sitting and 10 of slow walking.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

"Passing on the fire" is close to daynentang's lesson Việc nhỏ có ý nghĩa (in Vietnamese). If you feel unsteady while sitting, see Neo lại (in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Think of one good thing you learned from someone. Do it once today.
