Taoism 1 – Week 5: Three treasures

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read chapter 67 of the Tao Te Ching (paragraphs 2–4) slowly, choose one of the three "treasures" and practise it in one small situation each day.

Measurable goal: practise the chosen treasure once a day, on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] In chapter 67 the speaker of the Tao Te Ching names three things he prizes and holds fast: gentleness, economy, and shrinking from taking precedence of others. Each opens onto something that seems its opposite: gentle yet bold, economical yet liberal, standing behind yet honoured.

Reading it in context

  • [Expert judgement] The Chinese character for the first treasure is 慈 (ci), often used for a parent’s love for a child. Legge translates it as "gentleness", and the course follows him rather than translating it as "compassion", to avoid confusion with the Buddhist concept.
  • "Economy" here means using just enough, not wasting strength or goods. It is not financial advice.
  • The lines about "battle" and "death" belong to a text written in a time of war between states. [Inference] The text does not encourage war; the point is that hardness and the urge to win tend to end in ruin.
  • The course leaves out paragraph 1 of the chapter, where the speaker says his Tao "appears to be inferior to other systems of teaching" while being great, because tamhonkhoe does not set traditions side by side to rank them.
  • Paragraphs 2–4 were read in Convergence 1 – Week 7 "Loving-kindness". This week practises all three treasures, not only the first.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that evaluates practising the "three treasures".
  • [Verified] Closest to the first treasure: a 2024 meta-analysis of 154 studies with 10,189 people found that activities that calm the body (slow breathing, mindfulness, relaxation) reduced anger and aggression moderately (g = −0.63); arousal-raising activities meant to "let off steam" (hitting a punch bag, running) were ineffective overall. (Kjærvik & Bushman 2024)
  • [Verified] Closest to the third treasure: a 2025 meta-analysis of 65 studies found that more humble people had slightly better mental health. This is a correlation, and the associations are small. (Lee et al. 2025)
  • For the second treasure (economy), we found no suitable research.

Limits: this exercise is self-observation, not therapy.

3. Practice for adults

About 12 minutes a day, plus one moment of practice during the day.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read paragraphs 2 and 3. Pause on the three pairs "gentleness – bold", "economy – liberal", "the hindmost place – honour".
  2. Choose one treasure (first day, 2 minutes). Keep the same treasure all week. For example: - Gentleness: speak one notch more quietly when giving feedback; ask "what do you need?" before blaming. - Economy: take only as much food as you need; switch off lights, turn off taps; do no unnecessary extra work in a work session. - Not taking precedence: let others choose first; do not jump the queue; hear the other person’s idea fully before giving yours.
  3. Sit still (5 minutes) as in Week 2.
  4. Write one line in the evening (2 minutes). Where you practised, with whom, what happened afterwards.
  5. Tick the day. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "Three jewels" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Find three small objects of different colours (buttons, bottle tops, large marbles). Name them: the gentle words jewel, the just enough jewel, the you go first jewel.
  2. Tell the child: "Long ago there was a book about three precious jewels that wise people held on to."
  3. Each morning the child chooses one jewel to put in a pocket, and you think together of one thing to do: speak gently to a younger sibling; take just enough rice and finish your own portion; let a friend play first.
  4. In the evening, the child tells about one thing they did with today’s jewel.
  5. To finish: "The most precious jewel is the one we carry in what we do."

If there is a child under 3 in the house, keep the three objects out of their reach (choking risk). No scores, no rewards or punishments; letting others go first is the child’s own choice, and children are not made to give up their own things.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • "Economy" leads you to eat less, sleep less or not spend on real needs; or "not taking precedence" stops you speaking up when you are treated unfairly. Then switch to another treasure or stop the exercise.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
