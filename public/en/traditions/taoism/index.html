Taoism

Taoism 1 – Week 1: Like water

The highest excellence is like water: it benefits all things, takes the low place and does not strive. Each day, choose one moment to yield instead of contending.

Taoism 1 – Week 2: Letting muddy water clear

Who can make muddy water clear? Let it be still, and it will gradually become clear. Sit still for 10 minutes a day without trying to force the mind quiet.

Taoism 1 – Week 3: Returning to stillness

Plants flourish and then return to their root; returning to the root is called stillness. Each day, notice and note one moment when you settle.

Taoism 1 – Week 4: Overcoming oneself

He who overcomes others is strong; he who overcomes himself is mighty; he who is satisfied with his lot is rich. Keep one small task each day and notice one thing you already have enough of.

Taoism 1 – Week 5: Three treasures

Gentleness, economy, shrinking from taking precedence of others: the three treasures of chapter 67. Choose one treasure to practise all week.

Taoism 1 – Week 6: Cook Ding

A cook whose chopper stays sharp for nineteen years, because he follows the natural openings and slows down at the hard parts. Each day, do one familiar task very slowly, then review the whole course.

Taoism 2 – Week 1: Pairs of opposites

As soon as we know beauty as beauty, we have the idea of ugliness. The first week of the course on non-action: notice how you label things in pairs, then sit still for 20 minutes a day without needing to take a side.

Taoism 2 – Week 2: Useful emptiness

A wheel is useful because of the empty space at its hub, a bowl because of its hollow, a room because of the space inside. Sit for 20 minutes a day and leave a 10-minute gap with no plans and no phone.

Taoism 2 – Week 3: Bend and be whole

Free from self-display, and therefore he shines; free from self-boasting, and therefore his merit is acknowledged. Sit for 20 minutes a day, and once a day let go of showing off or arguing to win, in a small and safe matter.

Taoism 2 – Week 4: Less each day

The one who learns adds something each day; the one who follows the Tao lets go of something each day. Sit for 25 minutes a day with fewer instructions, and drop one unnecessary thing.

Taoism 2 – Week 5: Fasting of the mind

Yen Hui had gone without wine and meat for months, but Confucius told him that was not yet "the fasting of the heart". Fasting of the mind is a fast of the mind, not of food. Sit and listen for 25 minutes a day, letting hearing stop with the ears.

Taoism 2 – Week 6: Doing without forcing

Deal with what is difficult while it is easy, do what will become great while it is small. Sit for 25 minutes a day, ease off the effort a little, and take the smallest step of a task you have been putting off.

Taoism 2 – Week 7: Longer practice

No one cut down the li tree because its wood was good for nothing, and so it lived long. One 30-minute session a day, and a short stretch doing something not meant to be "useful".

Taoism 2 – Week 8: Review

The softest thing overcomes the hardest; few attain the teaching without words and the advantage of non-action. The last week: sit for 30 minutes a day, rate the course's seven habits, and keep two.

Taoism 3 – Week 1: The butterfly dream

Zhuangzi dreams he is a butterfly and wakes not knowing whether he is a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming he is a man. Each day, 30 minutes of sitting that notices moments of change, and one change noted from the day.

Taoism 3 – Week 2: When Zhuangzi's wife died

Hui Tzu comes to offer condolences and finds Zhuangzi drumming on a bowl and singing. Zhuangzi says that at first he too was grieved, then remembered that life and death follow each other like the four seasons. Each day, 30 minutes of sitting and one seasonal change in nature noted.

Taoism 3 – Week 3: Transformation

Four friends recognise each other because they see life and death as one body. When Tzu Yu falls ill, he says his left shoulder may become a cock crowing at dawn. Each day, 35 minutes of sitting that notices sensations in the body changing, and one small change in the body noted that needs no fixing.

Taoism 3 – Week 4: The fire passes on

When Lao Tzu died, Ch'in Shih came to mourn and said: the Master came because it was his time to be born, and went because it was his time to die; the fuel is consumed, but the fire may be passed on. Each day one 40-minute session, and one small act carrying on something you learned from someone.

Taoism 3 – Week 5: Reading with someone

The great furnace and the caster: metal does not demand to be made into a famous sword. Each day, 40 minutes of sitting; during the week, read this passage with someone you trust and talk for 15 minutes.

Taoism 3 – Week 6: Review

Near death, Zhuangzi does not want a grand funeral: heaven and earth are his coffin, the sun, moon and stars his burial regalia. Each day, 30–40 minutes of sitting; rate the course's six habits, keep two, and write a four-week plan.

Taoism 4 – Week 1: Skill as flow

A hunchback who practises balancing two, three, then five balls on his pole; an old wheelwright who works neither too slowly nor too fast. Each day, practise one small part of a skill for 5–10 minutes.

Taoism 4 – Week 2: A journey of a thousand miles

Tao Te Ching chapter 64: things are easy to handle while small, a great tree grows from a tiny sprout, a long journey begins with a single step. Each day, deal with one thing while it is still small, and write one "if… then…" sentence for the first step of something big.

Taoism 4 – Week 3: Knowing enough

Tao Te Ching chapter 44 asks which is dearer, fame or life, life or wealth; chapter 46 says the sufficiency of contentment is the one that lasts. Each day, stop once and say "that's enough"; each evening, note one thing that was enough.

Taoism 4 – Week 4: Work

A man who plays for counters plays well; stake gold and he loses his wits. People often ruin their work when it is nearly done. Each day, put the "stake" down for one minute before an important task, and be as careful at the last step as at the first.

Taoism 4 – Week 5: Family

Tao Te Ching chapter 54: the Tao nursed within oneself is then seen in the family and the neighbourhood; chapter 81: the more one gives to others, the more one has. Each day, do one thing for someone at home without keeping score.

Taoism 4 – Week 6: Review

Zhuangzi chapter III: life has a limit, knowledge has none; Tao Te Ching chapter 9: better to leave a vessel unfilled than to carry it full, and step back when the work is done. Drop one commitment this week, rate the course’s six habits, keep two and write a four-week plan.
