Taoism 4 – Week 5: Family

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read paragraphs 2–3 of chapter 54 and paragraph 2 of chapter 81 of the Tao Te Ching slowly. Each morning, start with yourself through one small thing; each day, do one thing for someone at home without mentioning it or counting it. Once in the week, do one task together as a family.

Measurable goal: one "no keeping score" act for someone at home each day, on at least 5 of 7 days; one task done together as a family at least once in the week.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 54 of the Tao Te Ching draws a widening circle: the Tao nursed in one person is then seen in the family, the neighbourhood, the state and the whole world. The text does not start by correcting others, but with oneself. Chapter 81, the last chapter of the book, speaks of the sage who does not accumulate for himself.

Reading it in context

  • Legge translates paragraph 2 of chapter 54 as rhymed verse. Paragraph 1 speaks of descendants offering sacrifices through the generations; the course does not quote it, as it concerns the rites of an older society.
  • [Expert judgement] Legge renders one line as "What riches will accrue!" The Chinese speaks of virtue (德) becoming abundant, not of money. The course reads it as an abundance of virtue, not a promise of wealth.
  • "Not accumulating" in chapter 81 is said of the sage. It does not mean not saving for your family, and it is not financial advice.
  • "No keeping score" in the exercise does not mean one person carrying all the housework. Sharing chores fairly still needs to be talked about. If there is violence or control at home, this exercise is not for that relationship; see Get help now.
  • If you live alone, "home" can mean a flatmate, a neighbour, or a relative far away (a call, a message).
  • [Inference] Starting "from oneself to the family" is close to the image of water in Taoism 1 – Week 1: not forcing others, only doing one’s own part fully.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A 2018 meta-analysis (27 experimental studies, 4,045 people): doing kind things for others raises the doer’s happiness by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
  • [Inference] The "no keeping score" exercise is a kind of deliberate kindness, aimed at the people at home. The result above does not prove this exercise works, and says nothing about the teaching of the Tao Te Ching.

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus one small act at home.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read both passages. The second time, pause at "Tao when nursed within one's self".
  2. Start with yourself (1 minute, morning). Choose one small thing for yourself today in how you behave at home: speak more slowly, do not snap at the first remark, greet each person when you come in.
  3. One act without keeping score (5 minutes, during the day). Do something for someone at home that someone else usually does: wash another person’s cup, hang out the laundry, charge a grandparent’s phone, pour water for someone who is busy. Do not mention it, and do not count who does more.
  4. One task together (once in the week, about 20 minutes). Cook a dish, tidy a corner of the house, water the plants. The adult works alongside, gives few instructions, and lets everyone have a part.
  5. One line in the evening (1 minute). What you did today, and how it felt.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "A kind deed at home": the child chooses someone at home who might need help and does a small "secret" job for them, without needing praise. See the full activity at Kids’ corner – Activity 6. Daynentang has a lesson for families with children aged 2–8: Children’s chores (in Vietnamese).

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The exercise makes you feel you must carry all the housework and you become exhausted, or it reopens an old hurt with someone at home. Then choose someone else for the exercise, or pause it.
  • If the signs are serious, stop and see Get help now.

7. Practice for today

Do one small thing for someone at home, and tell no one.
