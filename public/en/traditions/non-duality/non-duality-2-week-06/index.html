Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 6: Practice and letting go

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: reading Krishna's answer to Arjuna: the mind is hard to hold, but there is a way. The two parts of that way are steady practice and loosening desire.

Measurable goal: sit for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; once a day wait 10 minutes before one small craving (not about food), and note whether you managed to wait.

A note on context: [Inference] In Sanskrit, verse 6:35 names two words the tradition returns to often: abhyāsa (steady practice) and vairāgya (non-attachment, letting go of desire). Arnold renders them as "habit" and "wont of self-command", so "letting go" does not show clearly in this English version. The week's title follows the two Sanskrit words. Verse 6:26 says "as often … so oft": there is no limit on how many times one may come back.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. A small, self-reported study. (Lally et al. 2010)
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 33 studies of self-control training: a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017)
  • [Inference] Habits form slowly and differently in each person, so eight weeks is only a beginning. The evidence for "training willpower" is weaker than many people think; so the lesson puts the weight on regularity and gentle returning, not on straining.

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes). Read both passages. Read again "as often … so oft".
  2. Sit (25 minutes) in the place and posture from Week 3. Count each return as one "practice", not one "defeat".
  3. Let go of one small craving (during the day). Choose a small craving that comes often: picking up your phone when free, buying something online, opening the news. When it comes, wait 10 minutes before acting. After 10 minutes, doing it or not are both fine; the exercise is only the waiting.
  4. Note (1 minute). "Today I managed / did not manage to wait. While waiting, the craving …" (grew, faded, stayed the same).

Choose a craving that has nothing to do with food, medicines or health. If you miss a day of sitting, sit again the next day; do not double up.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "Catch the balloon again" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Blow up a balloon. The child keeps it in the air by tapping it gently upwards.
  2. Each time the balloon falls to the floor, the child picks it up, says "again!" and plays on. The adult counts the "again!"s, not the falls.
  3. At the end, ask: "However many times the balloon fell, you picked it up that many times, right? Is there something you are practising that often 'falls'?"

Play in an open space with nothing breakable. Clear away any burst balloon at once, as small children can choke on the pieces.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • "Self-command" turns into self-punishment: skipping meals, skipping sleep, scolding yourself. The exercise is only waiting 10 minutes and coming back gently.
  • The craving you are practising with is alcohol, drugs, gambling or eating behaviour that worries you. Please find someone with the right expertise rather than practising alone.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
