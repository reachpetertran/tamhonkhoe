Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 7: Daily action

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: back to chapter 3 to join meditation with everyday work. The Gita says that a person who acts well does not act only for themselves: others watch and follow.

Measurable goal: each day, one small useful thing for others that you would like people at home or colleagues to copy, on at least 5 of 7 days; sit for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] In chapter 3, after saying that no one lives without acting (Week 2), Krishna gives one more reason to act: other people look at what we do.

A note on context: [Inference] Janak (Janaka) is a king in Indian tradition, told of as both a ruler and a sage; the Gita names him as an example that one who has reached the goal can still live amid worldly work. "The upholding of thy kind" is Arnold's rendering of the Sanskrit lokasaṅgraha, usually understood as holding together and caring for the world and its people. In the next verses (3:22–24) Krishna says that he himself acts though he needs nothing; the lesson does not go into the part about the Divine.

"The unwise people" is Arnold's wording and not a judgement of anyone. The main point is that what we do is always being watched, especially by the children at home.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 27 experiments, 4,045 people: doing acts of kindness for others raises the doer's own well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
  • [Inference] This research measures the people doing kind acts, not whether others copy them. The lesson has no verified study on "setting an example"; that part rests on the text and on learners' own observation.

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes). Read the passage. Pause at "what best men do The multitude will follow".
  2. Day 1: choose a task (3 minutes). Think of a good habit you would like people at home or colleagues to have. Turn it into a small thing you yourself will do each day: putting the phone away at meals, tidying a shared space after using it, thanking the person who serves you, admitting a mistake.
  3. Do it without saying anything. Do not preach and do not ask anyone to copy you. Just do it, as your own part (Week 2) and without clinging to the fruit (Non-duality 1).
  4. Sit for 25 minutes as in Week 6.
  5. Write one line: "Today I … ; whether anyone noticed does not matter."

At the end of the week, if someone at home starts to do the same, just notice it; do not say "see?".

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "The mirror" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. The adult and child stand face to face. The adult makes very slow movements (raising a hand, pretending to brush hair, pretending to fold a shirt); the child copies like a mirror.
  2. Swap roles: the child leads and the adult copies.
  3. Ask: "At home, who do you see doing something and then you do it too? Is there a good thing you would like your little brother or sister (or a friend) to copy from you?"

Choose slow, safe movements; no fast spinning or jumping.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • "Setting an example" turns into living to be watched, tiring yourself out trying to be perfect, or blaming others for not following. Go back to one small task, done for its own sake.
  • You are in a relationship where nothing you do is ever enough for the other person. Setting an example does not mean accepting being treated badly; please find someone with the right expertise to help.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

For things a family can do together for the place they live, daynentang has a lesson: Cả nhà giúp cộng đồng (The whole family helps the community; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write down the good habit you would like to see at home, and the small thing you will do yourself today.
