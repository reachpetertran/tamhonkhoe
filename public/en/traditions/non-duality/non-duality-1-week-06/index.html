Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads – Week 6: One who hates no being (review)

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: a slow reading of the Gita's portrait of the one who is "loved by Me", choosing friendliness once towards a difficult person, and a review of the whole course.

Measurable goal: slow reading on at least 5 of 7 days; once this week, choose friendliness towards a difficult person; at the end of the week rate the six habits, keep two and write a 4-week plan.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 12 of the Gita is about the path of devotion (bhakti). At the end of the chapter, Krishna describes the person he loves through many qualities; each passage ends with "That man I love!". The first passage was read in Convergence 1 – Week 7: Loving-kindness, alongside teaching on loving-kindness from Buddhism and Taoism. It is read again here to close the course.

A note on context: [Inference] This passage gathers many ideas from earlier weeks: unchanged by good or ill (Week 2), mastering oneself (Weeks 3 and 4), free of love of self (Weeks 1 and 5). In the Gita, these qualities are tied to devotion directed to Krishna ("Seeking Me… vowed unto Me"). The lesson describes this; it does not ask learners to be devoted to anyone. Readers in the non-dual tradition and readers in the devotional traditions understand the "Me" here differently.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] 21 trials of loving-kindness and compassion meditation in the workplace: lower burnout and stress, higher self-compassion; results vary a lot between studies. (Wang et al. 2024)
  • [Verified] 27 experiments, 4,045 people: acts of kindness raise well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
  • [Inference] There is no study of being friendly to a "difficult person" in the way this lesson suggests. The lesson chooses one small, safe act, once in the week.

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day; 20 extra minutes for review at the end of the week.

  1. Read slowly (3 minutes). Read the Gita passage twice. Underline one quality you want to practise this week.
  2. Choose a difficult person (day 1, 2 minutes). A colleague who is often irritable, a noisy neighbour, a relative who criticises. Choose only someone who is unpleasant, not someone who has harmed you.
  3. One friendly act (once this week). Greeting them first, a word of thanks, holding a door, asking how they are. They do not need to respond. Write one line: "I did …; I noticed …".
  4. End-of-week review (20 minutes). Rate yourself 0–3 (0 = not done, 3 = now a habit) on six habits: - Doing one task fully and letting go of the result (Week 1) - Watching a small discomfort until it passes (Week 2) - One encouraging sentence to yourself each morning (Week 3) - Noticing the "horse" and pausing for one breath (Week 4) - Slow reading; noticing one moment of wanting and one thing you have (Week 5) - One friendly act towards a difficult person (Week 6)
  5. Keep two habits. Choose the two that are easiest to keep or that you like best, not necessarily the two with the highest scores.
  6. A 4-week plan. For each habit, write an "if… then…" sentence (for example: "If I have just sat down at my desk in the morning, then I write my encouraging sentence"). After 4 weeks, rate them again.

Very important: "Hating no being" does not mean you must be close to, forgive or put up with someone who is harming you. You can hold no hatred for someone and still keep your distance, say "no", or ask others to step in.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "Sending good wishes": the child thinks of a good wish for someone in the family, for a friend, and then for someone they do not know well. See the full activity in Kids' corner – Activity 7.

Do not make the child wish well to someone they are angry with or afraid of. At the end of the week, the child chooses the game from the course they liked best (the tower and the wind, soap bubbles, cheering myself on, the driver and the horse, look really closely) to play again.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • Thinking about the "difficult person" brings back memories of bullying, abuse or violence. Switch to an easier person, or leave this part out.
  • You feel you must act kindly while you are being treated badly. In that case, what you need to do is protect yourself.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

7. Practice for today

Underline one quality in the Gita passage and write one small thing you will do tomorrow to practise it.
