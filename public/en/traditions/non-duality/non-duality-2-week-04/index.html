Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 4: Moderation in food and sleep

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: a slow reading of the Gita's lines on moderation, and choosing a steady rhythm of sleeping, waking, working and resting, so that the sitting has a place to stand.

Measurable goal: wake within about 30 minutes of the same time on at least 5 of 7 days; sit for 20 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Right after describing how to sit, Krishna says that meditation does not go with any excess, including excess on the side of austerity.

A note on context: [Inference] Arnold uses "Religion" and "piety" for the word other versions usually translate as "yoga". The main point of the two verses is balance: the Gita does not praise fasting or sleepless nights as a path, but treats measured eating, sleeping, working and resting as the ground of meditation. The lesson gives no numbers for food or sleep; nor does the text.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 86 publications, 38,124 workers: people who detach from work when off duty report less exhaustion and better sleep. This is a correlation and does not prove cause. (Wendsche and Lohmann-Haislah 2017)
  • [Verified] Joint WHO and ILO estimates: in 2016 about 488 million people worked 55 or more hours a week, and about 745,000 deaths from ischemic heart disease and stroke were attributed to such long hours. These are model-based estimates. (Pega et al. 2021)
  • [Inference] These sources are about work and rest and do not measure the Gita's teaching. They only show that the line about not wearing one's strength to waste meets current understanding of health at work.

3. Practice for adults

About 25 minutes a day.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes). Read the passage. Underline the pairs of excess: fasting/feasting, sleeping/waking.
  2. Day 1: look back on a week (3 minutes). For each pair, which way do you lean? Just write it down; do not grade yourself.
  3. Choose one rhythm (1 minute). Choose one thing to keep steady all week, preferably your waking time: "I wake at about …". If you like, add a time to switch off screens in the evening.
  4. Sit for 20 minutes in the corner and posture from Week 3. If you can, sit after waking, before looking at your phone.
  5. Write one line: today's waking time; whether the sitting was sleepy or restless.

This lesson is not a diet and not medical advice. Do not skip meals and do not count calories. If you work shifts, travel a lot for work, have young children or are caring for someone who is ill, choose a rhythm that fits; keeping it steady for 3 days is already a step.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "Good night to each toy" (5–10 minutes before bedtime, with an adult)

  1. Before bedtime, the child picks three toys and puts each one back in its place, saying: "Good night, dinosaur" (or the toy's name).
  2. Switch off the lights one at a time while the child counts "one, two, three".
  3. Lie down, take three slow breaths together, and the adult asks: "Today your body ran and jumped and ate, and now it is time to rest. Which part of you feels most tired?"

Keep the same time each evening this week. This is a bedtime routine; it has nothing to do with eating or skipping meals.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The exercise makes you anxious or self-blaming about food, weight or body shape, or makes you want to skip meals. Stop this part and talk to a doctor or someone with the right expertise.
  • You are following a diet or sleep schedule set by a doctor (diabetes, pregnancy, a chronic illness). Keep to your doctor's advice; this lesson does not replace it.
  • Insomnia lasts for weeks, or you are dangerously sleepy while driving or working. See a doctor.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.

7. Practice for today

Write down the time you will wake tomorrow, and put your phone away from the bed tonight.
