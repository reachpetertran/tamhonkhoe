Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 8: Review – no one is lost

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: reading Krishna's comfort for the practitioner who has not reached the goal, looking back over eight weeks, and choosing two habits to keep.

Measurable goal: sit for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; rate the course's 8 habits; choose 2 habits and write a 4-week plan for them.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] After the answer in Week 6, Arjuna asks further: does someone who has faith but fails in the striving, falling back halfway, vanish like a cloud that breaks apart? Krishna answers:

A note on context: [Inference] "Son of Pritha" is Arjuna (Pritha is another name for his mother, Kunti). The verses right after (6:41–45) say that the practitioner who did not succeed continues the path in a later life; that is the tradition's belief in rebirth, and the course does not discuss it. The lesson takes only the meaning closest to today's learner: the effort of practice is not lost because you have not done it perfectly.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies, 19,951 people: monitoring your own progress made people more likely to reach their goals, by a small-to-moderate amount (d = 0.40); the effect was larger when progress was written down or shared with others. (Harkin et al. 2016)
  • [Verified] In a small study of habit formation, missing one occasion did not materially affect a habit becoming automatic. (Lally et al. 2010)
  • [Inference] These two findings fit the review below: write it down, choose little, and do not give up after one miss. No study has evaluated this course directly.

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes). Read the passage.
  2. Sit for 25 minutes each day. If you like, try 30 minutes on one day of the week; this is optional.
  3. Rate the 8 habits (day 1, 10 minutes). Rate each 0–3 (0 = not done, 3 = has become a habit):
    1. Noting what went well, what did not, and how stirred up you were (Week 1)
    2. An "if… then…" plan for something you tend to avoid (Week 2)
    3. A fixed place to sit, body, neck and head in one line (Week 3)
    4. A steady waking time (Week 4)
    5. Naming where the mind ran, then coming back (Week 5)
    6. Waiting 10 minutes before a small craving (Week 6)
    7. One small act as an example each day (Week 7)
    8. Sitting 20–25 minutes regularly (whole course)
  4. Keep 2 (5 minutes). Choose two habits to keep: one for "action", one for "meditation". Write an "if… then…" sentence for each.
  5. A 4-week plan. Each week, a box to tick for each day. If you miss a day, start again the next; if you miss a whole week, go back to a lighter level (15 minutes of sitting).

4. Kids' corner 6+

The child chooses a favourite game (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

Go over the course's games with the child: the paper boat and the wave, the mountain tree, good night to each toy, the lamp in the wind, catch the balloon again, the mirror. The child chooses one to play again. Then ask: "Which game would you like to keep playing every week?" Write it on the child's calendar.

Do not score the child. If they do not like any of the games, that is fine.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The rating sheet makes you blame yourself harshly. The sheet is only for choosing, not for judging you as a person.
  • You want to increase sitting to very long periods (more than 45 minutes a session) without guidance. This course stops at 25–30 minutes; longer sitting is best done with a teacher in the tradition or an experienced guide.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
