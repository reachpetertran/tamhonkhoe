Presence in ordinary tasks – Week 5: Caring for others

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: many of an adult's hours go into caring for others: feeding a child, taking a parent to the doctor, helping a colleague, checking on a friend who is ill. This week reads two Gita verses about seeing the same Life in all beings.

Measurable goal: each day, one act of caring for someone else that begins with a one-breath pause and includes at least 2 minutes of listening without interrupting, on at least 5 of 7 days; each day, one small act of caring for yourself, on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Near the end of chapter 6, after the teaching on meditation (read in Non-duality 2), Krishna describes how a person settled in meditation sees the world.

A note on context: [Inference] "Life-Soul" is Arnold's word for the Self. Verses 6:30–31, in between, speak of one who sees Krishna in all things; the lesson does not quote them, as they belong to the teaching on devotion.

[Expert judgement] "Taught by his own similitude" corresponds to a Sanskrit word that many translations take to mean: seeing others' pleasure and pain as one's own. Arnold renders it loosely as "the Evil and the Good". Non-dual readers take the verse to mean that all beings share one Self; devotional readers take it to mean that all beings dwell in God. The lesson uses what they share: the person you are caring for also wants to be at ease and not to suffer, just as you do.

"Taught by his own similitude" also means that you are inside that circle. Caring for others while neglecting yourself is not yet whole.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] 27 experiments, 4,045 people: doing acts of kindness for others raises the doer's own well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 148 studies, 308,849 people: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation. (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010)
  • [Inference] Neither study measured long-term care of a family member, which can be very heavy. That is why the lesson adds care for yourself. No study has evaluated this week's exercise.

3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes, inside the caring you already do.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes). Read both passages. Pause at "Taught by his own similitude".
  2. One breath before caring. Before feeding a child, helping a parent up, or answering a message from someone who is sad: pause for one breath and think, "This person, like me, wants to be at ease and not to suffer." This sentence is tamhonkhoe's suggestion.
  3. Two minutes of listening. While caring, listen to the other person for at least 2 minutes without interrupting, without rushing to advise and without holding your phone. With a baby who cannot talk yet, watch and listen to the baby.
  4. Do the caring task fully. Whatever your hands are doing, attend to it: the spoon, an older person's hand, the damp cloth.
  5. One thing for yourself. Each day one small thing: drink a glass of water when thirsty, sit down for 5 minutes, ask someone to take over for a while, go to bed earlier.
  6. Write one line: "Today I cared for … ; I heard … ; I cared for myself by …".

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "A kind deed at home": the child chooses a small task to do for someone in the family, then tells how that person felt. See the full activity at Kids' corner – Activity 6.

Add one small question at the end: "And what did you do today that was good for yourself?"

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • You have been caring for someone ill or elderly for months and feel exhausted, irritable, sleepless or numb. That is a sign you need help, not a sign you have not practised enough. Share the work, ask family for help and see a doctor.
  • The person you care for harms you in words or actions. Seeing Life in all beings does not mean accepting harm; protect yourself first.
  • The person you care for has thoughts of self-harm or is in danger. Do not manage alone; find someone with the right expertise straight away.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

Keeping and nourishing the relationships around you: Kết nối (Connection; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write down the name of one person you will care for tomorrow, and one small thing you will do for yourself.
