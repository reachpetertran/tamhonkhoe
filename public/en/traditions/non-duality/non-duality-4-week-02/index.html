Presence in ordinary tasks – Week 2: Working a full life

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read verse 2 of the Isha Upanishad, the verse about a human life lived in the middle of work. In Week 1 you did a small task you chose; this week choose a task you tend to put off.

Measurable goal: each day, do one task you tend to put off (5–15 minutes) fully, with a sentence of intention before starting, on at least 5 of 7 days; set a time to stop work in the evening and keep it on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Isha Upanishad opens with "all this … should be covered by the Lord" and "do not covet the wealth of any man" (read in Non-duality 1 – Week 5). The second verse speaks of a person who still lives in the world and still works.

A note on context: the words in brackets are the translator's. [Inference] In his commentary Paramananda reads this verse as a second path: someone who still clings to long life and possessions, and cannot yet follow the path of Self-knowledge in verse 1, follows the path of right action. "Karma" here, he says, means action without selfish motive; the same action binds when done from desire and frees when done in surrender.

[Expert judgement] The verse does not say "work as much as you can". "A hundred years" is a whole ordinary life, with work, rest and growing old. The lesson reads it as: do not wait to escape from work before beginning a spiritual life; live it inside the work you are already doing. Schools read verses 1 and 2 together in different ways; reviewers from within the tradition will add to this.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] Joint WHO and ILO estimates: in 2016 about 488 million people worked 55 or more hours a week; about 745,000 deaths from ischemic heart disease and stroke were attributed to such long hours. These are model estimates. (Pega et al. 2021)
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis covering 38,124 workers: people who detach from work when off duty report less exhaustion and better sleep. This is a correlation. (Wendsche and Lohmann-Haislah 2017)
  • [Inference] Neither study says anything about the Upanishads. The lesson uses them as a reminder: "living by working" does not mean working without rest. That is why this week also has a time to stop work.

3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day, plus a time to stop work.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes). Read the verse. Pause at "there is no other way".
  2. Choose a task you put off (day 1). An everyday task you tend to delay: answering a hard email, clearing a drawer, taking out the rubbish, scrubbing a pot, making a phone call. It can be a different task each day, as long as it is one you put off.
  3. A sentence of intention (10 seconds) as in Week 1, then do the whole task for 5–15 minutes. Notice where the reluctance sits in your body: shoulders, stomach, breath. No need to chase it away; just keep going.
  4. A time to stop work. Choose an evening time (for example 9 pm) to stop work for your job: close the laptop, do not open email. If your job has shifts, choose a real break within the shift.
  5. Write one line: "The task I put off today was …; when it was done I felt …; stop time: kept / not kept."

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "One song, one job" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Choose a song the child likes, about 3 minutes long.
  2. While it plays, the child does a job they tend to avoid: putting toys away, lining books up on the shelf. The adult also does a job they avoid.
  3. When the song ends, stop, finished or not. Ask: "Did you feel like not doing it at the start? How about now?"

No racing to see who is faster. The adult plays the music; the child does not use the device alone.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The task you "put off" is actually dangerous, against your conscience, or something you are being forced to do. This lesson does not teach putting up with that; ask someone with the right expertise.
  • You have been exhausted for weeks, feel nothing while working, or cannot stop working even when you try. Tell someone close to you and see a doctor.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

When a task you put off brings circling worry: Lo lắng vòng vo (Circling worry; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write down one task you are putting off, and the time you will stop work tonight.
