Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 2: Doing your part

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: a slow reading of the opening of chapter 3 of the Gita, where Krishna says that no one can live without acting, and practising doing "your own part" steadily.

Measurable goal: choose one thing you tend to avoid; do it following an "if… then…" plan on at least 5 of 7 days; sit quietly for 15 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] At the start of chapter 3 Arjuna asks: if understanding is higher than action, why tell him to act? Krishna answers that giving up action brings no one to perfection, because no one can really stand outside action.

A note on context: [Inference] The Sanskrit behind Arnold's "allotted task" is usually understood as the work that is one's duty to do. The tradition links this duty to dharma, and schools explain dharma differently, especially where it is tied to a person's place in society. The course does not go into that; the lesson takes only the meaning closest to everyday life: the work your roles (parent, worker, member of a household) ask of you. Verse 3:6, between the two passages, says that someone who sits still outwardly while inwardly dwelling on pleasures is a hypocrite; the lesson does not quote it.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests with more than 8,000 people: "if… then…" plans ("if it is 7 pm, then I open the tax folder") helped people reach goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65); goals and measures varied widely across studies. (Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006)
  • [Inference] The research measures how to get started on a task, not the Gita's spirit of "not seeking gain". The lesson uses it as a tool for starting; the "not seeking gain" part is something learners observe for themselves.

3. Practice for adults

About 20 minutes a day.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes). Read both passages and pause at "Do thine allotted task!".
  2. Day 1: choose one task (3 minutes). Something that is your part and that you tend to avoid: a phone call, clearing one corner of the house, reading a story to your child, sorting papers. It should be small enough to do in 10–15 minutes.
  3. Write an "if… then…" plan (1 minute). "If it is [time/moment], at [place], then I will [do what, for 10 minutes]."
  4. Do it. While doing it, check once: am I doing this because it needs doing, or because I want praise or fear blame? No need to change anything; just note it.
  5. Sit quietly (15 minutes) as in Week 1.
  6. Write one line: "Today I did my part: …".

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "A kind deed at home": the child chooses someone at home who might need help and does a small job for them, without needing praise. See the full activity at Kids’ corner – Activity 6. Daynentang has a lesson for families with children aged 2–8: Children’s chores (in Vietnamese).

Choose a job that suits the child; no knives, hot water or breakable things.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • "Doing your part" turns into carrying everyone else's work, working beyond your strength or giving up rest. The teaching speaks of your part, not the whole household's.
  • The thing you avoid is avoided out of fear or a painful memory, not laziness. Then talk with someone who has the right expertise rather than forcing yourself.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.

7. Practice for today

Write the "if… then…" sentence for the thing you tend to avoid, and put it where you will see it.
