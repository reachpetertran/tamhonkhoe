Presence in ordinary tasks – Week 6: Review

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: closing the course, and the whole Non-duality section (four courses). Read one Gita verse from chapter 2 that links meditation and action, then review the five habits of the course.

Measurable goal: each day, repeat one habit from the course (your choice), on at least 5 of 7 days; at the end of the week rate the five habits, keep two and write a 4-week plan.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] In chapter 2, after the teaching on acting without clinging to the fruit (read in Non-duality 1 – Week 1), Krishna says more about how the mind and action are linked.

A note on context: [Inference] "Devotion" and "meditation" here are Arnold's renderings of the word yoga. The verse is often quoted for its second half, which many translations give as "yoga is skill in action"; Arnold renders it loosely as "with perfect meditation Comes perfect act". "Casting aside good deeds and bad" does not mean no longer telling right from wrong; in the flow of chapter 2 it means not clinging to one's own merits or faults.

[Expert judgement] This verse ties the whole Non-duality section together: the first three courses read scripture and practised sitting still; this course carries that attention into action. The two go together; neither replaces the other.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies, 19,951 people: interventions that help people monitor their progress made them more likely to reach their goals, by a small-to-medium amount (d = 0.40); the effect was larger when progress was physically recorded or reported to others. (Harkin et al. 2016)
  • [Verified] 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally et al. 2010)
  • [Inference] That is why the lesson suggests writing the ratings on paper and keeping only two habits. Six weeks is often not enough for a habit to settle; missing a day is fine.

3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day; plus 20 minutes of review at the end of the week.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes). Read the passage. Pause at "with perfect meditation Comes perfect act".
  2. One habit each day. Choose any habit below and do it again.
  3. End-of-week review (20 minutes). Rate each of the five habits 0–3 (0 = not done, 3 = it has become routine):
    • One small household task done fully, with a sentence of intention (Week 1)
    • One task you tend to put off; keeping a time to stop work (Week 2)
    • One 25-minute block doing one thing only, then "enough for today" (Week 3)
    • Before a meal, 3 breaths, thanks, 5 slow mouthfuls (Week 4)
    • One breath and 2 minutes of listening when caring for someone; one thing for yourself (Week 5)
  4. Keep two habits. Choose the two that are easiest to keep or that you like most, not necessarily the two with the highest scores.
  5. A 4-week plan. For each habit, write one "if… then…" sentence (for example: "If I have just sat down to dinner, then I pause for 3 breaths"). Put it where you will see it. After 4 weeks, rate again.
  6. Next step (optional). If you want to go deeper in the Hindu tradition, look for a trustworthy teacher or community; tamhonkhoe is only a starting point and no substitute for a teacher. If you want to keep the sitting practice, go back to the schedule in Non-duality 2.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "Which game do you like best?" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Recall the course's five games: a small gift for the family, one song one job, the little worker, where did this rice come from, a kind deed at home.
  2. The child chooses one game to play again this week, and says why they like it.
  3. The adult also says which game they liked best.

There is no right or wrong game.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The rating makes you blame yourself or feel you have failed. A low score only shows where to start; drop the rating and keep the single easiest habit.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

One small meaningful action each day, step by step: Việc nhỏ có ý nghĩa (A small meaningful action; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write down the two habits you want to keep, each with one "if… then…" sentence.
