Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 5: The restless mind

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: reading Arjuna's complaint about the restless mind, and the image of the lamp in a sheltered place. Sitting grows to 25 minutes.

Measurable goal: sit for at least 20 minutes on 5 of 7 days, including at least 3 days of the full 25 minutes; after each sitting note where the mind tended to run (work, people, worries, plans).

A note on context: [Inference] "Slayer of Madhu" is a title of Krishna, from the story of his slaying the demon Madhu. The Gita does not treat Arjuna's words as wrong; Krishna's answer (next week) begins by agreeing that the mind is hard to hold. The unflickering lamp is an image of the goal, not something a beginner must have at once.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 29 randomised trials, 2,535 people: mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) reduced rumination by a moderate amount (SMD = −0.51), with effects held at follow-up. (Wei et al. 2025)
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 29 studies, 2,668 healthy adults: the MBSR programme reduced stress, anxiety and depression moderately (g = 0.53), but the studies varied widely. (Khoury et al. 2015)
  • [Inference] MBCT and MBSR are guided programmes rooted in Buddhist meditation, not meditation as taught in the Gita. Both teach the same basic move: noticing that the mind has gone and bringing it back. This lesson only borrows that move; no study has evaluated this course.

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day.

  1. Read slowly (3 minutes). Read both passages. Read again the line "To hold the wayward wind".
  2. Take the posture as in Week 3: the fixed place, body, neck and head straight, eyes lowered.
  3. Sit (20–25 minutes). Rest the mind on the point you chose in Week 3. Each time you notice the mind has run off, silently note one word for where it went ("work", "people", "worry", "plan"), then bring it back. Do not count the times and do not blame yourself.
  4. Note (2 minutes). Where did the mind run most today? Was there any short moment when the mind was like the "lamp"?

The mind running off and being brought back is the exercise itself, not a sign of doing it wrong. If 25 minutes is too long, sit for 20 and keep it steady.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "The lamp in the wind" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Cut a thin strip of paper about the length of a hand and tape one end to the edge of a table. This is the "lamp".
  2. The child blows gently: the lamp flickers. The adult cups their hands around the strip and the child blows again: the lamp stays stiller.
  3. Ask: "When is there wind inside you?" (noise, rushing, someone grabbing your toy). "What helps you shelter from the wind?" (slow breaths, a hug, sitting still for a moment).

Use paper; never candles or real flame.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • During long sittings painful memories or intrusive thoughts keep returning and growing stronger. Open your eyes, stand up, walk around; shorten the sitting and talk to someone with the right expertise.
  • You start criticising yourself every day for a mind that is "too scattered". Go back to 15 minutes.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
