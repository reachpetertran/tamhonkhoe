Presence in ordinary tasks – Week 3: Worship through one’s own work

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: from housework to your main work, the work you earn a living by, or your studies. In its last chapter the Gita says that each person's work, done carefully and contentedly, is also a form of worship.

Measurable goal: each day, one 25-minute block of main work doing one thing only (no email, no social media), starting with a sentence of intention, on at least 5 of 7 days; at the end of the block write one line: "enough for today".

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 18 is the Gita's summing-up. In this passage Krishna speaks about each person's work.

A note on context: [Inference] Just before this passage (18:41–44) the Gita describes the work of the four varnas, the social classes of ancient Indian society. How to understand those verses in today's society is a matter of many views, including within the Hindu tradition itself; the lesson does not discuss it, and does not take "the work allotted him" as work fixed by birth. It uses only the shared idea: the work in front of you, done carefully, can be a form of worship (for believers) or a way of being present (for others).

"Every work hath blame, as every flame is wrapped in smoke" is from verse 18:48. The lesson uses it to say: doing work carefully does not mean doing it without mistakes.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 88 studies with more than 11,000 people: the amount of deliberate practice explained about 26% of the differences in performance in games, 21% in music, 18% in sports, 4% in education and less than 1% in professions. This is mostly correlational. (Macnamara et al. 2014)
  • [Inference] The results of work, especially in professions, depend on many things beyond one's own effort. This fits the spirit of the passage: do your part fully, be content, and do not measure your worth only by results. No study has evaluated working "as worship".

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day, within your working or study hours.

  1. Read slowly (2 minutes, in the morning). Read both passages. Pause at "content".
  2. Choose a block of main work (25 minutes). The most important task of the day: writing a report, preparing a lesson, repairing a machine, cooking lunch for the shop, studying a chapter. Set a timer for 25 minutes.
  3. A sentence of intention (10 seconds). As in Week 1. Believers use the words of their own tradition; others might say: "For these 25 minutes I will do this one thing fully."
  4. One thing only. Close email and turn off notifications. When something else pulls at you, write it on paper and come back. If someone needs you urgently, stop; that too is the task in front of you.
  5. "Enough for today" (1 minute). When time is up, write one line: "I did …; still left …; enough for today." Remember "every flame is wrapped in smoke".

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "The little worker" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. The child chooses a "job" at home: table-wiper, chopstick-setter, towel-folder.
  2. The adult asks: "What will the … do today?" The child does the job very carefully for a few minutes.
  3. Afterwards the adult thanks them for exactly what they did ("Thank you for wiping right into the corner"). If a spot was missed, say: "Everyone misses a bit; we can do more tomorrow."

No scores, and no comparing with brothers or sisters.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • "Being content with your work" is being used to make you accept dangerous work, unfair pay, harassment or coercion. Contentment does not mean accepting those things; you have the right to seek help and to change jobs.
  • You notice you are working so as not to think about something painful, or you cannot stop. Tell someone close to you and see someone with the right expertise.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

When the main task is a problem to untangle step by step: Giải quyết vấn đề (Solving problems; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write down tomorrow's main task and the sentence of intention you will use before the 25-minute block.
