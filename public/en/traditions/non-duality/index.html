Non-duality / Presence

Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads – Week 1: Work without clinging to fruits

Bhagavad Gita, chapter 2, verse 47: let right deeds be the motive, not their fruit. Each day do one task as well as you can, then let go of the result.

Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads – Week 2: Heat and cold pass

Bhagavad Gita, chapter 2, verses 14–15: heat and cold, sorrow and joy come and go. Each day note one small discomfort and how long it takes to pass.

Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads – Week 3: Lifting yourself up

Bhagavad Gita, chapter 6, verses 5–6: raise yourself up, do not trample yourself down; you can be your own friend or your own enemy. Each morning, say one encouraging word to yourself.

Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads – Week 4: The chariot

Katha Upanishad 1.3, verses 3–6: the body is the chariot, the senses the horses, the mind the reins, the intellect the driver. All week, watch one ‘horse’ – one of your habits.

Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads – Week 5: All is covered by the Lord

Isha Upanishad, verses 1 and 6: all things should be covered by the Lord; do not covet anyone's wealth; see all beings in the Self. Slow reading for 10 minutes a day.

Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads – Week 6: One who hates no being (review)

Bhagavad Gita, chapter 12, verses 13–14: the one who hates nothing that lives, benign and compassionate. Once, choose friendliness towards a difficult person, and review the six habits of the course.

Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 1: Even-minded in success and failure

Bhagavad Gita 2:48 calls evenness of mind towards good and ill ‘yoga’. The first week of a Level 2 course: sit quietly for 15 minutes a day and note one thing that went well, one that did not, and how you reacted.

Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 2: Doing your part

Gita 3:5 and 3:7–8: no one lives without acting, so do the task that is yours. Each day do one thing you tend to avoid, with an ‘if… then…’ plan, and sit quietly for 15 minutes.

Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 3: The seat and posture

Gita 6:10–14 describes how to sit in meditation: a clean, quiet place, a seat neither too high nor too low, body, neck and head straight and still, the gaze towards the tip of the nose. Sit for 20 minutes a day following those directions.

Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 4: Moderation in food and sleep

Gita 6:16–17: yoga is not for one who eats too much or fasts too much, sleeps too much or keeps too many vigils, but for one who is moderate. This week keep a steady time for sleeping and waking, and sit for 20 minutes a day.

Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 5: The restless mind

Gita 6:33–34: Arjuna says holding the mind is as hard as holding the wind. Gita 6:19: the meditator's mind is like a lamp that does not flicker in a sheltered place. This week sit for 25 minutes and practise coming back whenever the mind runs off, without blaming yourself.

Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 6: Practice and letting go

Gita 6:35–36: the mind is hard to hold, but it can be restrained by habit and self-command. Gita 6:26: as often as the mind runs off, so often bring it back. This week sit for 25 minutes steadily and each day practise waiting 10 minutes before one small craving.

Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 7: Daily action

Gita 3:20–21: act for the upholding of others, because what the best do, the many follow. This week, each day, do one small useful thing for others as a quiet example, and sit for 25 minutes.

Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita – Week 8: Review – no one is lost

Gita 6:40: one who strove on the path but did not arrive is still not lost. Review week: sit for 25 minutes, rate the course's 8 habits, keep 2 and make a 4-week plan.

BN1 Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads

A Level 1 course of slow reading in short passages from the Bhagavad Gita and the Upanishads, two kinds of text that many Hindu traditions hold as scripture. Working without clinging to fruits (Gita 2:47), heat and cold that come and go (2:14–15), raising yourself up and being your own friend (6:5–6), the image of the chariot and the horses (Katha Upanishad 1.3.3–6), all things covered by the Lord and not coveting what is another's (Isha Upanishad 1, 6), the one who hates no being (Gita 12:13–14). The exercises are small everyday ones: doing a task fully and letting go of the result, watching a small discomfort until it passes, one encouraging sentence each morning, noticing a habit and pausing for one breath, slow reading, and one friendly act towards a difficult person.

6 weeks · Level 1 · 10–15 min/day

BN2 Karma yoga and meditation in the Gita

A Level 2 course reading chapters 2, 3 and 6 of the Bhagavad Gita on two paths: action without seeking gain (karma yoga) and meditation. Even-mindedness in success and failure (2:48, 2:56), no one lives without acting (3:5, 3:7–8), the seat, posture and gaze in meditation (6:10–14), moderation in food and sleep (6:16–17), a mind as hard to hold as the wind and the lamp sheltered from the wind (6:33–34, 6:19), steady practice and letting go (6:35–36, 6:26), acting for others (3:20–21), one who strives is not lost (6:40). Each day you sit for 15 minutes, rising to 25, following the Gita's description of seat and posture, along with one small everyday task.

8 weeks · Level 2 · 20–30 min/day
