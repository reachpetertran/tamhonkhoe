Buddhism

Buddhism 1 – Week 1: Sitting with the breath

The Discourse on Mindfulness of Breathing begins with sitting down and knowing the in-breath and the out-breath. Sit for 10 minutes a day, just knowing the breath.

Buddhism 1 – Week 2: The four truths in your own words

By tradition, the Buddha’s first discourse: the middle way and the four noble truths. Read slowly, then rewrite them in your own words.

Buddhism 1 – Week 3: Speech

Two Dhammapada verses on defeating anger with kindness and speaking the truth. Each day, pause for one breath before speaking, and practise speech that is true and gentle.

Buddhism 1 – Week 4: Non-harm

All tremble at the rod, all love life: treat others like yourself. Each day, choose and carry out one deliberate act of non-harm.

Buddhism 1 – Week 5: Loving-kindness

The Discourse on Love: live uprightly and gently, then unfold a heart of love to all beings. Ten minutes a day of good wishes in widening circles.

Buddhism 1 – Week 6: Testing for yourself

The discourse to the Kālāmas: when you know for yourselves what leads to harm and what leads to welfare. The last week looks back at five habits using your own notes.

Buddhism 2 – Week 1: Mindfulness of the body (1) – The breath

The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation (MN 10) names four places to set up mindfulness: body, feelings, mind and principles. The first week starts with the body, through the breath. Sit for 15 minutes a day, building up to 20.

Buddhism 2 – Week 2: Mindfulness of the body (2) – Postures, daily tasks, the elements

The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation takes mindfulness beyond sitting: knowing that you are walking, standing, sitting or lying down, and being aware in every small task. Walk slowly for 10 minutes and sit for 15 minutes a day.

Buddhism 2 – Week 3: Mindfulness of feelings

The second place of mindfulness is feeling: every experience has a tone that is pleasant, painful or neutral. Sit for 20 minutes a day and notice the tone of experience.

Buddhism 2 – Week 4: The second arrow

The Arrow discourse (SN 36.6) separates physical pain from the suffering the mind adds to it. Sit for 25 minutes a day and write down one time you noticed "the second arrow".

Buddhism 2 – Week 5: Mindfulness of mind

The third place of mindfulness is the mind: knowing whether it has greed, hate or delusion, whether it is constricted or scattered. Sit for 25 minutes a day and write one line of "mind weather".

Buddhism 2 – Week 6: Mindfulness of principles – The five hindrances

The fourth place of mindfulness is principles. Its first part covers the five hindrances: sensual desire, ill will, dullness, restlessness and doubt. Sit for 25–30 minutes a day and notice which "guest" is visiting.

Buddhism 2 – Week 7: Replacing thoughts

The discourse on how to stop thinking (MN 20) offers ways to handle an unskilful thought that keeps coming back: replace it with a skilful one, look at its drawbacks, pay it no attention, calm it step by step. Sit for 30 minutes a day and try these in order.

Buddhism 2 – Week 8: Two kinds of thought, and review

In the discourse on two kinds of thought (MN 19), the Buddha recalls dividing his thoughts into two classes before his awakening, and seeing that what you often think about becomes the mind’s inclination. Sit for 30 minutes a day, review the four kinds of mindfulness and choose how to practise after the course.

Buddhism 3 – Week 1: The characteristic of not-self

The discourse on the characteristic of not-self (SN 22.59) examines the five aggregates with one simple question: can we command it? Sit for 30 minutes a day and write down one thing each day that did not go the way you wanted.

Buddhism 3 – Week 2: The lump of foam

The discourse on a lump of foam (SN 22.95) compares the five aggregates to foam, a bubble, a mirage, a banana trunk and a magic trick. Sit for 30 minutes a day and practise seeing sensations and sounds arise and dissolve.

Buddhism 3 – Week 3: The cobra and the raft

The simile of the cobra (MN 22) teaches that the teaching is a raft for crossing over, not for carrying, and that grasping it wrongly is like grabbing a cobra by the tail. Sit for 25 minutes a day and write about one of your own 'rafts'.

Buddhism 3 – Week 4: The root of all things

The discourse on the root of all things (MN 1) describes how the mind perceives something and then builds 'mine' on top of it. Sit for 35 minutes a day and practise telling apart what you see and hear from what you add.

Buddhism 3 – Week 5: Longer practice – giving up what isn’t yours

The simile of the cobra (MN 22) ends with advice to give up what isn’t yours, using the image of grass and leaves in Jeta’s Grove. This week extends practice to 40–45 minutes, with slow walking in the middle, and lets go of one small thing each day.

Buddhism 3 – Week 6: Review

Read again the three key passages of the course: 'this is not mine', the foam verses and the raft. Sit for 30 minutes a day with the exercise that suits you best, rate the five exercises and write a four-week plan.
