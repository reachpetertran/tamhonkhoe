Christianity 2 – Week 4: A quieted soul

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: learn Psalm 131 (three verses) by heart, and each day begin by letting the body settle, then sit in silence. Sit for 15 minutes for the first three days, and 20 minutes from day four.

Measurable goal: sit in silence for at least 15 minutes on 5 of 7 days, with at least 3 of those days at 20 minutes.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Psalm 131 is one of the shortest psalms. The one who prays asks for nothing; they only say that their heart has stopped reaching for things beyond them, and has become still. The Christian tradition uses this psalm to speak of simple trust, like a small child's trust in its mother.

Reading it in context

  • Psalm 131 is also scripture in Judaism. In the Hebrew Bible it is headed "A Song of Ascents. By David." Jews and Christians both read it, each in their own tradition.
  • A "weaned child" no longer feeds at the breast. [Inference] A nursing baby seeks its mother in order to be fed; a weaned child rests beside her simply to be near. The image speaks of a peace that demands nothing.
  • "Nor do I concern myself with great matters" does not mean not learning or not trying. [Inference] It is about no longer demanding to understand and control everything that is beyond us.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study of reading psalms or of silent prayer with Psalm 131.
  • [Verified] Religious or spiritual interventions in general (a meta-analysis of randomised trials) reduced anxiety, with weaker results for depression; the programmes varied widely. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
  • [Verified] This week the sitting grows to 20 minutes. A review of 83 studies of meditation found about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience, most often anxiety or depression. Silent prayer has not been studied in this way; the figure is only a reminder to build up time gradually. (Farias et al. 2020)

Limits: this is spiritual practice, not treatment.

3. Practice for adults

About 22–25 minutes a day.

  1. Read (2 minutes). Read the three verses. By day three, try saying them from memory.
  2. Settle the body (3 minutes). Sit down. Let your shoulders drop, your jaw loosen, your hands rest on your thighs. Breathe out a little more slowly than you breathe in.
  3. Sit in silence (15 minutes; from day four, 20 minutes). Set a timer.
    • Christians: say verse 2 silently once, then stay in silence "like a weaned child with his mother": asking for nothing, simply being with God. When thoughts pull you away, say "stilled and quieted" silently again and come back.
    • People who are not Christian: sit in silence with the words "stilled and quieted", trying to do nothing more. When thoughts pull you away, say the words silently again. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation.
  4. Note (1 minute). How many minutes you sat, and one thing "too wonderful for me" that you could stop trying to grasp today.

If you do not feel "quieted", that is fine. The psalmist says the soul has been quieted; that is the work of time, not of one sitting.

4. Kids’ corner 6+

This week uses the activity "Listening to the bell fade": the family listens to a bell until they can no longer hear it. See the full activity at Kids’ corner – Activity 11.

Christian families can read verse 2 to their child before listening to the bell. Other families just play the listening part.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The image of mother and child brings up pain about your own mother, or about a child. You can stop this lesson, or sit only with verse 3; this is not your fault.
  • Then stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for longer than 20 minutes.

7. Practice for today

Before sleep, lie still for one minute, let your shoulders and jaw loosen, and say silently "stilled and quieted".
