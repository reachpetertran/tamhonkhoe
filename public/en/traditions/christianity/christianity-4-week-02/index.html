Christianity 4 – Week 2: Faith and works

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read the passage from James about the gap between words and deeds. Each morning turn what you mean to do into a very concrete plan, and check it in the evening.

Measurable goal: read on at least 5 of 7 mornings; write one "if… then…" plan each morning; carry out the plan on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Letter of James gives a simple picture: someone lacks clothes and food, and another person only wishes them "go in peace, be warmed and filled" and gives nothing. The Christian tradition teaches that real faith produces works, and a blessing without a deed is an empty blessing.

Reading it in context

  • James writes to communities of believers, so "a brother or sister" first means someone in the community. The following passage (2:18–26) goes on about faith and works, with the examples of Abraham and Rahab.
  • [Expert judgement] The question "are people saved through faith or through works" was a major point of dispute between Protestants and Catholics, when James is set beside Romans. The course does not go into that doctrine; it belongs to the deeper doctrinal comparisons kept in the internal area until reviewed. The lesson reads only what this passage says directly: good words need to go with deeds.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that evaluates reading this passage as such.
  • [Verified] The gap between "meaning to" and "doing" is something psychology can measure. A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests (more than 8,000 people) found that "if… then…" plans helped people reach goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65); goals and measures varied widely across studies. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
  • [Inference] The lesson uses this technique as a tool for keeping one's word, not to explain faith. The tradition speaks of faith; the science only speaks of how to turn intentions into action.

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus the act during the day.

  1. Morning reading (5 minutes). Read the whole passage. The second time, read verse 16 slowly. Ask: "Is there a kind word I said recently that I have not acted on?"
  2. Write one "if… then…" plan (1 minute). One small good act, for a specific person, at a specific moment. For example: "If I finish lunch, then I will call my mother for 5 minutes." "If I pass the neighbour's house, then I will ask if they need anything from the shop." Do not write "I will be better": it must be an act someone could see.
  3. Doing, during the day. When the "if" comes, do the "then".
  4. Evening look-back (2 minutes). Mark it: done / not done. If not, do not blame yourself: write tomorrow's plan, a little easier. Christians may pray in their own words; those who do not pray write one line (tamhonkhoe's adaptation).

4. Kids' corner 6+

"A small promise" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. In the morning, the child chooses one small thing to do for someone at home today: fetch grandma's slippers, put away a sibling's cup, water a plant for mum.
  2. The child says it aloud: "When …, I will …" For example: "When breakfast is over, I will put away the cups."
  3. The adult says a small promise of their own too.
  4. In the evening, everyone tells: did I keep my promise? If someone forgot, just say "I'll try again tomorrow". No punishment, no scolding.

The promise must be small and safe, something the child can do alone. Families with younger children also have Giữ lời hứa (keeping promises) on daynentang, currently in Vietnamese only.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The words "faith… is dead" make you so afraid for your faith or your salvation that you cannot settle. Then stop this lesson and talk with a guide within your own tradition.
  • The "done / not done" mark makes you blame yourself every evening. The plan is only a reminder, not a score of who you are.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

For turning intentions into a small daily act, daynentang has a lesson: Một việc nhỏ có ý nghĩa mỗi ngày (One small meaningful thing a day; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Recall an "I'll help with that" you said recently. Write an "if… then…" plan to do it today or tomorrow.
