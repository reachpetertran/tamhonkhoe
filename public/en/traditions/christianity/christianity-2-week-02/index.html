Christianity 2 – Week 2: The inner life

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read two passages of The Imitation of Christ slowly, sit in silence for 12 minutes each morning, and each evening note one moment in the day when outward things pulled you away and you came back.

Measurable goal: sit in silence for 12 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; write one "pulled away – came back" line on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Imitation of Christ (fifteenth century) is attributed to Thomas à Kempis, a monk in the Netherlands, and is one of the most widely read Christian devotional books. Book II is titled "Admonitions concerning the inner life". The tradition teaches that Christ is with the believer within the heart, and that whoever knows how to live from within can meet God anywhere, in the middle of anything.

Reading it in context

  • The first line quotes Luke 17:21. [Expert judgement] The Greek can be read as "within you" or "among you"; modern Bible translations differ. The Imitation of Christ reads it as "within the heart".
  • "This miserable world" and "despise outward things" are the language of a fifteenth-century book written for monks. [Inference] In this course the line is read as giving priority to the inner life, not as looking down on the body, family, work or other people.
  • Section 7 is close to Brother Lawrence in Week 1: the inward person does not need to leave their work to find God. The two books are about two centuries apart.
  • Other sections of this chapter advise placing little trust in people and seeking comfort in the wounds of Christ; the lesson does not quote them. A Christian reviewer will say how the passage is usually read.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study of reading The Imitation of Christ or of inward prayer in its manner.
  • [Verified] Religious or spiritual interventions in general (a meta-analysis of randomised trials) reduced anxiety, with weaker results for depression; study quality was limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
  • [Inference] Noticing when you are pulled away and coming back resembles a step in many meditation programmes. But this course is not a meditation programme and has not been studied.

Limits: this is spiritual practice, not treatment.

3. Practice for adults

About 20 minutes a day.

  1. Read slowly (4 minutes). Read both passages. The second time, read "requireth not places nor waiteth for seasons" three times, very slowly.
  2. Sit in silence (12 minutes). Set a timer.
    • Christians: pray silently. You may begin with "The kingdom of God is within you", then stay in silence with God. When thoughts run outward, gently turn back in.
    • People who are not Christian: sit in silence, attending to the feeling of the body sitting. When thoughts run off to outward things, notice "I've gone out", then come back. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation.
  3. During the day. When you notice you are being swept along at work (annoyed by a message, rushed by a deadline), pause for one breath and remember "the inward man quickly recollecteth himself".
  4. Write one line (evening, 2 minutes). "Today I was pulled away when… and came back by…". Write it on days you did not come back as well; it is still a line.

4. Kids’ corner 6+

Activity: "The quiet house" (10 minutes, an adult joins in)

  1. Everyone draws a small house on paper, with a room in the middle.
  2. In that room, draw something that makes you feel calm: a pillow, a cat, a tree, the sky.
  3. The adult says: "Everyone has a quiet place inside. When things are noisy, we can think of that room."
  4. Put the pictures up. When your child is upset, you can ask: "Shall we go to the quiet house for a moment?"

Christian families can add: "God is in that room with you." If your child does not want to draw, stop; do not push.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The words "forsake this world" make you want to withdraw from family, friends or work, or you feel more cut off from people.
  • Then stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for longer than 20 minutes.

7. Practice for today

The next time your phone buzzes, do not open it straight away. Wait one breath, then look.
