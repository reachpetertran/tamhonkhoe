Christianity 4 – Week 5: Family

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: bring the works of faith to the nearest place: the people you live with. "Family" here covers every kind of household: couples, parents and children, grandparents, siblings, or people who share a home. If you live alone, choose your closest relatives or friends.

Measurable goal: read on at least 5 of 7 mornings; during the week do at least 1 act of love for each person at home without being asked; have at least 3 shared meals without phones.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Letter to the Colossians uses the picture of "putting on" qualities like clothes each day: compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience, and above all love. The First Letter of John puts it briefly: love not only in words but in deeds. The Christian tradition teaches that the family is the first place to practise these things.

Reading it in context

  • Colossians 3:12–14 is written to the whole community of believers, not only to families. The course reads it within the family because that is the week's theme. "My little children" in John's letter is how the author addresses believers, not children.
  • Right after this passage, Colossians 3:18–21 gives instructions to wives, husbands, children and fathers. [Expert judgement] Churches understand and apply this part in different ways; the course does not quote or discuss it.
  • Very important: "bearing with" and "forgiving" in this lesson do not mean putting up with violence, abuse or control at home. The course does not read these verses as telling anyone who is being harmed to stay and endure it. If you or your child are not safe at home, the first step is to get help: see Get help now.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that evaluates reading these passages as such.
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 17 studies (182,836 children and adolescents) found that 3 or more family meals a week went with healthier eating and less disordered eating. This is a correlation. (Hammons & Fiese 2011)
  • [Verified] Acts of kindness raise the doer's well-being by a small-to-moderate amount (27 experiments); these studies are not specific to families. (Curry et al. 2018)

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus the acts during the week.

  1. Morning reading (5 minutes). Days 1–2 Colossians 3:12; day 3 verse 13; day 4 verse 14; day 5 1 John 3:18; days 6–7 both passages. Choose one quality from verse 12 to "put on" today.
  2. One act of love for each person. Write down the name of each person at home. During the week, do one thing for each that they did not ask for: make tea for your father, take over a chore your partner dislikes, listen to your child for 10 minutes without holding your phone, call your grandparents. Tick each name when done.
  3. Shared meals without phones. At least 3 meals in the week with everyone's phone put elsewhere. Each person tells one thing from their day.
  4. Evening look-back (2 minutes). Ask: "Whom did I love in deed today? Is there anyone at home I forgot?" Christians may pray for each person at home; those who do not pray write one line (tamhonkhoe's adaptation).

4. Kids' corner 6+

"Everyone has a job" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Before a meal, the family shares out jobs: the child sets out chopsticks and spoons, a younger sibling puts out napkins, parents carry the hot dishes, grandparents wait or choose a light task.
  2. The child counts whether there is a place for everyone, including someone coming home late.
  3. During the meal, each person thanks someone who did a job for the meal.
  4. After the meal, everyone clears their own place.

Adults carry the soup pot and hot dishes; children do not touch the stove or knives. If the child does not want to help, invite them again at the next meal; do not force it.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The lines about "bearing with" and "forgiving" make you think you must endure someone who is harming you or your child. Stop this lesson and get help now.
  • A lesson about family brings back the pain of someone who has died, a family that has broken apart, or relatives you are estranged from. Then choose a close friend in place of "someone at home", or pause the lesson.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

7. Practice for today

Choose one person at home. Do something small for them today that they have not asked for.
