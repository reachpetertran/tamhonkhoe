Christianity 4 – Week 1: The least of these

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: a Level 4 course adds no extra sitting time. Each morning read slowly for 5 minutes, go about your day's work as usual, and look back for 2 minutes in the evening. The first week reads the Gospel passage about six acts: feeding, giving drink, welcoming a stranger, clothing, visiting the sick, visiting those in prison.

Measurable goal: read on at least 5 of 7 mornings; do at least 2 acts during the week for someone in need; look back on at least 5 of 7 evenings.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] In Matthew's Gospel, Jesus speaks of the day the Son of Man comes to judge the nations. The King tells those on his right that they fed him, gave him drink, took him in, clothed him and visited him. Surprised, they ask: "when?" The Christian tradition teaches that what is done for the least is done for Christ himself, and that people often do it without knowing.

Reading it in context

  • This passage belongs to a teaching about the judgement (Matthew 25:31–46). Verses 41–46 speak of those on the left, who did not do these things, and of eternal punishment. The lesson reads only verses 34–40 to focus on the acts; you can read the whole passage in the Bible. The course does not explain the doctrine of judgement, because churches understand this part in different ways.
  • [Expert judgement] Churches read "the least of these my brothers" in two main ways: everyone in need, or first of all disciples in hardship. The lesson does not choose between them.
  • [Expert judgement] The Catholic tradition calls the six acts of verses 35–36 (together with burying the dead) the "corporal works of mercy". This name needs confirming by a reviewer within the tradition.
  • Visiting the sick and those in prison is the Bible's word. The course does not ask you to go into a prison or hospital; see the safety note below.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that evaluates reading this passage as such.
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 27 experiments (4,045 people) found that acts of kindness raised the well-being of the person doing them by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies were often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
  • [Verified] A review of volunteering found that in 5 cohort studies volunteers had lower mortality. This is a correlation; randomised trials have not shown clear benefit. (Jenkinson et al. 2013)
  • [Inference] In this tradition the reason for these acts is the other person and God, not one's own health. The science only tells us that they usually do not cost us in well-being.

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus two acts during the week. The whole course uses the same three-step rhythm.

  1. Morning reading (5 minutes). Read the whole passage once. The second time, read one verse closely each day: day 1 verse 35; day 2 verse 36; day 3 verses 37–39 (the question "when?"); day 4 verse 40; days 5–7 the whole passage. Note a word or sentence that stays with you.
  2. Doing, during the day. During the week, do 2 acts for someone in need, chosen from the six in the passage: bring a meal to someone who is ill, phone an older person who lives alone, give good clothes to a place that collects them, invite someone who has just moved in for a drink, visit someone you know who is in hospital. Choose what is within your strength.
  3. Evening look-back (2 minutes). Ask: "Who needed me today? Did I see them?" Christians may pray in their own words. Those who do not pray write one line. (Writing one line is tamhonkhoe's adaptation, not a form of the tradition.)

Safety first: help through places you know well (family, neighbours, a parish, a known group). Do not take strangers home, do not lend your documents or accounts, do not give money your own household needs. Follow the rules of the hospital or prison when visiting. If you are ill, do not visit someone who is seriously ill.

4. Kids' corner 6+

The passage is about giving others what they need. This week uses the activity "A small gift": the child draws a card or picks a toy they want to give, then takes it to someone with a parent. See the full activity at Kids’ corner – Activity 12.

Do not take children into hospitals or prisons. Do not make a child give away a toy they do not want to give.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • You feel so afraid or guilty about things left undone that you cannot settle. The reading is for opening your eyes to the people around you, not for condemning yourself.
  • Helping others is wearing you out or making you neglect your own health or family. Then cut back to one small act a week.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

For things a family can do together for the place they live, daynentang has a lesson: Cả nhà giúp cộng đồng (The whole family helps the community; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Think of someone you know who is ill, low, or living alone. Call or message them today.
