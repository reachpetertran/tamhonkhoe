Christianity 3 – Week 2: Loved, but not thought

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

A reminder: this is a Level 3 course. If you are depressed, newly bereaved, going through a major upheaval or having thoughts of harming yourself, please pause the course and see Get help now. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder, severe PTSD or long-lasting feelings of being detached from themselves should not study this course alone.

This week: read chapters 5 and 6 of The Cloud of Unknowing slowly. Sit in silence for 25 minutes each day; when a thought comes, even a beautiful one, let it pass without following it. Each evening, note one moment in the day when you "let a thought be" instead of running after it.

Measurable goal: sit in silence for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; note one "letting be" each evening on at least 5 of 7 evenings.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Last week, the book placed the "cloud of unknowing" above the person praying, between them and God. Chapter 5 adds a second cloud beneath: the "cloud of forgetting", covering everything else during prayer. Chapter 6 answers the question "What is God?" with a short sentence: "I wot not" (I do not know).

Reading it in context

  • The "cloud of forgetting" is only for the time of prayer. Chapter 5 itself says that thinking of other people and their deeds is "full profitable sometime", only of little use in this work. [Inference] It does not mean neglecting family, work or health, and it is not about trying to forget a painful memory.
  • The book also teaches (chapter 7, next week) that beginners first need much time meditating on God's goodness. "Forgetting" here is a step for someone who has long been on the path of prayer.
  • The "dart of longing love" is the author's image. Chapter 46 (Week 5) warns the reader not to strain body or heart; the dart here is a desire turned towards God, not tension.
  • [Expert judgement] The line "He may well be loved, but not thought" is often quoted as a summary of the whole book.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study of the "cloud of forgetting".
  • [Verified] A critical review describes "decentering" as three processes: knowing what one is experiencing, not identifying with inner experience, and being less carried away by the content of thoughts. It is a conceptual analysis, not a test of effectiveness. (Bernstein et al. 2015)
  • [Inference] This week's "letting thoughts pass without following them" is close to the third process above. The book's aim is different: not to reduce stress, but to turn the heart towards God.
  • [Verified] About 8% of meditators had an adverse experience, most often anxiety or depression, according to a review of 83 studies. (Farias et al. 2020)

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day, plus 2 minutes of notes in the evening.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read the three passages once. The second time, read only "He may well be loved, but not thought."
  2. Silent sitting (25 minutes).
    • Christians: turn your heart towards God as last week. When a thought comes, even a beautiful thought about Him, let it settle "under the cloud of forgetting": do not chase it, do not hold it, return to turning towards Him.
    • Those who do not pray: when a thought comes, note softly "let it be", then return to the feeling of the body sitting. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation.
    • If a thought keeps coming back, that is normal. Do not strain to drive it away.
  3. Back to the ground (2 minutes). Open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, name three things in the room.
  4. Evening note (2 minutes). One moment in the day when you "let a thought be" (a criticism, a worry, a plan) instead of running after it. What happened next?
  5. Tick it off. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

4. Kids' corner 6+

"Warmth in your hands" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Adult and child rub their palms together, counting slowly to ten.
  2. Put both hands on your cheeks. Ask: "Can you feel the warmth? What colour is it? What shape?"
  3. Let the child answer, including "I don't know".
  4. Finish with: "The warmth is real. We can feel it, even if we cannot say what it looks like."

Rub gently, not so hard that it hurts.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
  • A feeling that you or the world are "not real", or of being detached from your body, stays after you stand up. If it lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
  • You start using "forgetting" to avoid a task that needs doing, a hard conversation, or a memory that needs care. Then stop the course and talk to someone you trust.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

If you feel unsteady while sitting, daynentang has a lesson that brings your attention back to your body and surroundings: Neo lại (in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Next time someone asks you a question you cannot answer, try saying "I don't know" without rushing to add an explanation.
