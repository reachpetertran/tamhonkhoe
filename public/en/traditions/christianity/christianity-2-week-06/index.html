Christianity 2 – Week 6: A daily rhythm of prayer

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: keep the morning sitting, and add three short pauses during the day (starting work, a meal, before sleep), each about 10 seconds. In the evening, look back on the day in Brother Lawrence's way.

Measurable goal: sit in silence for 20 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; make at least 2 of the 3 pauses on 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Paul's letter to the believers in Thessalonica says "pray without ceasing". The Christian tradition understands this in many ways; Brother Lawrence understood it as living with God through very short moments of remembering Him, repeated through the day. Reading it in context

  • The seventh letter was written to someone near sixty-four; he himself was almost eighty. He advises them to leave the care of their affairs to someone else if they can. This lesson does not advise anyone to give up work or responsibilities to their family.
  • "Offer Him your sufferings" is language of the Catholic tradition. [Inference] It does not mean bearing things alone or not seeking help; people who suffer still need doctors, loved ones and practical help.
  • [Expert judgement] Many Christian traditions have fixed hours of prayer in the day (for example the Liturgy of the Hours in Catholic, Orthodox and Anglican practice). The three pauses in this lesson are much simpler and do not replace those hours.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study of very short prayers during the day.
  • [Verified] Psychotherapy that integrates the client's faith (97 studies, 7,181 people) reduced distress as well as standard therapy and added spiritual well-being. (Captari et al. 2018)
  • [Inference] Tying a new action to an existing point in the day (starting work, a meal, bedtime) is a common way to build a habit; we have no study of this approach for prayer.

Limits: this is spiritual practice, not treatment.

3. Practice for adults

About 20 minutes in the morning, plus three 10-second pauses and 3 minutes in the evening.

  1. Morning: read and sit (20 minutes). Read the three passages once, then sit in silence for 20 minutes in the way you practised in Weeks 3–5 (silent prayer, or silence with a short sentence).
  2. Three pauses (about 10 seconds each). When you start work, before lunch or dinner, and before sleep.
    • Christians: "lift up your heart" as Brother Lawrence says, with a short sentence such as "Lord, I am here with you", or simply turning towards Him without words.
    • People who are not Christian: pause for three breaths and ask yourself "Where am I, what am I doing?" This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation.
  3. Evening: look back (3 minutes). Recall the day. One thing done well: give thanks (Christians thank God; others write one line of thanks). One thing not done well: notice it, without blaming yourself, and think how to begin again tomorrow.
  4. Mark it. Note how many pauses you made today (0–3).

Missing a pause is normal. Brother Lawrence says he reached his habit "by rising after my falls".

4. Kids’ corner 6+

Game: "Three pauses" (one week, under a minute each time, an adult joins in)

  1. With your child, draw three boxes on a sheet of paper: a sun (morning), a bowl (a meal), a moon (evening). Put the sheet somewhere easy to see.
  2. Morning: your child says one thing they want to do today. At a meal: they thank someone who cooked or bought the food. Evening: they tell one happy thing from the day.
  3. After each one, your child ticks the box.
  4. Missing a box is fine; no punishment, no criticism.

Christian families can turn the three pauses into three short prayers of the child's own. Other families keep it as above. If your child does not want to, stop; do not push.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The pauses turn into a worry: you feel strong fear or distress whenever you forget, or have to redo them "properly".
  • Then stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for longer than 20 minutes.
