Christianity 2 – Week 8: Review

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: sit in silence for 25 minutes each day; at the end of the week, rate the 7 habits you practised, keep 2, and write a plan for the next 4 weeks.

Measurable goal: sit in silence for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; complete the self-rating table and the 4-week plan.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Brother Lawrence advises those practising the presence of God not to force themselves rigidly. The tradition teaches that prayer is a free relationship with God, not an exercise to be completed in full.

Reading it in context

  • "Therefore" links back to the previous paragraph of the letter, where he advises sometimes giving up small pleasures. The lesson does not quote that paragraph, because the course does not teach abstinence.
  • [Inference] This sentence gathers up the whole course: doing ordinary work fully (Week 1), living from within (Week 2), coming back gently when the mind wanders (Week 3), a quieted soul (Week 4), few words (Week 5), a rhythm through the day (Week 6), and persevering without forcing oneself (Week 7).
  • At the end of this letter he also writes that at first one often thinks it lost time, but should go on. [Inference] The rating table below helps you choose which habits are worth keeping, instead of trying to keep them all.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] Religious or spiritual interventions in general (a meta-analysis of randomised trials) reduced anxiety, with weaker results for depression; study quality was limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
  • [Verified] Psychotherapy that integrates the client's faith (97 studies, 7,181 people) reduced distress as well as standard therapy and added spiritual well-being. (Captari et al. 2018)
  • No study has evaluated this course directly, and there is no review of contemplative prayer as such.

Limits: this is spiritual practice, not treatment.

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day, plus 20 minutes of review at the end of the week.

  1. Read (2 minutes). Read the passage. The second time, read only "recalling our mind to GOD mildly, and with tranquility".
  2. Sit in silence (25 minutes). In the way that suited you best in the course: silent prayer with few words, a quieted soul as in Psalm 131, or a short sentence. People who are not Christian use their own way (attention to the body, the breath or a short sentence), as in earlier weeks.
  3. Note (1 minute). How many minutes you sat.
  4. Review (end of week, 20 minutes). Rate each habit below from 0 (not done) to 3 (done regularly and found helpful):
Week Habit Score 0–3
1 Doing one ordinary task fully each day
2 Noticing when pulled away and coming back
3 Sitting in silence in a fixed place
4 Settling the body before sitting
5 Repeating a short sentence
6 Three pauses in the day and an evening look back
7 Sitting the full time even on dry days

Keep the 2 habits with the highest scores. Write a 4-week plan: when, where, how many minutes. If you want to read further, you can read Brother Lawrence's letters in full, or go back to Christianity 1 with another Gospel passage.

4. Kids’ corner 6+

Game: "My favourite game" (5–10 minutes, an adult plays too)

  1. Remind your child of the games in this course: carry the cup, the quiet house, the breathing statue, listening to the bell fade, water flows low, three pauses, a bean in a cup.
  2. Your child chooses a favourite and plays it again with the family.
  3. Ask: "Why do you like this one?" The adult tells which game they liked best.
  4. If the beans have sprouted, plant one in a pot of soil together.

If your child does not want to play, stop; do not push.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
  • Sitting for 25 minutes leaves you restless or uneasy long after the sitting. Then go back to 15–20 minutes; there is no need to sit longer.
  • The rating makes you blame yourself for "not being devout enough" or "not trying enough". The table is only for choosing habits, not for judging you as a person.
  • Then stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for longer than 20 minutes.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

To turn the two habits you choose into small daily routines, see One small meaningful thing a day on daynentang (in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write "recalling the mind mildly, and with tranquility" on a slip of paper, and put it where you sit each day.
