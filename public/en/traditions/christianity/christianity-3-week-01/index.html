Christianity 3 – Week 1: The cloud of unknowing

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Before you start this course: this is a Level 3 course on how the Christian contemplative tradition speaks of God as beyond every concept, with 30–40 minutes of practice a day for 6 weeks. It is best taken after Christianity 1 and Christianity 2. If you are depressed, newly bereaved, going through a major upheaval, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, this course is not for now: please see Get help now and talk to someone you trust. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder, severe PTSD, or long-lasting feelings of being detached from themselves should not study this course alone; if you want to take it, ask a doctor and study with an experienced guide.

This course only describes two texts of the Christian tradition: The Cloud of Unknowing (England, late 14th century) and The Mystical Theology by an author writing under the name of Dionysius (around the late 5th or early 6th century). It does not compare them with other traditions' views of God or ultimate reality, it does not ask you to believe anything, and it does not replace a guide within the tradition.

This week: read three passages from chapters 3 and 4 of The Cloud of Unknowing slowly. Sit in silence for 25 minutes each day, without trying to picture anything. Each evening, write one line about a moment when your mind wanted to "grasp" an image or an explanation.

Measurable goal: sit in silence for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; write one line each evening on at least 5 of 7 evenings.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Cloud of Unknowing is a guide to contemplative prayer written in Middle English by an unknown author, addressed to a young person who wished to live a contemplative life. It teaches that human understanding cannot grasp God. In prayer, one simply turns towards Him in love, and at first meets only a "darkness", a "cloud of unknowing".

Reading it in context

  • The book's prologue asks readers to pass it only to people who truly wish to live a contemplative life, and to read it whole, because reading one part without another may lead to error. This course reads only a few passages, in order to describe them. Anyone who wants to follow the path the book teaches should read the whole book with a guide within the tradition.
  • Chapter 4 makes clear that the "darkness" is not a real darkness. So this week's practice does not involve closing your eyes and imagining blackness or a cloud.
  • In the middle of chapter 3, the lesson does not quote the lines about saints, angels, fiends and the souls in purgatory. The sentence omitted from the third passage says that one could imagine darkness on a summer's day, or light on a winter's night, and the author calls that falsehood.
  • The English is Evelyn Underhill's 1922 edition, with modernised spelling but many old words kept: "thou" (you), "ween" (suppose), "ghostly" (spiritual), "letteth" (hinders).

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study that directly evaluates reading The Cloud of Unknowing.
  • [Expert judgement] Centering Prayer is a modern Christian way of praying influenced by this book.
  • [Verified] An online randomised trial of 702 self-identified Christians, practising for 28 days, compared three groups: Centering Prayer with a religious framing, the same practice without a religious framing, and simply reflecting on the day. On the main measure of well-being one week after the practice period, the three groups did not differ; all three improved from the start. The religious framing changed only measures of spiritual experience. (Rohde et al. 2024)
  • [Verified] An interview study of Western meditators recorded 59 kinds of experience, including difficult ones, with an impact ranging from minimal and passing to severe and lasting. (Lindahl et al. 2017)

Limits: the trial above measured a modern way of praying, not this course. This is the practice of a tradition, not treatment.

3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day, plus 2 minutes of notes in the evening.

  1. Slow reading (3 minutes). Read the three passages once. The second time, read only "For when I say darkness, I mean a lacking of knowing."
  2. Silent sitting (25 minutes). Sit upright but at ease, eyes closed or lowered.
    • Christians: turn your heart towards God without trying to picture Him. When an image or a thought about Him appears, do not push it away and do not follow it; just let it pass, then return to turning towards Him.
    • Those who do not pray: sit in silence, notice the moments when the mind wants to grasp an image or an explanation, then gently return to the feeling of the body sitting. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation, not a form of the tradition.
    • Do not try to produce any special feeling. A sitting where "nothing happens" is still a full sitting.
  3. Back to the ground (2 minutes). Open your eyes, put both feet on the floor, look around and name three things in the room before you stand up.
  4. Evening note (2 minutes). One line: today, when did your mind want to "grasp" an image, an explanation, or a feeling of certainty?
  5. Tick it off. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

4. Kids' corner 6+

"Where is the wind?" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Stand with the child at a window or outside, holding a thin strip of paper or a light scarf.
  2. Watch the strip flutter. Ask: "Can you see the wind? How do you know there is wind?"
  3. Let the child answer: leaves shaking, hair blowing, a cool feeling on the cheek.
  4. Finish with: "Some things are real even though we cannot see them. We only know them by what they do."

Talk only about the wind. Do not stand near the edge of a balcony or a window without bars.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
  • A feeling that you or the world are "not real", or of being detached from your body, stays after you stand up. If it lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
  • The "darkness" in your sitting turns into a lasting feeling of fear, emptiness or abandonment outside your sitting time. Then stop the course and talk to someone you trust or to a guide.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

If you feel unsteady while sitting, daynentang has a lesson that brings your attention back to your body and surroundings: Neo lại (in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Choose something you use every day without really understanding how it works. Sit with it for one minute, without looking it up, and simply notice the feeling of "I do not know".
