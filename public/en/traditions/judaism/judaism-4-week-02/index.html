Judaism 4 – Week 2: Rest for workers too

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: slow reading of one short verse about who gets to rest. The family lists the household tasks, sees who rests least, and swaps one task so that person gets time off. Respect the rest time of people who work for you.

Measurable goal: read slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; a household task list for the whole family; the person who rests least gets at least 1 extra hour of rest this week through one swapped task; no work messages to anyone outside their working hours all week.

1. Traditional teaching

A note on context: this verse sits in the section of laws right after the Ten Commandments (Exodus 21–23), next to verses on fair treatment of the poor and the stranger (23:6–9). Here the reason for the day of rest is not creation but that the weakest members of the household may rest too. Deuteronomy 5:14 says something close ("that thy man-servant and thy maid-servant may rest as well as thou"); that passage is quoted in Judaism 1 – Week 3.

[Expert judgement] The word JPS renders "may be refreshed" shares its root with the last word of Exodus 31:17 in Week 1. [Inference] Put the two verses side by side: what God has on the seventh day, the hired worker and the stranger must have too.

[Traditional teaching] The Jewish tradition has many further laws about hired workers, for example paying wages the same day (Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:14–15). The course only names them and does not explain them.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] Joint WHO/ILO estimates: in 2016 about 488 million people worked 55 or more hours a week, and about 745,000 deaths from ischemic heart disease and stroke were attributed to such long hours. These are model estimates. (Pega et al. 2021)
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 86 publications (38,124 workers) found that people who detach from work when off duty report less exhaustion and better sleep. This is a correlation. (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017)
  • [Inference] These studies measure paid work, not unpaid housework or care. Applying the idea "everyone needs rest" to housework this week is tamhonkhoe's own approach.

3. Practice for adults

Five minutes of reading each morning, two minutes of looking back each evening; one 15-minute family meeting at the start of the week.

  1. Read slowly (5 minutes, morning). Read Exodus 23:12 twice. Ask: "In our home, who is like 'the son of the handmaid', the one who works a lot and rarely rests?"
  2. Household task list (start of the week, 15 minutes, whole family). Make a table: what task, who does it, how many times a week, how long it takes. Include tasks people rarely notice: cooking, washing up, laundry, school runs, remembering appointments, caring for grandparents.
  3. Swap one task. Together, choose one task of the person who rests least and give it to someone else this week (children take tasks they can manage). The aim: that person gets at least 1 extra hour of rest.
  4. Respect the rest time of people who work for you. All week, do not send work messages to colleagues, staff, home helpers or tradespeople outside their working hours. Give one specific thank-you to a service worker (a delivery driver, a security guard, a cleaner).
  5. Look back (2 minutes, evening). One line: who at home got to rest today, and thanks to whom.

Very important. The task list is for sharing work more fairly, not for blaming anyone. If someone at home uses housework to control, threaten or harm others, this is no longer about sharing tasks; see Get help now.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "A good deed for someone at home": the child chooses one small task to do for someone at home so that person can rest a little. See the full activity in Kids' corner – Activity 6.

One extra question for this week: "Who does the most jobs in our home? What could you help them with?"

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The family meeting turns into arguing or blaming. Stop, wait until everyone is calm, and discuss only one task at a time.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
