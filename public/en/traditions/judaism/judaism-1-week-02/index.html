Judaism 1 – Week 2: Love your neighbour

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: slow reading of two short passages from Leviticus on loving your neighbour and loving the stranger, then each day one act of kindness to someone near you.

Measurable goal: do and note one kindness to someone near you (at home, at work, a neighbour), on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

Love your neighbour as yourself

[Traditional teaching] Leviticus chapter 19 opens with "Ye shall be holy", then moves through many very concrete duties: leaving the corner of the field for the poor, paying a hired worker on the same day, not putting a stumbling block before the blind. "Love thy neighbour as thyself" sits in the middle of these.

A note on context: the two verses move from the inside outwards: no hatred kept in the heart, speaking openly instead of holding it in, no revenge, no grudge, and only then "love". "I am the LORD" is a refrain repeated many times in this chapter: how people treat one another is placed under God's authority. [Inference] Verse 17 shows that "love" here does not mean silent endurance; but how to rebuke rightly is something the tradition discusses at length, and the Jewish reviewer will say more.

Love the stranger too

A note on context: the reason given is Israel's own memory: they were once strangers in Egypt. [Inference] The teaching rests on remembering that one has stood where the other person stands.

Hillel: what is hateful to you, do not do to another

[Traditional teaching] The Talmud (Shabbat 31a) tells of a gentile who asked Hillel to teach him the whole Torah while he stood on one foot. Hillel answered: what is hateful to you, do not do to another; that is the whole Torah, the rest is its interpretation; go and study. The passage is quoted in full in Convergence 0 – Week 3: The Golden Rule, next to similar teachings from Christianity and Islam, so here it is only summarised.

[Inference] Hillel's saying is one way of reading Leviticus 19:18 in the negative: it starts from not doing harm. In Christianity, the Gospels also join 19:18 with Deuteronomy 6:5 (read in Week 1); see Christianity 1 – Week 4.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 27 experiments (4,045 people) found that performing acts of kindness raises the well-being of the person doing them, by a small-to-moderate amount. (Curry et al. 2018)
  • [Inference] The studies are often small and measured over a short time. They concern how the giver feels; they do not measure "loving your neighbour" in the Torah's sense, which is a religious duty, not an exercise for feeling better.

Limits: no study has evaluated this exercise itself.

3. Practice for adults

About 10–12 minutes a day.

  1. Read slowly (3 minutes). Days 1–3 read Leviticus 19:17–18; days 4–7 read 19:33–34. Read twice, pausing after each verse the second time.
  2. Choose a person (2 minutes). Think of someone near you: someone at home, a colleague, a neighbour, a shopkeeper you know. On at least one day this week, choose someone new or different from you (someone who has moved here, a new colleague).
  3. Ask yourself (1 minute). "If I were in this person's place, how would I want to be treated?"
  4. Do one kindness that day, small and concrete: ask how they are, help with a task, give up a seat, say thank you using their name.
  5. Write one line (2 minutes) in the evening: "Today I … for …". Mark your calendar.
  6. Optional (verse 17). If there is something you are holding against someone, write one sentence you would like to say to them openly and gently. You do not have to say it; say it only when it feels safe.

Very important. Loving your neighbour does not mean you must stay close to, give in to, or silently endure someone who has harmed you or is harming you. The exercise does not ask you to do a kindness for that person; choose someone else.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "Swapping shoes": the adult and the child each draw a shoe and swap drawings, "walking in the other person's shoes", think about how that person feels in a small everyday situation, then choose one thing the child can do to help right away. See the full activity at Kids' corner – Activity 3.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • Thinking about verse 17 brings back memories of being harmed and leaves you unsettled. If so, skip the optional step, and talk with a trusted friend or a mental health professional.
  • The exercise makes you feel guilty for "not being kind enough". One small act is enough.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

7. Practice for today

Name one person near you whom you rarely notice, and do one small thing for them before the day ends.
