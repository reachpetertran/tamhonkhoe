Judaism

Judaism 1 – Week 1: The Shema

Deuteronomy 6:4–9, the Shema, "Hear, O Israel". Ten minutes of slow reading a day: choose one word and write one line. The course describes; it does not teach religious law.

Judaism 1 – Week 2: Love your neighbour

Leviticus 19:17–18 and 19:33–34: love your neighbour and the stranger as yourself. Slow reading each day and one kindness to someone near you.

Judaism 1 – Week 3: The day of rest

Genesis 2:1–3 and Deuteronomy 5:12–15: two reasons for Shabbat, remembering creation and remembering liberation. One intentional rest period this week. The course describes; it does not teach Shabbat observance as law.

Judaism 1 – Week 4: If not now

Pirkei Avot, chapter I, 14: Hillel's three questions. Each day answer one of them in a line, and this week do one thing you have put off.

Judaism 1 – Week 5: Not to finish, but not to quit

Pirkei Avot, chapter II: Rabbi Tarfon's "the day is short, the task is great" and "it is not thy duty to complete the work, but neither art thou free to desist from it". Ten minutes a day on one long-term task.

Judaism 1 – Week 6: Justice, kindness, humility

Micah 6:6–8: "only to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God". Choose one of the three to practise this week, and review the whole course.

Judaism 2 – Week 1: Who is wise

Pirkei Avot, chapter IV, 1: "Who is wise? He who learns from all men." The opening of the Mussar course: rate seven traits, start an evening notebook, and each day learn one thing from one person.

Judaism 2 – Week 2: Greeting everyone cheerfully

Pirkei Avot, chapter I, 15: Shammai's "say little and do much, and receive all men with a cheerful countenance". Three full greetings a day, and once this week promise little and do much.

Judaism 2 – Week 3: Do not judge too quickly

Pirkei Avot II, 5 (2:4 in many editions): "judge not thy neighbor until thou art come into his place", and I, 6: "judge every man in the scale of merit". Each day, find two other explanations for something that annoyed you.

Judaism 2 – Week 4: Honouring others

Pirkei Avot IV, 15 (4:12 in many editions): "Let the honor of thy disciple be as dear to thee as thine own." Each day, one specific act of respect for someone below or alongside you.

Judaism 2 – Week 5: Watchfulness

Mesillat Yesharim, chapter 2: watchfulness means considering whether what we do is good, rather than "walking through the course of habit like a blind man in darkness". Three pauses a day before something done out of habit.

Judaism 2 – Week 6: Zeal

Mesillat Yesharim, chapter 6: zeal is engaging early in good deeds and completing them; and the familiar list of excuses of the lazy. Each day, do one chosen good deed at the earliest moment you can, and write down your own excuses.

Judaism 2 – Week 7: Humility

Pirkei Avot IV, 4: "Be exceedingly lowly of spirit", and Mesillat Yesharim, chapter 22: there is no sage who does not make mistakes, and what we have is for helping others. Each day, learn from someone, admit a mistake, or use a strength to help someone.

Judaism 2 – Week 8: Review – keeping accounts like a merchant

Mesillat Yesharim, chapter 3: weigh your ways each day as great merchants review their business, at fixed times. Re-rate the seven traits, choose two to keep, and write a four-week plan.

Judaism 3 – Week 1: An upright man

Job 1–2: a whole-hearted, upright man loses his wealth, his children and his health; three friends come and sit with him for seven days without a word. Each day: slow reading, 25 minutes of sitting, and one question written down without rushing to answer it.

Judaism 3 – Week 2: Job speaks out

Job 7:11 and 23:3–9: "I will not refrain my mouth". Job's lament, and how the Jewish tradition regards words spoken in pain. Three 10-minute sessions of writing a lament about a manageable difficulty; 25 minutes of sitting on the other days.

Judaism 3 – Week 3: The friends

Job 4:3–8 and 16:2–5: Eliphaz explains Job's suffering, and Job calls his friends "sorry comforters". Each day: 25 minutes of sitting and one moment of listening for at least 3 minutes without advice or explanation.

Judaism 3 – Week 4: The voice from the whirlwind

Job 38:1–7: "Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth?" God answers Job with questions about heaven and earth. Three 15-minute walks noticing something vast; 30 minutes of sitting on the other days.

Judaism 3 – Week 5: Job's reply

Job 42:1–6: "I had heard of Thee by the hearing of the ear; But now mine eye seeth Thee." A verse that can be translated in several ways. Each day, 30 minutes of sitting; once this week, read with someone you trust.

Judaism 3 – Week 6: Review – praying for friends, eating bread together

Job 42:7–11: God says the friends have not spoken rightly of Him; Job prays for his friends; his family come to eat bread and comfort him. Review the course's six habits, keep two, and once this week visit or check in on someone going through a hard time.

Judaism 4 – Week 1: The seventh day, a delight

Exodus 31:16–17 calls the sabbath a sign; Isaiah 58:13–14 calls it a delight. A description of Friday evening in a Jewish home. The family prepares ahead and shares one rest period this week. The course describes; it does not teach Shabbat observance.

Judaism 4 – Week 2: Rest for workers too

Exodus 23:12: the seventh day so that the animals, the son of the handmaid and the stranger may be refreshed. The family looks at who rests least, swaps one household task, and respects the rest time of people who work for them.

Judaism 4 – Week 3: Open your hand to the poor

Deuteronomy 15:7–11: do not harden your heart or shut your hand. Tzedakah, from a root meaning "justice", and Maimonides' eight levels of giving (summarised). The family sets up a sharing box and decides together where it goes.

Judaism 4 – Week 4: Leaving the corner of the field

Leviticus 19:9–10 and Deuteronomy 24:19: do not reap the corner of the field, do not go back for a forgotten sheaf. Each day set something aside for others, and give in a way that protects the receiver's dignity.

Judaism 4 – Week 5: The shared table

Pirkei Avot I, 5: let thy house be open wide; let the poor be members of thy household. The table in the Jewish tradition: grace after meals, words of Torah at the table, welcoming guests. Two shared meals without screens each week, and one invitation to a guest.

Judaism 4 – Week 6: Review – a song for the sabbath day

Psalm 92:1–5, "a song for the sabbath day": giving thanks in the morning, remembering faithfulness at night. Review the course's six habits, keep two, and write a 4-week plan for the family.

DT2 Mussar: training character

Mussar is Judaism's tradition of training character traits (middot). One trait a week through Pirkei Avot and Luzzatto's Mesillat Yesharim: learning from everyone (Avot IV, 1), greeting people warmly (I, 15), not judging quickly (II, 5 and I, 6), honouring others (IV, 15), watchfulness and zeal (Mesillat Yesharim, chapters 2 and 6), humility (Avot IV, 4; Mesillat Yesharim, chapter 22), then a review built on daily "accounting" (chapter 3). Each day: slow morning reading, one small act of the week's trait, an evening trait notebook.

8 weeks · Level 2 · 20–30 min/day

DT3 Job and the question of suffering

Six passages from the Book of Job (JPS 1917): the opening (chapters 1–2), Job’s lament (7, 23), Eliphaz and Job’s reply to his friends (4, 16), the voice from the whirlwind (38), Job’s final reply (42:1–6) and the ending (42:7–11), with a few Talmud discussions. Each day, slow reading and 25–30 minutes of sitting; each week one small practice: writing a question without rushing to answer, writing a lament, listening without explaining, walking to notice something vast, reading with someone, checking in on someone who is suffering. The course does not explain why any particular person suffers.

6 weeks · Level 3 · 30–40 min/day

DT4 Shabbat in the family

A Level 4 course bringing three things from the Jewish tradition into family life: the day of rest, giving to the poor (tzedakah) and the shared table. Readings: the sabbath as a sign and a delight (Exodus 31:16–17, Isaiah 58:13–14), rest for workers too (Exodus 23:12), opening your hand to the needy (Deuteronomy 15:7–11) with Maimonides' eight levels of giving (summarised), the corner of the field and the forgotten sheaf (Leviticus 19:9–10, Deuteronomy 24:19), the open house (Pirkei Avot I, 5), and the song for the sabbath day (Psalm 92). The exercises are for any family: a shared rest period prepared in advance, sharing housework again, a sharing box, setting something aside for others, meals without screens and inviting guests.

6 weeks · Level 4 · Fits daily life
