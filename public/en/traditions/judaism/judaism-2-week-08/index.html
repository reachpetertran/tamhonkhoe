Judaism 2 – Week 8: Review – keeping accounts like a merchant

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: reading Luzzatto on "keeping accounts" every day, then looking back over the seven weeks of the course.

Measurable goal: keep the evening notebook at a fixed time on at least 5 of 7 days; re-rate the seven traits and compare with Week 1; choose two traits and write a four-week plan.

1. Traditional teaching

The great merchant and the ledger

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 3 of Mesillat Yesharim explains how to keep watchfulness. Luzzatto quotes the Talmud (Eruvin 13a): now that a person has been created, let him examine his deeds, in two ways: looking for any deed that is wrong, and "feeling out" even the good deeds to see whether anything not good is mixed into them, as one feels a garment to see whether it is still strong or has become frayed.

A note on context: straight after this, Luzzatto quotes the Talmud (Bava Batra 78b) reading the words "let us enter into an accounting" in the Book of Numbers as an invitation to "consider the accounting of the world": the loss and gain of each deed. The title of the book Cheshbon HaNefesh ("Accounting of the soul"), mentioned in Week 1, uses this same word, cheshbon, "account, reckoning". [Inference] Segment 12 makes two very practical points: keep accounts daily, and set a fixed time for it. A merchant does not look at the books whenever it comes to mind; there is a schedule. Segment numbers follow Sefaria's division of the text.

Looking back over the seven traits

Week Trait Main exercise
1 Learning from everyone One thing learned from one person

5 Watchfulness Three pauses before a habit

7 Humility Learn, admit a mistake, or help

[Verified] According to the book description on Sefaria, Cheshbon HaNefesh works on 13 traits, one a week, repeating the cycle four times a year, and suggests ways to adapt the programme to each person. [Expert judgement] The four-week plan below is a small cycle in that spirit.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies with 19,951 people: monitoring one's own progress made people more likely to reach their goals, by a small-to-medium amount (d = 0.40); the effect was larger when progress was written down or reported to others. (Harkin et al. 2016)
  • [Verified] A small daily action became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person (82 people with enough data); missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally et al. 2010)
  • [Inference] Eight weeks is short compared with the time a habit needs to settle. Your self-ratings may change very little; that does not mean the course has failed. No study has evaluated this programme directly.

3. Practice for adults

About 20–25 minutes a day.

  1. Day 1: choose a time (1 minute). Choose a fixed time for the evening notebook, for example straight after brushing your teeth. Put it in your calendar.
  2. Day 1: re-rate (10 minutes). Rate the seven traits again from 0 to 10, before opening your Week 1 ratings. Then put the two pages side by side. Write down which traits changed, which did not, and one reason you can guess at.
  3. Morning: slow reading (3 minutes). Read segment 12 twice. The second time, stop at "definite times and hours".
  4. Days 2–3: reflection (10 minutes). Write: "Which exercise was easiest to keep? Which changed the way I treat others the most? Which did I drop early, and why?"
  5. Day 4: choose two traits and write a four-week plan (10 minutes). For example: weeks 1 and 3 practise trait A, weeks 2 and 4 practise trait B, each week keeping that trait's main exercise (table above) and the evening notebook.
  6. The remaining days: practise trait A. Re-read the passage from the matching week.
  7. Evening, at your chosen time: trait notebook (5 minutes). Mark ✓ or ✗, then write two lines: "Today, what I want to keep is …"; "What I want to fix is …".

If you have a study companion, tell them your four-week plan.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Activity: "The trait tree" (10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. Draw a tree with many branches on a big sheet of paper.
  2. Together, remember the past week: what the child learned from whom, whom they greeted warmly, whom they helped, when they owned up to something. Each thing the child can tell you is a leaf; the child colours it and writes (or the adult helps write) a few words on it.
  3. The adult also draws a leaf for something of their own.
  4. Hang the tree on the wall. Next week you can add more leaves.

Do not count leaves to compare, and give no rewards or punishments by number of leaves.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • Comparing your Week 1 and Week 8 scores leaves you very disappointed or self-critical. If so, put the scores away and keep only the evening notebook with one good thing a day.
  • The evening notebook turns into examining every small fault without being able to stop, or keeps you awake. If so, cut it to one line, and ask a doctor if it continues.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
