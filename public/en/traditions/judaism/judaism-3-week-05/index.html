Judaism 3 – Week 5: Job's reply

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

Before you start: this Level 3 course deals with suffering and loss. If you are newly bereaved, seriously ill, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, please save the course for another time and see Get help now straight away.

This week: slow reading of Job's final reply. Sit for 30 minutes each day. Once this week, read the passage together with a friend you trust (the course plan said "reading with a reviewer"; as the course has no reviewer yet, this became "reading with someone").

Measurable goal: a full session of reading and sitting on at least 5 of 7 days; one 15-minute reading with another person this week; each evening, note one thing you once only "heard about" and have since lived through yourself.

1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] After God speaks from the whirlwind, Job answers one last time. These are Job's final words in the book.

Reading in context

  • [Inference] Verses 3 and 4 closely echo God's words in 38:2–3 ("Who is this that darkeneth counsel…", "I will demand of thee…"). Some readers take Job to be repeating God's words and then answering them. JPS translates verse 4 as Job speaking to God. A reviewer will explain how the sages read it.
  • [Expert judgement] Verse 6 is very short in Hebrew and can be translated in several ways. The words "my words" are added by JPS for clarity; the verb rendered "repent" can also be understood as "be comforted" or "change my mind". So some read the verse as Job's remorse, others as Job ceasing to argue and finding comfort as he sits in dust and ashes. The course does not choose one reading.
  • [Inference] Verse 5 is where many readers pause longest: Job is still not told why he suffered, but what he knows has changed, from "heard of" to "seeth".
  • "Canst", "withholden", "seeth" and "abhor" are old English for "can", "held back", "sees" and "loathe".
  • The course only describes what the book tells. It does not say that a suffering person must "accept" or must stop asking.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any study of reading Job 42, or of reading scripture with another person.
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 97 studies (7,181 people) on psychotherapy that integrates the client's faith: it reduced distress as much as standard therapy and increased spiritual well-being. (Captari et al. 2018)
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 148 studies (308,849 people): those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation. (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010)
  • [Inference] Neither study measured reading scripture with someone. The "reading with someone" exercise draws on the Jewish tradition of learning in pairs (chavruta), not on research findings.

Limits: the evidence is indirect.

3. Practice for adults

About 40 minutes a day.

  1. Settle (1 minute).
  2. Read slowly (5 minutes). Read the six verses. Each day, linger over one verse; on days five and six, linger over verse 5.
  3. Sit for 30 minutes.
    • First 10 minutes: notice the breath.
    • Middle 15 minutes: recall something you once only "heard about" (illness, old age, loss, being a parent) and later lived through yourself. How did what you know change? Choose something manageable, not something still too painful.
    • Last 5 minutes: just sit, without thinking further.
  4. Come back to the ground (2 minutes).
  5. Evening note (2 minutes). One thing you once only "heard about" and have since lived through.

One reading with someone (15 minutes, once this week): choose a friend you trust, a family member, or someone from your own faith community. Each of you reads the passage aloud once. Each of you names one verse that made you pause, and why. The other only listens, then asks one question. You do not need to reach a shared conclusion.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "Who helped me today?": at the end of the day, adult and child each name one person who helped them, especially when something small went wrong (dropping something, forgetting homework, a little fall), then say thank you or draw a card. See the full activity at Kids' corner – Activity 2.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
  • Verse 6 ("I am dust and ashes") leaves you feeling worthless, or feeling you must blame yourself for your suffering. If so, stop the course and talk with someone you trust; the course does not teach that.
  • The reading with another person turns into an argument about beliefs. Stop the reading; the only aim is to listen to each other.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

Send a message to a friend you trust: "This week I'd like to read something with you for 15 minutes. Are you free?"
