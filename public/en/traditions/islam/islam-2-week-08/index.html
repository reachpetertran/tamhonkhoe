Islam 2 – Week 8: Review

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read the next-to-last verse of chapter 7, about quiet remembrance within oneself at morning and evening. Then look back over eight weeks, choose two habits to keep and write a plan for the next four weeks.

Measurable goal: sit still for 20 minutes on at least 3 days of the week; rate the course's eight habits; choose two habits and write a 4-week plan (what, after which point, where you will tick it off).

1. Traditional teaching

Within oneself, morning and evening

[Traditional teaching] Verse 205 of chapter 7 (Al-A'raf) speaks of how to remember: within oneself, humbly, not aloud, at morning and evening, and without becoming neglectful.

Reading in context

  • Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. Words in brackets are the translator's: Pickthall notes that the verse is addressed to the Prophet Muhammad; the Vietnamese translation on QuranEnc notes in brackets that it is addressed to the Muslim believer. [Expert judgement] The two notes do not exclude each other: many commentators read words addressed to the Prophet as teaching for believers too.
  • [Inference] The verse gathers up what the course has read: remembrance within oneself (the hadith in Week 4), morning and evening (33:42 in Week 2), and not being neglectful (dhikr means "remembering").
  • The course has described dhikr and the five prayers, not taught how to do them. Muslims who want to go deeper should learn with a teacher in their own community.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] "If… then…" plans helped people reach goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65), in a meta-analysis of 94 independent tests with more than 8,000 people; goals and measures varied widely. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
  • [Inference] Your 4-week plan reuses this method. No study has evaluated this course directly; all the evidence in it is indirect.

3. Practice for adults

About 25–30 minutes a day; on day 7, an extra 20 minutes for the review.

  1. Morning: slow reading (3 minutes). Read 7:205. On days 1–6, also read one verse from an earlier week each day (13:28, 2:152, 11:114, 2:186, 3:190, 62:10).
  2. Sitting still (20 minutes, on at least 3 days). Other days 15 minutes. If 20 minutes does not suit you, 15 minutes is enough.
    • Muslims: keep dhikr as your community teaches.
    • Those who are not Muslim: sit still with the out-breath. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation, not dhikr.
  3. Three pauses. As in earlier weeks.
  4. Day 7: review (20 minutes). Rate each habit from the eight weeks: 0 = not done, 1 = a little, 2 = fairly regular, 3 = very regular.
Week Habit Score 0–3
1 Sitting still for 10–20 minutes
2 One thing to be grateful for at each pause
3 Three pauses: morning, midday, evening
4 Reading one passage slowly each morning
5 One minute looking at the sky
6 One block of time for others each week
6 One stretch away from buying and selling each week
7 A minimum version; never missing two days in a row

Then choose two habits to keep (they need not be your two highest scores). Write a 4-week plan using "If… then…": what, after which point, where you will tick it off. Include the minimum version for hard days.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "Telling the day" (5 minutes before sleep, with an adult)

  1. The adult asks three questions, and the child answers each with one thing: "What fun thing did you do this morning?", "What did you eat at midday?", "Who did you play with this afternoon?"
  2. Then ask: "Who, or what, would you like to say thank you for today?"
  3. The adult also tells the child one thing from their own day.

If the child doesn't want to talk, the adult can simply tell their own; don't press. Muslim families can end with a thank-you or a bedtime prayer in their own family's way.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The rating table leaves you discouraged or blaming yourself. There is no "pass" mark; the table is only for choosing two things to keep.
  • The words "be not thou of the neglectful" make you anxious or preoccupied that you have not remembered enough. Then stop reading this verse and talk to someone you trust or a teacher in your community.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting still for 20 minutes or more.
  • Then stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

To keep the two habits you chose: Một việc nhỏ có ý nghĩa mỗi ngày (One small meaningful thing a day; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write two "If… then…" sentences for the two habits you are keeping, and stick them where their points are.
