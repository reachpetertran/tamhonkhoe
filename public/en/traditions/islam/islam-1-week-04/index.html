Islam 1 – Week 4: Patience, within one's capacity

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: learn about sabr, patience, and the Qur'an verse saying that no one is given a burden beyond their capacity.

Measurable goal: each day sort the things weighing on you into "within my capacity" and "beyond my capacity", on at least 5 of 7 days; take one small step on the same hard task on at least 5 of 7 days; for one thing beyond your capacity, ask for help or drop part of it once.

1. Traditional teaching

Time and endurance

[Traditional teaching] Sabr is usually translated as patience, endurance or perseverance. The Islamic tradition teaches sabr in several senses: steadfastness in hardship, perseverance in doing good, and holding oneself back from wrongdoing. Chapter 103 (Al-'Asr) has only three verses and is one of the shortest chapters of the Qur'an.

Not beyond capacity

[Traditional teaching] The last verse of chapter 2 (Al-Baqarah) is known by heart by many Muslims. This tradition teaches that Allah knows each person's strength, and that believers both do their part and ask for help.

Reading in context

  • Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. In this file verse 103:2 reads "man is a state of loss". [Inference] A word ("in") is probably missing; the page quotes the file exactly as it stands.
  • Pickthall renders the opening oath as "By the declining day"; the Vietnamese translation on QuranEnc renders it as "by time". The Arabic word al-'asr can carry both meanings.
  • In chapter 103, endurance is not one person's private matter: believers "exhort one another". [Inference] Patience here goes together with community, not enduring alone.
  • Verse 2:286 is quoted in full, including the closing prayer, to match the Vietnamese page, whose translation may not be cut. The course Convergence 1, Week 10 sets this verse beside Epictetus and the Dhammapada.
  • [Inference] In this tradition, patience and "not beyond capacity" belong together: steadfastness does not mean carrying everything, and accepting one's limits is not giving up.
  • Very important: patience does not mean putting up with violence, abuse, exploitation or an unsafe workplace. The course does not read these verses as telling anyone to stay and endure it. If you are not safe, the first step is to get help: see Get help now.

2. What science finds

  • We have not found any review that evaluates the practice of sabr itself.
  • [Verified] The closest is research on acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT), which teaches people to accept what cannot be changed while still acting on what they value. A review of 20 meta-analyses found ACT effective for anxiety, depression, substance use and chronic pain, on a par with established therapies. (Gloster et al. 2020)
  • [Inference] ACT is a therapy led by professionals, not set within faith. This week's "within / beyond capacity" exercise resembles it only in accepting limits while still acting; it has not been tested on its own.

Limits: the quality of the meta-analyses in the review varies. The exercise does not replace treatment if you are overwhelmed or burnt out.

3. Practice for adults

About 10–12 minutes a day.

  1. Morning: read slowly (3 minutes). Read chapter 103, then the first sentence of 2:286 ("Allah tasketh not a soul…").
  2. Sort (4 minutes). Write down three things weighing on you. Beside each, write one letter:
    • W (within my capacity): I can do it, even if it is hard.
    • B (beyond my capacity): I cannot manage it alone, or it is not in my power.
  3. One small step (during the day). Choose one hard W task and keep it all week. Each day take one small step, 5–15 minutes. When it is done, stop; you do not need to finish everything.
  4. One B (once in the week). For one B, choose one of two: ask someone for help, or drop part of it.
  5. Evening: write one line (2 minutes). "Today I was patient with…".
  6. Muslims can say the closing prayer of 2:286 in their own way. Those who are not Muslim stop at step 5.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "The muddy jar": shake a jar of water with a little sand in it, then sit together and wait for the sand to settle. The game helps children practise waiting without rushing. See the full activity in Kids' corner – Activity 5.

While you wait, the adult can say: "Some things just need us to wait a little longer."

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The list makes you feel more overwhelmed. If so, write just one thing, or rest from the exercise for a few days.
  • You realise you are being "patient" with violence, abuse or exploitation. That is not what this lesson teaches; please seek help.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

When a hard problem drags on, daynentang has a lesson on breaking it into steps: Giải quyết vấn đề (Problem solving; in Vietnamese). When worry keeps going round in circles, see also Lo lắng vòng vo (Worry that goes in circles; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write down one thing you are trying to carry alone, and the name of someone you could ask.
