Islam 2 – Week 6: Friday and community

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: learn about the Friday congregational prayer (Jumu'ah), when remembrance is no longer private but shared by the whole community. This week's exercise takes your daily rhythm out of solitude.

Measurable goal: give at least one 60-minute block in the week to other people (a shared meal, helping someone, a group activity), without your phone; one 30-minute block "leaving your trading" (no work, no shopping, online included); keep the three pauses and 15 minutes of sitting on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

Friday

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 62 is called Al-Jumu'ah, meaning "the gathering", which is also the Arabic name for Friday. Verses 9–10 speak of the Friday congregational prayer.

The Jumu'ah prayer (described)

[Traditional teaching] At midday on Friday, Muslims gather at the mosque. The service includes a sermon (khutbah) and a short congregational prayer, which takes the place of the usual midday prayer. The tradition teaches that it is a duty for adult men; women may attend. The details and the cases of exemption are set by Islamic law and differ in places between schools.

Reading in context

  • Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. Pickthall calls Friday "the day of congregation".
  • The Vietnamese translator on QuranEnc adds a note that the Friday prayer consists of a sermon in two parts and a prayer of only two units (rak'ah).
  • [Inference] Verse 10 shows that Friday is not a full day of rest: after the prayer, believers return to work "seeking Allah's bounty", and keep remembering while they work. The tradition has no compulsory day of rest like the Jewish Sabbath.
  • [Inference] The same passage speaks both of leaving trade and of returning to it: religious life and working life do not exclude each other.
  • The hadith in Week 4 says that Allah remembers the one who remembers Him "among a group". Friday is when dhikr is most clearly done together.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 148 studies, 308,849 people: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation and does not prove that relationships are the cause. (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010)
  • [Inference] The research measures social relationships in general, not attendance at religious services. This week's exercise (a block of time for others) rests only on the general point that people need one another.

3. Practice for adults

About 20 minutes a day, plus two blocks of time in the week.

  1. Morning: slow reading (3 minutes). Read 62:9–10.
  2. Sitting still (15 minutes). As last week.
  3. Three pauses (1–2 minutes each). As last week.
  4. One 60-minute block for others (once in the week). Choose the day and time at the start of the week. For example: a shared meal with family or friends, helping a neighbour, a session with a group you belong to. Put your phone away for this time.
  5. One 30-minute block "leaving your trading" (once in the week). No work, no shopping, no browsing shops online. Use the time to walk, read, or sit with the people you live with.
  6. Evening: write one line (3 minutes). "Who was I with today, and for how long was I fully there?"

Muslims keep Jumu'ah as their community teaches. Those who are not Muslim do not need to (and should not, uninvited) attend the prayer in order to "practise"; if you would like to learn about a Muslim community, ask someone in that community first.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "Sending good wishes": the family sits together, thinks of one person (a grandparent, a friend, a teacher, a neighbour) and sends them a good wish, in words or with a card. See the full activity at Kids' corner – Activity 7.

The adult can say: "Some things we do alone, and some things we do together."

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The block of time for others exhausts you or puts you next to someone with whom you are not safe. This lesson does not ask you to meet anyone who harms you; choose someone else, or a way of helping from a distance.
  • You feel more hurt or lonely because there is no one to do it with. Then start with one message asking after someone, and see the daynentang lesson on connection below.
  • Then stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

For more people to turn to and spend time with: Kết nối, người mình có thể nhờ (Connection, people I can turn to; in Vietnamese). For families who want to do something for the community together: Cả nhà giúp cộng đồng (The whole family helps the community; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Put this week's two blocks in your calendar: 60 minutes for others and 30 minutes away from buying and selling.
