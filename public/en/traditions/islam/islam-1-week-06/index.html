Islam 1 – Week 6: Small regular deeds (and review)

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: learn the teaching that no good deed is too small, and that doing something regularly is worth more than doing a lot at once. At the end of the week, review the whole course.

Measurable goal: keep one small good deed (2–5 minutes) at the same point in the day, on at least 6 of 7 days; at the end of the week rate the course's six habits, choose two to keep, and write a 4-week plan.

1. Traditional teaching

An atom's weight

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 99 (Az-Zalzalah, "The Earthquake") speaks of the Last Day, when everything people have done is laid open. Its two closing verses are often quoted to say that no deed is too small.

Small deeds, done regularly

[Traditional teaching] This tradition teaches doing good deeds with sincerity and moderation, without straining beyond one's strength. Hadith 6464 in Sahih al-Bukhari, narrated by Aisha, sits in a chapter on taking a middle course and keeping deeds regular. It teaches that

even if the deed is small. The same hadith also teaches that no one enters Paradise by their deeds alone. The English translation on sunnah.com is in copyright, so this page quotes only a short phrase. Read the full hadith at sunnah.com, Bukhari 6464. The course Convergence 0, Week 4 sets this hadith beside the Tao Te Ching and Seneca.

Reading in context

  • Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. The Vietnamese translation on QuranEnc renders "an atom's weight" as "as light as a mustard seed".
  • Verse 8 says that even a small evil deed will be seen. [Inference] Read beside hadith 6464, the main point is that every deed matters and people are responsible for what they do; the final outcome, in this tradition, still rests on Allah's mercy, not on a tally of points.
  • [Inference] Here regularity is a moral and religious matter before God, not a time-management trick.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] In a study that followed 96 people who each chose a small daily action (eating, drinking or activity) in the same setting for 12 weeks, the action gradually became automatic. The time to come close to full automaticity varied widely, from 18 to 254 days. Missing one occasion did not materially affect the process. (Lally et al. 2010)
  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 33 studies of self-control training programmes found a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017)
  • [Inference] Together these suggest choosing a very small deed, tying it to a fixed point in the day, and not giving up after missing a day, rather than relying on willpower. There is no study of small good deeds within an Islamic framework.

Limits: the study by Lally et al. is small, participants reported for themselves, and only 82 provided enough data.

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day; on day 7, 20 minutes for the review.

  1. Day 1: choose a small deed (5 minutes). A good deed of 2–5 minutes that you can do even when tired: washing a cup for someone at home, sending a message to check on someone, picking up one piece of litter, slowly reading a verse from an earlier week. Tie it to a fixed point: "After breakfast, I will…".
  2. Every day: read slowly (2 minutes). Read verses 99:7–8.
  3. Every day: do the small deed and mark it. The goal is 6 of 7. If you miss a day, simply carry on the next day; do not double up.
  4. Day 7: review (20 minutes). Rate each habit from the six weeks: 0 = not done, 1 = a little, 2 = fairly regular, 3 = very regular.
Week Habit Score 0–3
1 Slow reading and writing one line
2 Keeping one small promise in full
3 Noting gratitude; thanking people
4 One small step on a hard task; asking for help when it is beyond you
5 Learning about someone different from you
6 One small good deed each day

Then choose two habits to keep (they need not be the two with the highest scores). Write a 4-week plan: what, when, after which fixed point, and where you will mark it.

Muslims who want to go deeper should continue learning with a teacher in their community. The course Islam 2 on dhikr and the rhythm of the day is being prepared in the internal area.

4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity "A pebble a day": each day the child does a small good deed and puts a pebble in a jar, and the family watches the jar slowly fill. See the full activity in Kids' corner – Activity 4.

The adult can say: "Each pebble is small, but there is one every day."

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The verse about evil deeds (99:8) leaves you frightened or blaming yourself for a long time, or you feel you must count and record everything you do in case it is "not enough". If so, stop and talk with a trusted friend, a teacher in your community, or a mental health professional.
  • The rating table discourages you. There is no "pass" score; the table is only for choosing two things to keep.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

For choosing and keeping one small meaningful deed, daynentang has a lesson: Một việc nhỏ có ý nghĩa mỗi ngày (One small meaningful thing a day; in Vietnamese).

7. Practice for today

Write the sentence "After …, I will …" for this week's small deed, and stick it where that fixed point happens (the dining table, the front door).
