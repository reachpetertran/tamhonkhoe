Islam

Islam 1 – Week 1: The Opening

Al-Fatihah, the first chapter of the Qur'an, seven short verses. Ten minutes of slow reading a day: choose one word and write one line. The course describes; it does not teach ritual.

Islam 1 – Week 2: The five pillars (described)

The five pillars of Islam as described in the hadith of Jibril, and Qur'an 2:177: righteousness is not turning your faces to the East and the West. Described, not taught as ritual.

Islam 1 – Week 3: Gratitude

Qur'an 14:7 and 14:34: "If ye give thanks, I will give you more"; blessings too many to count. And a hadith: whoever does not thank people is not thankful to Allah. Note one thing you are grateful for each evening; thank two people this week.

Islam 1 – Week 4: Patience, within one's capacity

Chapter 103 (Al-'Asr): "exhort one another to endurance"; and Qur'an 2:286: "Allah tasketh not a soul beyond its scope." Each day, sort what is within and beyond your capacity, and take one small step on a hard task.

Islam 1 – Week 5: That you may know one another

Qur'an 49:13: people were made nations and tribes "that ye may know one another". One conversation to understand someone from another culture, region or generation.

Islam 1 – Week 6: Small regular deeds (and review)

Qur'an 99:7–8: whoever does good an atom's weight will see it; and the hadith Bukhari 6464: the deed most beloved to Allah is the one done regularly, even if small. Keep one small deed on 6 of 7 days, then review the six weeks.

Islam 2 – Week 1: Hearts at rest in remembrance

Qur'an 13:28: "in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest". The first week of a course that describes dhikr and the rhythm of the day: slow reading, 10 minutes of sitting still, one neutral pause during the day.

Islam 2 – Week 2: Remember Me

Qur'an 2:152 ("remember Me, I will remember you. Give thanks to Me") and 33:41–42 (remember much, early and late). The common phrases of dhikr described; a morning pause added, each pause paired with one thing to be grateful for.

Islam 2 – Week 3: The five prayers (described)

The five daily Salah prayers described, through Qur'an 11:114 ("at the two ends of the day and in some watches of the night"), 29:45 and the hadith of the river at the door (Bukhari 528, summarised). Described, not taught as ritual; a neutral evening pause added.

Islam 2 – Week 4: Dhikr in the hadith

Qur'an 2:186 ("surely I am nigh") and two hadith on dhikr (Bukhari 6407, 7405; summarised): the one who remembers and the one who does not are like the living and the dead; Allah is with the one who remembers Him. Sitting still rises to 15 minutes.

Islam 2 – Week 5: A learner’s daily rhythm

Qur'an 3:190–191: the difference of night and day are tokens for people of understanding, who remember Allah standing, sitting and reclining. This week you set your own daily rhythm with "if… then…" plans, and once a day look at the sky.

Islam 2 – Week 6: Friday and community

Qur'an 62:9–10: when the call is heard for the Friday prayer, haste to the remembrance of Allah and leave your trading; when the prayer is ended, disperse and seek Allah's bounty, and remember Allah much. The Jumu'ah prayer described; this week, one block of time for community and one block set apart from buying and selling.

Islam 2 – Week 7: Perseverance

Qur'an 2:153 ("Seek help in steadfastness and prayer") and 94:5–6 ("with hardship goeth ease"). Keeping the daily rhythm through hard days: a minimum version, and never missing two days in a row.

Islam 2 – Week 8: Review

Qur'an 7:205: remember your Lord within yourself, humbly, below your breath, at morn and evening. Rate the course's eight habits, keep two, and write a 4-week plan.
