Islam 2 – Week 5: A learner’s daily rhythm

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read two verses of the Qur'an about those who remember Allah "standing, sitting, and reclining" and reflect on night and day. You add no new pause; instead you make the three pauses your own: tie them firmly into your life with "if… then…" plans, and let one of them turn towards the sky.

Measurable goal: write three "if… then…" plans for the three pauses; keep all three pauses on at least 5 of 7 days; at one pause each day, look at the sky or at trees for at least 1 minute on at least 5 of 7 days; sit still for 15 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

1. Traditional teaching

Night and day as tokens

[Traditional teaching] Verses 190–191 near the end of chapter 3 (Al 'Imran) speak of people who look at the heavens and the earth, at night following day, and are led by this to remember Allah. The tradition teaches that remembrance is not only at prayer but in every posture of life: standing, sitting, lying down.

The Muslim daily rhythm (described)

[Traditional teaching] For many Muslims the day is divided by the five prayers (Week 3), and between them come short phrases of dhikr tied to ordinary moments: waking, eating, leaving home, going to sleep. This rhythm is handed down by the tradition, not set by each person.

Reading in context

  • Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. Words in brackets are the translator's.
  • Verse 191 ends with a plea to be saved from punishment. The course reads these verses for their reflection on heaven and earth, and does not go into teaching about the afterlife.
  • [Inference] "Standing, sitting, and reclining" shows that in this tradition remembrance can enter every moment, not only set hours.
  • A difference to state clearly: your pauses are a rhythm you set for your own life. They are different from the Muslim rhythm of prayer, which is a duty of faith with set times and forms, kept together with a community.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests, more than 8,000 people: "if… then…" plans helped people reach goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65); goals and measures varied widely across studies. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
  • [Inference] "If… then…" plans help people remember to do something; they say nothing about its spiritual value. No study is cited here for the sky-watching part of the exercise.

3. Practice for adults

About 25–27 minutes a day.

  1. Day 1: write your plans (10 minutes). Write three sentences, one per pause, on this pattern: "If [specific point], then I stop for three breaths and think of one thing I am grateful for." For example: "If I have just closed my laptop for lunch, then…". The point must be something that happens every day without fail.
  2. Morning: slow reading (3 minutes). Read 3:190–191, pausing at "the difference of night and day".
  3. Sitting still (15 minutes). As last week.
  4. Three pauses, one looking at the sky. Choose one of the three: go to a window, balcony or yard and look at the sky, clouds or trees for at least 1 minute. Just look; you do not need to think of anything.
  5. Evening: write one line (3 minutes). "What was the sky like today?" and "Which plan didn't work? Which point needs changing?"

Muslims do not need to set a rhythm of their own: yours already exists. You can use this week to read the two verses slowly and to notice one phrase of dhikr each day that you tend to say in a hurry.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "Looking at the sky three times" (5–10 minutes each time, on a weekend day, with an adult)

  1. In the morning, at midday and in the early evening, go with your child to a window or the yard and look at the sky for one minute.
  2. Each time the child says what colour the sky is, whether there are clouds, birds or wind.
  3. In the evening the child draws the three skies on a sheet of paper, in order.
  4. The adult says: "The sky changes colour all day, every day. Many people look at the sky and feel thankful."

Never look straight at the sun. Stand somewhere safe: no climbing onto balconies, railings or roofs.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
  • The plea at the end of verse 191 leaves you frightened or preoccupied for a long time. Then read only verse 190, and talk to someone you trust or a teacher in your community.
  • You feel anxious about not keeping a "perfect" rhythm. The goal is 5 of 7 days, not 7 of 7.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting still for longer than 20 minutes.
  • Then stop and see Get help now.
