Introduction: gratitude, patience, small regular deeds

Al-Fatihah, the five pillars (as described in the hadith of Jibril) and Qur'an 2:177, then gratitude (14:7, 14:34), patience and not beyond capacity (103, 2:286), knowing one another (49:13), and small regular deeds (99:7–8, Bukhari 6464). Ten minutes of slow reading and one small deed a day. The course describes; it does not teach ritual.

Lessons with a link are published. Lessons without a link are still a planned outline; their titles and passages may change during writing and review.

What science finds

  • 97 studies, 7,181 people: therapy that integrates the client’s faith reduces distress as well as standard therapy and adds spiritual well-being. There is no review of lectio divina itself. (Captari et al. 2018, Journal of Clinical Psychology 74(11) · source)
  • Patient-level meta-analysis of 3 trials, 266 people (Turkey, Pakistan, UK): faith-adapted behavioural activation for Muslims (BA-M) reduced PHQ-9 depression scores by about 2.7 points more than standard treatment, with better retention. Still small; a therapist-led treatment. (Mir et al. 2026, Cambridge Prisms: Global Mental Health 13 · source)
  • Review of 22 papers: Ramadan’s effect on mental health is generally small, but people with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder have a higher risk of relapse. (Heun 2018, Global Psychiatry 1(1) · source)
  • 27 studies, 3,675 people: gratitude exercises reduce depression and anxiety symptoms by a small amount; less so against active control groups. (Cregg & Cheavens 2021, Journal of Happiness Studies 22(1) · source)
  • Review of 20 meta-analyses: Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) works for anxiety, depression and chronic pain, about as well as CBT. This is guided therapy. (Gloster et al. 2020, Journal of Contextual Behavioral Science 18 · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 515 studies, 713 independent samples: contact between groups typically reduces prejudice, extending to the whole other group; more rigorous studies show clearer effects. (Pettigrew & Tropp 2006, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 90(5) · source)
  • 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6) · source)
  • 33 studies of self-control training: a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017, Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(6) · source)

Limits, honestly

The course describes and practises what the Islamic tradition teaches about gratitude, patience and small good deeds done regularly; learners do not need to be Muslim. It does not teach ritual (Salah, fasting, Zakah and Hajj are only described), does not teach Arabic recitation, has no exercise of pausing at prayer times, and does not ask anyone to change their beliefs. For those who are not Muslim, the reading is for understanding, not prayer. The Vietnamese pages quote Hasan Abdulkarim’s translation on QuranEnc.com word for word, footnotes included; the English pages quote Pickthall. Hadith are only summarised (Vietnamese) or quoted in a short phrase (English), because the translations on sunnah.com are in copyright. The course reads only a few short passages and does not go into disputed interpretations. ‘Patience’ does not mean putting up with violence, abuse or exploitation. No reviewer from within the Islamic tradition has checked it yet, and no study has evaluated this programme directly; the evidence in the lessons is only indirect.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
