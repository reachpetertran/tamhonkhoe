What science finds

  • 65 studies: humility goes with slightly better mental health (small associations). This is a correlation. There is no meta-analysis of humility training yet. (Lee et al. 2025, The Counseling Psychologist 53(6) · source)
  • 47 randomised trials, 3,515 people: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; not better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine 174(3) · source)
  • Review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020, Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica 142(5) · source)
  • Critical review: "mindfulness" has no agreed definition, many studies are methodologically weak and rely heavily on self-report, and adverse effects are not monitored enough. (Van Dam et al. 2018, Perspectives on Psychological Science 13(1) · source)
  • 33 studies of self-control training: a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017, Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(6) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 154 studies, 10,189 people: activities that calm the body (slow breathing, mindfulness, relaxation) reduced anger and aggression moderately (g = −0.63); arousal-raising activities (hitting a punch bag, running to let off steam) were ineffective overall. (Kjærvik & Bushman 2024, Clinical Psychology Review 109 · source)

Limits, honestly

The course reads Taoist texts; it does not teach Taoist ritual or cultivation methods. The Vietnamese is tamhonkhoe's own translation from James Legge (1891) and Herbert Giles (1889), not yet checked against the Chinese original. The course leaves out paragraph 1 of chapter 67 because it compares the Tao with other teachings. "Not striving" and "yielding" do not mean accepting harm; "economy" is not financial advice. No reviewer from within the Taoist tradition has checked it yet, and no study has evaluated this programme directly; the science in the lessons is only nearby evidence.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
