Mussar: training character

Mussar is Judaism's tradition of training character traits (middot). One trait a week through Pirkei Avot and Luzzatto's Mesillat Yesharim: learning from everyone (Avot IV, 1), greeting people warmly (I, 15), not judging quickly (II, 5 and I, 6), honouring others (IV, 15), watchfulness and zeal (Mesillat Yesharim, chapters 2 and 6), humility (Avot IV, 4; Mesillat Yesharim, chapter 22), then a review built on daily "accounting" (chapter 3). Each day: slow morning reading, one small act of the week's trait, an evening trait notebook.

What science finds

  • Meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies, 19,951 people: interventions that help people monitor their progress made them more likely to reach their goals, by a small-to-medium amount (d = 0.40); the effect was larger when progress was physically recorded or reported to others. It measured behavioural goals, not character. (Harkin et al. 2016, Psychological Bulletin 142(2) · source)
  • 27 experiments, 4,045 people: acts of kindness raise well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 76 · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 148 studies, 308,849 people: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation. (Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton 2010, PLoS Medicine 7(7) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 94 independent tests, more than 8,000 people: "if… then…" plans helped people reach goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65); goals and measures varied widely. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38 · source)
  • 33 studies of self-control training: a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017, Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(6) · source)
  • 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6) · source)
  • 65 studies: humility goes with slightly better mental health (small associations). This is a correlation. There is no meta-analysis of humility training yet. (Lee et al. 2025, The Counseling Psychologist 53(6) · source)

Limits, honestly

A Level 2 course, about 20–25 minutes a day; best taken after Judaism 1. It describes what the Mussar tradition teaches about character through Pirkei Avot and Mesillat Yesharim; learners do not need to be Jewish. In the tradition, training character is bound up with serving God and keeping the commandments; the course does not teach Jewish law (halakha) or ritual, does not teach Hebrew, and does not ask anyone to change their beliefs. The exercises (slow reading, one small act a day, an evening notebook) are tamhonkhoe's own, adapted from the "one trait a week" approach, and do not replace study with a teacher, a study partner or a Mussar group. The lesson on not judging quickly is not for excusing someone who is causing harm; the lesson on zeal involves no less sleep, food or rest; the lesson on humility is not about putting yourself down. Pirkei Avot is translated from Gorfinkle's edition (Gutenberg); Mesillat Yesharim from Rabbi Yosef Sebag's English translation (Sefaria, CC BY), not from Hebrew; the English pages quote both word for word. The descriptions of practice are the writer's judgement; no reviewer from within the Jewish tradition has checked it yet, and no study has evaluated this programme directly; the evidence in the lessons is only indirect.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
