What science finds

  • Randomised trial, 68 older adults who had fallen: walking meditation 20–30 minutes a day for 24 weeks improved balance about as much as standard balance training; the groups did not differ; no serious adverse events. (Phoobangkerdphol et al. 2022, Clinical Rehabilitation 36(4) · source)
  • Review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020, Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica 142(5) · source)
  • Overview of 97 systematic reviews (1,039 trials, 128,119 adults): physical activity programmes reduced depression (median effect size −0.43) and anxiety (−0.42) compared with usual care; higher intensity went with larger gains; effects were smaller in longer programmes; most reviews were of critically low quality. (Singh et al. 2023, British Journal of Sports Medicine 57(18) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 42 studies, 1,843 adults in outdoor walking groups: systolic blood pressure about 3.7 mmHg lower, lower resting heart rate, lower depression scores (SMD −0.67); no notable side effects; mostly before–after comparisons without a control group. (Hanson & Jones 2015, British Journal of Sports Medicine 49(11) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 15 cohorts, 47,471 adults: more daily steps went with a lower risk of death, levelling off at about 6,000–8,000 steps (aged 60+) and 8,000–10,000 steps (under 60); correlational. (Paluch et al. 2022, The Lancet Public Health 7(3) · source)

Limits, honestly

Gentle movement, not aimed at weight loss or performance, no step counting, and not a replacement for an exercise programme from a doctor or physiotherapist. People with heart disease, uncontrolled blood pressure, joint or bone problems, recent surgery, pregnancy or a tendency to fall should ask a doctor first. The passages are read to understand how each tradition sees the body; none is exercise advice. There is no meta-analysis of walking meditation or slow stretching and mental health; the evidence is mostly about physical activity in general, and the study on steps is correlational. Sleep has its home on daynentang.com; we only link there.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
