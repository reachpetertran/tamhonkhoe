What science finds

  • 38,124 workers: people who detach from work when off duty report less exhaustion and better sleep. This is a correlation. (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017, Frontiers in Psychology 7 · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 154 studies, 10,189 people: activities that calm the body (slow breathing, mindfulness, relaxation) reduced anger and aggression moderately (g = −0.63); arousal-raising activities (hitting a punch bag, running to let off steam) were ineffective overall. (Kjærvik & Bushman 2024, Clinical Psychology Review 109 · source)
  • 27 experiments, 4,045 people: acts of kindness raise well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 76 · source)
  • Pre-registered study, 1,307 people in 3 countries: a popular but mistaken reading of Stoicism (wanting to show fewer emotions and to feel less) went with lower well-being. This is a correlation. (Karl et al. 2022, Journal of Happiness Studies 23(7) · source)
  • 5 cohort studies: volunteers had lower mortality. This is a correlation; randomised trials have not shown clear benefit. (Jenkinson et al. 2013, BMC Public Health 13:773 · source)
  • 33 studies of self-control training: a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017, Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(6) · source)

Limits, honestly

Acute anger and quick calming skills have their home on daynentang.com; we only link there. The course does not follow the passages about working without eating or sleeping, the treatment of slaves in Roman times, or submitting to a father who strikes you; no exercise is meant for situations involving violence, threats or harassment. Hierocles’ circles are given as a summary, not quoted. No study has evaluated this programme directly.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
