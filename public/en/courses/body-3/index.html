Optional eating disciplines: plant-based eating and 16:8

For healthy adults only. The first week is a list of people who should not do this and a self-check; then you try plant-based eating at a manageable level, narrow your eating window step by step to 16:8 if it feels fine, and listen to your body to keep, reduce or stop. No calorie counting, not aimed at weight loss. Not yet reviewed by a dietitian.

What science finds

  • 99 randomised trials, 6,582 adults (most with overweight or illness): intermittent fasting gives weight loss similar to daily calorie restriction; time-restricted eating (such as 16:8) is no better; no difference in HbA1c. (Semnani-Azad et al. 2025, BMJ 389:e082007 · source)
  • 30 randomised trials: vegetarian or vegan diets lowered total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B compared with an omnivorous diet; no difference in triglycerides. Measures blood fats, not mental health. (Koch et al. 2023, European Heart Journal 44(28) · source)
  • 13 observational studies: mixed results on vegetarian diets and mood. This is a correlation; science has not shown that a vegetarian diet improves mood. (Iguacel et al. 2021, Nutrition Reviews 79(4) · source)
  • 12-week randomised trial, 116 adults with overweight or obesity: a 16:8 eating window gave no more weight loss than three regular meals; the 16:8 group lost more lean mass. (Lowe et al. 2020, JAMA Internal Medicine 180(11) · source)
  • 12-month randomised trial, 139 people with obesity: adding an 8-hour eating window to calorie restriction did not reduce weight, fat or metabolic markers more than calorie restriction alone. (Liu et al. 2022, NEJM 386(16) · source)

Limits, honestly

Not for: pregnant or breastfeeding women; children and teenagers; people with diabetes on medication (especially insulin or a sulfonylurea); anyone with a current or past eating disorder. People who are underweight, take medication that must be taken with food, are older and frail, or are being treated for a chronic illness should ask a doctor first. The course has not been reviewed by a dietitian or doctor; the practice steps are the writer's judgement. The passages are read to understand how each tradition sees eating; none is dietary advice, and the course does not replace religious fasting. Evidence: 16:8 gives no more weight loss than daily calorie restriction; vegetarian diets lower blood fats in trials; there is no good evidence that either discipline helps inner life. Intermittent fasting is not magic.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
