What science finds

  • 27 experiments, 4,045 people: acts of kindness raise well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 76 · source)
  • 146 randomised studies of writing about experiences and feelings: a positive but very small overall effect (r = 0.075). (Frattaroli 2006, Psychological Bulletin 132(6) · source)
  • A survey, a follow-up and a randomised experiment: spending money on others went with, and in the experiment raised, happiness. The survey part is a correlation; one research group. (Dunn, Aknin & Norton 2008, Science 319(5870) · source)

Limits, honestly

A Level 4 course: no long sitting, only about 10 minutes of reading a day and practice in housework, work and relationships. The discourses reflect ancient Indian society (bondservants, marriages arranged by parents, roles divided by gender); the course keeps the idea that duties go both ways, not those divisions of roles. The course does not use the discourses to advise anyone to stay in a relationship with violence or control. The money lessons only describe the teaching and are not financial advice; the course asks for no money and recommends no organisation. Learners do not need to believe in rebirth. No reviewer from within the Buddhist tradition has checked it yet, and no study has evaluated this programme directly; weeks 2 and 4 have no scientific evidence.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
