Rumi and the Sufi tradition

Slow reading of Rumi's Masnavi in Whinfield's version (1898): the reed flute, the pitcher of greed, love, the man at the door and the mirror, the elephant in a dark room; with Qur'an 2:156, 3:31 and 50:16. Describes Rumi, Shams, the Mevlevi order and the sema ceremony; no Sufi ritual is taught. The exercises are neutral rhythms set by tamhonkhoe: slow reading, 20–25 minutes of sitting and listening, a day buying nothing extra, acts of care and unseen good deeds, talking with a companion.

Lessons with a link are published. Lessons without a link are still a planned outline; their titles and passages may change during writing and review.

What science finds

  • 47 randomised trials, 3,515 people: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; not better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine 174(3) · source)
  • Review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020, Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica 142(5) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 753 effect sizes from 259 independent samples: people who put more weight on money and possessions reported slightly lower well-being (r = −0.19 for measures of materialist values and beliefs). This is a correlation. (Dittmar, Bond, Hurst & Kasser 2014, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 107(5) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 148 studies, 308,849 people: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation. (Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton 2010, PLoS Medicine 7(7) · source)
  • 27 experiments, 4,045 people: acts of kindness raise well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 76 · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 94 independent tests, more than 8,000 people: "if… then…" plans helped people reach goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65); goals and measures varied widely. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38 · source)
  • 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6) · source)

Limits, honestly

A Level 3 course, 30–45 minutes a day. It describes Rumi and the Sufi tradition (tasawwuf) as part of Islam; learners do not need to be Muslim. It does not teach Sufi ritual: no dhikr of any order, no sema turning, no initiation into an order, and it does not ask anyone to change their beliefs. The idea of fana (the passing away of the self in God) is only named. It does not quote the Masnavi lines on submitting to a guide even when the guide's actions make no sense. The course's exercises are neutral rhythms set by tamhonkhoe; they are not Sufi practice and do not replace it. Rumi's poetry comes from Whinfield's translation and abridgement (1898, public domain); the Vietnamese is tamhonkhoe's own translation from the English, so it has passed through two languages. Qur'an: the Vietnamese pages quote Hasan Abdulkarim's translation on QuranEnc.com word for word; the English pages quote Pickthall. The course notes that some Muslims criticise certain Sufi practices, without taking sides. Long sitting can be uncomfortable; every lesson has a Stop practising section. No reviewer from within the Islamic or Sufi tradition has checked it yet, no reader of Persian has compared it with the original, and no study has evaluated this programme directly; the evidence in the lessons is only indirect.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
