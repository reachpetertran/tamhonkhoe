What science finds

  • Critical review of "decentering" and related constructs such as cognitive defusion: proposes three processes (meta-awareness, disidentification from internal experience, reduced reactivity to thought content). A conceptual analysis, not a test of effectiveness. (Bernstein et al. 2015, Perspectives on Psychological Science 10(5) · source)
  • Interview study of Western Buddhist meditators and teachers: 59 kinds of meditation-related experience in 7 domains, including "sense of self"; impact ranged from minimal and passing to severe and lasting; 26 categories of influencing factors. (Lindahl et al. 2017, PLoS ONE 12(5) · source)
  • Review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020, Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica 142(5) · source)
  • 47 randomised trials, 3,515 people: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; not better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine 174(3) · source)
  • Critical review: "mindfulness" has no agreed definition, many studies are methodologically weak and rely heavily on self-report, and adverse effects are not monitored enough. (Van Dam et al. 2018, Perspectives on Psychological Science 13(1) · source)
  • Review of 20 meta-analyses: Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) works for anxiety, depression and chronic pain, about as well as CBT. This is guided therapy. (Gloster et al. 2020, Journal of Contextual Behavioral Science 18 · source)

Limits, honestly

A Level 3 course: 30–45 minutes a day, best taken after Buddhism 1 and 2. Contemplating not-self can bring feelings of being detached from oneself or of the world being ‘not real’; every session ends with a ‘back to the ground’ step and every lesson has a ‘Stop practising if’ section. People who are depressed, recently bereaved or having thoughts of self-harm should not take it now; people with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder, severe PTSD or lasting feelings of detachment should not take it alone. The course only describes Buddhist teaching, does not compare it with other traditions’ views of the soul or the self, does not quote the verses on the body left behind (SN 22.95), and does not ask learners to believe in rebirth or stages of attainment. It includes no tonglen. No reviewer from within the Buddhist tradition has checked it yet, and no study has evaluated this programme directly.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
