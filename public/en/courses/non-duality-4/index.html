Presence in ordinary tasks

A Level 4 course that carries attention into things everyone does: housework, main work, meals, caring for others. Every act as an offering (Gita 9:26–27), living a hundred years doing right action (Isha Upanishad verse 2), worship through one’s own work and every flame has smoke (Gita 18:45–46, 18:48), the meal as a small offering (Gita 3:13–14, 4:24), seeing one Life in all beings (Gita 6:29, 6:32), with perfect meditation comes perfect act (Gita 2:50). About 15 minutes a day, inside ordinary life.

Lessons with a link are published. Lessons without a link are still a planned outline; their titles and passages may change during writing and review.

What science finds

  • 47 randomised trials, 3,515 people: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; not better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine 174(3) · source)
  • Joint WHO/ILO estimates: in 2016 about 488 million people worked 55 or more hours a week; about 745,000 deaths from ischemic heart disease and stroke were attributed to such long hours. These are model estimates built on systematic reviews. (Pega et al. 2021, Environment International 154 (ước tính chung WHO/ILO) · source)
  • 38,124 workers: people who detach from work when off duty report less exhaustion and better sleep. This is a correlation. (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017, Frontiers in Psychology 7 · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 88 studies, more than 11,000 people: deliberate practice explained about 26% of the differences in performance in games, 21% in music, 18% in sports, 4% in education and less than 1% in professions. Mostly correlational. (Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014, Psychological Science 25(8) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 24 experimental studies: eating while distracted increased intake moderately in that meal (SMD 0.39) and more at a later meal (SMD 0.76); remembering the meal reduced later intake. (Robinson et al. 2013, The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 97(4) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 22 studies: eating slowly went with lower intake in the meal (SMD 0.45); hunger at the end of the meal and up to 3.5 hours later did not differ; studies varied widely. (Robinson et al. 2014, The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 100(1) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 17 studies, 182,836 children and adolescents: 3 or more family meals a week went with healthier eating and less disordered eating (correlational). (Hammons & Fiese 2011, Pediatrics 127(6) · source)
  • 27 experiments, 4,045 people: acts of kindness raise well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 76 · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 148 studies, 308,849 people: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation. (Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton 2010, PLoS Medicine 7(7) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies, 19,951 people: interventions that help people monitor their progress made them more likely to reach their goals, by a small-to-medium amount (d = 0.40); the effect was larger when progress was physically recorded or reported to others. It measured behavioural goals, not character. (Harkin et al. 2016, Psychological Bulletin 142(2) · source)
  • 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6) · source)

Limits, honestly

A Level 4 (Integration) course, about 15 minutes a day for 6 weeks, inside housework, main work, meals and caregiving; best taken after Non-duality 1. It describes what the Bhagavad Gita and the Isha Upanishad teach about daily work; learners do not need to be Hindu, and the course does not ask anyone to change their beliefs or to be devoted to anyone. The section name "Non-duality" comes from one reading (Advaita Vedanta); other schools read the same passages differently, and the course takes no side. It does not teach ritual, mantras or Sanskrit, does not discuss varna, does not take "the work allotted" as work fixed by birth, and does not teach putting up with dangerous, unfair or coerced work. The meal lesson has no fasting, dieting, weighing or calorie counting. The caregiving lesson is no substitute for expert help when a carer is exhausted or the person cared for is in danger. The original plan gave no passages for Weeks 4–6; the course adds Gita 2:50, 3:13–14, 4:24, 6:29, 6:32 and the second half of 18:48 from the same Arnold translation. The exercises (a sentence of intention, a task you put off, a stop-work time, a 25-minute single-task block, three breaths before a meal, two minutes of listening, self-rating and a 4-week plan) are tamhonkhoe's own and no substitute for a teacher within the tradition. On the Vietnamese pages the scripture is tamhonkhoe's own translation from the English of Edwin Arnold (Gita) and Swami Paramananda (Isha Upanishad); nothing is translated from Sanskrit. The English pages quote these two translations word for word. No reviewer from within the Hindu tradition has checked it yet, and no study has evaluated this programme directly; the evidence in the lessons is only indirect.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
