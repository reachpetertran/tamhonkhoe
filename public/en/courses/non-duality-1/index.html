Introduction to the Gita and the Upanishads

A Level 1 course of slow reading in short passages from the Bhagavad Gita and the Upanishads, two kinds of text that many Hindu traditions hold as scripture. Working without clinging to fruits (Gita 2:47), heat and cold that come and go (2:14–15), raising yourself up and being your own friend (6:5–6), the image of the chariot and the horses (Katha Upanishad 1.3.3–6), all things covered by the Lord and not coveting what is another's (Isha Upanishad 1, 6), the one who hates no being (Gita 12:13–14). The exercises are small everyday ones: doing a task fully and letting go of the result, watching a small discomfort until it passes, one encouraging sentence each morning, noticing a habit and pausing for one breath, slow reading, and one friendly act towards a difficult person.

What science finds

  • Review of 20 meta-analyses: Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) works for anxiety, depression and chronic pain, about as well as CBT. This is guided therapy. (Gloster et al. 2020, Journal of Contextual Behavioral Science 18 · source)
  • Meta-analysis of controlled studies: trying to suppress a thought makes it come back more often, by a small-to-moderate amount; the effect varied with the kind of thought and how it was measured. (Abramowitz, Tolin & Street 2001, Clinical Psychology Review 21(5) · source)
  • 39 randomised trials, 1,821 people: ACT did better than control conditions (g = 0.57), with no clear difference from CBT. (A-Tjak et al. 2015, Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics 84(1) · source)
  • 21 trials: lower burnout and stress, higher self-compassion; results vary a lot between studies. (Wang et al. 2024, Stress and Health 40(1) · source)
  • 33 studies of self-control training: a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017, Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(6) · source)
  • 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies, 19,951 people: interventions that help people monitor their progress made them more likely to reach their goals, by a small-to-medium amount (d = 0.40); the effect was larger when progress was physically recorded or reported to others. It measured behavioural goals, not character. (Harkin et al. 2016, Psychological Bulletin 142(2) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 753 effect sizes from 259 independent samples: people who put more weight on money and possessions reported slightly lower well-being (r = −0.19 for measures of materialist values and beliefs). This is a correlation. (Dittmar, Bond, Hurst & Kasser 2014, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 107(5) · source)
  • 27 experiments, 4,045 people: acts of kindness raise well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 76 · source)

Limits, honestly

A Level 1 course, about 10–15 minutes a day. It describes what the Bhagavad Gita and the Upanishads teach; learners do not need to be Hindu, and the course does not ask anyone to change their beliefs or to be devoted to anyone. The section name "Non-duality" comes from one reading (Advaita Vedanta); other schools, such as those of Ramanuja and Madhva, read the same texts differently, and the course takes no side. It does not discuss war in the Gita's setting, does not teach ritual, mantras or Sanskrit, and does not discuss the Katha Upanishad's question about death. The exercises (splitting "my part", watching small discomforts, the encouraging sentence, counting the "horse", the friendly act towards a difficult person) are tamhonkhoe's own, not practices of the tradition. "Bear with it" and "hating no being" do not mean putting up with pain, illness or being harmed by others. On the Vietnamese pages the Gita is tamhonkhoe's own translation from Edwin Arnold's verse version (Gutenberg #2388; Arnold does not number verses, so verse numbers are matched by content) and the Upanishads from Swami Paramananda's version (Gutenberg #3283); nothing is translated from Sanskrit. The English pages quote these two versions word for word. No reviewer from within the Hindu tradition has checked it yet, and no study has evaluated this programme directly; the evidence in the lessons is only indirect.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
