Kids’ corner 6+

Twelve 5–10 minute games that go with the 12 Convergence weeks: pausing, gratitude, swapping places, a small daily task, settling down, helping at home, good wishes, making up, letting others go first, change in nature, listening, giving. Always with an adult playing too. Not yet trial-taught.

What science finds

  • Meta-analysis of 33 randomised trials, 3,666 children and adolescents: mindfulness programmes improved attention, anxiety/stress and depression by a small amount (d 0.16–0.30); against active controls, benefits remained only for mindfulness, depression and anxiety/stress. (Dunning et al. 2019, Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry 60(3) · source)
  • 221 early adolescents, 11 classes randomly assigned: writing down things to be grateful for went with more gratitude, optimism and life satisfaction and less negative feeling; clearest for satisfaction with school. (Froh, Sefick & Emmons 2008, Journal of School Psychology 46(2) · source)
  • Children under 2 looked happier giving treats to others than receiving them, and happier still when giving their own. One small study, measured once. (Aknin, Hamlin & Dunn 2012, PLoS ONE 7(6) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 20 studies, 1,472 children and adolescents (mean age about 12): forgiveness education programmes increased forgiveness (g = 0.54) and reduced anger (g = 0.29). (Rapp, Wang Xu & Enright 2022, Child Development 93(5) · source)

Limits, honestly

Published before trial teaching, by decision of the project owner; each page says "not yet trial-taught" and has a section for notes after playing. The games do not teach any religion’s doctrine. No fasting, no dieting, no talk of trauma, no death in the games; always with an adult. No study has measured these particular games; research on mindfulness, gratitude and forgiveness in children involves multi-session programmes, and many studies are of children older than 10. Emotional skills for children aged 2–8 have their home on daynentang.com; we only link there.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
