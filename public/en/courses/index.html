Christianity

The Bible, Brother Lawrence, The Imitation of Christ, The Cloud of Unknowing, Dionysius.

Open

KT1 Slow reading of Scripture (lectio divina)

Psalm 23, the Beatitudes, the Lord’s Prayer, the parable of the Samaritan, "the birds and the lilies" and 1 Corinthians 13, read slowly in the four steps of lectio divina: reading, meditation, response, stillness; from week 4, one small action is added.

6 weeks · Level 1 · 10–15 min/day

Open

KT2 Prayer and presence

Brother Lawrence, The Imitation of Christ, Matthew 6:5–8, Psalm 131 and the tax collector’s prayer: silent prayer with few words, living in the presence of God in ordinary work, and persevering when prayer feels dry. The silent part grows from 10 to 25 minutes.

8 weeks · Level 2 · 20–30 min/day

Open

KT3 God beyond concepts

The Cloud of Unknowing (14th century) and The Mystical Theology by the author writing as Dionysius: the Christian contemplative tradition teaches that understanding cannot grasp God, only love turned towards Him. Each day, 25–40 minutes of silent sitting: not trying to picture anything, letting thoughts pass, returning to one short word, setting down an image, and practising gently without straining.

6 weeks · Level 3 · 30–45 min/day

Open

KT4 Faith in action

Matthew 25 (the least of these), James (faith and works), Acts 2 (shared life), Colossians 3 (working heartily; putting on love), 1 John 3:18, Micah 6:8 and Matthew 6:3–4: carrying faith into daily deeds for people in need, for a community, at work and in the family. Each day, 5 minutes of reading in the morning, one act during the day, 2 minutes of looking back in the evening.

6 weeks · Level 4 · Fits daily life

Islam

The Qur'an and hadith, presented for learners, not as ritual instruction.

Open

HG1 Introduction: gratitude, patience, small regular deeds

Al-Fatihah, the five pillars (as described in the hadith of Jibril) and Qur'an 2:177, then gratitude (14:7, 14:34), patience and not beyond capacity (103, 2:286), knowing one another (49:13), and small regular deeds (99:7–8, Bukhari 6464). Ten minutes of slow reading and one small deed a day. The course describes; it does not teach ritual.

6 weeks · Level 1 · 10–15 min/day

Open

HG2 Dhikr and the rhythm of the day (described)

Dhikr (remembrance of Allah) and the five daily prayers described through Qur'an 13:28, 2:152, 33:41–42, 11:114, 29:45, 2:186, 3:190–191, 62:9–10, 2:153, 94:5–6, 7:205 and three hadith (summarised). No ritual instruction. The exercises are neutral rhythms set by tamhonkhoe: slow reading in the morning, 10–20 minutes of sitting still, three pauses tied to your own day (not to prayer times), one line written in the evening.

8 weeks · Level 2 · 20–30 min/day

Open

HG3 Rumi and the Sufi tradition

Slow reading of Rumi's Masnavi in Whinfield's version (1898): the reed flute, the pitcher of greed, love, the man at the door and the mirror, the elephant in a dark room; with Qur'an 2:156, 3:31 and 50:16. Describes Rumi, Shams, the Mevlevi order and the sema ceremony; no Sufi ritual is taught. The exercises are neutral rhythms set by tamhonkhoe: slow reading, 20–25 minutes of sitting and listening, a day buying nothing extra, acts of care and unseen good deeds, talking with a companion.

6 weeks · Level 3 · 30–45 min/day

Open

HG4 Zakat, Ramadan and daily life

Who zakat is for (Qur'an 9:60) and giving without reproach (2:262–263); the month of Ramadan and the Night of Power (97:1–5); guarding speech (49:12, Bukhari 1903) and who is exempt from fasting; parents (17:23–24); neighbours and the wayfarer (4:36, Bukhari 6014); giving without wanting thanks (76:8–9). Zakat and fasting are only described; the exercises are everyday actions: one act of giving, slow evening reading, guarding one speech habit, asking after parents, learning a neighbour's name, and an optional light restraint exercise for healthy adults.

6 weeks · Level 4 · Fits daily life

Judaism

Torah, Talmud, Pirkei Avot.

Open

DT1 Torah, Shabbat and the Ethics of the Fathers

The Shema, "Hear" (Deuteronomy 6:4–9); loving the neighbour and the stranger (Leviticus 19:17–18, 33–34; Hillel, Shabbat 31a); the two reasons for Shabbat (Genesis 2:1–3, Deuteronomy 5:12–15); Hillel's three questions (Pirkei Avot I, 14); Rabbi Tarfon's "not to finish, but not to quit" (Pirkei Avot II); justice, kindness, humility (Micah 6:6–8). Law and ritual are only described; the exercises are everyday actions: slow reading, one kindness, one intentional rest period, doing something put off, ten minutes on a long-term task.

6 weeks · Level 1 · 10–15 min/day

Open

DT2 Mussar: training character

Mussar is Judaism's tradition of training character traits (middot). One trait a week through Pirkei Avot and Luzzatto's Mesillat Yesharim: learning from everyone (Avot IV, 1), greeting people warmly (I, 15), not judging quickly (II, 5 and I, 6), honouring others (IV, 15), watchfulness and zeal (Mesillat Yesharim, chapters 2 and 6), humility (Avot IV, 4; Mesillat Yesharim, chapter 22), then a review built on daily "accounting" (chapter 3). Each day: slow morning reading, one small act of the week's trait, an evening trait notebook.

8 weeks · Level 2 · 20–30 min/day

Open

DT3 Job and the question of suffering

Six passages from the Book of Job (JPS 1917): the opening (chapters 1–2), Job’s lament (7, 23), Eliphaz and Job’s reply to his friends (4, 16), the voice from the whirlwind (38), Job’s final reply (42:1–6) and the ending (42:7–11), with a few Talmud discussions. Each day, slow reading and 25–30 minutes of sitting; each week one small practice: writing a question without rushing to answer, writing a lament, listening without explaining, walking to notice something vast, reading with someone, checking in on someone who is suffering. The course does not explain why any particular person suffers.

Tao Te Ching chapters 8, 15, 16, 33 and 67, and the story of the cook in Zhuangzi chapter III; each day slow reading, a short time sitting still and one small action in daily life.

Tao Te Ching chapters 2, 11, 22, 48, 37, 63 and 43, and two stories from Zhuangzi chapter IV (‘fasting of the mind’, the li tree); each day 20–30 minutes of sitting and one small action: doing less, listening, not forcing. ‘Fasting of the mind’ is of the mind, not of the body.

Five stories from Zhuangzi chapters II, XVIII, VI, III and XXXII (the butterfly dream, the death of Zhuangzi’s wife, the four friends, the great furnace, the fire that passes on); each day 30–45 minutes of sitting and one small action: noticing change, watching the seasons turn, passing on what you have learned. The kids’ corner is only about change in nature.

The stories of the hunchback catching cicadas, the wheelwright and the gambler in the Zhuangzi (chapters XIX, XIII and III), and Tao Te Ching chapters 64, 44, 46, 54, 81 and 9; each day a few minutes of reading, then practice in work, housework and family life: practising small parts, dealing with things while small, knowing enough, care at the end, giving without keeping score.

Body

Eating and movement as practice. Eating disciplines are only an optional choice for healthy adults.

Open

TT1 Eating slowly, eating gratefully

Grace before meals in many traditions, one slow meal a day, screen-free meals and thanking the people who make our food. No calorie counting, no dieting.

4 weeks · Level 1 · 15 min/day

Open

TT2 Mindful movement

Walking meditation, slow stretching and one continuous 10–15 minute movement session, with teachings on the body from several traditions. Not aimed at weight loss or performance.

4 weeks · Level 2 · 10–15 min/day

Open

TT3 Optional eating disciplines: plant-based eating and 16:8

For healthy adults only. The first week is a list of people who should not do this and a self-check; then you try plant-based eating at a manageable level, narrow your eating window step by step to 16:8 if it feels fine, and listen to your body to keep, reduce or stop. No calorie counting, not aimed at weight loss. Not yet reviewed by a dietitian.

4 weeks · Level 4 · 10 + meals min/day

Open

Ramadan, Yom Kippur, Lent and the Buddhist eight precepts: read the texts and see what each tradition says about timing, purpose and who is exempt. How a course goes live

  • Published once the project owner approves: less sensitive courses that do not set out the doctrine or ritual of one religion (Stoicism, Body 1–2, this outline). The page says clearly that no reviewer from a tradition has checked it.
  • Needs a reviewer from the tradition: courses about one religion need at least one reviewer from within it; Convergence needs two reviewers from two traditions.
  • Drafted internally first: level 3 (God, self, death), detailed fasting, and anything still being debated.

Every lesson still has to be descriptive, non-divisive, use cleanly licensed sources and include safety notes. See About and principles.
