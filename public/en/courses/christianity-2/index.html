Prayer and presence

Brother Lawrence, The Imitation of Christ, Matthew 6:5–8, Psalm 131 and the tax collector’s prayer: silent prayer with few words, living in the presence of God in ordinary work, and persevering when prayer feels dry. The silent part grows from 10 to 25 minutes.

What science finds

  • Meta-analysis of randomised trials: religious/spiritual interventions reduce anxiety; programmes vary widely and study quality is limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015, Psychological Medicine 45(14) · source)
  • 97 studies, 7,181 people: therapy that integrates the client’s faith reduces distress as well as standard therapy and adds spiritual well-being. There is no review of lectio divina itself. (Captari et al. 2018, Journal of Clinical Psychology 74(11) · source)
  • 47 randomised trials, 3,515 people: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; not better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine 174(3) · source)
  • Review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020, Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica 142(5) · source)
  • Interview study of Western Buddhist meditators and teachers: 59 kinds of meditation-related experience in 7 domains, including "sense of self"; impact ranged from minimal and passing to severe and lasting; 26 categories of influencing factors. (Lindahl et al. 2017, PLoS ONE 12(5) · source)

Limits, honestly

A Level 2 course: 20–30 minutes a day, with the silent part growing to 25 minutes; best taken after Christianity 1. The course describes how the Christian tradition prays; learners do not need to be Christian, and each lesson offers a way of sitting in silence for those who do not pray, which is tamhonkhoe’s adaptation rather than a form of the tradition. The English pages quote Brother Lawrence (Project Gutenberg #13871), The Imitation of Christ (Benham) and the World English Bible, all public domain. The course does not teach the breathing method of the Jesus Prayer tradition, does not teach abstinence or ‘severe labours’, and does not use ‘God tries our love’ to explain illness, violence or loss. Long silent sitting can bring difficult experiences; every lesson has a ‘Stop practising if’ section, Week 7 explains how dryness differs from depression, and people with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor first. No reviewer from within the Christian tradition has checked it yet, and there is no review of contemplative prayer as such; the evidence in the lessons is only indirect.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
