Fasting in the traditions (described)

Ramadan, Yom Kippur, Lent and the Buddhist eight precepts: read the texts and see what each tradition says about timing, purpose and who is exempt. Described, not taught; each week's practice has nothing to do with food (giving, putting a wrong right, a good deed in secret, an evening without entertainment).

What science finds

  • Review of 22 papers: Ramadan’s effect on mental health is generally small, but people with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder have a higher risk of relapse. (Heun 2018, Global Psychiatry 1(1) · source)
  • 11 studies, 1,436 people, on fasting interventions in general: in randomised trials at low risk of bias, fasting groups had lower anxiety and depression scores than controls. The authors call the results preliminary; no study included people with psychiatric conditions. (Berthelot et al. 2021, Nutrients 13(11) · source)
  • 53 studies, 2,323 people: guided forgiveness programmes clearly increase forgiveness compared with no treatment, and reduce depression and anxiety; longer programmes work better. (Wade et al. 2014, Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology 82(1) · source)
  • 27 experiments, 4,045 people: acts of kindness raise well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 76 · source)
  • 99 randomised trials, 6,582 adults (most with overweight or illness): intermittent fasting gives weight loss similar to daily calorie restriction; time-restricted eating (such as 16:8) is no better; no difference in HbA1c. (Semnani-Azad et al. 2025, BMJ 389:e082007 · source)

Limits, honestly

The course only describes; it does not teach fasting or invite anyone to fast. Anyone who fasts as part of their faith should ask their religious leaders about the rules and a doctor about their health, especially with a chronic illness, diabetes treated with medication, pregnancy or breastfeeding, or a history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder. Anyone with a current or past eating disorder should talk to a doctor before reading. The descriptions of each tradition's practice are the writer's judgement and have not yet been reviewed by people within the traditions. There are no reviews on Yom Kippur, Lent or the eight precepts; studies of Ramadan can hardly have a true control group.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
