Dhikr and the rhythm of the day (described)

Dhikr (remembrance of Allah) and the five daily prayers described through Qur'an 13:28, 2:152, 33:41–42, 11:114, 29:45, 2:186, 3:190–191, 62:9–10, 2:153, 94:5–6, 7:205 and three hadith (summarised). No ritual instruction. The exercises are neutral rhythms set by tamhonkhoe: slow reading in the morning, 10–20 minutes of sitting still, three pauses tied to your own day (not to prayer times), one line written in the evening.

Lessons with a link are published. Lessons without a link are still a planned outline; their titles and passages may change during writing and review.

What science finds

  • 97 studies, 7,181 people: therapy that integrates the client’s faith reduces distress as well as standard therapy and adds spiritual well-being. There is no review of lectio divina itself. (Captari et al. 2018, Journal of Clinical Psychology 74(11) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of randomised trials: religious/spiritual interventions reduce anxiety; programmes vary widely and study quality is limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015, Psychological Medicine 45(14) · source)
  • 27 studies, 3,675 people: gratitude exercises reduce depression and anxiety symptoms by a small amount; less so against active control groups. (Cregg & Cheavens 2021, Journal of Happiness Studies 22(1) · source)
  • 38,124 workers: people who detach from work when off duty report less exhaustion and better sleep. This is a correlation. (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017, Frontiers in Psychology 7 · source)
  • 47 randomised trials, 3,515 people: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; not better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine 174(3) · source)
  • Review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020, Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica 142(5) · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 94 independent tests, more than 8,000 people: "if… then…" plans helped people reach goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65); goals and measures varied widely. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38 · source)
  • Meta-analysis of 148 studies, 308,849 people: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation. (Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton 2010, PLoS Medicine 7(7) · source)
  • 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6) · source)
  • 33 studies of self-control training: a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017, Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(6) · source)

Limits, honestly

A Level 2 course, 20–30 minutes a day. It describes dhikr and the five daily prayers (Salah) as Muslims understand and practise them; learners do not need to be Muslim. It does not teach ritual: it does not teach how to pray, perform ablution, recite or read Arabic, and does not ask anyone to change their beliefs. The course's exercises are neutral rhythms set by tamhonkhoe (three pauses tied to your own day, sitting still, writing): they do not follow prayer times, involve no bowing, recite no Arabic words of scripture, are not dhikr or Salah and do not replace them. Muslims keep dhikr and Salah as their own community and teachers teach them. The Vietnamese pages quote Hasan Abdulkarim’s translation on QuranEnc.com word for word, footnotes included; the English pages quote Pickthall. Hadith are only summarised, because the translations on sunnah.com are in copyright. The course notes some differences between Sunni and Shi'a practice without comparing them, and does not go into disputed interpretations or teaching about the afterlife. Long sitting can be uncomfortable; every lesson has a Stop practising section. No reviewer from within the Islamic tradition has checked it yet, and no study has evaluated this programme directly; the evidence in the lessons is only indirect.

Stop practising if

Stop or shorten practice and talk to a professional if you notice: rising anxiety or panic; low mood that lingers; feeling that you or the world is ‘not real’; flooding memories of trauma; several nights of poor sleep; hearing or seeing things others do not; thoughts of harming yourself. People with a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD should ask a doctor before practising more than 20 minutes a day.
