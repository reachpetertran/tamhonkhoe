Convergence 0 – Week 1: Pause

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read three short passages slowly, practise for 10 minutes a day, and try pausing for one breath before you react in daily life.

1. Traditional teaching

The three traditions below differ deeply in what they believe. This week only places them side by side on one small act: stopping for a moment instead of being carried away.

Buddhism: knowing how the breath is

[Traditional teaching] This tradition teaches that practice begins by sitting down, setting the body straight and bringing awareness to the present. The practitioner does not force the breath to be long or short, but simply knows clearly how the breath is, and lets the whole body settle.

Christianity: being still before God

[Traditional teaching] In this tradition, stillness is not only a way to calm body and mind. It is tied to faith in God: the believer stops trying to manage everything alone, in order to remember that God is present.

A note on context: the verse just before (verse 9) speaks of God making wars cease and breaking weapons. [Inference] In its original setting, "be still" can therefore be read as words addressed to nations at war, not only as an invitation to personal quiet. Many readers today use the verse for silent prayer. The Christian reviewer will say which reading is common in their tradition.

Stoicism: wait, let me see what you are

[Traditional teaching] This tradition teaches that what troubles us is often not the event itself but the impression we have of it, and our haste to agree with that impression. The practitioner learns to delay for a moment, examine the impression, and only then decide.

A note on context: in this chapter Epictetus is speaking mainly about impressions that stir up desire. [Inference] The "wait a little" method is used more broadly here, for anger or worry too. Right after this passage he also tells the practitioner to remember God and call on him for help; ancient Stoicism was not a purely secular way of life.

Where the traditions differ

[Expert judgement] On the surface, all three passages are about pausing. Underneath, they differ on core points:

Buddhism (MN 10) Christianity (Psalm 46) Stoicism (Epictetus)
Why pause To see body and mind as they are To know God and trust him To test whether an impression is true before assenting
Directed towards One's own experience A God beyond oneself One's own reason and choice
Wider aim in the tradition Liberation from suffering (Nibbāna) Relationship with God Living in accordance with reason and virtue

This week borrows only one shared action: a single pause. You do not need to accept the beliefs of any tradition to do it, and the week should not be read as saying the three traditions are "really the same".

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A 2014 systematic review pooled 47 randomised trials with 3,515 participants. Mindfulness meditation programmes (usually around 8 weeks long) reduced anxiety, depression and pain by small to moderate amounts. For anxiety, for example, the effect size was about 0.38 at 8 weeks and about 0.22 at 3–6 months. The authors rated the strength of evidence as "moderate". (Goyal et al. 2014)
  • [Verified] The same review found no evidence that meditation was better than other active treatments such as medication, exercise or behavioural therapies. For positive mood, attention and sleep, the evidence was low or insufficient.
  • [Verified] A 2018 critical review by many mindfulness researchers warned that the hype runs ahead of the evidence: "mindfulness" has no agreed definition, many studies are methodologically weak, and possible adverse effects have not been monitored enough. (Van Dam et al. 2018)
  • [Inference] This week's practice is much shorter than the 8-week programmes in those studies. So we cannot say that 10 minutes a day will produce the same effects.

Limits: these figures are averages across many different studies and do not predict what will happen for any one person. Meditation does not replace treatment if you have a mental health problem.

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day in total, plus a few short pauses during the day.

  1. Read slowly (3 minutes). Choose one of the three passages above. Read it slowly twice, the second time quietly aloud. Stay with the sentence that stands out most clearly.
  2. Sit still (5 minutes). Sit on a chair or on the floor, back upright but not stiff. Let the breath be natural. With each breath, simply know "breathing in", "breathing out". When the mind wanders, notice it, and gently come back to the breath. There is no need to breathe deeply or slowly on purpose.
  3. Write one line (2 minutes). Write one sentence: which passage you read today, and what you noticed while sitting.
  4. Pause once during the day. Choose in advance a situation you meet often, such as before replying to a message that annoys you, or before speaking to your child. When it comes, stop long enough for one breath and ask yourself: "What am I seeing? What am I about to do?" Then respond.

At the end of the week, read back your seven lines. There is no need to score yourself.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game "The breathing statue" (5–10 minutes, with an adult playing too): everyone moves around, freezes like a statue on "Stop!", counts 3 breaths and says one thing they notice. Full instructions, age adjustments and safety notes are on the page Kids’ corner – Activity 1.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
  • If so, stop and see Get help now.
  • If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before increasing sitting time beyond 20 minutes.

6. Foundation skills on daynentang

If you need a very short way to pause when emotions run high, daynentang has two 1–3 minute lessons, currently in Vietnamese only: Gọi tên cảm xúc, thở chậm (naming feelings, slow breathing) and Neo lại khi cảm xúc dâng cao (anchoring when emotions rise). Your browser's translate option can help.

7. Practice for today

Choose something you do every day, such as unlocking your phone or opening the front door. Today, before you do it, pause for one breath.
