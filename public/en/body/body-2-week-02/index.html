Body 2 – Week 2: Slow stretching

Published after review by the project owner. No reviewer from within a tradition has checked this page yet.

Not yet trial-taught Not yet expert-reviewed

This week: read two passages about the body. Each day, stretch slowly for 10 minutes with six simple movements, paying attention to the breath and to a gentle feeling of stretch.

Measurable goal: stretch slowly for 10 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

A reminder: stretching here is not about being more flexible than anyone else or reaching a particular pose. Stretch only to a gentle feeling of tension, never into pain.

1. Traditional teaching

Buddhism: awareness when bending and extending the limbs

[Traditional teaching] The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation teaches knowing what you are doing in every everyday movement, including bending and extending the limbs.

A note on context: this passage was also read in Buddhism 2, Week 2 and Body 1, Week 2. This week, attend to one phrase only: "when bending and extending the limbs". The discourse does not teach stretching exercises; it teaches knowing clearly while you move.

Christianity: the body is a temple

[Traditional teaching] In his letter to the believers in Corinth, Saint Paul teaches that the believer's body is not something of little worth.

A note on context: the verses just before (6:12–18) are about sexual conduct, not exercise. The main point is that the body belongs to God, so the believer uses the body to glorify Him. The 1925 Vietnamese Bible does not have the phrase "and in your spirit, which are God’s" in verse 20. This reflects a difference between the manuscripts each translation follows; the Christian reviewer will say more.

Where the traditions differ

  • The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation treats the body as something to observe, so as to see clearly and cling less. The letter to the Corinthians treats the body as a temple of the Holy Spirit that belongs to God.
  • These are different foundations. This lesson borrows only the shared idea of treating the body with attention and respect; it does not teach the doctrine of either.

2. What science finds

  • [Verified] A 2023 overview of 97 systematic reviews (1,039 trials, 128,119 adults): programmes to increase physical activity reduced symptoms of depression and anxiety by a medium amount compared with usual care (median effect size about −0.43 for depression and −0.42 for anxiety). Higher-intensity activity went with larger improvements. Most of the reviews were rated "critically low" in quality. (Singh et al. 2023)
  • To be honest: we have not found a meta-analysis that looks specifically at slow stretching and mood. The result above is about physical activity in general; gentle stretching is low-intensity, so any benefit for mood may be smaller. [Inference]
  • Note: stretching in this course is for attending to the body, not for treating back pain or joint disease. If you are in pain, ask a doctor or physiotherapist.

3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day. Hold each movement for about 20–30 seconds, breathe evenly, do not bounce, do not hold your breath. Doing fewer movements is fine.

  1. Stand or sit still (1 minute). Feel where your feet touch the floor and where your body touches the chair.
  2. Neck. Tilt your head slowly to the right, back to the middle, to the left. Do not roll the neck in circles.
  3. Shoulders. Lift both shoulders, move them back, lower them. Five slow circles.
  4. Reach. Interlace your fingers, raise your arms, breathe out and lean gently to one side, then the other.
  5. Back, on a chair. Sit on the edge of a chair, feet flat on the floor, and lean forward until you feel a gentle stretch in your back or the backs of your thighs.
  6. Calves. Hands on a wall, step one foot back, keeping the heel on the floor. Change legs.
  7. Sit still (1 minute). Notice which parts of the body feel different from the start.

While stretching, silently say "bending" as you bend and "extending" as you extend, like the phrase in this week's passage.

4. Kids' corner 6+

Game: "Zoo stretch" (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

  1. The adult names an animal and everyone copies it very slowly: a cat stretching (hands on the floor, back arched up), a giraffe (on tiptoe, arms reaching high), a young tree growing (crouch small, then slowly stand and reach up).
  2. Hold each pose for a slow count of five, then relax.
  3. At the end, the child says which animal they liked best and which part of their body felt "longer".

Do not push the child to bend too far, and do not compare who is more flexible. Play on a mat, away from table corners.

5. Stop practising if

  • You feel sharp pain, numbness or tingling spreading down an arm or leg, or dizziness when you bend forward or tilt your head back. Stop that movement and ask a doctor.
  • You are pregnant, have had recent surgery, or have osteoporosis, a slipped disc or joint disease. Ask a doctor or physiotherapist before doing forward bends.
  • You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting feeling that you or the world are "not real", cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop practising and see Get help now.
