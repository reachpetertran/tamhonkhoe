Body

Body 1 – Week 1: Grace before meals

Deuteronomy 8:10, Matthew 14:19 and Qur'an 2:172 speak of giving thanks when eating. Each day, pause for one minute before one meal and say or think a thank-you in your own words.

Body 1 – Week 2: One slow meal

The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation (MN 10) teaches awareness when eating, drinking, chewing and tasting; chapter 12 of the Tao Te Ching says the five flavours take away the mouth's taste. Each day, eat one meal slowly over about 15 minutes.

Body 1 – Week 3: A screen-free meal

Pirkei Avot, the Acts of the Apostles and Ecclesiastes speak of shared meals with good words and gladness. This week, have at least three meals without screens, with other people if you can.

Body 1 – Week 4: Thanking growers and cooks

Qur'an 80:24–32 invites people to consider their food; the Talmud tells how Ben Zoma remembered all the effort behind a loaf of bread. This week, say one specific thank-you to someone who made your food, then review the course.

Body 2 – Week 1: Walking meditation

The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation (MN 10) teaches knowing that you are walking, standing, sitting or lying down; chapter 64 of the Tao Te Ching says the journey of a thousand li begins with a single step. Each day, walk very slowly for 10 minutes.

Body 2 – Week 2: Slow stretching

The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation (MN 10) teaches awareness when bending and extending the limbs; 1 Corinthians 6:19–20 calls the body a temple. Each day, stretch slowly for 10 minutes, never into pain.

Body 2 – Week 3: Flowing like water

Chapter 78 of the Tao Te Ching says nothing is softer and weaker than water; Isaiah 40:29–31 speaks of those who run and are not weary; Seneca recommends short, simple exercises and a quick return to the mind. Each day, join stretching and walking into one 15-minute session.

Body 2 – Week 4: Review and a rhythm of your own

Epictetus (Encheiridion XLI) and 1 Timothy 4:8 put bodily exercise in second place; a hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari reminds us that the body has its rights. Review the three weeks, rate yourself and choose a rhythm of movement for next month.

Body 3 – Week 1: Who should not do this

The Bhagavad Gita says the path is not for one who fasts too much or feasts too much; the Dhammapada calls health the ultimate blessing. This week you change nothing in how you eat: check whether you should do this course at all, and note your current eating rhythm.

Body 3 – Week 2: Plant-based eating as a discipline

Genesis tells that the first food was plants; Daniel asks for pulse for ten days; Seneca tells of a year without meat. This week, choose a manageable level of plant-based eating and keep it for 7 days.

Body 3 – Week 3: The 16:8 eating window

Epictetus teaches training in endurance for yourself, without showing anyone; Ecclesiastes says everything has its time. This week, narrow your eating window step by step, from 12 hours down to 8 if it feels fine, without cutting portions.

Body 3 – Week 4: Listening to the body and stopping

Chapter 44 of the Tao Te Ching says that one who knows when to stop is free from danger; hadith Bukhari 1975 tells how the Prophet advised a man not to fast every day, because the body has its rights. This week, listen to your body for two minutes each day, then choose to keep, reduce or stop.

Body 4 – Week 1: Ramadan

Qur'an 2:183–185 speaks of fasting in the month of Ramadan and of those who fast other days or feed a person in need instead. This lesson describes; it does not teach fasting. This week's practice is slow reading and one act of giving.

Body 4 – Week 2: Yom Kippur

Leviticus 16:29–31 sets the Day of Atonement, a day to "afflict your souls" and do no work; Isaiah 58:6–7 asks what fast is chosen. This lesson describes; it does not teach fasting. This week's practice is putting right one small wrong you did to someone.

Body 4 – Week 3: Lent

Matthew 4:1–2 tells of Jesus fasting forty days in the wilderness; Matthew 6:16–18 teaches fasting in secret. This lesson describes Lent; it does not teach fasting. This week's practice is a good deed done in secret.

Body 4 – Week 4: The eight precepts and review

The Numbered Discourses AN 8.41 describes the lay sabbath with eight factors, one of which is not eating at the wrong time. This lesson describes; it does not teach fasting. Practice: one evening without entertainment, then a review of the four traditions.
