**This week:** read three short passages slowly, sit in silence each day (starting at 5 minutes and building up to 15), and do one household task in silence.

**Measurable goal:** sit in silence for 15 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days (the first two days can be shorter).

## 1. Traditional teaching

Week 1 practised pausing for one breath. Week 11 practises staying longer in silence. The three traditions below all value inner silence, but each finds something very different in it.

### Christianity: God amid the clatter of the kitchen

[Traditional teaching] Brother Lawrence (Lawrence of the Resurrection, 17th century) was a Carmelite lay brother in Paris who worked for many years in the monastery kitchen. This tradition teaches that believers can live in the presence of God at all times, not only at times of prayer.

::: kinh lawrence-practice nguyen-van | end of the conversations, before the Letters
He was never hasty nor loitering, but did each thing in its season, with an even, uninterrupted composure and tranquility of spirit. "The time of business," said he, "does not with me differ from the time of prayer; and in the noise and clatter of my kitchen, while several persons are at the same time calling for different things, I possess GOD in as great tranquility as if I were upon my knees at the blessed sacrament."
:::

A note on context: the book records Brother Lawrence's conversations and letters, written down by others. [Inference] Here silence is not the absence of noise, but a mind turned towards God in the middle of work. The "blessed sacrament" is the consecrated bread in which Catholics believe Christ is present.

### Judaism: a still small voice

[Traditional teaching] The Book of Kings tells how the prophet Elijah fled to Mount Horeb, where God appeared to him. This tradition teaches that God is recognised not only in great power, but also in stillness.

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | 1 Kings 19:11–12
And He said: ‘Go forth, and stand upon the mount before the LORD.’ And, behold, the LORD passed by, and a great and strong wind rent the mountains, and broke in pieces the rocks before the LORD; but the LORD was not in the wind; and after the wind an earthquake; but the LORD was not in the earthquake;

and after the earthquake a fire; but the LORD was not in the fire; and after the fire a still small voice.
:::

A note on context: the Hebrew phrase at the end of verse 12 (qol demamah daqqah) is translated in many ways: "a still small voice" (JPS 1917), "a sound of thin silence". Right afterwards God questions Elijah and gives him a new task. [Inference] In the story, silence is where God's word is heard, not an end in itself. The Jewish reviewer will say how it is commonly read.

### Non-dual traditions (Upanishads): when senses and mind are still

[Traditional teaching] The *Katha Upanishad* tells of a dialogue between the boy Nachiketas and Yama, lord of death, about the Self (Atman). Its last part teaches how to still the senses and the mind in order to realise the Self.

::: kinh upan-paramananda nguyen-van | Katha Upanishad, Part Sixth, verses X–XI
When the five organs of perception become still, together with the mind, and the intellect ceases to be active: that is called the highest state.

This firm holding back of the senses is what is known as Yoga. Then one should become watchful, for Yoga comes and goes.
:::

A note on context: Swami Paramananda divides the Katha Upanishad into six parts; these verses are often numbered II.3.10–11. [Inference] "Yoga" here does not mean physical postures, but keeping the mind still. "Yoga comes and goes" is a reminder that stillness does not hold itself in place forever. A reviewer from this tradition will say how it is commonly read.

### Where the traditions differ

[Expert judgement] All three value inner silence. But they differ on these points:

| | Christianity (Brother Lawrence) | Judaism (1 Kings 19:11–12) | Non-dual (Katha Upanishad) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Silence for what | Living in God's presence at all times | Hearing the word of God | Realising the Self (Atman) |
| Turned towards whom or what | A personal God | A personal God who speaks to people | The Self, what is real deep in each person |
| Where silence is found | In the middle of noisy work | After the wind, the earthquake and the fire | When the senses and mind settle |

[Expert judgement] Buddhism (not quoted this week) also teaches sitting still, but does not turn towards a God or a Self. This week does not compare those differences in depth.

This week's exercise is simply sitting in silence; it is not a prayer or a meditation method of any tradition. If you have a faith, you can use the time in the way of your own tradition. You do not need to accept the beliefs of any tradition, and the week should not be read as saying the three traditions are "really the same".

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] The review of 47 randomised trials (3,515 people) cited in Week 1: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety, depression and pain by a small-to-moderate amount, but are not better than other active treatments such as exercise or behavioural therapy. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 23 randomised trials of religious or spiritual interventions in mental health care (excluding prayer for others at a distance): these interventions reduced anxiety symptoms; for depression the results were weaker. The programmes varied widely and study quality was limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
- [Verified] A review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators had an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety and depression; this can happen in people with no previous mental health problems. (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Inference] There is no review of "sitting in silence" without any particular method. Sitting longer does not mean better; that is why this week builds up gradually.

Limits: the figures are averages across many different studies and do not predict what will happen for any one person. This exercise does not replace treatment if you have a mental health problem.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 8 minutes a day at first, building up to about 18 minutes.

1. **Read slowly (2 minutes).** Choose one of the three passages. Read it slowly twice, the second time quietly aloud.
2. **Sit in silence (5 minutes on days 1–2; 10 minutes on days 3–4; 15 minutes from day 5).** Sit on a chair, back straight but not stiff, phone notifications off. Do nothing: no reading, no listening, no trying to think or trying not to think. When you notice you are thinking, just know "thinking" and come back to the breath or the sounds around you.
3. **Note it (1 minute).** One line: "Today I sat for ___ minutes. What I noticed was ___."
4. **One household task in silence (during the week).** Like Brother Lawrence, choose an everyday task (washing up, sweeping, hanging out laundry) and do it in silence, with no phone and nothing to listen to.

Note: [Expert judgement] if 15 minutes is too long, stay at 10; the aim is to sit regularly, not to sit long. Sitting in silence can bring up old thoughts or feelings; if they are too strong, open your eyes, get up and walk about, and shorten the time.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

Game "Listening to the bell fade" (5–10 minutes, with an adult playing too): the adult strikes a bell gently, the child listens as the sound fades and raises a hand when it has gone; each silence lasts only a few dozen seconds. Full instructions, age adjustments and safety notes are on the page [Kids’ corner – Activity 11](/en/kids-corner/activity-11/).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Anxiety or low mood rises after sitting, or you hear or see things other people do not.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for long; this week keep each sitting under 10 minutes until you have asked.

## 7. Practice for today

Before you sleep, turn off the light, sit in silence for one minute, and just listen to the sounds around you.
