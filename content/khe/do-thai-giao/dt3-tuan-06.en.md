**Before you start:** this Level 3 course deals with suffering and loss. If you are newly bereaved, seriously ill, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, please save the course for another time and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/) straight away.

**This week:** slow reading of the ending of the Book of Job. Sit for 30–40 minutes each day. Rate the course's six habits, keep two, and write a four-week plan. Once this week, visit, share a meal with, or message someone going through a hard time.

**Measurable goal:** a full session of reading and sitting on at least 5 of 7 days; rate the six habits, choose two, write a four-week plan; one visit or check-in this week.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The ending returns to prose, like the opening. God speaks to the first friend:

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Job 42:7
And it was so, that after the LORD had spoken these words unto Job, the LORD said to Eliphaz the Temanite: ‘My wrath is kindled against thee, and against thy two friends; for ye have not spoken of Me the thing that is right, as My servant Job hath.
:::

In brief, verses 8–9: God tells the three friends to bring an offering, and Job will pray for them. They do so.

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Job 42:10
And the LORD changed the fortune of Job, when he prayed for his friends; and the LORD gave Job twice as much as he had before.
:::

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Job 42:11
Then came there unto him all his brethren, and all his sisters, and all they that had been of his acquaintance before, and did eat bread with him in his house; and they bemoaned him, and comforted him concerning all the evil that the LORD had brought upon him; every man also gave him a piece of money, and every one a ring of gold.
:::

### Reading in context

- God's words in verse 7 continue into verse 8, so JPS opens the quotation in verse 7 and closes it at the end of verse 8.
- [Inference] Verse 7 does not say that Job was right in every word. It says only that the friends, who defended God with the explanation "you reap what you sow", did not speak rightly of Him, while Job, who lamented and questioned, did. This is one of the things that makes the Book of Job unusual.
- [Inference] Job's fortune is restored "when he prayed for his friends": the very people who had hurt him with their explanations.
- [Expert judgement] The ending (verses 12–17) tells how Job became wealthy again, had seven more sons and three more daughters, and lived long. Many readers note that the children who died do not come back; new children do not replace the ones who were lost. The course does not read the ending as a promise that everyone who suffers will be repaid, nor as saying that anyone who is not repaid must be at fault.
- [Inference] Verse 11 tells how Job's family came to eat bread with him and comfort him after it was all over. Comfort that comes late is still comfort.
- "Brethren" is old English for "brothers"; "bemoaned him" means "grieved with him".

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates this course directly.
- [Verified] A review of 47 randomised trials (3,515 people): mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; they are no better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Verified] A critical review: "mindfulness" has no agreed definition, many studies are methodologically weak, and adverse effects are not monitored enough. (Van Dam et al. 2018)
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 148 studies: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation. (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010)

Limits: the evidence is indirect. Judge the course by your own notebook, not by expectations.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 40 minutes a day.

1. **Settle (1 minute).**
2. **Read slowly (5 minutes).** Days 1–3: read this week's ending. Days 4–7: reread one passage from an earlier week each day.
3. **Sit for 25–30 minutes.** In whichever way suited you best in the course: noticing the breath, the body, near and far sounds, or sitting quietly "present, without speaking" as in Week 1.
4. **Come back to the ground (2 minutes).**
5. **Review (5 minutes, days 1–2).** Rate each habit from 0 to 3 (0 = hardly did it, 3 = did it regularly and found it helpful):
    - Slow reading, writing a question without rushing to answer (Week 1).
    - Writing a lament about a manageable difficulty (Week 2).
    - Listening for 3 minutes without advice or explanation (Week 3).
    - Walking and noticing something larger than yourself (Week 4).
    - Reading with someone (Week 5).
    - Visiting or checking in on someone going through a hard time (Week 6).
6. **Choose two and write a four-week plan (day 3).** For example: "Listen for 3 minutes every day; walk for 15 minutes every Saturday."
7. **One visit or check-in this week.** Visit, share a meal with, or message someone going through a hard time. Bring what you have practised: sit with them, listen to the end, do not rush to explain.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"A small gift"**: with the child, make a simple gift (draw a card, choose a toy in good condition) for a friend or relative who is sad about something small. The child decides whether to give it; do not push. See the full activity at [Kids' corner – Activity 12](/en/kids-corner/activity-12/).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
- The ending leaves you feeling that your suffering "ought to be repaid", or that you are at fault because it has not been. Stop and talk with someone you trust; the course does not teach that.
- Visiting someone who is suffering exhausts you, or brings back a loss of your own. You can simply send a short message; caring for others does not mean neglecting yourself.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
