**This week:** slow reading of the Shema, six short verses from Deuteronomy that Jews recite every morning and every evening.

**Measurable goal:** read the six verses slowly and write one line, on at least 5 of 7 days.

**How to use this course:** Judaism 1 reads six short passages from the Torah, the Talmud, Pirkei Avot ("Ethics of the Fathers") and the book of Micah, and describes what the Jewish tradition teaches about listening, loving one's neighbour, the day of rest, acting in time, persistence and living uprightly. You do not need to be Jewish. The course does not teach religious law (halakha) or ritual, does not teach Hebrew, and does not ask anyone to change their beliefs. Jews taking the course should treat it as extra reading, not a substitute for learning with a rabbi or their own community.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### The Torah and the translation used here

[Traditional teaching] In the Jewish tradition, "Torah" in the narrow sense means the first five books of the Hebrew Bible (Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy); in the wide sense it means the whole of the teaching, including the oral tradition later written down in the Mishnah and Talmud. The original text is Hebrew.

The English pages of this course quote the JPS 1917 translation by the Jewish Publication Society (public domain). JPS prints "the LORD" in capitals where the Hebrew has the personal name of God; the Jewish tradition does not pronounce this name aloud.

### The Shema: "Hear"

[Traditional teaching] Shema is the first word of verse 4 and means "Hear". The Jewish tradition teaches that the Shema is recited every morning and every evening, understanding verse 7 ("when thou liest down, and when thou risest up") in this way. Verse 4 declares faith in one God, and many Jews learn it by heart as children.

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Deuteronomy 6:4–9
HEAR, O ISRAEL: THE LORD OUR GOD, THE LORD IS ONE.

And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.

And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be upon thy heart;

and thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thy house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.

And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thy hand, and they shall be for frontlets between thine eyes.

And thou shalt write them upon the door-posts of thy house, and upon thy gates.
:::

### Reading in context

- The speaker in Deuteronomy is Moses, addressing the people of Israel before they enter the promised land. So "I" in verse 6 is Moses, not God.
- JPS prints verse 4 in capitals to set it apart. The Hebrew is short and can be translated in several ways; "THE LORD IS ONE" is one of them. "Thou", "thy" and "thee" are old English forms of "you" and "your".
- [Traditional teaching] The Jewish tradition understands verse 8 as tefillin, two small leather boxes holding handwritten Torah passages, worn on the arm and the forehead during weekday morning prayer. Verse 9 is the mezuzah, a parchment with the Shema in a small case fixed to the doorpost. These are sacred objects; the course only describes them and does not suggest making or using anything like them.
- In the prayer service the Shema is longer than these six verses. The course reads only the opening.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates slow reading of the Shema itself.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 97 studies (7,181 people) on psychotherapy that integrates the client's religious faith found that this integration reduced distress as much as standard therapy, and increased spiritual well-being. (Captari et al. 2018)
- [Inference] That is therapy led by professionals, for people with mental health problems and usually for believers. Reading on your own for 10 minutes a day has not been tested; any benefit is only inferred.

Limits: the evidence above is indirect. Reading scripture is learning about a tradition, not treatment.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10–12 minutes a day, the same passage all week.

1. **Settle (1 minute).** Sit still and take a few slow breaths.
2. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read the six verses once. Read them a second time very slowly, pausing for a moment after each verse. On the first day also read "Reading in context".
3. **Choose one word (2 minutes).** Notice a word or phrase that makes you stop, for example "Hear", "upon thy heart", "teach them diligently unto thy children", "when thou walkest by the way".
4. **Write one line (3 minutes).** Write: "Today the word … reminds me of …". It need not be right or beautiful, only honest.
5. **Sit quietly (1–2 minutes).** No need to think further.
6. **Mark** your calendar. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days.

Optional: in the evening before sleep, reread the line you wrote in the morning. Jews can recite the Shema in the way they are used to. Those who are not Jewish read it as one reads a classic text: to understand, not to pray on someone else's behalf.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

The Shema begins with "Hear". This week uses the activity **"Listening to the bell fade"**: an adult strikes a bell or a glass, and everyone listens until they can no longer hear the sound, then raises a hand. See the full activity at [Kids' corner – Activity 11](/en/kids-corner/activity-11/).

Jewish families can read verse 4 to their child. Other families just play the listening game. Do not make toys that look like tefillin or a mezuzah, as these are sacred objects in the tradition.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Reading the scripture of another tradition leaves you feeling guilty or tense for a long time. If so, stop and talk with a trusted friend, a teacher in your community, or a mental health professional.
- Taking this course causes tension in your family or with people around you about belief. You can pause; learning here is voluntary.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
