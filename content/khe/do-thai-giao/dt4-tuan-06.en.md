**This week:** slow reading of the psalm for the sabbath day. Each morning one thank-you, each evening remembering one good thing from the day. The family rates the course's six habits, keeps two, and writes a 4-week plan.

**Measurable goal:** read slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; a morning thank-you and an evening good thing on at least 5 of 7 days; the family rates six habits, chooses two and writes a 4-week plan.

## 1. Traditional teaching

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Psalm 92:1–5
A Psalm, a Song. For the sabbath day.

It is a good thing to give thanks unto the LORD, And to sing praises unto Thy name, O Most High;

To declare Thy lovingkindness in the morning, And Thy faithfulness in the night seasons,

With an instrument of ten strings, and with the psaltery; With a solemn sound upon the harp.

For Thou, LORD, hast made me glad through Thy work; I will exult in the works of Thy hands.
:::

A note on context: [Expert judgement] this is the only psalm whose title mentions the sabbath. JPS counts the title line as verse 1, so the reading is 92:1–5; many Bibles print the title separately, so the same passage appears as 92:1–4. [Expert judgement] In the Jewish tradition Psalm 92 is recited in the Shabbat prayers. [Inference] Verse 3 puts two things at the two ends of the day: lovingkindness in the morning, faithfulness at night. Verse 5 links back to Week 1: the joy of the day of rest comes from looking at "works" that are finished.

### Looking back over the course

The six weeks have moved from the day of rest (Week 1), to making sure everyone at home can rest (Week 2), opening your hand to the needy (Week 3), setting something aside for others (Week 4), and opening the table (Week 5). [Inference] In the Torah passages of this course, the day of rest and giving to the poor often go together: the stranger, the hired worker and the poor appear both in the sabbath laws and in the harvest laws.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally et al. 2010)
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests (more than 8,000 people): "if… then…" plans helped people reach goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65); goals and measures varied widely. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
- [Inference] Because habits take many weeks to settle, the review keeps only two habits, not all six.

## 3. Practice for adults

Five minutes of reading each morning, two minutes of looking back each evening; a 20-minute family meeting at the end of the week.

1. **Read slowly (5 minutes, morning).** Read Psalm 92:1–5 twice, then say or write a thank-you for one specific thing.
2. **Look back (2 minutes, evening).** One line: who today kept their word, was kind, or was trustworthy towards me?
3. **Family meeting at the end of the week (20 minutes).** Each person rates the six habits from 0 to 3 (0 = not done, 3 = has become a habit):
   - A shared rest period, prepared in advance (Week 1)
   - Sharing housework so everyone can rest; no work messages out of hours (Week 2)
   - The sharing box (Week 3)
   - Setting something aside for others, giving with dignity (Week 4)
   - Shared meals without screens, one sentence each (Week 5)
   - Morning thanks, evening good things (Week 6)
4. **Keep two habits.** Together choose the two habits that are easiest to keep or most enjoyed, not necessarily the two with the highest scores.
5. **A 4-week plan.** For each habit, write one "if… then…" sentence (for example: "If it is Saturday afternoon, then we put our phones away and go for a walk"). Put it where everyone can see it. After 4 weeks, meet again for 10 minutes.

**Very important.** Do not use the scores to compare or blame each other. Missing a week is not failure; start again the next week.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"Who helped me today?"**: in the evening, the child tells or draws someone who helped them during the day, then the family thanks that person. It fits verse 3 of the psalm: remembering good things in the evening. See the full activity in [Kids' corner – Activity 2](/en/kids-corner/activity-02/).

At the family meeting, the child chooses the habit they liked best in the course and draws it on the plan.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Rating yourself makes you feel guilty or blame yourself. If so, skip the scoring and simply choose one habit to keep.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
