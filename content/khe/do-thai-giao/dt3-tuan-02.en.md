**Before you start:** this Level 3 course deals with suffering and loss. If you are newly bereaved, seriously ill, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, please save the course for another time and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/) straight away.

**This week in particular:** the lament-writing exercise uses a **manageable** difficulty, not the worst trauma of your life. If the only thing you want to write about is a trauma (an accident, violence, abuse, a sudden death), do only the sitting days, and talk about it with a professional.

**This week:** slow reading of three passages in which Job laments. On three days, write a 10-minute "lament" about a manageable difficulty. On the other days, sit for 25 minutes.

**Measurable goal:** write a 10-minute lament on at least 3 days this week; a full session of reading and sitting on at least 5 of 7 days (writing days count as sessions).

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] After seven days of silence, Job opens his mouth. In chapter 3 he curses the day he was born; the course does not quote that chapter. From there to chapter 31, Job and his three friends speak in turn. Job no longer says "Blessed be the name of the LORD" as in chapter 1; he laments, he questions, he demands to argue his case.

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Job 7:11
Therefore I will not refrain my mouth; I will speak in the anguish of my spirit; I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.
:::

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Job 23:3–4
Oh that I knew where I might find Him, That I might come even to His seat!

I would order my cause before Him, And fill my mouth with arguments.
:::

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Job 23:8–9
Behold, I go forward, but He is not there, And backward, but I cannot perceive Him;

On the left hand, when He doth work, but I cannot behold Him, He turneth Himself to the right hand, but I cannot see Him.
:::

### Reading in context

- [Traditional teaching] The Talmud (Bava Batra 16b) sets two verses side by side: one saying that Job "has spoken without knowledge", and God's words in chapter 42 that the friends "have not spoken of Me the thing that is right, as My servant Job hath". From this Rava concludes that a person is not held responsible for what he says in distress.
- [Expert judgement] The Hebrew Bible contains many laments addressed to God, such as many of the Psalms and the Book of Lamentations. In this reading, lament is not abandoning faith; it is continuing to speak to God while in pain. A reviewer from the Jewish tradition will explain how the sages read Job's laments.
- [Inference] In 23:3–4 Job does not ask for his suffering to end; he asks to meet God and be heard. In 23:8–9 he speaks of feeling that God is absent. Both are the words of someone who is still searching.
- "Refrain", "order my cause" and "doth" are old English for "hold back", "set out my case" and "does".
- The lament-writing exercise this week is tamhonkhoe's own, not a ritual of the tradition. You may write to yourself, to a person, or to God, according to your own beliefs.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study of writing a "lament" in the manner of Job.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 146 randomised studies of writing about experiences and feelings found a positive but very small overall effect (r = 0.075). (Frattaroli 2006)
- [Inference] A very small effect means you should not expect the writing to make you feel lighter straight away. This week's writing is practice in naming what is hard honestly, not a cure.

Limits: writing is practice, and does not replace therapy when you need it.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 35 minutes a day.

**Writing days (3 days, for example Monday, Wednesday, Friday):**

1. **Settle (1 minute).**
2. **Read slowly (5 minutes).** Read one of the three passages.
3. **Sit for 10 minutes.** Notice your breath. When the difficult thing comes to mind, notice where it sits in your body.
4. **Write a lament (10 minutes).** Choose a manageable difficulty. Write without stopping or correcting:
    - What happened (2–3 sentences).
    - Where it hurts.
    - What I want to ask, or what I want to be able to say (as Job does in 23:3–4).
5. **Fold it away (3 minutes).** Read it once. Add one last sentence: "Today I have said this out loud." Put the page away; you do not have to send it to anyone.
6. **Come back to the ground (2 minutes).** Feet on the floor, look around, name three things, drink a glass of water.

**Sitting days (the other days):** settle for 1 minute, read slowly for 5 minutes, sit for 25 minutes noticing breath and body, come back to the ground for 2 minutes.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**The game "Say it and feel lighter"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

Job did not keep what hurt him locked inside. With children, this week practises saying a small sad thing honestly and gently: the adult asks "Was there anything today that made you unhappy?", listens, then says it back in one sentence: "You were sad because…". No advice, no fixing straight away. daynentang has a lesson, [Nói thật, lòng nhẹ](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-1/g1-noi-that-long-nhe/) (Speak honestly, feel lighter; in Vietnamese), for families with children aged 2–8.

Talk only about small everyday sadness. If a child tells you about something serious (being hit, bullied, or hurt by an adult), stop the game, thank the child for telling you, and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
- After writing you feel clearly worse, and it lasts into the next day. If so, drop the writing sessions, keep only a short sitting (10–15 minutes), and talk with someone you trust.
- The writing pulls you towards a trauma you did not mean to write about. Stop writing at once, come back to the ground, and find a professional.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

## 7. Practice for today

Say one honest sentence to someone you trust: "Lately I've been finding it hard because…".
