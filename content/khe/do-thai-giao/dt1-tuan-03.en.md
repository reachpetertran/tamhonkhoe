**This week:** slow reading of two Torah passages on the seventh day, learning how Shabbat is kept, and setting aside one intentional rest period of your own this week.

**Measurable goal:** read slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; take one intentional rest period of at least 90 minutes this week and write three lines afterwards.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### How Shabbat is kept (described)

[Traditional teaching] Shabbat begins at sunset on Friday and ends when it is fully dark on Saturday night. In many Jewish homes Shabbat opens with lighting candles, a blessing over a cup of wine (Kiddush) and challah bread, and a shared meal, followed by prayer and Torah reading in the synagogue, and closes with the Havdalah ceremony. The tradition calls the kinds of work set aside on that day melakhah and lists them in many categories; Jewish communities keep these at different levels.

The above is description only. The course does not teach Shabbat observance, and the exercise below is not Shabbat.

### First reason: God rested on the seventh day

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Genesis 2:1–3
And the heaven and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.

And on the seventh day God finished His work which He had made; and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had made.

And God blessed the seventh day, and hallowed it; because that in it He rested from all His work which God in creating had made.
:::

A note on context: this closes the account of the six days of creation in chapter 1. The Ten Commandments in Exodus (20:8–11) give this same reason for the sabbath; that passage is quoted in [Convergence 0 – Week 5: Rest](/en/convergence/week-05/).

### Second reason: remembering slavery

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Deuteronomy 5:12–15
Observe the sabbath day, to keep it holy, as the LORD thy God commanded thee.

Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work;

but the seventh day is a sabbath unto the LORD thy God, in it thou shalt not do any manner of work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, nor thy man-servant, nor thy maid-servant, nor thine ox, nor thine ass, nor any of thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates; that thy man-servant and thy maid-servant may rest as well as thou.

And thou shalt remember that thou was a servant in the land of Egypt, and the LORD thy God brought thee out thence by a mighty hand and by an outstretched arm; therefore the LORD thy God commanded thee to keep the sabbath day.
:::

A note on context: when the Ten Commandments are repeated in Deuteronomy, the first word changes from "Remember" (Exodus) to "Observe", and the reason changes from creation to liberation from slavery. "Thou was" is as printed in JPS 1917. [Inference] Verse 14 makes clear that the day of rest is not only for the head of the household but also for servants, strangers and animals: rest is something the whole household shares, not one person's privilege.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 86 publications (38,124 workers) found that people who detach from work when off duty, neither working nor thinking about work, report less exhaustion and better sleep. This is a correlation; it does not prove that rest is the cause. (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017)
- [Verified] Joint WHO/ILO estimates: in 2016 about 488 million people worked 55 or more hours a week, and about 745,000 deaths from ischemic heart disease and stroke were attributed to such long hours. These are model estimates. (Pega et al. 2021)
- [Inference] These studies concern rest in general; they do not measure keeping Shabbat. For observant Jews, Shabbat is a commandment and a holy day, not a health measure.

## 3. Practice for adults

Ten minutes a day of reading and writing, plus one rest period this week.

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Days 1–3 read Genesis 2:1–3; days 4–7 read Deuteronomy 5:12–15. Read twice.
2. **Write one line (2 minutes).** "This week, the thing I find hardest to stop is …".
3. **Choose your rest period (start of the week, 5 minutes).** Choose a period of at least 90 minutes this week and put it in your calendar like an appointment. Beforehand, write down what you will stop (for example: no work, no work messages, no shopping) and what you will do (eat slowly with family, walk, read, sit quietly).
4. **Keep the rest period.** If you slip into work partway through, that is fine; return to the rest.
5. **Write three lines afterwards:** what I stopped; what I noticed; what I want to keep.
6. **Optional (following verse 14).** Help someone else get a rest: take over a household task, mind a child, do not send a colleague work messages out of hours.

This is a general exercise for everyone and does not imitate Shabbat ritual (no candle lighting or Kiddush as a ceremony). Jews keep Shabbat in the way of their community; people of other traditions can keep their own tradition's day of rest.

**Very important.** Shift workers, people caring for someone ill, and parents of young children may not have 90 minutes in one block. Choose a shorter period (30 minutes) or split it in two. Do not drop care that someone else needs in order to keep the exercise.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Seven boxes, one for rest"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Divide a strip of paper into seven boxes. The child draws in six boxes the things they do every day (school, homework, tidying toys).
2. The seventh box is for a restful thing the whole family does together: a walk, a story, a meal together, lying down listening to the rain.
3. Ask: "What if every day were only work? Everyone at home needs rest, including Mum, Dad and grandparents."
4. Choose one thing from the seventh box to do together this week.

The game does not teach how to keep Shabbat. Jewish families can tell their child about it in their own way.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The rest period makes you strongly anxious or restless about work. If so, shorten it, and if the anxiety lasts, talk with a trusted friend or a professional.
- Resting causes tension with people at home or at work. Talk with them first, or choose another time.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
