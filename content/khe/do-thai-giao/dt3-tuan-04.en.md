**Before you start:** this Level 3 course deals with suffering and loss. If you are newly bereaved, seriously ill, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, please save the course for another time and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/) straight away.

**This week:** slow reading of the opening of God's answer to Job. On three days, walk outdoors for 15 minutes and notice something vast (the sky, an old tree, water, a cloud). On the other days, sit for 30 minutes.

**Measurable goal:** a 15-minute walk at least 3 times this week; a full session of reading and sitting (or walking) on at least 5 of 7 days; each evening, note one thing larger than yourself that you noticed that day.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] After the speeches of Job and his friends, God speaks. The answer runs for four chapters (38–41). God does not explain the scene in heaven, nor say why Job suffers. He asks Job about the foundations of the earth, the sea, light, rain, the stars and wild animals.

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Job 38:1–7
Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind, and said:

Who is this that darkeneth counsel By words without knowledge?

Gird up now thy loins like a man; For I will demand of thee, and declare thou unto Me.

Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? Declare, if thou hast the understanding.

Who determined the measures thereof, if thou knowest? Or who stretched the line upon it?

Whereupon were the foundations thereof fastened? Or who laid the corner-stone thereof,

When the morning stars sang together, And all the sons of God shouted for joy?
:::

### Reading in context

- [Expert judgement] Readers across the ages have understood this answer in many ways: some read it as a rebuke, some as an invitation to Job to see more widely, and some find that what matters most is that God came and spoke to Job. The course does not choose one reading. A reviewer from the Jewish tradition will explain how the sages read it.
- [Inference] In chapter 23, Job longed to find God and argue his case. Here God comes, but as the one who asks the questions. Verse 3 echoes what Job had asked for ("I will demand of thee, and declare thou unto Me").
- "Gird up thy loins" is an old expression: tie up your long robe so you are ready to work or to answer. "Darkeneth counsel" means "obscures the plan".
- [Inference] This week's walking exercise is not meant to "explain" suffering by the beauty of the world. It only borrows the direction of chapter 38: looking up and away from oneself for a while.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study of reading chapter 38 of Job.
- [Verified] A randomised trial with 60 healthy older adults: a 15-minute outdoor walk each week for 8 weeks. The group taught to notice what filled them with awe reported more emotions directed towards others (such as compassion and gratitude) and less distress in daily life than the group taking ordinary walks. (Sturm et al. 2022)
- [Inference] This is one small study in healthy older adults, not in people who are bereaved or suffering a great deal. Any benefit for learners on this course is only inferred.

Limits: one small study, not yet widely replicated.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 30–40 minutes a day.

**Walking days (3 days):**

1. **Read slowly (5 minutes).** Read the passage, pausing at verse 4.
2. **Walk outdoors for 15 minutes.** Walk a little slower than usual. No phone calls. Every few minutes, look up and find something larger or older than you: the sky, a long-lived tree, a river, a hill, a drifting cloud. Stop and look at it for 30 seconds.
3. **Sit for 10 minutes** (at home or on a bench). Recall what you looked at. Notice your body.
4. **Come back to the ground (2 minutes).**

**Sitting days (the other days):**

1. **Settle (1 minute).**
2. **Read slowly (5 minutes).** Each day, pause at one of the questions in the passage.
3. **Sit for 30 minutes.** 10 minutes noticing the breath. 15 minutes noticing the farthest sound you can hear, then the nearest. In the last 5 minutes, repeat silently "when the morning stars sang together", then let the words fall quiet.
4. **Come back to the ground (2 minutes).**

**Evening note (1 minute):** one thing larger than yourself that you noticed today.

If you cannot walk: sit by a window for 15 minutes looking at the sky instead.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**The game "Looking up at the sky"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. With the child, go out to the yard, a balcony or a window, in the afternoon or on a clear evening.
2. Each person looks for three things in the sky: a cloud shaped like something, a bird, a star (or the moon).
3. Ask: "What is the biggest thing you can see? What is the farthest away?" Let the child answer in their own words.
4. Lie or sit still for one minute looking at the sky, without talking.
5. To finish: "There are so many things bigger and more beautiful than we think."

Never look straight at the sun. On a balcony, an adult always stands next to the child.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
- The feeling of "being small" turns into "I am worth nothing" or "my suffering does not matter", and keeps coming back for several days. If so, stop the course and talk with someone you trust.
- Walking alone somewhere unsafe (busy roads, deserted places at night). Choose a safe place and time, or sit by a window.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
