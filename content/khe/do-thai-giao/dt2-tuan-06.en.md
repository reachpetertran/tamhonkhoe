**This week:** reading the second step in Mesillat Yesharim. The trait of the week: zeal, in Hebrew *zerizut*.

**Measurable goal:** do one chosen good deed at the earliest moment you can, on at least 5 of 7 days; write down at least three of your own excuses this week; keep the evening notebook on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### After watchfulness comes zeal

[Traditional teaching] Luzzatto opens chapter 6 by setting the two steps side by side: watchfulness concerns the commandments of "do not", while zeal concerns the commandments of "do", as in the Psalm "turn from evil and do good". *Mitzvot* (singular *mitzvah*) are the commandments of the Torah; in everyday speech the word also means a good deed.

::: kinh mesillat-sebag nguyen-van | chapter 6, segment 2
The matter of "Zeal" is clear. It is the early engaging in mitzvot and their completion as the sages of blessed memory said: "the zealous are early to perform the mitzvot" (Pesachim 4a).
:::

A note on context: the Talmud saying quoted (Pesachim 4a) is a familiar one in the tradition. Straight after, Luzzatto writes that human nature feels heavy and does not want to exert itself, so whoever wants to serve the Creator must strengthen himself against that very nature; he quotes Pirkei Avot: be "mighty as a lion to do the will of your Father in Heaven".

### The excuses

::: kinh mesillat-sebag nguyen-van | chapter 6, segment 19
He thus says: "I will eat a bit", or "I will sleep a little", or "it is difficult for me to leave my house". "I have put off my coat; how shall I put it on again?". "It is very hot outside". "It is very cold" or "it is raining" and all the other sorts of excuses and rationalizations that the mouths of lazy people are full of. Either way, in the meantime the Torah is abandoned, the service of G-d is idle, and man abandons his Creator. This is what Shlomo said: "by laziness the ceiling decays; and through lowering of the hands the house leaks" (Kohelet 10:18).
:::

A note on context: Shlomo is King Solomon, and Kohelet is the Book of Ecclesiastes. Luzzatto borrows "I have put off my coat; how shall I put it on again?" from the Song of Songs. [Inference] Luzzatto does not list big reasons, but very small ones that sound very reasonable. In segment 21 he adds that the lazy person thinks himself wiser than seven people who can answer sensibly: laziness finds its own arguments. Segment numbers follow Sefaria's division of the text.

### Zeal is not overwork

[Inference] Chapter 6 is about delay in doing good, not about sleeping less, skipping meals or working without rest. The same Jewish tradition keeps the day of rest, Shabbat (see [Judaism 1, Week 3](/en/traditions/judaism/judaism-1-week-03/)). Resting at the right time is not laziness.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not verified any study that evaluates practice based on Mesillat Yesharim as such.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests (more than 8,000 people) found that "if… then…" plans helped people get started on and do what they intended more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65). (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
- [Verified] A study following 96 people who each chose a small daily action (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally et al. 2010)
- [Inference] "Doing it early" is easier when you have decided in advance when and where. The research does not measure zeal for good deeds.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 20 minutes a day.

1. **Morning: slow reading (3 minutes).** Read both passages. The second time, stop at "early".
2. **Reminder phrase (1 minute).** "Good deeds, done early."
3. **Choose and plan (3 minutes).** Choose one good deed for today: call your parents, reply to someone who is waiting, help a colleague, do the household job you tend to avoid. Write: "If [the first possible moment], then I will [the first step]."
4. **Reflection (5 minutes).** Read Luzzatto's list of excuses again. Write your own excuses, as close as possible to the words you actually say: "Let me have a coffee first", "I'm too tired tonight".
5. **During the day: do it early.** Do the chosen deed at the first possible moment. When you hear an excuse in your head, write it down word for word.
6. **Evening: trait notebook (5 minutes).** Mark ✓ or ✗, then write two lines: "The good deed I did early today was …"; "My excuse today was …".

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"A kind deed at home"**. See the full activity at [Kids' corner – Activity 6](/en/kids-corner/activity-06/).

One extra step this week: the child chooses the kind deed the evening before, and does it *before playing* the next morning, for example watering a plant, bringing grandparents a glass of water, or lining up everyone's shoes. The adult praises exactly what the child did, with no prizes.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- You start sleeping less, skipping meals or not letting yourself rest in order to be more "zealous". The exercise does not ask for that; enough sleep and rest are what make it possible to keep doing good over time.
- You lose interest in everything and find it hard to start anything for weeks. That may be a sign of depression, not laziness; ask a doctor.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
