**This week:** read Paul's passage on love slowly, choose one quality from it to practise each day, and at the end of the week look back over the whole course.

**Measurable goal:** practise the chosen quality on at least 5 of 7 days; at the end of the week, rate the course's 6 habits, keep 2 and write a 4-week plan.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Paul writes to the church in the city of Corinth, a community divided over who had the more remarkable spiritual gifts. In the middle of the letter he stops to say that all those gifts are nothing without love, and then describes love through concrete actions. The Christian tradition teaches that this love comes from God and is the measure of a believer's life.

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | 1 Corinthians 13:4–7
Love is patient and is kind. Love doesn’t envy. Love doesn’t brag, is not proud,

doesn’t behave itself inappropriately, doesn’t seek its own way, is not provoked, takes no account of evil;

doesn’t rejoice in unrighteousness, but rejoices with the truth;

bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, and endures all things.
:::

### Reading it in context

- This passage is often read at weddings, but Paul wrote it to a community in conflict, not specifically to married couples.
- The Greek word translated "love" here is *agapē*; older English Bibles sometimes render it "charity".
- **[Expert judgement] "Bears all things… endures all things" does not mean putting up with violence, abuse or control.** Verse 6 itself says love "doesn't rejoice in unrighteousness". No one may use this passage to keep someone in a harmful relationship. If you are in that situation, your safety comes first; see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates practising the qualities in this passage as such.
- [Verified] On concrete acts of kindness: 27 experiments (4,045 people) found that acts of kindness raised the well-being of the person doing them by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies were often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
- [Verified] On building a habit: 33 studies of self-control training showed a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017) [Inference] That is why the course suggests keeping few habits, and keeping them small.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 12–15 minutes a day, plus 20 minutes of review at the end of the week.

1. **Settle (1 minute).**
2. **Read (3 minutes).** Read the whole passage. The second time, read slowly, pausing at each "is" and "doesn't".
3. **Choose (day 1).** Choose one quality to practise all week, for example: patient (let others finish speaking), kind (one good word), doesn't brag, is not provoked (pause for three breaths before answering).
4. **Meditate (3 minutes, days 2–7).** Where did I practise that quality yesterday, and when did I slip?
5. **Respond and be still (3 minutes).** Christians: pray in your own words. Others: write one sentence in response. Then sit quietly for a little while.
6. **Review (end of the week, 20 minutes).** Rate each habit below from 0 (did not do it) to 3 (did it regularly and found it useful):

| Week | Habit | Score 0–3 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Slow reading for 10 minutes in four steps | |
| 2 | Writing one line after reading | |
| 3 | Pausing 10 seconds before a meal: "enough for today" | |
| 4 | One act of help for someone else | |
| 5 | Separating "today" and "tomorrow" worries | |
| 6 | Practising one quality of love | |

Keep the 2 habits with the highest scores. Write a 4-week plan: when, where, and which passage you will read. If you want to keep reading, choose another short Gospel passage and read it in the four steps.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**The game "Waiting for my turn"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Take a box of building blocks (Lego or wooden blocks). Adult and child take turns, each placing one block on a shared tower.
2. Rule: only place a block on your own turn, and wait until the other person has finished before reaching out.
3. If the tower falls, laugh together and build again; no one is blamed.
4. Ask: "Was it hard to wait for your turn?" The adult says: "Waiting for someone is one way of loving them."

Christian families can read verse 4 to their child. Other families can just play the turn-taking game.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- You feel you must "endure" someone who is harming you. That is not what this lesson teaches; find someone to help.
- Scoring makes you blame yourself. The table is only for choosing habits, not for scoring you as a person.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
