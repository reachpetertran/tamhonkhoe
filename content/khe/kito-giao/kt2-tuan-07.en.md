**This week:** sit in silence for 20 minutes each day, including days when you feel bored or feel nothing, and label each sitting "warm", "ordinary" or "dry". Read the section "Dryness or depression?" carefully before you start.

**Measurable goal:** sit in silence for 20 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; label each sitting "warm / ordinary / dry".

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Teachers of prayer in the Christian tradition all speak of times when prayer feels dry: no joy, no sense of God being near. The tradition teaches that this is normal on the path of prayer, even for the saints, and that at such times one need only stay, patiently.

::: kinh kempis-benham nguyen-van | Book II, Chapter IX, section 4
When, therefore, spiritual comfort is given by God, receive it with giving of thanks, and know that it is the gift of God, not thy desert. Be not lifted up, rejoice not overmuch nor foolishly presume, but rather be more humble for the gift, more wary and more careful in all thy doings; for that hour will pass away, and temptation will follow. When comfort is taken from thee, do not straightway despair, but wait for the heavenly visitation with humility and patience, for God is able to give thee back greater favour and consolation. This is not new nor strange to those who have made trial of the way of God, for with the great saints and the ancient prophets there was often this manner of change.
:::

::: kinh lawrence-practice nguyen-van | First Conversation
That there needed fidelity in those dryness, or insensibilities and irksomenesses in prayer, by which GOD tries our love to him; that *then* was the time for us to make good and effectual acts of resignation, whereof one alone would oftentimes very much promote our spiritual advancement.
:::

### Reading it in context

- *The Imitation of Christ* calls being "sometimes in the warmth and sometimes in the cold" normal; right after this passage it retells a psalm (Psalm 30) that moves from joy to sorrow and back to joy.
- [Expert judgement] The Catholic tradition has writers who go much deeper into this theme, for example St John of the Cross on the "dark night"; the course does not cover that.
- [Inference] "GOD tries our love" is about dryness in prayer. It is not used to explain illness, violence or loss, and does not mean having to bear them alone.
- Other sections of chapter IX recommend choosing "severe labours" and speak of battle with the devil; the lesson does not quote them.

### Dryness or depression?

Dryness in prayer usually stays within the time of prayer: outside it, you can still eat and sleep, still enjoy other things at times, and still manage daily life.

See a doctor or mental health professional, and do not treat it as just "dryness", if for two weeks or more you feel sad or empty most of the time and have lost interest in almost everything, together with changes in sleep or appetite, tiredness, poor concentration, or feeling worthless or hopeless. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, go straight to [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

The two can happen together. People of faith can also have depression, and treating depression does not conflict with prayer.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study of "dryness" in prayer.
- [Verified] An interview study of Western Buddhist meditators and teachers recorded 59 kinds of meditation-related experience in 7 domains, including difficult ones, with impact ranging from minimal and passing to severe and lasting. (Lindahl et al. 2017)
- [Verified] A review of 83 studies found about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience, most often anxiety or depression. (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Inference] Both studies are about meditation, not Christian prayer. They are only a reminder that long practice can bring difficult experiences worth paying attention to; not every unpleasant feeling should be taken as spiritual progress.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 25 minutes a day.

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read both passages. The second time, read only "When comfort is taken from thee, do not straightway despair…".
2. **Sit in silence (20 minutes).** As in earlier weeks: silent prayer, or silence with a short sentence. On dry days, do not try to produce feelings. Christians may simply say "Lord, I am still here", as Brother Lawrence speaks of an "act of resignation". People who are not Christian: just sit for the full time without demanding a good sitting. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation.
3. **Label (1 minute).** "Warm", "ordinary" or "dry". No label is better than another.
4. **End of week (5 minutes).** Look at the seven labels. If all seven days were dry and you feel sad or empty outside the sittings too, reread "Dryness or depression?" above.

If 20 dry minutes feel too heavy, 10–15 minutes is fine; regularity matters more than length.

## 4. Kids’ corner 6+

**Activity: "A bean in a cup"** (5 minutes a day, all week, an adult joins in)

1. Put a layer of damp cotton wool in the bottom of a clear plastic cup, and place a few mung beans on it.
2. Each day, your child adds a few drops of water and looks at the beans. For the first days there may be nothing to see.
3. The adult says: "Some days we can't see anything change, but inside the bean it is still growing. We keep watering."
4. When the beans sprout, draw the seedlings together.

Remind your child not to eat raw beans and to wash hands after playing. Christian families can add: "Sometimes when we pray we don't feel anything, but God is still there." If your child does not want to, stop; do not push.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
- You have the signs listed under "Dryness or depression?". Then see a doctor or professional; you can keep praying alongside if you wish, but prayer does not replace treatment.
- The dryness comes with a sense of being detached from yourself or from loved ones, or no longer recognising your own feelings.
- Then stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for longer than 20 minutes.
