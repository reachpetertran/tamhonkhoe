**This week:** read the parable of the Samaritan slowly, and add the fifth step mentioned in Week 1: carrying what you read into an action.

**Measurable goal:** read in four steps on at least 5 of 7 days; help a stranger at least once during the week.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] A lawyer asks Jesus what he must do to inherit eternal life. The answer is two commandments from the Hebrew Bible: love God with all your heart, and love your neighbour as yourself. When the man asks "who is my neighbor?", Jesus tells a story. The Christian tradition teaches that the right question is not "who deserves my help" but "do I become a neighbour to the one in need".

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Luke 10:25–37
Behold, a certain lawyer stood up and tested him, saying, “Teacher, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?”

He said to him, “What is written in the law? How do you read it?”

He answered, “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your strength, and with all your mind; and your neighbor as yourself.”

He said to him, “You have answered correctly. Do this, and you will live.”

But he, desiring to justify himself, asked Jesus, “Who is my neighbor?”

Jesus answered, “A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and he fell among robbers, who both stripped him and beat him, and departed, leaving him half dead.

By chance a certain priest was going down that way. When he saw him, he passed by on the other side.

In the same way a Levite also, when he came to the place and saw him, passed by on the other side.

But a certain Samaritan, as he traveled, came where he was. When he saw him, he was moved with compassion,

came to him, and bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and wine. He set him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and took care of him.

On the next day, when he departed, he took out two denarii, gave them to the host, and said to him, ‘Take care of him. Whatever you spend beyond that, I will repay you when I return.’

Now which of these three do you think seemed to be a neighbor to him who fell among the robbers?”

He said, “He who showed mercy on him.” Then Jesus said to him, “Go and do likewise.”
:::

### Reading it in context

- The lawyer's answer (verse 27) joins two verses of the Hebrew Bible: Deuteronomy 6:5 and Leviticus 19:18. Jesus and the people in the story are all Jews, except the Samaritan. "Love your neighbour as yourself" is also read in [Convergence 0 – Week 3 "The Golden Rule"](/en/convergence/week-03/).
- Jews and Samaritans at that time had a long-standing religious quarrel. [Inference] The weight of the story lies in the helper being someone from "the other side".
- [Expert judgement] The parable is not meant to say that Jews or priests are bad. Reading it as an attack on a religion is a misreading that has done harm; this course does not read it that way.
- A denarius was a Roman silver coin, about a day's wage.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates reading this parable as such.
- [Verified] On helping: a meta-analysis of 27 experiments (4,045 people) found that acts of kindness raised the well-being of the person doing them by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies were often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
- [Inference] In this tradition the reason to help is love, not feeling better oneself. The science only tells us that helping usually does not cost us in well-being.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 12–15 minutes a day, plus one action during the week.

1. **Settle (1 minute).**
2. **Read (4 minutes).** Read the whole story once. The second time, read one part closely each day: day 1 verses 25–29 (the question); day 2 verses 30–32 (the two who pass by); day 3 verses 33–35 (the Samaritan); day 4 verses 36–37 (the question turned around); days 5–7 the whole story.
3. **Meditate (4 minutes).** Who am I like in today's part: the man who was robbed, those who pass by, the one who stops, the innkeeper? Why do people "pass by on the other side"?
4. **Respond (2 minutes).** Christians: pray in your own words. Others: write one sentence in response.
5. **Be still (1–2 minutes).**
6. **Do likewise (once in the week).** Help a stranger with something small: giving directions, holding a door, carrying something, calling emergency services when you see someone in trouble, checking on someone who seems lost. Write it down.

**Safety first:** do not stop on a motorway, do not take strangers home, do not get into a stranger's car, do not lend your documents or accounts. When you see an accident or someone badly hurt, the best thing is usually to call your local emergency number and stay nearby if it is safe, and not to give first aid you have not learned.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

The parable asks "who am I a neighbour to?". This week uses the activity **"Swapping shoes"**: practising imagining how someone else sees things. See the full activity at [Kids’ corner – Activity 3](/en/kids-corner/activity-03/).

Do not let children go and help strangers on their own. For a child, "neighbours" are classmates, family members, the people next door, always with an adult nearby.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The story of a man robbed and beaten brings back a time when you yourself were attacked. Then stop this lesson.
- You feel so guilty about having once "passed by" someone in need that you cannot settle. This tradition speaks of compassion, not of condemning yourself.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

When you go out, notice someone who seems to need help. If it is safe, ask one question: "Can I help with anything?"
