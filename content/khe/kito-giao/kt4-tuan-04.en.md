**This week:** bring faith into everyday work, whether at a workplace, at home or in study. "Heartily" here means doing it fully and truly, not working longer hours.

**Measurable goal:** read on at least 5 of 7 mornings; each day do one task fully (not two at once) on at least 5 of 7 days; stop work at the time you set on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Letter to the Colossians says that everything, in word or in deed, can be done "in the name of the Lord Jesus" and with thanks. The Christian tradition teaches that ordinary daily work is also a place of serving God: doing it sincerely, as for the Lord, not only to be seen by others.

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Colossians 3:17
Whatever you do, in word or in deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.
:::

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Colossians 3:23
And whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men,
:::

### Reading it in context

- Verse 23 sits in a passage of instructions to the members of a household (3:18–4:1). Verse 22, just before it, speaks to "servants", that is, enslaved people in the society of the time; verse 4:1 tells masters to give their servants "that which is just and equal".
- [Expert judgement] Today many churches read verse 23 as addressed to everyone about all work. The course reads it that way. The course does not read this passage as approving slavery, nor as telling anyone to obey an unsafe or unlawful order at work.
- [Inference] "Not for men" does not mean looking down on the people we work with, but not working only when someone is watching (verse 22 says "not just when they are looking").

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates reading this passage as such.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis (38,124 workers) found that people who detach from work when off duty report less exhaustion and better sleep. This is a correlation. (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017)
- [Verified] Joint WHO and ILO estimates: in 2016 about 488 million people worked 55 or more hours a week, and about 745,000 deaths from ischemic heart disease and stroke were attributed to working such long hours. These are model estimates built on systematic reviews. (Pega et al. 2021)
- [Inference] So the lesson takes "heartily" to mean doing work fully within the hours you set, and then truly resting.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day for reading and looking back; the rest lies within the work you already do.

1. **Morning reading (5 minutes).** Read the two verses. Days 1–3 verse 23; days 4–5 verse 17; days 6–7 both. Choose one task you will do "heartily" today, and write down the time you will stop work.
2. **Do one task fully.** When you do the chosen task, do only that for 20–30 minutes: turn off notifications, open nothing else. Do it properly and carefully, including the part nobody sees (a clean data file, a corner of the kitchen wiped well, an honest answer to a customer). It can be very ordinary: washing up, fixing a report, teaching your child a lesson.
3. **Stop on time.** At the time you wrote, stop. Note one line about what is unfinished for tomorrow, then leave your work.
4. **Evening look-back (2 minutes).** Ask: "Who did I work for today? Did I stop on time?" Christians may offer the day's work in their own prayer; those who do not pray write one line (tamhonkhoe's adaptation).

**To be clear:** "heartily" does not mean taking on more work, skipping breaks, or accepting unsafe working conditions. At work, safety and the rest breaks set by labour law come first. Rest has its own lesson in [Convergence 0 – Week 5 "Rest"](/en/convergence/week-05/).

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**"One job, done really well"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The child chooses one small household job: lining up shoes at the door, folding towels, wiping the study desk, putting books on the shelf.
2. The adult asks: "What does it look like when it's done really well?" The child says it: shoes in straight pairs, towels folded with even edges.
3. The child works slowly, doing only that, with no TV at the same time.
4. When finished, look at it together. The adult says what they see ("the shoes are in a straight line now"), without giving a score.
5. Stop when the 10 minutes are up, even if the job is not finished.

Choose only safe jobs: no knives or scissors, no cleaning chemicals, no climbing. Families who want children to have regular chores can also see [Việc nhà của con](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-5/g5-viec-nha-cua-con/) (Children’s chores; in Vietnamese) on daynentang.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- "As for the Lord" turns into pressure to be perfect, so that you cannot stop or you blame yourself for a small mistake.
- You feel exhausted for a long time, or sleep badly for weeks because of work. Then talk with family, your manager or a doctor; this is not something a reading can solve.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
