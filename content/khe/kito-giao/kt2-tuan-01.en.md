**Before you start this course:** this is a Level 2 course, 20–30 minutes a day for 8 weeks, with the silent part growing from 10 to 20–25 minutes. If you are not used to slow reading, take [Christianity 1](/en/courses/christianity-1/) first. If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for longer than 20 minutes. The course describes how the Christian tradition prays; people who are not Christian can practise the silent part in the way set out separately in each lesson.

**This week:** read two short passages from Brother Lawrence, sit in silence for 10 minutes each morning, and each day do one ordinary task fully.

**Measurable goal:** sit in silence for 10 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; each day, do one chosen ordinary task fully, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Brother Lawrence (Nicholas Herman, from Lorraine) was a Carmelite friar in seventeenth-century Paris. He cooked for the community for many years, although he disliked kitchen work. The book *The Practice of the Presence of God* records his conversations and letters. The tradition teaches that a believer can live with God at all times, not only at the hours of prayer, and that small things done out of love count as much as great ones.

::: kinh lawrence-practice nguyen-van | Fourth Conversation
That our sanctification did not depend upon *changing* our works, but in doing that for GOD's sake, which we commonly do for our own. That it was lamentable to see how many people mistook the means for the end, addicting themselves to certain works, which they performed very imperfectly, by reason of their human or selfish regards.

That the most excellent method he had found of going to GOD, was that of doing our common business without any view of pleasing men, and (as far as we are capable) purely for the love of GOD.
:::

::: kinh lawrence-practice nguyen-van | Fourth Conversation
That we ought not to be weary of doing little things for the love of GOD, who regards not the greatness of the work, but the love with which it is performed. That we should not wonder if, in the beginning, we often failed in our endeavors, but that at last we should gain a habit, which will naturally produce its acts in us, without our care, and to our exceeding great delight.
:::

### Reading it in context

- The book was written down by someone else after talking with him; each sentence begins "That…" because it is reported speech. The English is a translation from the French.
- "Without any view of pleasing men" is about motive: not working in order to be praised. It does not mean ignoring other people; elsewhere he still works with others and follows his Superior's directions.
- [Inference] The "little things" are one's own tasks, done fully. The lesson does not mean that one person should take on the whole household's work in order to count as good.
- [Expert judgement] "Presence" here means the presence of God, in Christian belief. It is close to "attention to what you are doing" in the Convergence lessons, but not the same: for Brother Lawrence, attention is turned towards Someone, not only towards knowing clearly what one is doing.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates Brother Lawrence's "practice of the presence" as such.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of randomised trials of religious or spiritual interventions in mental health care found that they reduced anxiety, with weaker results for depression. The programmes varied widely and study quality was limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 97 studies (7,181 people) of psychotherapy that integrates the client's faith: the faith-integrated part reduced distress as well as standard therapy and added spiritual well-being. (Captari et al. 2018)
- [Inference] Both are guided programmes, mostly for people of faith. Practising on your own as in this course has not been tested; any benefit is only inferred.

Limits: prayer and silent sitting are spiritual practice, not treatment.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 20 minutes a day, in two parts.

**Morning (15 minutes)**

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read both passages. The second time, read only the second passage, very slowly.
2. **Sit in silence (10 minutes).** Set a timer. Sit on a chair, back upright but not stiff.
    - Christians: pray silently in Brother Lawrence's way, speaking to God simply, as to someone beside you, about the day ahead. When thoughts wander, come back gently, without blaming yourself.
    - People who are not Christian: sit in silence, resting attention on a phrase from the passage, for example "little things". When thoughts wander, come back to the phrase. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation, not a form of the tradition.
3. **Choose one task (2 minutes).** Choose an ordinary task you will do today: washing up, sweeping, writing an email, cooking a dish. Christians may offer the task to God before doing it, as Brother Lawrence did. Others: choose to do it fully, or for someone you love.

**During the day (5 minutes)**

4. **Do the chosen task.** Go a little slower than usual and do nothing else at the same time. When you notice your mind has moved elsewhere, come back to what your hands are doing.
5. **Write one line (evening).** Which task, when you remembered, when you forgot. Forgetting is normal; Brother Lawrence says that at the beginning we will often fail.

## 4. Kids’ corner 6+

**Game: "Carry the cup"** (5–10 minutes, an adult plays too)

1. Pour half a cup of water into a plastic cup.
2. Your child carries the cup very slowly from one end of the room to the other and gives it to someone in the family, trying not to spill.
3. The adult follows, also carrying a cup, without talking.
4. Ask: "While you carried the water, what were you thinking about? Your feet, your hands, or the cup?" There is no right or wrong answer.

Christian families can say: "Brother Lawrence did each small thing out of love for God." Other families can say: "We do small things carefully, because we love the person they are for." If your child does not want to play, stop; do not push. Spilling is normal; wipe it up together.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
- The practice makes you feel you must do ever more work to be a good person, or you keep blaming yourself for "not paying enough attention".
- Then stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for longer than 20 minutes.
