## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The "negative way" does not stand alone. The same author who wrote *The Mystical Theology* also wrote *The Divine Names*, explaining the names Scripture gives to God (Good, Light, Life, Wisdom…). This tradition teaches that one both calls God by these names and knows that He is beyond every name.

::: kinh dionysius-rolt nguyen-van | The Divine Names, chapter 1, section 1
We must not then dare to speak, or indeed to form any conception, of the hidden super-essential Godhead, except those things that are revealed to us from the Holy Scriptures.
:::

::: kinh dionysius-rolt nguyen-van | The Mystical Theology, chapter 5, final part
… nor can any affirmation or negation apply to it; for while applying affirmations or negations to those orders of being that come next to It, we apply not unto It either affirmation or negation, inasmuch as It transcends all affirmation by being the perfect and unique Cause of all things, and transcends all negation by the pre-eminence of Its simple and absolute nature—free from every limitation and beyond them all.
:::

::: kinh cloud-underhill nguyen-van | The Cloud of Unknowing, chapter 6
He may well be loved, but not thought. By love may He be gotten and holden; but by thought never.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Inference] In this tradition, "God cannot be spoken of" does not mean unbelief, nor that everything said about Him is equally good. The author writes from within faith in the Trinity and the Scriptures, and the first passage makes Scripture the limit of all speech about God.
- The second passage ends *The Mystical Theology*. Just before it comes a long series of "It is not…" sentences, including "nor is it darkness, nor is It light". So even this course's image of "darkness" is only a way of speaking.
- This course has read only a few passages of two texts. The Christian contemplative tradition includes many other writers (for example, Saint Gregory of Nyssa and Saint John of the Cross) that the course does not discuss. Anyone who wants to go further should find a guide within the tradition.
- The course does not compare this with how other traditions speak of God or ultimate reality.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A critical review points out that "mindfulness" has no agreed definition, many studies are methodologically weak and rely heavily on self-report, and adverse effects are not monitored enough. (Van Dam et al. 2018)
- [Verified] In the 28-day Centering Prayer trial with 702 Christians, all three groups (with a religious framing, without it, and simply reflecting on the day) improved in well-being, and they did not differ on the main measure. (Rohde et al. 2024)
- [Inference] The current evidence does not show contemplative prayer to be better than other approaches for mental health. This course describes a spiritual tradition; for those within the tradition, its value does not lie in those measures.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 40 minutes a day, plus 15 minutes at the end of the week.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the three passages. Each day, choose one sentence to carry with you.
2. **Sitting (30–40 minutes).** In the way that suits you best: silent sitting (Weeks 1–2), with a short word (Week 3), after setting down an image (Week 4), or sitting and slow walking (Week 5).
3. **Back to the ground (2 minutes).** Open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, name three things in the room.
4. **Self-rating (end of the week, 10 minutes).** Rate each habit 0 (could not do it), 1 (did it sometimes), 2 (did it regularly):

| Habit | Week | Score 0–2 |
|---|---|---|
| Sitting in silence without trying to picture anything | 1 | |
| Letting thoughts pass without following them | 2 | |
| Returning to one short word | 3 | |
| Noticing and setting down an image | 4 | |
| Practising gently, without straining | 5 | |
| Reading and talking with another person | 5 | |

5. **Choose two, write a plan (5 minutes).** Choose two habits to keep. Write a 4-week plan of 20–30 minutes a day, with one fixed day a week to look back. There is no need to keep to 40 minutes.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**"Three things our family doesn't know yet"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The family sits together and each person says one thing they don't know (Why do cats purr? What is at the bottom of the sea? How many stars are there?).
2. The adult writes three of them on a sheet of paper titled "Things our family doesn't know yet".
3. Ask: "Is it all right not to know?" Let the child answer.
4. Finish with: "Not knowing is normal. Some things we will find out, and some things are much bigger than us."

Do not use this game to test the child's knowledge.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
- A feeling that you or the world are "not real", or of being detached from your body, stays after you stand up. If it lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- After the course, you feel you have lost your footing in faith, or that everything you believe is meaningless. Then talk to a guide in your own tradition or to someone you trust.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
