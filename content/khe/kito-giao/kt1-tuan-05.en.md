**This week:** read slowly the "birds of the sky, lilies of the field" passage from the Sermon on the Mount, and once in the week set three of your own worries beside it.

**Measurable goal:** read in four steps on at least 5 of 7 days; once in the week, write down 3 worries and then reread the passage.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Right after the teaching on wealth ("No one can serve two masters"), Jesus tells his disciples not to be anxious about food, drink and clothing. The Christian tradition teaches that God is a Father who knows what his children need; believers are invited to put first what matters most ("God's Kingdom and his righteousness") and to live today fully.

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Matthew 6:25–34
Therefore I tell you, don’t be anxious for your life: what you will eat, or what you will drink; nor yet for your body, what you will wear. Isn’t life more than food, and the body more than clothing?

See the birds of the sky, that they don’t sow, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns. Your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren’t you of much more value than they?

“Which of you by being anxious, can add one moment to his lifespan?

Why are you anxious about clothing? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow. They don’t toil, neither do they spin,

yet I tell you that even Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these.

But if God so clothes the grass of the field, which today exists and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, won’t he much more clothe you, you of little faith?

“Therefore don’t be anxious, saying, ‘What will we eat?’, ‘What will we drink?’ or, ‘With what will we be clothed?’

For the Gentiles seek after all these things; for your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things.

But seek first God’s Kingdom and his righteousness; and all these things will be given to you as well.

Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Inference] The passage speaks of gnawing anxiety; it does not forbid work or planning. Birds still look for food, and Jesus's listeners still sowed, reaped and spun. What is questioned is worry that cannot "add one moment" to life.
- "Gentiles" here means people outside Israel. The line is a comparison addressed to the listeners, not a criticism of others.
- The last verse does not say to ignore tomorrow; it says not to carry tomorrow's troubles into today.
- [Expert judgement] This passage is not financial or medical advice. Reading it does not mean skipping medical care, neglecting your family's needs, or not saving.
- (The quotation marks are as in the source: in the World English Bible the speech continues across chapters 5–7.)

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates reading this passage for worry.
- [Verified] The closest evidence on living with worry: a review of 20 meta-analyses found that Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), which practises letting worry be present while still doing what matters, works for anxiety and depression about as well as cognitive behavioural therapy. This is therapy guided by a professional. (Gloster et al. 2020)
- [Verified] Religious or spiritual interventions in general reduced anxiety in trials, but the programmes varied widely and study quality was limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
- [Inference] Writing worries down and separating "today" from "tomorrow" is a simple way of sorting them; it does not cure an anxiety disorder.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 12–15 minutes a day.

1. **Settle (1 minute).**
2. **Read (3 minutes).** Read the whole passage once. The second time, read one part closely each day: day 1 verses 25–27 (the birds); day 2 verses 28–30 (the lilies); day 3 verses 31–33 (seek first); day 4 verse 34 (tomorrow); days 5–7 the whole passage.
3. **Meditate (4 minutes).** Which of my worries does today's line touch?
4. **Respond (2 minutes).** Christians: pray in your own words, perhaps telling God about that worry. Others: write one sentence in response.
5. **Be still (2 minutes).**
6. **Three worries (once in the week, about 10 extra minutes).** Write down 3 worries. Next to each, write "today" if you can do one small thing about it today, or "tomorrow" if not. For each "today", write one small step and do it. Then reread verse 34.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**The game "Watching birds, watching flowers"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Go with your child onto a balcony or into a yard, or stand at a window.
2. Find a bird (or a butterfly, an ant) and a flower (or a leaf).
3. Look for a long time, about a minute each: what is the bird doing? How many petals does the flower have, what colour is it?
4. Your child draws one of the things they watched. The adult says: "When we look closely at one small thing, our heads get a little rest too."

Christian families can read verse 26 to their child. Do not ask children about big worries; if they bring one up themselves, just listen.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Worry lasts for weeks, makes it hard to sleep or work, or comes with a racing heart or shortness of breath. This may be an anxiety disorder; see a doctor or mental health professional. "You of little faith" does not mean that anxiety is a failure of faith.
- Writing worries down makes you go over them more. Then leave out step 6.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

When a worry about tomorrow comes, say quietly: "Each day's own evil is sufficient," then carry on with what you are doing.
