**This week:** read the parable of the two men who prayed slowly, choose one short sentence, and each day sit in silence for 20 minutes, repeating it very slowly.

**Measurable goal:** sit in silence for 20 minutes with one short sentence on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Jesus tells this parable to people who were sure of their own righteousness and looked down on others. Two men go up to the temple to pray. One lists his good deeds and compares himself with others; the other says only one sentence. The Christian tradition teaches that God looks at a humble and honest heart, not at being better than anyone.

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Luke 18:9–14
He also spoke this parable to certain people who were convinced of their own righteousness, and who despised all others:

“Two men went up into the temple to pray; one was a Pharisee, and the other was a tax collector.

The Pharisee stood and prayed by himself like this: ‘God, I thank you that I am not like the rest of men: extortionists, unrighteous, adulterers, or even like this tax collector.

I fast twice a week. I give tithes of all that I get.’

But the tax collector, standing far away, wouldn’t even lift up his eyes to heaven, but beat his chest, saying, ‘God, be merciful to me, a sinner!’

I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other; for everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, but he who humbles himself will be exalted.”
:::

### Reading it in context

- The Pharisees of that time were a devout Jewish group, respected by many; tax collectors gathered taxes for the Roman authorities and were seen as traitors. The first hearers would have been surprised by an ending that reversed what they expected.
- [Expert judgement] Later, the word "Pharisee" came to be used as an insult meaning "hypocrite", and that usage has fed contempt for Jewish people. tamhonkhoe does not read the parable that way. Verse 9 says the parable is about a temptation in every group, Christians included: trusting in one's own righteousness and despising others.
- [Expert judgement] Eastern Orthodox Christianity has the "Jesus Prayer", a very short prayer repeated many times a day. It draws on verse 13 and on the blind man's cry in Luke 18:38. That tradition also has an accompanying way of breathing, which needs a teacher; this course does not teach it.
- [Inference] "A sinner" in the parable is honesty before God, not self-contempt. The one praised is the one who tells the truth and needs grace, not one who hates himself.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any review of the Jesus Prayer or of short repeated prayers.
- [Verified] Religious or spiritual interventions in general (a meta-analysis of randomised trials) reduced anxiety, with weaker results for depression; study quality was limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
- [Verified] Psychotherapy that integrates the client's faith (97 studies, 7,181 people) reduced distress as well as standard therapy and added spiritual well-being. (Captari et al. 2018)
- [Inference] Neither study evaluates repeating a prayer as such; any benefit of this lesson is only inferred.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 25 minutes a day.

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read the whole parable. The second time, read only verse 13.
2. **Choose a sentence (day 1).** Choose one short sentence and keep it all week.
    - Christians: the tax collector's words ("God, be merciful to me"), or a short prayer familiar in your church.
    - People who are not Christian: a short sentence of your own that says something true and does not put yourself down, for example "I am here" or "Let my heart be gentle". This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation.
3. **Sit in silence (20 minutes).** Set a timer. Repeat your sentence very slowly, silently. It does not need to match the breath; if it falls into step naturally, let it. When thoughts pull you away, return to the sentence. At times the sentence may fall quiet and only silence remains; stay there, and when thoughts come again, return to the sentence.
4. **Note (1 minute).** How the sentence felt today: heavy, light, dry, or warm.
5. **During the day (optional).** Repeat the sentence a few times while waiting, walking or washing your hands.

If 20 minutes is still hard, split it into two sittings of 10 minutes, morning and evening.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
- The words "a sinner" make you feel worthless or hateful, or you keep blaming yourself. Then change to another sentence, or stop this lesson; the prayer in the parable is not meant to make anyone hate themselves.
- Repeating the sentence turns into a compulsion: you feel you must say it enough times or something bad will happen.
- Then stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting in silence for longer than 20 minutes.
