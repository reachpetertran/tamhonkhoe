**This week:** read two passages that gather up the whole course. Each evening, look back with Micah's three questions. During the week, do one good act nobody knows about. At the end of the week, rate six habits, keep two, and write a plan for the next 4 weeks.

**Measurable goal:** read on at least 5 of 7 mornings; look back with the three questions on at least 5 of 7 evenings; do at least 1 hidden good act; complete the self-rating and the 4-week plan.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The prophet Micah sums up what God requires in three parts: act justly, love mercy, walk humbly with God. In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus teaches doing good quietly, not to be praised by others. The Christian tradition teaches that the works of faith are turned towards the one in need and towards God, not towards praise.

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Micah 6:8
He has shown you, O man, what is good. What does the LORD require of you, but to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God?
:::

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Matthew 6:3–4
But when you do merciful deeds, don’t let your left hand know what your right hand does,

so that your merciful deeds may be in secret, then your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly.
:::

### Reading it in context

- Micah is a book of the Hebrew Bible, and also scripture for Judaism. Micah 6:8 is read in both traditions. "The LORD" in capitals stands for the divine name.
- Matthew 6:2, just before this passage, mentions "the hypocrites" who sound a trumpet "in the synagogues and in the streets". [Expert judgement] The verse criticises giving in order to show off; it is not aimed at Judaism or the synagogue, and the course does not read it as a criticism of a religion.
- [Inference] In the same Sermon on the Mount, Matthew 5:16 says to let people see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven. The two do not contradict each other: what is criticised is doing good in order to be praised, not good deeds being seen.

## 2. What science finds

- No study has evaluated this course directly.
- [Verified] Acts of kindness raise the doer's well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
- [Verified] "If… then…" plans help people reach goals more than intentions alone (d = 0.65; goals and measures varied widely). The lesson uses them for the 4-week plan. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)

Limits: this is a practice course for daily life, not treatment.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus 20 minutes of review at the end of the week.

1. **Morning reading (5 minutes).** Days 1–3 read Micah 6:8, pausing each day on one part (justly; mercy; humbly). Days 4–5 read Matthew 6:3–4. Days 6–7 both.
2. **One hidden good act.** During the week, do one good thing without telling anyone or posting about it: quietly pay for something small for someone, take over a task at work, give to a fund without your name.
3. **Evening look-back with Micah (2 minutes).** Three questions: "Was I fair to anyone today? Was I kind to anyone? Where do I need more humility?" Christians may pray in their own words; those who do not pray write one line (tamhonkhoe's adaptation).
4. **Review (end of week, 20 minutes).** Rate each habit below from 0 (did not do it) to 3 (did it regularly and found it helpful):

| Week | Habit | Score 0–3 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two acts a week for someone in need | |
| 2 | One "if… then…" plan each morning for a good act | |
| 3 | Contributing to something shared by a group | |
| 4 | Doing one task fully and stopping on time | |
| 5 | Acts of love for people at home; meals without phones | |
| Whole course | 5 minutes of morning reading and 2 minutes of evening look-back | |

Keep the 2 habits with the highest scores. Write a 4-week plan, one "if… then…" sentence for each habit: when, where, and for whom. If you want to go further, return to [Christianity 1](/en/courses/christianity-1/) to read another Gospel passage slowly, or take [Christianity 2](/en/courses/christianity-2/) to add silent prayer.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

Matthew 6:3 says to do good without letting "your left hand know". This week uses the activity **"A kind deed at home"**: the child chooses someone at home and does a small "secret" good deed for them. See the full activity at [Kids’ corner – Activity 6](/en/kids-corner/activity-06/).

At the end of the week, ask the child which game from the course they liked best (a small gift, a small promise, enough for everyone, one job done really well, everyone has a job, the secret good deed) and play it again with the whole family.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Rating yourself makes you blame yourself for "not enough good works" or "not enough faith". The rating is only for choosing habits, not for judging who you are.
- "Hidden" becomes a reason to hide a difficulty of your own, such as debt or exhaustion from caring for others. What you need help with is worth telling someone you trust.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
