**This week:** read the passage in Acts about the shared life of the first Christian community in Jerusalem. The week's act is to contribute to something shared, not to act alone.

**Measurable goal:** read on at least 5 of 7 mornings; contribute at least once to a shared meal or shared task of a group; look back on at least 5 of 7 evenings.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] After Pentecost, the new believers in Jerusalem lived closely together: they shared their goods according to each person's need, went to the temple every day, and ate together in one another's homes. The Christian tradition teaches that faith is lived in a community, not only alone, and that the shared meal is part of that life.

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Acts 2:44–47
All who believed were together, and had all things in common.

They sold their possessions and goods, and distributed them to all, according as anyone had need.

Day by day, continuing steadfastly with one accord in the temple, and breaking bread at home, they took their food with gladness and singleness of heart,

praising God and having favor with all the people. The Lord added to the assembly day by day those who were being saved.
:::

### Reading it in context

- This describes one community at one time. Acts 4:32–37 adds that owners of land or houses sold them and laid the money at the apostles' feet, to be shared with those in need. In Acts 5:4, Peter says the field was still the owner's own before it was sold. [Inference] So the sharing was voluntary, not a rule.
- [Expert judgement] Churches understand "all things in common" in several ways: religious orders hold goods in common; most believers read it as a call to generosity and to caring for those in need in the community. The course does not use this passage to discuss economic or political theories.
- [Expert judgement] "Breaking bread" may mean an ordinary meal, and many churches also connect it with the Lord's Supper (the Eucharist, or Mass, for Catholics). The course does not explain this rite.
- "The temple" here is the Temple in Jerusalem: the first Christians were Jews and still went up to the Temple to pray.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates reading this passage as such.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 148 studies (308,849 people) found that people with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation; it does not show that joining a group makes people live longer. (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010)
- [Verified] In one study combining a survey, a follow-up and a randomised experiment, spending money on others went with, and in the experiment raised, happiness. The survey part is a correlation; it is one research group. (Dunn, Aknin & Norton 2008)

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus one shared act during the week.

1. **Morning reading (5 minutes).** Read the whole passage. The second time, read one verse closely each day (44, 45, 46, 47), and the whole passage on days 5–7. Ask: "Which groups do I belong to? Who in them is in need?"
2. **Doing, during the week.** Contribute to something shared by a group you already belong to (wider family, neighbourhood, class, workplace, parish, association): bring a dish to a shared meal, stay to tidy up after a meeting, give an afternoon to a neighbourhood clean-up, invite a few people to a simple meal. Eat slowly, put phones away, ask after one another.
3. **Evening look-back (2 minutes).** Ask: "What did I receive from others today, and what did I give?" Christians may pray in their own words; those who do not pray write one line (tamhonkhoe's adaptation).

**Safety first:** give within your means; the course does not advise anyone to sell property or hand savings to a group. This lesson is not financial advice. If a group asks you to hand over property or documents, or to cut contact with your family, stop and talk with relatives you trust.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**"Enough for everyone"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The adult sets out a plate of fruit (or biscuits) already cut, a few more pieces than there are people.
2. The child is "the sharer": counts everyone in the household, including anyone who is out, and shares so that everyone has a portion.
3. Ask: "How many pieces are left over? Who shall we keep them for?" Let the child think: a share for grandparents, for someone coming home late.
4. Everyone sits down to eat together; nobody starts first.

The adult does the cutting; children do not use knives. Watch for any food allergies in the family. Do not label the child "selfish" or "good": just ask and do it together.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- You feel pressed to give money or effort beyond your strength, or guilty for not belonging to any community. The course does not ask you to join any group.
- Being in a large group makes you very anxious. Then choose a small shared task with one or two people you trust.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
