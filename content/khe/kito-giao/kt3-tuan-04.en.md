**This week:** read chapter 1 of *The Mystical Theology* slowly, the text on which *The Cloud of Unknowing* draws. Each day, before sitting, write down one image you hold of God (or, if you do not pray, of something vast such as the sea, the sky or the universe) and set it down. Then sit in silence for 30 minutes.

**Measurable goal:** write one image and sit in silence for 30 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] *The Mystical Theology* is a very short treatise of five chapters by an author who calls himself Dionysius, the Athenian whom Saint Paul brought to faith (Acts 17:34). The Christian tradition calls this text's way of speaking about God the "negative way": because God is beyond everything we can say or think, the one who prays leaves behind every image in turn, even the most beautiful, to enter a "darkness" beyond light.

::: kinh dionysius-rolt nguyen-van | The Mystical Theology, chapter 1, opening prayer
Trinity, which exceedeth all Being, Deity, and Goodness! Thou that instructeth Christians in Thy heavenly wisdom! Guide us to that topmost height of mystic lore which exceedeth light and more than exceedeth knowledge, where the simple, absolute, and unchangeable mysteries of heavenly Truth lie hidden in the dazzling obscurity of the secret Silence, outshining all brilliance with the intensity of their darkness, and surcharging our blinded intellects with the utterly impalpable and invisible fairness of glories which exceed all beauty!
:::

::: kinh dionysius-rolt nguyen-van | The Mystical Theology, chapter 1, counsel to Timothy
Such be my prayer; and thee, dear Timothy, I counsel that, in the earnest exercise of mystic contemplation, thou leave the senses and the activities of the intellect and all things that the senses or the intellect can perceive, and all things in this world of nothingness, or in that world of being, and that, thine understanding being laid to rest, thou strain (so far as thou mayest) towards an union with Him whom neither being nor understanding can contain.
:::

::: kinh dionysius-rolt nguyen-van | The Mystical Theology, chapter 1, on Moses
Nevertheless he meets not with God Himself, yet he beholds—not Him indeed (for He is invisible)—but the place wherein He dwells.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Expert judgement] Scholars today think the author was an unknown writer of around the late 5th or early 6th century, probably in Syria, and so call him "Pseudo-Dionysius". His writings were very influential in both Eastern and Western Christianity; the author of *The Cloud of Unknowing* is thought to have translated *The Mystical Theology* into English.
- The opening prayer is addressed to the Trinity, that is, to God as Christian faith confesses Him. "Darkness" here is not God's absence but a way of saying that He is beyond human understanding. The passage on Moses draws on his ascent of Mount Sinai into the dark cloud where God was (the Book of Exodus).
- [Inference] "Leave the senses and the activities of the intellect" describes the stance in prayer of someone far along the contemplative path. It does not ask learners to detach themselves from their bodies, and this week's practice still begins and ends with the feeling of the body.
- Rolt's word "strain" refers to the desire that reaches towards God, not to tensing the body. Chapter 46 of *The Cloud of Unknowing* (Week 5) clearly advises against straining.
- Straight after the counsel to Timothy, the text has a passage telling him not to disclose these things to "the uninitiated" and criticising certain ways of thinking about God; the lesson does not quote it. Nor does it use the translator's notes.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates reading *The Mystical Theology* or practising the "negative way".
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 23 randomised trials of religious or spiritual interventions in mental health care found that they reduced anxiety; for depression the results were weaker. The programmes varied widely and study quality was limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
- [Verified] About 8% of meditators had an adverse experience, according to a review of 83 studies; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020)

Limits: the evidence is only indirect. This is the practice of a tradition, not treatment.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 40 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the three passages. The second time, read only "he beholds—not Him indeed (for He is invisible)—but the place wherein He dwells."
2. **One image (3 minutes).** Write one line about an image you hold.
    - Christians: an image of God (for example: father, judge, light, friend). Then write: "He is greater than this image." There is no need to throw the image away; the tradition still uses such images, only it does not take them as complete.
    - Those who do not pray: a thought about something vast (the sea, the night sky, the universe). Then write: "It is greater than this thought." This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation.
3. **Silent sitting (30 minutes).** For the first 5 minutes, notice the feeling of the body sitting. For the next 25, sit in silence as in earlier weeks, using your short word from Week 3 if you wish.
4. **Back to the ground (2 minutes).** Open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, name three things in the room.
5. **Tick it off.** The goal is at least 5 of 7 days.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**"The sky on a sheet of paper"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Give the child a small sheet of paper, about the size of a hand, and crayons. Invite them to draw "the whole sky".
2. Go together to a window or outside and look at the real sky.
3. Ask: "Does your picture have all of the sky? What is missing?"
4. Finish with: "Your picture is lovely. And the sky is even bigger."

Praise the child's drawing; the game is not about pointing out what it lacks. Do not look straight at the sun.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
- A feeling that you or the world are "not real", or of being detached from your body, stays after you stand up. If it lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- "Setting images down" leaves you feeling you have lost your footing, afraid of losing your faith, or with a lasting sense that God is far away. Then stop the course and talk to a guide in your own tradition.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
