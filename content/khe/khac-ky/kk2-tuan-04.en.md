**This week:** choose a single thought each day to "digest" well. In the morning copy one sentence by hand and carry it with you; in the evening note what that sentence helped with, or did not.

**Measurable goal:** copy one sentence by hand and write one journal line about it, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Stoicism teaches that what we take into the mind needs discipline too, like what we eat. Seneca (about 4 BC – AD 65) writes to his friend Lucilius that reading much in a hurry leaves nothing behind.

::: kinh seneca-gummere nguyen-van | Letter 2 "On discursiveness in reading", excerpt
The primary indication, to my thinking, of a well-ordered mind is a man’s ability to remain in one place and linger in his own company. Be careful, however, lest this reading of many authors and books of every sort may tend to make you discursive and unsteady. You must linger among a limited number of master-thinkers, and digest their works, if you would derive ideas which shall win firm hold in your mind. Everywhere means nowhere. […] a plant which is often moved can never grow strong. There is nothing so efficacious that it can be helpful while it is being shifted about. And in reading of many books is distraction.
:::

::: kinh seneca-gummere nguyen-van | Letter 2, excerpt
Each day acquire something that will fortify you against poverty, against death, indeed against other misfortunes as well; and after you have run over many thoughts, select one to be thoroughly digested that day. This is my own custom; from the many things which I have read, I claim some one part for myself.
:::

At the end of the letter, the thought Seneca keeps that day is about having enough:

::: kinh seneca-gummere nguyen-van | Letter 2, closing (excerpt)
It is not the man who has too little, but the man who craves more, that is poor. […] Do you ask what is the proper limit to wealth? It is, first, to have what is necessary, and, second, to have what is enough.
:::

### Reading it in context

- The closing thought comes from Epicurus, founder of a school different from Stoicism. Seneca says he crosses into the "enemy’s camp" not as a deserter but as a scout. [Expert judgement] That attitude fits how tamhonkhoe learns: learn from many sources, but keep one thought at a time and really use it.
- "In reading of many books is distraction" is advice to someone in training, not a criticism of wide learning.
- "To have what is enough" is the discipline of desire (Week 1). Seneca was wealthy; in another letter he says he himself has not fully done what he advises.
- "Death" appears only in a list of things to prepare the mind for; that subject belongs to Stoicism 3.

## 3. Practice for adults

20–25 minutes a day in total.

1. **Choose and copy (5 minutes, morning).** Choose one sentence from the passages of Weeks 1–4, or from a book you have chosen to read for the long term. Copy it by hand onto a slip of paper. One sentence a day; repeating an earlier one is fine.
2. **Slow reading (10 minutes).** Read one page of your chosen book slowly, or reread this week’s passages. During those 10 minutes, don’t skim anything else.
3. **Carry it (during the day).** Keep the slip in a pocket or by your desk. Each time you see it, read it once.
4. **Evening journal (5–10 minutes).** Write: what did that sentence help with today? If it didn’t, why not?
5. **Tick it.** At the end of the week, count the days you copied and wrote; the goal is at least 5 of 7. At the end of the week, pick one sentence you want to keep for the long term.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "One sentence, three times"** (5–10 minutes, reading with an adult)

1. The child picks a familiar storybook and points to their favourite sentence.
2. Read that sentence three times in three voices: normal, whispering, very slowly.
3. The child draws a small picture for the sentence and sticks it on the wall or fridge.
4. Next day ask: "Do you remember yesterday’s sentence?"

Don’t turn the game into a test. If the child cannot read yet, the adult reads and the child repeats.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The journal makes you blame yourself for "not using it". Then keep only steps 1 and 3.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
