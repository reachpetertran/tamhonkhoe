**This week:** read a Qur'an verse that speaks of differences between peoples as a reason to learn about one another, and try doing that in one conversation.

**Measurable goal:** read Qur'an 49:13 slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; have one conversation of about 15 minutes to learn about someone different from you, then note three things you learned.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 49 (Al-Hujurat) says a great deal about how people should treat one another. Verse 13 opens with "O mankind", addressing everyone rather than believers only. This tradition teaches that all people share one origin, and that a person's worth before Allah lies in righteousness, not in lineage or nation.

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 49:13
O mankind! Lo! We have created you male and female, and have made you nations and tribes that ye may know one another. Lo! the noblest of you, in the sight of Allah, is the best in conduct. Lo! Allah is Knower, Aware.
:::

### Reading in context

- Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. The Vietnamese translation on QuranEnc adds the names Adam and Hawwa (Eve) in brackets.
- "That ye may know one another": [Inference] in the flow of the verse, differences between peoples are given as a reason to learn about each other, not to rank one above another.
- The second half says the noblest in the sight of Allah is "the best in conduct". [Traditional teaching] In this tradition that is for Allah to judge, not for people to rank one another.
- The two verses just before (49:11–12) tell believers not to mock one another, not to call one another by insulting names, to avoid suspicion, and not to spy on or speak ill of one another. The course reads only verse 13.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates reading this verse.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 515 studies (713 independent samples) on contact between groups found that contact typically reduces prejudice towards the other group. The effect is not limited to the people one meets but extends to the whole group, and more rigorous studies show clearer results. (Pettigrew & Tropp 2006)
- [Inference] Most of these studies measure lasting contact (classmates, colleagues, neighbours). One conversation in a week is a small step; there is no evidence it is enough to change prejudice.

Limits: the result is an average over very different settings; contact under tension or pressure may not help.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes of reading and writing a day, plus one conversation during the week.

1. **Every day: read slowly (3 minutes).** Read verse 49:13. On days 1–2 notice the phrase "know one another"; on days 3–4 notice the second half of the verse.
2. **Days 1–2: choose someone (5 minutes).** Think of someone you know but do not really understand, who differs from you in one way: home region, ethnicity, religion, generation, work. Choose someone you already know and a safe place to meet.
3. **Days 1–2: prepare three open questions.** For example: "How did your family celebrate New Year (or another festival) when you were small?", "Which food makes you think of home?", "What do people often misunderstand about where you come from?"
4. **During the week: talk for about 15 minutes.** Listen more than you speak. Do not argue about who is right in religion or politics, and do not press for personal matters the other person does not want to share.
5. **Afterwards: note three things (5 minutes).** Three things you learned, and one thing you had misunderstood before (if any).
6. **Other days: write one line.** "Today I noticed…" about someone different from you.

This is not an exercise in talking about your own religion or inviting anyone to any faith.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Hello in many languages"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The adult prepares three greetings in advance: "Xin chào" (Vietnamese), "Hello" (English), and one other greeting the family knows for sure how to say, for example "Sawasdee" (Thai) or "As-salamu alaykum" (the Muslim greeting, meaning "Peace be upon you").
2. Put three toys in three corners of the room, each one "a friend from another country". The child walks to each friend and greets them in their language.
3. Ask: "Does a friend from another country like to play the same games as you? How could we find out?" (ask them, play with them).
4. The adult says: "Everyone speaks a different language and eats different food. We ask so we can learn more about our friends."

Do not compare which country or people is better. Teach only greetings the adult knows the meaning of for sure.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The conversation touches a painful memory (war, discrimination) for the other person or for you. If so, leave the topic, thank the other person, and do not ask further.
- You do not feel safe with the person or the place. You do not need to do the exercise; choose someone else or skip this week.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
