**This week:** read two verses of the Qur'an that gather up the spirit of the whole course: giving for something greater than yourself, without waiting to be repaid. Then look back over five weeks and keep two habits to carry on.

**Measurable goal:** read slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; rate all seven habits of the course; choose two and write an "if… then…" plan for each for the next four weeks.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Wishing for no thanks

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 76:8–9
And feed with food the needy wretch, the orphan and the prisoner, for love of Him,  
(Saying): We feed you, for the sake of Allah only. We wish for no reward nor thanks from you;
:::

### Reading in context

- Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. The passage continues into verse 10.
- "For love of Him" can be read in two ways: out of love for Allah, or even though they themselves love, and need, that food. Pickthall chooses "for love of Him". Commentators give both readings.
- "For the sake of Allah" renders *wajh Allah*, literally "the face of Allah"; in the Qur'an's usage it means Allah's good pleasure.

### Looking back over the course

[Inference] The five weeks moved from giving (zakat, sadaqah), through the month of Ramadan (reading, restraint, guarding speech), to the people closest to us (parents) and then further out (neighbours, the wayfarer). Verses 76:8–9 tie these together: giving, restraint and kindness are all done without seeking praise.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests with more than 8,000 people: plans of the form "if situation X arises, then I will do Y" helped people reach their goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65); goals and measures varied widely. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
- [Verified] 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally et al. 2010)

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day.

1. **Morning: slow reading (3 minutes).** Read 76:8–9. On one day this week, reread the verse you liked best from the previous five weeks.
2. **Once in the week: rate yourself (15 minutes).** Rate each habit from 1 (hardly did it) to 5 (did it regularly and found it helpful):

| Habit | Week | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|---|
| One act of giving, never brought up | 1 | |
| One deliberate kind word a day | 1 | |
| A 10-minute slow reading in the evening | 2 | |
| Light restraint from one snack (optional) | 2 | |
| Guarding one speech habit | 3 | |
| Asking after a parent or older person | 4 | |
| Knowing and helping a neighbour | 5 | |

3. **Choose two habits and write a four-week plan (10 minutes).** For each, write one "if… then…" sentence, for example: "If I finish dinner on Sunday evening, then I call my mother for 10 minutes." Add: "If I miss a week, then the next week I do the shortest version."
4. **A last act of giving (optional).** As in Week 1: within your means, and not talked about.
5. **Evening: write one line (2 minutes).** "Did I do something today without wanting anyone to thank me?"

You do not need to keep the light restraint exercise. If you choose to, keep to the limits of Week 2: one snack only, no skipped meals, and not for anyone on the list of people who should not do it.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+: The good-deed tree

About 5–10 minutes, with an adult.

1. Draw a tree with a trunk and branches on a large sheet of paper with the child, and stick it on the wall.
2. Cut out some paper leaves in advance (the adult handles the scissors, or use sticky notes).
3. Each time the child does a good deed this week (sharing a toy, helping a parent, greeting a neighbour), they write or draw it on a leaf and stick it on the tree.
4. At the end of the week, count the leaves together. Ask: "Which one did you do without anyone knowing?"

The adult can say: "Some of our Muslim friends do good things without needing anyone to say thank you. Our tree grows from deeds like that."

Do not reward each leaf with money or presents; the joy is in the deed itself.

## 5. Stop practising if

- Rating yourself makes you blame yourself for "not doing enough". The point is to choose two things you can manage, not to grade yourself.
- You have kept up the restraint exercise and notice yourself thinking more about food or weight, or wanting to cut out more foods or skip meals. Stop at once and talk to a doctor.
- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself. Stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
