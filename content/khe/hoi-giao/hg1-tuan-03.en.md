**This week:** learn about *shukr*, gratitude in the Islamic tradition: gratitude to Allah, and to the people who have helped us.

**Measurable goal:** each evening note one specific thing you are grateful for, on at least 5 of 7 days; during the week, thank 2 people in person.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Gratitude brings more

[Traditional teaching] The Islamic tradition teaches that every good thing people have is a blessing from Allah, and that gratitude (*shukr*) is part of faith. Chapter 14 (Ibrahim) has two verses that are often quoted on gratitude.

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 14:7
And when your Lord proclaimed: If ye give thanks, I will give you more; but if ye are thankless, lo! My punishment is dire.
:::

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 14:34
And He giveth you of all ye ask of Him, and if ye would count the bounty of Allah ye cannot reckon it. Lo! man is verily a wrong-doer, an ingrate.
:::

### Thanking people

[Traditional teaching] This tradition also teaches that gratitude to Allah is not separate from gratitude to people. Hadith 4811 in *Sunan Abi Dawud*, narrated by Abu Hurayrah, teaches:

::: kinh sunnah-com trich | Abu Dawud 4811 (part)
He who does not thank the people is not thankful to Allah
:::

sunnah.com records that al-Albani graded this hadith *sahih* (authentic). The English translation on sunnah.com is in copyright, so this page quotes only a short phrase. Read the full hadith at [sunnah.com, Abu Dawud 4811](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4811).

### Reading in context

- Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. "Thankless" and "ingrate" mean ungrateful.
- Verse 14:7 sits in a passage recounting what Moses said to his people. Verse 14:5, just before it, says these events are signs for everyone who is patient and grateful: the two virtues of this course appear side by side in the scripture itself. Verse 14:7 contains both a promise ("I will give you more") and a warning ("My punishment is dire").
- [Inference] These verses do not say that anyone who meets hardship has been ungrateful. Many other verses of the Qur'an say that good people are tested too (Week 4 reads about patience). The course does not use this verse to explain anyone's suffering.
- Verse 14:34 ends by calling man "an ingrate": in the flow of the verse, this is a reminder that because the blessings are so many, people easily forget them, not a judgement on any one person.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates the Islamic practice of *shukr* itself.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 27 studies (3,675 people) found that gratitude exercises reduce symptoms of depression and anxiety by a small amount; compared with control groups doing another activity, the effect was smaller still. (Cregg & Cheavens 2021)
- [Inference] The gratitude-writing exercises in these studies are close to this week's exercise, but not set within faith. We have not found a review of thanking people in person.

Limits: the effect is small and may partly come from expectation. Gratitude does not replace treatment if you are severely depressed or anxious.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10–12 minutes a day.

1. **Morning: read slowly (3 minutes).** Read verses 14:7 and 14:34. Notice the phrase "ye cannot reckon it".
2. **During the day: notice one thing.** A meal, someone letting you pass, a moment of feeling well. It need not be big.
3. **Evening: note one thing you are grateful for (5 minutes).** Be specific: what happened, who was involved, why it matters to you. Try not to repeat the same sentence each day.
4. **Twice in the week: thank someone (5 minutes).** Choose someone who has helped you (family, a colleague, a security guard, a shopkeeper). Say or send a thank-you that names what they did.
5. **Muslims** can join step 3 to thanks to Allah in their own way. **Those who are not Muslim** simply note and thank people.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"Who helped me today?"**: in the evening the child names someone who helped them that day, and the family makes a small thank-you card together. See the full activity in [Kids' corner – Activity 2](/en/kids-corner/activity-02/).

Muslim families can add that in their faith, thanking people is one way of thanking Allah.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The exercise makes you feel guilty for being sad, or as if you must be "grateful" for something that hurt you. Gratitude does not mean denying pain, and no one has to thank a person who harmed them.
- The warning in verse 14:7 leaves you frightened for a long time. If so, stop and talk with a trusted friend, a teacher in your community, or a mental health professional.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
