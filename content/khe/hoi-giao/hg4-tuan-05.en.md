**This week:** read a verse of the Qur'an that draws a widening circle of people around you: from parents out to neighbours and strangers far from home. The exercise takes kindness beyond your own front door.

**Measurable goal:** read slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; learn the name of at least one neighbour whose name you did not know, and greet them by name; one small act of help for a neighbour in the week; one act of kindness to someone far from home; one line in your notebook each evening.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### A circle of people

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 4:36
And serve Allah. Ascribe no thing as partner unto Him. (Show) kindness unto parents, and unto near kindred, and orphans, and the needy, and unto the neighbour who is of kin (unto you) and the neighbour who is not of kin, and the fellow-traveller and the wayfarer and (the slaves) whom your right hands possess. Lo! Allah loveth not such as are proud and boastful,
:::

### Reading in context

- Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. The verse ends with a comma because the sentence runs on into verse 37.
- "The neighbour who is of kin and the neighbour who is not of kin": the Vietnamese translation says "neighbours near and far". Commentators read "near" and "far" in several ways: near or far from home, related or not, of the same faith or not.
- "The wayfarer" (*ibn al-sabil*, literally "son of the road") is someone travelling and in need while away from home. The same group appears in the list of those who may receive zakat in Week 1.
- The verse mentions "those whom your right hands possess" (Pickthall: "the slaves"), an institution of that time; the lesson does not go into that history.
- [Traditional teaching] Al-Bukhari placed verse 4:36 at the head of his chapter "To recommend to be kind to one's neighbour". The first hadith of the chapter (number 6014, narrated by Aishah), in summary: the Prophet said the angel Gabriel kept urging him about the neighbour until he thought the neighbour would be made an heir. In al-Bukhari's *al-Adab al-Mufrad* (number 105), Abdullah ibn Amr has a sheep slaughtered and asks again and again whether a share has been given to his Jewish neighbour, then repeats the same hadith. The lesson only summarises.
- [Inference] The verse ends by rejecting the proud and boastful: kindness to everyone in that circle is set against self-importance.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 148 studies, 308,849 people: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period (OR = 1.50). This is a correlation and does not prove that relationships are the cause. (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010)
- [Inference] The research measures social relationships in general, not neighbourly relationships in particular. This week's exercise rests only on the general point that people need one another.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day, plus the actions in the week.

1. **Morning: slow reading (3 minutes).** Read 4:36. On day 1, write each group of people in the verse on its own line, and next to it one real person around you (no real names needed).
2. **Learn a neighbour's name.** Choose someone you see often but whose name you do not know (the person downstairs, the security guard, the woman who sells vegetables at the corner). Ask their name, and greet them by it from then on.
3. **One small act of help for a neighbour (once in the week).** Carry something for them, take in a parcel, bring over a plate of fruit, check on someone who lives alone.
4. **Kindness to someone far from home (once in the week).** A new colleague from another province, a student renting a room, a worker far from their home town, a visitor asking the way. Invite them to lunch, give directions carefully, ask whether they need anything.
5. **Evening: write one line (2 minutes).** "Who did my circle widen to include today?"

Stay safe: do not invite strangers in when you are home alone, and do not give personal information or money to people you do not know well. Kindness does not mean dropping caution.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"Sending good wishes"**: the family thinks of one neighbour and sends them a good wish, in words or with a card. See the full activity at [Kids' corner – Activity 7](/en/kids-corner/activity-07/).

The adult can say: "We have neighbours on the left, on the right, upstairs and downstairs. When we greet and help each other, everyone feels better."

The child greets neighbours or gives a card only with an adult.

## 5. Stop practising if

- Meeting strangers makes you very anxious. Start with something smaller: a smile, a thank-you; do not push yourself.
- A neighbour or acquaintance makes you feel unsafe. This lesson does not ask you to go near that person; choose someone else.
- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself. Stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
