**This week:** put Rumi back in his own place: a Muslim scholar, a Sufi, a teacher with students. Learn about the Mevlevi order, the *sema* ceremony, and why many sayings attributed to Rumi online have lost that context.

**Measurable goal:** sit still for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; one 15-minute conversation with a companion about what you are practising; trace the source of one "Rumi" quote found online and note the result.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### A Muslim scholar meets Shams

[Verified] Rumi's father, Baha al-Din Walad, was a scholar invited to Konya, where he taught until his death; Rumi too became a teacher in the city. In 1244 Shams al-Din Tabrizi, a wandering Sufi, came to Konya. The meeting changed how Rumi taught and practised, and it was perhaps from then that he began to write poetry. The closeness between the two upset Rumi's family and students; Shams left in 1246, came back, and disappeared for good around 1248. Many of Rumi's lyric poems speak of that loss, and are often written in the voice of Shams himself. (Lewis, *Encyclopedia of Islam and the Muslim World*)

### The Mevlevi order and the sema ceremony (described)

- [Verified] After Rumi's death, his son Sultan Walad played a central part in founding the Mevlevi order in the late thirteenth century (Lewis). UNESCO gives the founding as 1273 in Konya, from where the order spread through the Ottoman Empire.
- [Verified] The Mevlevi *sema* ceremony was proclaimed by UNESCO in 2005 and inscribed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008. The whirlers (*semazen*) turn on the left foot, pushing with the right; the music is performed by a singer, a *ney* (reed flute) player, a kettledrummer and a cymbal player. In the past, learners trained for 1,001 days in a Mevlevi lodge in ethics, conduct and belief, through prayer, music, poetry and the turning, and then went back to work and family.
- [Verified] In 1925 all Mevlevi lodges in Turkey were closed. UNESCO notes that today many *sema* ceremonies are performed for tourists, shortened and simplified, away from their religious context.
- [Traditional teaching] In a Sufi order (*tariqa*), the learner (*murid*) follows a guide (*shaykh*, or *pir*). The passage below speaks of this.

::: kinh masnavi-whinfield nguyen-van | Book I, p. 46
A friend is needed; travel not the road alone,  
Take not thy own way through this desert!  
Whoso travels this road alone  
Only does so by aid of the might of holy men.
:::

### "Nearer to him than his jugular vein"

[Traditional teaching] The Sufi tradition reads many verses of the Qur'an about God's nearness. This is one of the most often quoted.

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 50:16
We verily created man and We know what his soul whispereth to him, and We are nearer to him than his jugular vein.
:::

### Reading in context

- Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955.
- Just before the poem above, Whinfield's version has lines urging the learner to submit to the guide even when the guide's actions make no sense, compared with the story of Moses and Khidr (Qur'an chapter 18). The course does not quote them, because they need someone within the tradition to explain them.
- [Expert judgement] Tasawwuf is found among both Sunni and Shi'a Muslims, in many different orders. There are also scholars and groups in the Muslim world who criticise some Sufi practices, such as music in worship or visiting the tombs of saints. The course notes these views and takes no side.
- [Verified] In January 2017 the journalist Rozina Ali wrote in *The New Yorker* about the erasure of Islam from Rumi's poetry in English: Rumi was a lifelong scholar of the Qur'an and Islam, yet is seldom described as a Muslim. We could not open the original article; the main point above follows the Poetry Foundation's summary.
- **Very important:** The course does not teach the *sema* turning. It is a ritual with teachers and its own framework of practice. Turning at home can easily cause dizziness and falls. Non-Muslims who want to see *sema* should choose a ceremony held by a Mevlevi community and ask beforehand how to behave.
- [Expert judgement] In any tradition, a good guide does not ask you to harm yourself or others, does not cut you off from your family, and does not demand unusual sums of money. If you meet these things, stop and ask someone you trust.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not been able to verify any review that evaluates the *sema* ceremony or following a Sufi order.
- [Verified] As in earlier weeks: a review of 83 studies found that about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience, most often anxiety or depression. (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 148 studies, 308,849 people: those with stronger social relationships were more likely to survive over the follow-up period. This is a correlation. (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010)
- [Inference] The advice "travel not the road alone" fits the general idea that companions help us on a long road. The research did not evaluate this in spiritual practice.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 35–40 minutes a day.

1. **Morning: slow reading (5 minutes).** On odd days read 50:16; on even days read "A friend is needed".
2. **Sitting still (25 minutes).** As in Week 4.
    - Muslims, and people following a Sufi order: keep your practice as your guide teaches.
    - Those who are not Muslim: sit and listen. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation, not a Sufi practice.
3. **Once in the week: talk with a companion (15 minutes).** Choose someone you trust (a friend, a relative, a guide in your own community). Tell them what you are practising, what is hard, what seems to be changing. Then ask them one question and listen to the whole answer without interrupting.
4. **Once in the week: trace one "Rumi" quote (15 minutes).** Pick a "Rumi" saying you have seen online. Find which book and poem it comes from, and who translated it. Note the result: you found the source, you only found sites copying each other, or you found nothing.
5. **Evening: write one line (3 minutes).** "Who walked part of the road with me today?"

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
- You feel as if you are "disappearing", unable to feel your body or emotions, and this lasts after the sitting. Open your eyes, stand up, feel both feet on the floor, talk to someone; if it continues, tell a doctor.
- A group or a guide asks you to leave your family, keep secrets from your loved ones, hand over unusual sums of money, or do something you feel is wrong. Stop and ask someone you trust.
- Sitting for 25 minutes keeps bringing up painful memories or thoughts. Go back to 15–20 minutes, open your eyes, or walk slowly instead of sitting.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting still for longer than 20 minutes.
- Then stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
