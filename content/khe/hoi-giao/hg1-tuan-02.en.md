**This week:** learn about the five pillars that Muslims regard as the foundation of religious life, and read slowly a Qur'an verse saying that righteousness lies in faith and deeds, not only in outward form.

**Measurable goal:** write a short paragraph (under 100 words) describing the five pillars in your own words; read Qur'an 2:177 slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; keep one small promise in full during the week.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### The hadith of Jibril

[Traditional teaching] Hadith are the words and deeds of the Prophet Muhammad as reported by later generations. One very well-known hadith, usually called the hadith of Jibril, is in *Sahih Muslim* and is number two in an-Nawawi's collection *Forty Hadith*. In the account of 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, a stranger dressed in white came and sat before the Prophet and asked about Islam, Iman, Ihsan and the Hour. At the end the Prophet said that the questioner was the angel Jibril (Gabriel), who had come to teach people their religion.

The three answers in the hadith are often used to sum up the structure of the faith:

| Part | Content according to the hadith (summary) |
|---|---|
| Islam (submission) | The five pillars: the testimony of faith, the Salah prayer, Zakah, fasting in Ramadan, the Hajj pilgrimage for those able |
| Iman (faith) | Belief in Allah, the angels, the Books, the Messengers, the Last Day, and the divine decree (qadar), both good and bad |
| Ihsan (doing what is beautiful) | Worshipping Allah as though you see Him; and if you do not see Him, He sees you |

The table above is tamhonkhoe's summary, not a translation. The English translation on sunnah.com is in copyright, so this page does not reproduce it; read the full hadith at [sunnah.com, an-Nawawi's Forty Hadith, no. 2](https://sunnah.com/nawawi40:2).

### The five pillars (described)

[Traditional teaching] This tradition teaches the five pillars as follows. This is a short description so that learners can grasp the main idea; the details and conditions of each pillar are set by the schools of Islamic law and differ in places.

1. **Shahadah – the testimony of faith:** saying and believing that there is no god but Allah, and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.
2. **Salah – prayer:** five times a day at set times, facing the Ka'bah in Makkah.
3. **Zakah – obligatory giving:** those whose wealth is above a certain level give a share each year to specified groups of people, such as the poor.
4. **Sawm – fasting in Ramadan:** healthy adults do not eat or drink from dawn to sunset; the sick, travellers and some others are excused or make up the days later.
5. **Hajj – pilgrimage to Makkah:** once in a lifetime, for those with the health and the means.

This course does not teach how to perform these rites. For fasting across the traditions, see the course [Body 4 "Fasting in the traditions (described)"](/en/courses/body-4/).

### Righteousness is not only outward form

[Traditional teaching] Verse 177 of chapter 2 (Al-Baqarah) joins faith to deeds: righteousness includes belief, giving for love of Allah, keeping prayer, paying Zakah, keeping promises and patience in hardship.

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 2:177
It is not righteousness that ye turn your faces to the East and the West; but righteous is he who believeth in Allah and the Last Day and the angels and the Scripture and the prophets; and giveth wealth, for love of Him, to kinsfolk and to orphans and the needy and the wayfarer and to those who ask, and to set slaves free; and observeth proper worship and payeth the poor-due. And those who keep their treaty when they make one, and the patient in tribulation and adversity and time of stress. Such are they who are sincere. Such are the Allah-fearing.
:::

### Reading in context

- Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. "Proper worship" is his rendering of Salah, and "the poor-due" of Zakah.
- [Expert judgement] Many commentators link the opening ("turn your faces to the East and the West") to the change in the direction of prayer (qiblah) discussed in earlier verses of the same chapter. Verse 177 does not say outward form is useless: the verse itself mentions prayer and Zakah. The point is that form should go together with faith and deeds.
- The verse mentions setting slaves free, a reality of the society of that time. The course does not discuss this history.
- "The patient in tribulation and adversity" at the end leads into the theme of patience in Week 4.

## 2. What science finds

- Science cannot evaluate faith, and the five pillars are religious duties, not health techniques. The course does not present them as ways to become healthier.
- [Verified] The pillar studied most for mental health is the Ramadan fast. A review of 22 papers found its effect on mental health generally small, but people with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder had a higher risk of relapse during the month. (Heun 2018)
- [Inference] This week's exercise (describing in your own words, keeping a promise) is an exercise in understanding and character, with no study of its own.

Limits: Heun's review brings together small and varied studies. Anyone with a chronic illness who wants to fast should ask their own doctor.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10–12 minutes a day.

1. **Days 1–3: understanding the five pillars.** Reread the table and list above (5 minutes). Then close the page and write the five pillars in your own words (5 minutes), one line each. The next day, open the page, compare and correct. By day 3, write a paragraph of under 100 words.
2. **Every day: read Qur'an 2:177 slowly (3 minutes).** Underline the deeds the verse names.
3. **Days 4–7: one deed from verse 177 that is not a rite.** Choose "keep their treaty when they make one": recall a small promise you have made to someone (to call someone back, return something borrowed, arrive on time) and keep it in full this week.
4. **Evening: write one line (2 minutes).** "Today I understood more about…" or "My promise has…".

The aim is understanding, not imitating ritual. If you know a Muslim, you could ask them to read your description and correct it, if they would like to.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Five pillars"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Use wooden blocks or Lego to build five pillars of the same height, and lay a piece of card on top as a roof.
2. Put a light toy on the roof. Take one pillar away: is the roof still steady?
3. The adult says: "Some families follow Islam. They have five important things, like five pillars that keep a house standing."
4. Ask: "What things help our family stay steady?" (eating together, helping each other, saying thank you…). The child names each pillar.

There is no need to explain the rites to children. Muslim families can tell the five pillars in their own family's way.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- You feel pushed to believe, or to give up a belief, by others or by yourself. The course only describes; you can stop at any time.
- You plan to fast in Ramadan and are pregnant, breastfeeding, have diabetes treated with medication, have had an eating disorder, or have a chronic illness: ask your doctor first. This course does not teach fasting.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
