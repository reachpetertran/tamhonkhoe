**This week:** learn about *dhikr*, the remembrance of God in Islam, through a much-quoted verse of the Qur'an, and begin a simple daily rhythm: reading in the morning, sitting still, and one pause during the day.

**Measurable goal:** read verse 13:28 slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; sit still for 10 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; pause once in the middle of the day on at least 5 of 7 days.

## What this course is, and is not

- The course **describes** dhikr and the five daily prayers (Salah) as Muslims understand and practise them. It **does not teach ritual**: it does not teach how to pray, how to perform ablution (wudu), how to recite Arabic or how to do dhikr.
- Muslims keep dhikr and Salah as their own community and teachers teach them; the course does not replace them.
- Those who are not Muslim read for understanding. The course's exercises are **neutral rhythms** set by tamhonkhoe: stopping, breathing slowly, sitting still. They do not follow prayer times, involve no bowing, recite no Arabic words of scripture, and are not dhikr or Salah.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### What dhikr is

[Traditional teaching] *Dhikr* (also written *zikr*) originally means remembering or mentioning. In Islam the word refers to remembering Allah: in the heart, in words and through deeds. The tradition teaches that the Qur'an itself is a "reminder", and that the five daily prayers are a form of remembrance. Outside prayer times, many Muslims repeat short phrases praising and thanking Allah at many points in the day.

### Hearts at rest

[Traditional teaching] Verse 28 of chapter 13 (Ar-Ra'd, The Thunder) is part of a passage about those who turn to Allah. It is often quoted when speaking of dhikr.

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 13:28
Who have believed and whose hearts have rest in the remembrance of Allah. Verily in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest!
:::

### Reading in context

- Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955.
- The Vietnamese translation on QuranEnc renders the heart as being "satisfied" (thỏa lòng). [Inference] The word in the verse carries both being at peace and being content.
- [Inference] The verse speaks of believers and the remembrance of *Allah*. The course does not read it as a promise that some relaxation technique will bring peace, nor does it say that people who are not Muslim "receive" what the verse speaks of.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not been able to verify any review that evaluates dhikr itself for people learning on their own.
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 97 studies, 7,181 people, found that therapy integrating the client's religious faith reduced distress as well as standard therapy and added spiritual well-being. (Captari et al. 2018)
- [Verified] A meta-analysis of randomised trials found that religious/spiritual interventions reduced anxiety, but programmes varied widely and study quality was limited. (Gonçalves et al. 2015)
- [Inference] Both concern guided programmes, mostly for people who already had faith. This course's sitting exercise has not been tested on its own.

Limits: science cannot measure faith. These studies are about mental health, not about the religious meaning of dhikr.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 20 minutes a day.

1. **Morning: slow reading (3 minutes).** Read verse 13:28 twice. The second time, pause at "rest".
2. **Sitting still (10 minutes).** Set a timer. Sit on a chair, back upright but not stiff, both feet on the floor.
    - Muslims: keep dhikr as your community teaches.
    - Those who are not Muslim: sit still with your attention on the out-breath. When thoughts wander, come back gently without blaming yourself. This is tamhonkhoe's adaptation, not dhikr.
3. **One pause in the middle of the day (1–2 minutes).** Choose a point you already have every day, for example just before lunch. At that point: stop what your hands are doing, take three slow breaths, look around at where you are, then carry on.
4. **Evening: write one line (3 minutes).** "When was my heart most at rest today?"

If you miss a day, carry on the next day; do not make up for it.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"Listening to the bell fade"**: tap a bell or a glass gently and sit still together, listening until the sound has completely gone. See the full activity at [Kids' corner – Activity 11](/en/kids-corner/activity-11/).

Afterwards the adult can ask: "When in the day do you feel most calm?"

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
- Sitting still leaves you restless or sadder for several days in a row. Then cut it to 3–5 minutes, or open your eyes and simply sit listening to the sounds around you.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting still for longer than 20 minutes (later weeks of the course go up to 20 minutes).
- Then stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
