**This week:** read a teaching which shows that fasting in Islam is not only going without food but also guarding speech and action; and look again at who is exempt. This week's exercise has nothing to do with food: guarding one speech habit.

**Measurable goal:** read slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; choose one speech habit and write an "if… then…" plan for it; each evening note how many times you held back (or nearly slipped), on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Fasting from speech too

[Traditional teaching] In al-Bukhari's collection of hadith (number 1903, in the chapter "Whoever does not give up lying speech while fasting"), Abu Hurayrah reports the Prophet Muhammad as saying, in summary: whoever does not give up lying speech and acting on it, Allah has no need of their giving up food and drink. The lesson only summarises, because the translations on sunnah.com are in copyright.

[Traditional teaching] Commentators usually read this hadith as saying that the purpose of the fast is to train oneself to avoid wrongdoing, so someone who fasts yet keeps lying and deceiving has only gone hungry.

### Shun suspicion, spying and backbiting

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 49:12
O ye who believe! Shun much suspicion; for lo! some suspicion is a crime. And spy not, neither backbite one another. Would one of you love to eat the flesh of his dead brother? Ye abhor that (so abhor the other)! And keep your duty (to Allah). Lo! Allah is Relenting, Merciful.
:::

### Who is exempt from fasting (in brief)

[Traditional teaching] Verses 2:184–185 (read in full in [Body 4, Week 1](/en/body/body-4-week-01/)) let the sick and travellers make up the days later, and speak of feeding a needy person in place of fasting for those who fast only with great difficulty. Verse 185 says: "Allah desireth for you ease; He desireth not hardship for you."

[Expert judgement] In common practice, children who have not reached maturity do not fast; many scholars also exempt the frail elderly, the chronically ill and women who are menstruating, and allow pregnant or breastfeeding women not to fast when they fear for their own or their child's health. Details differ between schools; Muslims ask their own teachers, and a doctor about their health.

### Reading in context

- Qur'an: Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955.
- Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 1903 (sunnah.com: Book 30, Hadith 13); chapter title and narrator checked on 10 November 2026.
- [Inference] Verse 49:12 uses a very strong image for speaking ill of someone who is absent: they cannot defend themselves, like someone who has died.
- [Inference] The hadith and verse 2:185 both show that what is aimed at is a person being trained, not suffering.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests with more than 8,000 people: making plans of the form "if situation X arises, then I will do Y" helped people reach their goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65). The studies differ widely in their goals and measures, so this figure is an average. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
- [Inference] No study cited here measures guarding speech in this week's way. The lesson only borrows the "if… then…" form of planning.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day. No fasting.

1. **Morning: slow reading (3 minutes).** On odd days read 49:12, on even days the summary of the hadith.
2. **Choose one speech habit (day 1, 5 minutes).** Only one: speaking ill of someone who is absent; complaining; exaggerating to make a story better; guessing someone's bad motives and saying it aloud; a habitual sharp remark to someone at home.
3. **Write an "if… then…" plan.** For example: "If the talk at the table turns to criticising someone who isn't there, then I ask about something else." "If I want to complain, then I say one thing I will do instead."
4. **During the day.** Keep to the habit you chose. If you slip, do not blame yourself: note it, and if you hurt someone, put it right with a short apology.
5. **Evening: write one line (3 minutes).** "Today I held back ___ times and slipped ___ times. I slip most easily when ___."

This exercise is not fasting. If you did the light restraint exercise last week, you do not need to repeat it.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+: Toothpaste and words

About 5–10 minutes, with an adult.

1. Give the child a tube of toothpaste (one already in use) and a plate. Let them squeeze a little onto the plate.
2. Now try to put it back into the tube. Very hard, isn't it?
3. Ask: "How are words like toothpaste?" Hint: once they are out, they are hard to take back.
4. Think together of something the child can say after saying something unkind: "I'm sorry, I shouldn't have said that."

The adult can say: "Some of our Muslim friends, in their month of fasting, also try to guard their words: no lies, no saying unkind things about anyone."

Wash hands after playing. Do not let the child taste the toothpaste. Do not use this game to scold the child for past mistakes.

## 5. Stop practising if

- Watching your speech makes you blame yourself a lot, afraid to speak, or silent even when you need to speak up. This lesson does not ask you to stay quiet when you are treated unfairly; telling the truth calmly is not "backbiting".
- At work or at home, you cannot say what is right without being harmed. Then find someone you trust to turn to; see the daynentang lesson on connection below.
- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself. Stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
