**This week:** learn about the five daily prayers (Salah), the second pillar of Islam, as a rhythm that divides the Muslim day. Your own daily rhythm gains a third pause, in the evening.

**Measurable goal:** write the names of the five prayers and when each takes place in your own words; sit still for 10 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; pause three times a day (morning, midday, evening) on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Salah through the day (described)

[Traditional teaching] The tradition teaches that adult Muslims offer the Salah prayer five times a day, within time windows that follow the position of the sun. Prayer times therefore change with the season and the place.

| Prayer | Time window (general description) |
|---|---|
| Fajr | From dawn until before sunrise |
| Dhuhr | After the sun passes its highest point, midday |
| 'Asr | The afternoon |
| Maghrib | Just after sunset |
| 'Isha | The evening, once it is fully dark |

[Traditional teaching] Before praying, the worshipper washes certain parts of the body (*wudu*) and stands in a clean place, facing the Ka'bah in Makkah (*qiblah*). The prayer is made up of units (*rak'ah*); each unit includes standing and reciting the Qur'an (always including the Opening chapter, Al-Fatihah), bowing, prostrating with the forehead to the ground, and sitting. In many places the call to prayer (*adhan*) sounds from the mosque before each prayer.

The table and paragraph above are tamhonkhoe's short description, so that learners can grasp the main ideas. The details, the timings and the cases where the prayer is lightened or excused (the sick, travellers…) are set by Islamic law and differ in places between schools. The course does not teach how to pray.

### The two ends of the day

[Traditional teaching] The Qur'an does not list five prayer times in a single verse. Verse 114 of chapter 11 (Hud) is one of the verses that mention times of prayer in the day and night.

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 11:114
Establish worship at the two ends of the day and in some watches of the night. Lo! good deeds annul ill-deeds. This is reminder for the mindful.
:::

### Prayer and remembrance

[Traditional teaching] Verse 45 of chapter 29 (Al-'Ankabut) links prayer with moral life, and says that remembrance of Allah is "more important".

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 29:45
Recite that which hath been inspired in thee of the Scripture, and establish worship. Lo! worship preserveth from lewdness and iniquity, but verily remembrance of Allah is more important. And Allah knoweth what ye do.
:::

### The river at the door (hadith, summarised)

[Traditional teaching] As narrated by Abu Hurairah, the Prophet Muhammad asked: if there were a river at someone's door and they bathed in it five times a day, would any dirt remain on them? The people answered that none would. The Prophet said the five prayers are like this: through them Allah wipes away evil deeds.

This is tamhonkhoe's summary, not a translation, because the English on sunnah.com is in copyright. Read the full text at [sunnah.com, Sahih al-Bukhari 528](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:528).

### Reading in context

- Pickthall's translation, from Project Gutenberg #16955. Pickthall renders *salah* as "worship".
- [Expert judgement] The number five and the exact time of each prayer come from hadith and transmitted practice, not from any single verse of the Qur'an. Commentators link 11:114 with particular daily prayers in different ways.
- [Expert judgement] Sunni and Shi'a Muslims both keep the five prayers. Many Shi'a Muslims commonly combine Dhuhr with 'Asr and Maghrib with 'Isha, and so pray at three times of day. The course simply notes this difference, without comparing.
- [Inference] Both verses and the hadith tie prayer to conduct: good deeds annul ill-deeds, and prayer keeps one from wrongdoing. In this tradition, prayer is not separate from daily life.

## 2. What science finds

- Salah is a religious duty, not a health technique. The course does not present it as a way to be healthier, and does not use studies of the body during prayer.
- [Verified] The closest research to your pauses is on rest: a meta-analysis of 38,124 workers found that those who detach from work when off duty report less exhaustion and sleep better. This is a correlation. (Wendsche & Lohmann-Haislah 2017)
- [Inference] This research is about rest after work, not short pauses during the day. The course's exercise has not been tested on its own.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 22 minutes a day.

1. **Morning: slow reading (3 minutes).** On odd days read 11:114; on even days read 29:45. On one day in the week, read the summary of the hadith of the river.
2. **Sitting still (10 minutes).** As last week.
3. **Three pauses (1–2 minutes each).** Keep the morning and midday pauses and add an evening one, tied to a point of your own (getting home, after dinner). Each time: stop what your hands are doing, three slow breaths, one thing to be grateful for.
4. **Once in the week (10 minutes).** Write the names of the five prayers and when each takes place in your own words, then compare with the table.
5. **Evening: write one line (3 minutes).** "Which pause is easiest to keep? Which do I tend to forget?"

**Very important:** your pauses follow your own day, not prayer times, and there are only three of them. They involve no bowing or prostration, no facing a particular direction and no Arabic words. They are not Salah and are not meant to imitate Salah. Muslims keep Salah as their community teaches; the pauses do not replace prayer.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "My day clock"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Divide a sheet of paper into three boxes: morning, midday, evening.
2. In each box the child draws the sun where they see it (low on one side, high up, already set) and one thing they usually do at that time: breakfast, school, bath, reading.
3. The adult asks: "When in the day could we stop for a moment to say thank you?" The child marks one box.
4. The adult says: "Every day has a morning, a midday and an evening. Some families stop several times a day to remember what matters to them."

Draw the sun from memory or by looking out of the window; never look straight at the sun. Muslim families can tell their child about the family's prayer times. Other families just play the drawing game.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", several nights of poor sleep, or thoughts of harming yourself.
- Three pauses make you anxious about forgetting, or you feel you must do each step "correctly". Then keep just one pause; there is no right or wrong way to pause.
- Then stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
