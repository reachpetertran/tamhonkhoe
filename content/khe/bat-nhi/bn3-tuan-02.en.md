**This week:** reading the story of Indra learning from Prajapati about three states: waking, dreaming and deep sleep. Practising noticing the moment just after waking and the moment before sleep.

**Measurable goal:** sit for 30 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; each morning pause for one breath to notice "I am awake" before picking up your phone, on at least 5 of 7 mornings.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 8 of the Chandogya Upanishad tells how Prajapati (the creator god) spoke of a Self to be sought:

::: kinh upanishads-muller nguyen-van | Chandogya Upanishad VIII, 7, 1, p. 134
Pragâpati said: 'The Self which is free from sin, free from old age, from death and grief, from hunger and thirst, which desires nothing but what it ought to desire, and imagines nothing but what it ought to imagine, that it is which we must search out, that it is which we must try to understand. He who has searched out that Self and understands it, obtains all worlds and all desires.'
:::

Indra, king of the gods, comes to learn. The first time Prajapati points to the reflection of the body in a pan of water; Indra sees that the reflection changes with the body, and is not satisfied. The second time Prajapati says:

::: kinh upanishads-muller nguyen-van | Chandogya Upanishad VIII, 10, 1 (first sentence), p. 138
'He who moves about happy in dreams, he is the Self, this is the immortal, the fearless, this is Brahman.'
:::

Again Indra sees a difficulty: in dreams one is still as if struck, as if chased, and still weeps. The third time:

::: kinh upanishads-muller nguyen-van | Chandogya Upanishad VIII, 11, 1, p. 140
'When a man being asleep, reposing, and at perfect rest sees no dreams, that is the Self, this is the immortal, the fearless, this is Brahman.'

Then Indra went away satisfied in his heart. But before he had returned to the Devas, he saw this difficulty. In truth he thus does not know himself (his self) that he is I, nor does he know anything that exists. He is gone to utter annihilation. I see no good in this.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Traditional teaching] The story goes step by step: the body (the reflection in the water), the one in the dream, the one in deep sleep. Each time Indra himself sees that the answer is not enough and comes back to ask. In the last step (VIII, 12), Prajapati speaks of "the one who knows": the one who knows "let me smell this", "let me hear this". We read that passage in Week 4.
- [Inference] What is worth learning from Indra is his attitude: not quickly satisfied, not giving up either; asking again when something does not fit.
- [Expert judgement] The Mandukya Upanishad also teaches about waking, dreaming, deep sleep and a fourth state. The course does not quote the Mandukya because tamhonkhoe has not yet found a public-domain translation with a checked licence.
- This week's exercise is not "lucid dreaming" practice and does not ask you to stay up late or sleep less. Enough sleep is part of the practice.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] An interview study of Western Buddhist meditators and teachers recorded 59 kinds of meditation-related experience in 7 domains, including "sense of self"; impact ranged from minimal and passing to severe and lasting. (Lindahl et al. 2017)
- [Inference] That study is about Buddhist meditation, not Upanishadic self-inquiry, and it gives no rates. But it shows that changes in the sense of "me" are real and sometimes unpleasant. That is why the course goes slowly, has a back-to-the-ground step, and does not practise during sleep.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 35 minutes a day.

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read the three passages.
2. **Sit (25 minutes).** 5 minutes with the breath. Then 20 minutes as in Week 1: "this is known". Add one step: now and then recall the moment you woke up this morning, and notice that right now, too, there is the same knowing.
3. **Back to the ground (5 minutes).** Open your eyes, feel your feet, name three things, take a sip of water, stretch.
4. **Two moments of passing (1 minute each).** Morning: just awake, pause for one breath and notice "awake", before picking up your phone. Evening: lying down, three slow breaths, doing nothing more, then sleep.
5. **Write one line.** Did you remember the one-breath pause this morning?

If the evening step makes it harder to fall asleep, drop it and keep only the morning step.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Eyes closed, eyes open"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The child closes their eyes and counts three sounds (the fan, a bird, a car).
2. The child opens their eyes and finds three colours in the room.
3. Ask: "With your eyes closed, what could you still know?" The child might say: "I could still hear, I still knew I was sitting."

Do not ask about the child's dreams unless they bring them up.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The question about "I" leaves you feeling unmoored, as if you had nowhere to stand, or a sense of being outside your body, watching yourself from outside, stays after you stand up. Then stop the exercise, do the "back to the ground" step, talk to someone you trust, and for the next few days only sit for 20 minutes with the breath. If the feeling lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- Your sleep gets worse, or you start to dread going to sleep. Drop the evening step and go back to Week 1.
- You start thinking that this body, this life or the people close to you "do not matter", or you skip meals, sleep, work or time with loved ones to sit longer.
- If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before doing exercises longer than 20 minutes.
- If the signs are serious, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
