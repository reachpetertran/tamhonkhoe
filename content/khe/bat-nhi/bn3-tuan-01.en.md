**Before you start this course:** this is a Level 3 course, 30–45 minutes a day for 6 weeks, best taken after [Non-duality 1](/en/courses/non-duality-1/) and [Non-duality 2](/en/courses/non-duality-2/). The question "who am I?" can leave some people feeling shaken, as if they or the world were "not real". If you are depressed, recently bereaved, going through a major upheaval, or having thoughts of wanting to die or harm yourself, this course is not for now: see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/) and talk to someone you trust. People who have had psychosis, bipolar disorder, severe PTSD, or a lasting sense of being detached from themselves should not study this course alone; if you want to, ask your doctor and work with an experienced guide.

### What this course is

- It describes how the Upanishads ask about the Self (Atman), and the "Who am I?" inquiry of Ramana Maharshi (1879–1950), a 20th-century Indian teacher (Week 4). You do not need to be Hindu, and the course does not ask anyone to change their beliefs or to be devoted to anyone.
- The section name "Non-duality" comes from one reading, Advaita Vedanta. Other schools, such as those of Ramanuja and Madhva, read these same passages differently. The course takes no side.
- It does not teach ritual, mantras or Sanskrit, does not discuss death or rebirth, and does not ask you to believe that the world is "not real".

**This week:** reading the "No, no!" of the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, then practising noticing that whatever you know (sounds, sensations, thoughts) is "something known".

**Measurable goal:** sit for 30 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; each evening note the one "something known" you noticed most clearly that day, on at least 5 of 7 evenings.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad is one of the oldest Upanishads. In its second chapter, after many images of Brahman (ultimate reality), the text closes like this:

::: kinh upanishads-muller-15 nguyen-van | Brihadaranyaka Upanishad II, 3, 6 (last paragraph), p. 108
Next follows the teaching (of Brahman) by No, no! for there is nothing else higher than this (if one says): 'It is not so.' Then comes the name 'the True of the True,' the senses being the True, and he (the Brahman) the True of them.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Inference] "No, no!" is Müller's rendering of the Sanskrit *neti neti*, often read as "not this, not this". The main point: no image and no word says all of Brahman, so the text teaches by setting each name aside.
- [Expert judgement] In the Advaita reading, the practitioner applies the same setting-aside to themselves: body, sensations and thoughts are things that are known, so they are not yet the knower. This week only practises noticing that for a few minutes, without needing to conclude anything.
- "Not this" does **not** mean that your body, your feelings or other people do not matter. The body still needs food, sleep and care; feelings still need to be listened to. If you find yourself reading it that way, stop and read this section again.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] The critical review by Bernstein and colleagues (2015) describes "decentering" as three processes: meta-awareness of what you are experiencing, disidentification from internal experience, and reduced reactivity to the content of thoughts. It is a conceptual analysis, not a trial of effectiveness. (Bernstein et al. 2015)
- [Verified] A review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Inference] This week's exercise is close to the "disidentification" process Bernstein describes, but that is a psychological framework, not the teaching of the Upanishads. No study has evaluated this course. Farias's figures are the reason the course has a "back to the ground" step and a "Stop practising if" section.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day.

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read the passage and the "Reading it in context" section.
2. **Sit with the breath (5 minutes).** Sit as in Non-duality 2, Week 3: body, neck and head straight, eyes lowered. Know the breath coming in and going out.
3. **"This is known" (15 minutes).** Notice in turn: sounds, then sensations in the body, then feelings, then thoughts. With each one you notice, say very softly to yourself: "this is known", then leave it there, not pushing it away, not holding on. You do not need to answer "then who knows?". Just notice and leave it be.
4. **Back to the ground (5 minutes).** Open your eyes, feel your feet on the floor, name three things you can see, take a sip of water, stand up and stretch.
5. **Write one line.** Which "something known" did you notice most clearly today? For example: "a motorbike in the lane", "tension in my shoulders", "a thought about tomorrow's meeting".

Do not sit through panic or strange feelings by force. The sitting is not an endurance test.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "The mystery bag"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Put 4–5 safe objects into a cloth bag: an orange, a wooden spoon, an eraser, a soft cloth, a Lego brick.
2. The child reaches in without looking and guesses each one. Ask: "How do you know it is round? With your eyes or with your hands?"
3. Swap: the parent guesses, the child holds the bag.

The game is only practice in noticing the senses. Do not ask the child "who are you?".

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The question about "I" leaves you feeling unmoored, as if you had nowhere to stand, or a sense of being outside your body, watching yourself from outside, stays after you stand up. Then stop the exercise, do the "back to the ground" step, talk to someone you trust, and for the next few days only sit for 20 minutes with the breath. If the feeling lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- You start thinking that this body, this life or the people close to you "do not matter", or you skip meals, sleep, work or time with loved ones to sit longer.
- If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before doing exercises longer than 20 minutes.
- If the signs are serious, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
