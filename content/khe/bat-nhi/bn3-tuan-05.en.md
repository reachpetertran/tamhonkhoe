**This week:** reading the image of the two birds in the Mundaka Upanishad. The longest practice of the course: 40–45 minutes, which can be split into two sessions.

**Measurable goal:** practise for 40–45 minutes (in one or two sessions) on at least 5 of 7 days; each day rate how "unmoored" you feel (0–10) and note one moment when you "watched like the second bird".

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Mundaka Upanishad uses an image also found in the Rig Veda and the Shvetashvatara Upanishad:

::: kinh upanishads-muller-15 nguyen-van | Mundaka Upanishad III, 1, 1–2, p. 38
1. Two birds, inseparable friends, cling to the same tree. One of them eats the sweet fruit, the other looks on without eating.

2. On the same tree man sits grieving, immersed, bewildered by his own impotence (an-îsâ). But when he sees the other lord (îsa) contented and knows his glory, then his grief passes away.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Inference] The bird eating the fruit is a person tasting the joys and sorrows of life; the bird looking on is what only witnesses, without being swept along.
- [Expert judgement] The schools read "the other lord" differently. Advaita usually reads the second bird as the Self, the witness. Theistic schools such as those of Ramanuja and Madhva read it as the Supreme Lord, distinct from the individual soul yet always beside it. The course takes no side.
- "Looks on without eating" does not mean stopping eating, stopping living or stopping caring. This week's exercise is to do ordinary things while also, a little, seeing what you are doing.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A review of 83 studies: about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Verified] An interview study of Western Buddhist meditators and teachers: 59 kinds of experience in 7 domains, including "sense of self"; impact ranged from minimal and passing to severe and lasting; 26 categories of influencing factors. (Lindahl et al. 2017)
- [Inference] Longer practice raises both benefits and risks. So this week allows two sessions, includes slow walking, and keeps the back-to-the-ground step. No study has evaluated this course.

## 3. Practice for adults

40–45 minutes in total each day. You can split it into two sessions: morning (steps 1–3) and evening (steps 4–6).

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read the passage.
2. **Sit with the breath (10 minutes).**
3. **Inquiry (15–20 minutes).** As in Week 4. The first three days 15 minutes; from day four 20 minutes if your "unmoored" rating is below 7.
4. **Slow walking as "the second bird" (10 minutes).** Walk slowly in a room or yard, knowing the foot lifting, moving, placing. Now and then notice: "there is something knowing the walking".
5. **Back to the ground (5 minutes).** Open your eyes, feel your feet, name three things, take a sip of water, stretch.
6. **Write one line.** Rate how "unmoored" you feel, 0–10. Note one moment in the day when you "watched like the second bird", for example: "stuck in traffic and annoyed, I saw the annoyance and didn't shout".

Do not sit through panic or strange feelings by force. If 45 minutes is too long, keep to 35 minutes as in Week 4.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Two little birds"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Together, draw a tree with two little birds: one eating a fruit, one watching.
2. Act it out: one person is the "eating bird" and slowly eats a piece of fruit; the other is the "watching bird" and describes it: "Dad is biting, chewing, smiling."
3. Swap roles.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- During long sittings you feel as if you are outside your body, watching yourself from outside, or everything becomes empty and strange, and the feeling stays after you stand up. Then stop the inquiry, do the "back to the ground" step, talk to someone you trust, and for the next few days only sit for 20 minutes with the breath. If the feeling lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- Your "unmoored" rating is 7 or more two sittings in a row.
- You start thinking that this body, this life or the people close to you "do not matter", or you skip meals, sleep, work or time with loved ones to sit longer.
- If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before doing exercises longer than 20 minutes.
- If the signs are serious, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
