**This week:** a slow reading of the Gita passage saying that heat and cold, sorrow and joy all come and go, and practice in watching one small discomfort until it passes.

**Measurable goal:** each day note one small discomfort, when it began and roughly how long it took to pass or ease; on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Context

[Traditional teaching] At the start of chapter 2, Arjuna weeps at having to face his own relatives on the battlefield. Krishna begins his teaching by distinguishing what changes from what does not: the body passes through childhood, youth and old age, while the soul, according to the Gita, is neither born nor dies. Straight after comes the passage on feelings that come and go.

::: kinh gita-arnold nguyen-van | chapter II (the lines corresponding to verse 2:14)
This that irks--
Thy sense-life, thrilling to the elements--
Bringing thee heat and cold, sorrows and joys,
'Tis brief and mutable! Bear with it, Prince!
As the wise bear.
:::

::: kinh gita-arnold nguyen-van | chapter II (the lines corresponding to verse 2:15)
The soul which is not moved,
The soul that with a strong and constant calm
Takes sorrow and takes joy indifferently,
Lives in the life undying!
:::

A note on context: [Inference] In the Gita this verse is tied to the teaching on the undying soul; "bear with it" here means not letting passing sensations decide what you do. It does not say to put up with everything. Within the Gita itself, the point of steadiness is to act rightly, not to stand still before what is wrong. "Takes sorrow and takes joy indifferently" does not mean you may not feel sad or glad either; on a common reading, the steady person still feels, but is not carried away by feeling. Both passages begin in the middle of a printed line in Arnold's text.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of controlled studies found that trying to suppress a thought makes it come back more often, by a small-to-moderate amount; the effect varied with the kind of thought and how it was measured. (Abramowitz, Tolin & Street 2001)
- [Verified] 39 randomised trials, 1,821 people: Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), which includes practice in letting discomfort be there without fighting it, did better than control conditions (g = 0.57), with no clear difference from cognitive behavioural therapy. (A-Tjak et al. 2015)
- [Inference] ACT is guided therapy, measured in people with health problems; the research does not measure the Gita's teaching. The lesson uses only small, everyday discomforts.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10–12 minutes a day.

1. **Read slowly (2 minutes).** Read the two Gita passages once, then read the line "'Tis brief and mutable!" again.
2. **Choose one small feeling in your day.** For example: waiting at a red light, a stuffy room, an itch, boredom in a queue, irritation at a slow reply.
3. **Watch it (2–5 minutes).** Note the time it began. Name it ("hot", "impatient"). Notice where it sits in the body, how strong it is, how it changes. You do not need to make it go away, nor to force yourself to stay with it.
4. **Write it down (2 minutes):** what the feeling was, how strong out of 10 at first, roughly how long until it eased or passed.
5. **End of the week (5 minutes).** Read your 5–7 notes again. Did any feeling last longer than you expected? Did any pass more quickly?

Practise only with discomforts that are small, safe and pass on their own. If the discomfort signals something to do (too hot, hungry, thirsty, in pain), do it.

**Very important:** "Bear with it" in this lesson does not apply to pain, symptoms of illness, danger, or someone harming you. Those need care, treatment or stopping, not watching until they pass.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Soap bubbles"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The adult blows a few soap bubbles. The child watches one bubble from the moment it floats out until it pops, without touching it.
2. Count together: how many seconds did this bubble float?
3. Tell the child: "Feelings are a bit like bubbles. Cross, hot, impatient: they come and then they go."
4. Ask: "Did you have a feeling today that came and went?" Ask only about small things (waiting for a turn, being hot, being bored).

If the child is very sad or scared, do not play this game; hug them and stay close. The child's feelings need to be heard, not only "waited out".

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- You start using the exercise to ignore pain, symptoms of illness or a real danger. If so, see a doctor or get help.
- A feeling does not pass but keeps getting stronger, or you notice you are trying to "feel nothing".
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
