**This week:** reading Yajnavalkya's words to his wife Maitreyi, then looking back over the five weeks of practice. Sitting comes back down to 30 minutes.

**Measurable goal:** sit for 30 minutes with the exercise of your choice on at least 5 of 7 days; at the end of the week rate the course's five exercises, keep one or two, and write a 4-week plan of 20–30 minutes a day.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Before leaving home for the forest, Yajnavalkya wants to divide his property between his two wives. Maitreyi asks: if she had the whole earth full of wealth, would that make her immortal? He says no, then explains that people love a husband, a wife, children and everything else for the sake of the Self. His teaching closes like this:

::: kinh upanishads-muller-15 nguyen-van | Brihadaranyaka Upanishad II, 4, 5 (last sentences), p. 110
'Verily, the Self is to be seen, to be heard, to be perceived, to be marked, O Maitreyî! When we see, hear, perceive, and know the Self, then all this is known.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Inference] The Advaita tradition reads this sentence as three stages of learning: hearing the teaching (*śravaṇa*), reflecting on it (*manana*), and deep meditation until it sinks in (*nididhyāsana*). This course follows the same rhythm: Weeks 1–3 are mostly hearing and reflecting, Weeks 4–5 are meditation.
- Maitreyi chooses to ask about what wealth cannot buy. That question is worth keeping, but it does not mean you must leave home or work. The passage tells of a way of life of its time; the course does not suggest anyone follow it.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 138 randomised studies, 19,951 people: interventions that help people monitor their progress made them more likely to reach their goals, by a small-to-medium amount (d = 0.40); the effect was larger when progress was physically recorded or reported to others. (Harkin et al. 2016)
- [Verified] 96 people each chose a small daily action for 12 weeks (82 with enough data): it became automatic after 18 to 254 days depending on the person; missing one occasion did not materially affect this. Small study, self-reported. (Lally et al. 2010)
- [Inference] These two studies are about habits and goals in general, not self-inquiry. They suggest that recording your practice and keeping one small steady habit helps more than trying to do a lot.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 35 minutes a day.

1. **Read slowly (2 minutes).** Read the passage.
2. **Sit (25 minutes).** Choose the exercise that suited you best over the past five weeks: "this is known" (Week 1), noticing the moments of passing (Week 2), listening to sounds like salt in water (Week 3), inquiry (Week 4), or slow walking as "the second bird" (Week 5). If you choose inquiry, keep it to 20 minutes at most.
3. **Back to the ground (5 minutes).** Open your eyes, feel your feet, name three things, take a sip of water, stretch.
4. **End of the week: rate (15 minutes, once).** For each of the five exercises, give two scores from 0 to 10: "helps me feel calmer and clearer" and "leaves me shaken". Keep one or two exercises with a high first score and a low second score.
5. **A 4-week plan.** 20–30 minutes a day with the exercise you kept, one line written each evening. If you want to practise more than 45 minutes a day or go on a long retreat, find an experienced guide; do not increase it on your own.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Five senses"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Draw five boxes: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, hands.
2. Walk once around the house or yard. Find one thing for each box: a nice colour, a lovely sound, a good smell, a tasty flavour, something soft.
3. Ask the child which game from the past weeks they would like to play again: the mystery bag, eyes closed and open, salt in water, the bell, the two birds.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The question about "I" leaves you feeling unmoored, as if you had nowhere to stand, or a sense of being outside your body, watching yourself from outside, stays after you stand up. Then stop the exercise, do the "back to the ground" step, talk to someone you trust, and for the next few days only sit for 20 minutes with the breath. If the feeling lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- You start thinking that this body, this life or the people close to you "do not matter", or you skip meals, sleep, work or time with loved ones to sit longer.
- If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before doing exercises longer than 20 minutes.
- If the signs are serious, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
