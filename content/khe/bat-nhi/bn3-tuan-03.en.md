**This week:** reading the lesson of salt in water, one of the best-known passages of the Upanishads. Taking the practice out of the sitting: three pauses a day in ordinary tasks.

**Measurable goal:** sit for 30 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; each day pause for one breath three times in ordinary tasks to notice "knowing", on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 6 of the Chandogya Upanishad is a conversation between a father, Uddalaka, and his son Svetaketu. Several lessons in a row end with the same sentence. This is one of them:

::: kinh upanishads-muller nguyen-van | Chandogya Upanishad VI, 13, 1–3, pp. 104–105
'Place this salt in water, and then wait on me in the morning.'

The son did as he was commanded.

The father said to him: 'Bring me the salt, which you placed in the water last night.'

The son having looked for it, found it not, for, of course, it was melted.

The father said: 'Taste it from the surface of the water. How is it?'

The son replied: 'It is salt.'

'Taste it from the middle. How is it?'

The son replied: 'It is salt.'

'Taste it from the bottom. How is it?'

The son replied: 'It is salt.'

The father said: 'Throw it away and then wait on me.'

He did so; but salt exists for ever.

Then the father said: 'Here also, in this body, forsooth, you do not perceive the True (Sat), my son; but there indeed it is.

'That which is the subtile essence, in it all that exists has its self. It is the True. It is the Self, and thou, O Svetaketu, art it.'

'Please, Sir, inform me still more,' said the son.

'Be it so, my child,' the father replied.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Inference] "Thou art it" is Müller's rendering of *tat tvam asi*, often given as "you are that". The salt cannot be seen but can be tasted everywhere; the father uses that image to speak of the True, unseen yet present everywhere.
- [Expert judgement] The Vedanta schools read this sentence differently. Advaita reads it as "you and Brahman are one". Ramanuja reads it as you belonging to Brahman as a body belongs to its soul, inseparable but not identical. Madhva reads it as keeping a difference between you and the Supreme. The course takes no side and goes no further into this.
- The lesson is not praise or blame of anyone. Svetaketu at the start of the chapter is a rather proud student; his father teaches with a small experiment and patiently answers each "please, tell me more".

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 47 randomised trials, 3,515 people: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; not better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Inference] No study has measured the effect of reflecting on "you are that". Goyal's analysis only shows that meditation in general has small benefits; it neither proves nor disproves what the Upanishads teach about the True. Science and the traditional teaching answer two different questions here.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 35 minutes a day.

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read the passage, pausing at the three "It is salt".
2. **Sit (25 minutes).** 5 minutes with the breath. Then 20 minutes listening to sounds: near, far, loud, soft. As the salt is at the top, the middle and the bottom, notice that in every sound there is knowing. You do not need to come up with an answer to "what is knowing?".
3. **Back to the ground (5 minutes).** Open your eyes, feel your feet, name three things, take a sip of water, stretch.
4. **Three pauses in the day (one breath each).** Link them to three things you already do, for example: washing your hands, opening the car door, sitting down to eat. Pause for one breath, notice "knowing", then carry on.
5. **Write one line.** How many of the three pauses did you manage today?

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Where did the salt go?"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Show the child a small pinch of salt in a spoon. Drop it into a glass of clear water and stir.
2. Ask: "Where is the salt?" The child looks: it cannot be seen.
3. With a clean spoon, the adult gives the child one drop of water from the top to taste, then one from the middle. "Salty or plain?"
4. Tell the child: "Some things we cannot see are still there."

Use only a pinch of salt and let the child taste just a drop; do not let them drink salt water. Pour the water away afterwards.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The question about "I" leaves you feeling unmoored, as if you had nowhere to stand, or a sense of being outside your body, watching yourself from outside, stays after you stand up. Then stop the exercise, do the "back to the ground" step, talk to someone you trust, and for the next few days only sit for 20 minutes with the breath. If the feeling lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- You start thinking that this body, this life or the people close to you "do not matter", or you skip meals, sleep, work or time with loved ones to sit longer.
- If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before doing exercises longer than 20 minutes.
- If the signs are serious, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
