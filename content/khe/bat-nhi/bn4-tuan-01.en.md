**This week:** the last course in the Non-duality section begins. The three earlier courses practised sitting still and slow reading; this one carries attention into things everyone does: washing up, cooking, going to work, caring for family. Week 1 reads the Gita passage that says every act can be done as an offering.

**Measurable goal:** each day choose one small household task (5–10 minutes), say a one-sentence intention silently before starting and do the whole task without your phone, on at least 5 of 7 days; write one line each evening.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### What this course is and is not

- A Level 4 (Integration) course, 6 weeks, with no fixed sitting time: about 15 minutes a day of reading, one ordinary task and a short note. If you want to keep sitting as well, keep the sitting schedule from [Non-duality 2](/en/courses/non-duality-2/).
- It describes what the Bhagavad Gita and the Isha Upanishad teach about daily work. Learners do not need to be Hindu, and the course does not ask anyone to be devoted to anyone. Hindu learners can follow the way of their own teacher and family.
- The section name "Non-duality" comes from one reading (Advaita Vedanta). Other schools, such as those of Ramanuja or Madhva, read the same passages differently; the course takes no side.

### Every act as an offering

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 9 of the Gita is about devotion. Near the end of the chapter Krishna says he accepts even the simplest offerings, as long as they are given sincerely, and then widens this to every act of the day.

::: kinh gita-arnold nguyen-van | chapter IX (the lines corresponding to verses 9:26–27)
Whoso shall offer Me in faith and love
A leaf, a flower, a fruit, water poured forth,
That offering I accept, lovingly made
With pious will. Whate'er thou doest, Prince!
Eating or sacrificing, giving gifts,
Praying or fasting, let it all be done
For Me, as Mine.
:::

A note on context: [Inference] "Me" here is Krishna. Leaves, flowers, fruit and water are common offerings in Hindu worship; the verse says the value lies in the sincerity, not in whether the thing is costly or cheap. The next verse widens this from offerings to every act: eating, giving, praying. "Fasting" in the passage is only one item in the list; this course does not suggest fasting.

[Expert judgement] Devotional readers take "for Me" as offering one's work to Krishna. Readers in the Non-dual tradition often take it as working without putting the "I" at the centre. The lesson does not choose between them; it uses only what they share: even a small task can be done fully and sincerely.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A review of 47 randomised trials with 3,515 people: mindfulness meditation programmes (usually around 8 weeks) reduced anxiety, depression and pain by small to moderate amounts, but were not better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Inference] This research measured meditation programmes with sitting practice, not doing housework with an attitude of "offering". No study has evaluated this week's exercise. The lesson promises nothing about health; it only helps you notice a task you already do every day.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day, inside ordinary life.

1. **Read slowly (3 minutes).** Read the passage twice. Pause at "lovingly made With pious will".
2. **Choose one task (day 1).** A small household task you do every day, 5–10 minutes: washing up, wiping the table, folding clothes, watering plants, making tea. Keep the same task all week.
3. **One sentence of intention (10 seconds).** Before you start, say one sentence silently. If you follow a religion, use the words of your own tradition. If not, you might say: "I will do this fully, for whoever will use it." This sentence is tamhonkhoe's suggestion, not a scripture.
4. **Do the whole task (5–10 minutes).** Put the phone away and turn off any programme you were watching. Notice your hands, the water, the sounds, the smells. When your mind runs off, notice it and come back to the bowl or the cloth in your hand.
5. **Write one line in the evening:** "Today I … ; while doing it I noticed …".

There is no need to move slowly for show or to do it perfectly. The bowl may still have a mark on it; just be there while you do it.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "A small gift for the family"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The child chooses a small, safe task: lining up shoes at the door, setting out chopsticks, watering one plant.
2. Before starting, the child says: "I'm doing this as a present for the family."
3. The child does it very carefully; the adult does another small task right beside them.
4. Afterwards, look together: "Who will be happy because of this?"

No scores and no criticism. Do not give children tasks with knives, the stove or hot water.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The idea of "offering" makes you feel you must do all the housework alone, or guilty whenever you rest. Offering one task does not mean carrying every task; share the work with the people you live with.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have ever had psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask your doctor before sitting in meditation for longer than 20 minutes.
