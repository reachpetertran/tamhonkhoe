**This week:** read three passages that sum up the spirit of the course. Rate the five habits practised over the past five weeks, choose two to keep, and write a four-week plan.

**Measurable goal:** do your two chosen habits on at least 5 of 7 days; complete the rating table and the four-week plan.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Advice to Sigālaka ends with verses on four ways of holding people together, which the Buddhist tradition often calls the "four bases of inclusion": giving, kindly words, taking care, and treating people equally. The discourse compares them to the linchpin that keeps a wheel on a chariot. The Advice to Rāhula ends with a short instruction. The discourse with Dīghajāṇu ends with verses that sum up lay life.

::: kinh dn31-sujato nguyen-van | DN 31, sections 34.21–34.26
Giving and kindly words,
taking care here,
and treating equally in worldly conditions,
as they deserve in each case;
these ways of being inclusive in the world
are like a moving chariot’s linchpin.
:::

::: kinh mn61-sujato nguyen-van | MN 61, section 18.4
So Rāhula, you should train yourself like this: ‘I will purify my physical, verbal, and mental actions after repeatedly checking.’”
:::

::: kinh an8-54-sujato nguyen-van | AN 8.54, sections 15.1–16.4
They’re enterprising in the workplace,
diligent in managing things,
they balance their finances,
and preserve their wealth.

Faithful, accomplished in ethics,
bountiful, rid of stinginess,
they always purify the path
to well-being in lives to come.
:::

### Reading it in context

- The four ways in DN 31 echo the five ways of treating friends in section 31.2 (giving, kindly words, taking care, equality, not using tricky words). The earlier weeks practised them one by one.
- "Treating equally… as they deserve in each case" does not mean treating everyone exactly the same; it means not playing favourites and not looking down on anyone.
- "Lives to come" belongs to the Buddhist belief in rebirth. Learners do not need to accept that belief to keep the habits of the course.
- This course quotes only a small part of the teachings for lay people. Anyone who wants to go deeper should study with a teacher in the tradition.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that directly evaluates a programme based on the discourses in this course.
- [Verified] A 2018 meta-analysis (27 experiments, 4,045 people): doing acts of kindness for others raises the doer's well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
- [Inference] Keeping a few habits and practising them regularly is usually easier than keeping many at once; this is the author's judgement, not a research finding.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the three passages.
2. **Rate yourself (once, at the start of the week, 15 minutes).** Score each habit 0–3 (0: didn't do it; 3: did it regularly and found it useful):

   | Habit | Week | Score |
   |---|---|---|
   | One small thing for one "direction" | 1 | |
   | One task seen through; staying close to a good friend | 2 | |
   | Checking three times in the mirror | 3 | |
   | One kind word to someone close | 4 | |
   | Tracking money; giving once | 5 | |

3. **Choose two habits.** One that is easy (a high score) and one you feel you need (even if its score is low).
4. **Write a four-week plan.** For each habit: when, where, and how you will note it. For example: "After dinner, say one specific thank-you to my partner; note it before bed."
5. **Each evening (3 minutes).** Tick whether you did your two habits.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "The wheel's linchpin"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Take a toy car. Show the child where the wheel joins the axle. Ask: "What happens if the wheel comes off?"
2. Explain that four things keep people together like wheels on a car: giving, kind words, helping, and not playing favourites.
3. The child draws four wheels, and on each writes or draws one thing they did this week.
4. Put the picture up on the wall.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The rating table makes you blame yourself. A low score only shows which habit doesn't fit right now; it says nothing about your worth.
- If there are serious warning signs, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
