**This course:** Level 4, "Integration". There is no long sitting; each day you read for a few minutes, then carry the exercise into housework, work and relationships. Best taken after Buddhism 1, but not required.

**This week:** read the last part of a discourse the Buddha gave to a young lay person. Draw your own "six directions", then do one small thing for one direction each day.

**Measurable goal:** do and note one small thing for one "direction" each day, on at least 5 of 7 days; by the end of the week, reach at least 4 of the 6 directions.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Advice to Sigālaka (Sigālaka Sutta, Long Discourses 31) tells of a young man, Sigālaka, who goes out of the city early in the morning, hair and clothes wet, and raises his joined palms to the six directions because his father asked him to before he died. The Buddha does not tell him to drop his father's words. He shows him another way to "revere the six directions": each direction is a group of people in his life, and every relationship has duties on both sides. The Buddhist tradition often calls this discourse "the lay person's code of discipline".

::: kinh dn31-sujato nguyen-van | DN 31, sections 27.1–27.3
“And how, householder’s son, does a noble disciple cover the six directions? These six directions should be recognized: parents as the east, tutors as the south, partner and children as the west, friends and colleagues as the north, bondservants and workers as beneath, and ascetics and brahmins as above.
:::

::: kinh dn31-sujato nguyen-van | DN 31, sections 28.1–28.4
A child should serve their parents as the eastern quarter in five ways, thinking: ‘I will support those who supported me. I’ll do my duty for them. I’ll maintain the family lineage. I’ll take care of the inheritance. When they have passed away, I’ll make an offering on their behalf.’ Parents served by the children in these five ways show sympathy to them in five ways. They keep them from doing bad. They support them in doing good. They train them in a profession. They connect them with a suitable partner. They transfer the inheritance in due time.
:::

::: kinh dn31-sujato nguyen-van | DN 31, sections 32.1–32.4
A master should serve their bondservants and workers as the lower quarter in five ways: by organizing work according to ability, by paying food and wages, by nursing them when sick, by sharing special treats, and by giving time off work. Bondservants and workers served by a master in these five ways show sympathy to him in five ways. They get up first, and go to bed last. They don’t steal. They do their work well. And they promote a good reputation.
:::

### Reading it in context

- The main point of the discourse: duties go both ways. A child serves their parents, and parents also have five things to do for the child. Workers serve their employer, and the employer also has five things to do for the workers.
- "Bondservants" (dāsa) in ancient Indian society served in a household and were sometimes not free to leave. The discourse sets out an employer's duties within that society; this course does not treat it as a model to follow. Today you can read this passage as how to treat the people you hire, work with or are served by.
- Duty does not mean putting up with harm. If someone in one of your "directions" has harmed you or is harming you, you do not need to choose that direction for the exercise.
- The start of the discourse says that someone who keeps these things is reborn in a good place (section 3.3), and section 28 includes offerings for parents who have died. This course presents the practice; learners do not need to accept the belief in rebirth.
- The part on husband and wife (section 30) is kept for Week 4; friends, for Week 2; money, for Week 5.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that directly evaluates a "six directions" exercise.
- [Verified] A 2018 meta-analysis (27 experiments, 4,045 people): doing acts of kindness for others raises the doer's well-being by a small-to-moderate amount; the studies are often small. (Curry et al. 2018)
- [Inference] This week's exercise is a kind of "intentional kindness" aimed at the people close to you. The result above does not prove that this exercise works; it only shows the direction is not at odds with the current evidence.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus one small action during the day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the three passages. You can reread them every day.
2. **Draw the six directions (once, at the start of the week, 10 minutes).** Draw a circle and divide it into six parts: parents (or whoever raised you); teachers and those who taught you a trade; your partner and children; friends and colleagues; people who work for you or with you (junior colleagues, a home helper, a delivery driver); people who lead a spiritual life or whom you respect for their inner life. Write one or two names in each part. Leaving a part empty is fine.
3. **One direction a day.** Choose one part and do one small thing in the spirit of the five ways the discourse lists. For example: call your parents to ask how they are; send a thank-you message to someone who taught you; give a colleague work that fits their ability; pay on time; let a co-worker leave on time.
4. **One line in the evening (3 minutes).** Which direction, what you did, how it felt.
5. **End of the week.** Look at the circle again: which direction do you tend to forget? Which one do you mostly receive from and rarely give to?

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "My six directions"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Draw a flower with six petals. On each petal, the child draws or writes one person: parents, grandparents, a teacher, a close friend, a brother or sister, the school guard or cleaner.
2. Ask: "What has this person done for you?" The child tells one thing.
3. The child picks one petal and thinks of a small thing to do for that person this week: saying thank you, drawing a card, helping clear the table.
4. At the end of the week, colour in the petal they did.

No scoring, no comparing with brothers or sisters.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Thinking about a "direction" brings back an old hurt, for example with a parent or a former employer. Then leave that direction out and choose another.
- You feel you must carry every duty to everyone and become exhausted. One small thing a day is enough.
- If there are serious warning signs, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
