**This week:** read the opening of the Discourse on Mindfulness of Breathing (MN 118) slowly, then sit for 10 minutes a day, just knowing the in-breath and the out-breath. For the first two days, 5 minutes is fine.

**Measurable goal:** sit with the breath for 10 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Ānāpānassati Sutta belongs to the Middle Discourses (Majjhima Nikāya), one of the collections of the Pali canon. Buddhism teaches that mindfulness of breathing is a practice of great benefit, which can lead step by step to insight and liberation. The discourse describes 16 steps; this week we read only the opening and the first four steps, which rest on the body.

::: kinh mn118-sujato nguyen-van | MN 118, sections 15.1 and 17.1–17.2
Mendicants, when mindfulness of breathing is developed and cultivated it is very fruitful and beneficial.

It’s when a mendicant—gone to a wilderness, or to the root of a tree, or to an empty hut—sits down cross-legged, sets their body straight, and brings mindfulness to the present.
Just mindful, they breathe in. Mindful, they breathe out.
:::

::: kinh mn118-sujato nguyen-van | MN 118, sections 18.1–18.4 (the first four steps)
Breathing in heavily they know: ‘I’m breathing in heavily.’ Breathing out heavily they know: ‘I’m breathing out heavily.’
Breathing in lightly they know: ‘I’m breathing in lightly.’ Breathing out lightly they know: ‘I’m breathing out lightly.’
They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in experiencing the whole body.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out experiencing the whole body.’
They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe in stilling the physical process.’ They practice like this: ‘I’ll breathe out stilling the physical process.’
:::

### Reading it in context

- The discourse was given to a large gathering of monks (people who had left home) on a full-moon night. Lay people in this tradition also practise mindfulness of breathing, usually at home or at a temple; there is no need to go into the forest.
- The Pali words in steps 1–2 are *dīgha* and *rassa*, literally "long" and "short"; many translations say "breathing long, breathing short". Bhikkhu Sujato renders them "heavily" and "lightly". Both point to the same thing: knowing the breath as it is, without fixing it.
- Steps 3–4 use the word "practise" (sikkhati): here the meditator takes a more active part, feeling the whole body and letting it settle.
- [Traditional teaching] The later steps of the discourse move on to feelings, the mind and impermanence and, in this tradition, lead to liberation. That part is kept for the course Buddhism 2. This course presents the practice; learners do not need to accept the tradition’s beliefs.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A 2014 systematic review pooled 47 randomised trials with 3,515 participants. Mindfulness meditation programmes (usually around 8 weeks) reduced anxiety, depression and pain by small to moderate amounts. The same review found no evidence that meditation was better than other active treatments such as medication, exercise or behavioural therapies. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Verified] A 2018 critical review by many mindfulness researchers warned that the hype runs ahead of the evidence: "mindfulness" has no agreed definition, many studies are methodologically weak, and possible adverse effects have not been monitored enough. (Van Dam et al. 2018)
- [Verified] A review of 83 studies found that about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety or depression; it can happen to people with no history of mental health problems. (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Inference] This week’s practice is much shorter than the programmes in those studies, so we cannot say that 10 minutes a day will produce the same effects.

Limits: these figures are averages across many studies and do not predict what will happen for any one person. Meditation does not replace treatment if you have a mental health problem.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10–15 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read the two passages, the second time softly aloud.
2. **Sit (1 minute).** Sit on a chair or a cushion, back upright but not stiff, hands resting on your thighs. Eyes closed, or looking down at the floor in front of you.
3. **Know the breath (5–10 minutes).** Let the breath be natural. Breathing in, know you are breathing in; breathing out, know you are breathing out. If you like, note silently: "heavy" or "light". There is no need to breathe deeply or slowly.
4. **When the mind wanders.** This will happen many times, to everyone. When you notice it, say "thinking" silently, then gently come back to the breath. Noticing that you wandered is itself a moment of mindfulness.
5. **The last two minutes.** Try steps 3 and 4 of the discourse: breathing in, feel the whole body sitting; breathing out, let the shoulders, jaw and belly soften a little.
6. **Tick the day.** Write down how many minutes you sat. At the end of the week, count the days with 10 minutes; the goal is at least 5 of 7.

If paying attention to the breath makes you feel anxious or short of breath, move your attention to your feet on the floor, or open your eyes and look at an object in front of you. That is mindfulness too.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Teddy takes the lift"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The child lies on their back on a bed or a mat. Put a small teddy on the child’s tummy.
2. The adult says: "Let’s watch teddy ride the lift. Breathe in, teddy goes up. Breathe out, teddy goes down."
3. Count five rides up and down together. No need to breathe hard; just watch the teddy.
4. Ask: "Did teddy go fast or slow? How does your body feel now?"

If the child giggles or wants another game, stop; do not make them lie still. Daynentang has a lesson for families with children aged 2–8: [Walking slowly, eating slowly](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-9/g9-di-cham-an-cham/) (in Vietnamese).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Attending to the breath makes you short of breath, gives you a racing heart or dizziness, and this does not ease when you move to your feet or open your eyes.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.

## 7. Practice for today

Before you sleep, lie still and count 10 out-breaths. If you lose count, start again from 1, with no need to blame yourself.
