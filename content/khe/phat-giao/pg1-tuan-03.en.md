**This week:** read verses 223–224 of the *Dhammapada* slowly, sit with the breath for 5 minutes a day, then look back on one conversation from yesterday with three questions: is it true, is it gentle, is it needed.

**Measurable goal:** note at least 3 moments of "true and gentle speech" during the week.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Right speech is the third factor of the eightfold path read in Week 2. Buddhism commonly describes right speech as four things: not lying, not speaking divisively, not speaking harshly, and not engaging in idle talk. The two verses below come from the chapter on Anger of the *Dhammapada*, a collection of short verses often learned by heart in Buddhist countries.

::: kinh dhp-sujato nguyen-van | Dhp 223–224
Defeat anger with kindness,
villainy with virtue,
stinginess with giving,
and lies with truth.

Speak the truth, do not be angry,
and give when asked, if only a little.
By these three means,
you may enter the presence of the gods.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Inference] "The presence of the gods" relates to Buddhist beliefs about realms of rebirth: good deeds lead to a good future life. This course presents the practice; learners do not need to accept that belief.
- "Defeat anger with kindness" is about how we respond; it does not tell anyone to suffer in silence. Speaking gently is still speaking truthfully: wrongdoing, danger or bullying still needs to be told to someone who can help.
- The translation is the same as in Convergence 1, Week 12, so that the two readings match.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found a peer-reviewed review that evaluates training in Buddhist "right speech" as such.
- [Verified] The verse begins with defeating anger. A meta-analysis of 154 studies (184 samples, 10,189 people) found that activities that calm the body (slow breathing, mindfulness, relaxation…) reduced anger and aggression by a moderate amount (g = −0.63); activities that raise arousal (hitting a punch bag, running to "let off steam") were ineffective overall (g = −0.02). (Kjærvik & Bushman 2024)
- [Inference] This supports pausing for a breath before speaking when annoyed, but the study measured anger-calming activities in general, not this week’s exercise.

Limits: intense or frequent anger, or anger that comes with violence, needs professional help; this reading is not enough.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10–15 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read the two verses. Pick one line to carry with you through the day.
2. **Sit with the breath (5 minutes)** as in Week 1.
3. **Look back on one conversation (5 minutes).** Recall one conversation from yesterday, at home or at work. Write one line for each question:
   - *Was it true?* Did I exaggerate, play down or misstate anything?
   - *Was it gentle?* Did my tone and words hurt the other person more than needed?
   - *Was it needed?* Did it need saying at that moment?
4. **Prepare one sentence (2 minutes).** Think ahead to one thing you will need to say today, and write it so that it is both true and gentle.
5. **Tick the moments.** Each time this week you manage a sentence that is both true and gentle at a hard moment (when annoyed, when giving feedback, when saying no), write one line. The goal is at least 3.

Tip: when you notice you are annoyed, let out one breath before you speak. If you are still hot, say: "Let me think for a moment and get back to you."

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Flower words, thorn words"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The adult writes six sentences on cards, for example: "Can I play too?", "You’re so slow!", "Sorry, I spilled it", "Stupid!", "Thank you", "I didn’t do it" (when you did).
2. The child puts each card into one of two baskets: "Flower words" and "Thorn words".
3. For one "thorn word" card, think together how to say it truthfully but gently: "You’re so slow!" → "Let’s slow down and wait for each other."

Daynentang has a lesson for families with children aged 2–8: [Telling the truth, a lighter heart](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-1/g1-noi-that-long-nhe/) (in Vietnamese). Do not use this game to make a child confess in front of others.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Looking back on a conversation sets off a stream of self-blame. Then just write "next time I will say…", with no need to score your mistakes.
- If you are in a relationship with threats or violence, do not practise "gentle speech" towards the person harming you; find someone who can help.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
