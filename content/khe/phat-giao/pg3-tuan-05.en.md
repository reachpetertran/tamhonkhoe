**A reminder:** this is a Level 3 course. If you are depressed, recently bereaved or having thoughts of self-harm, please leave this course for another time and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/). This week has the longest practice of the course; if you felt unsteady at any point in the last four weeks, keep to 30 minutes as before.

**This week:** extend practice to 40 minutes (first three days) and then 45 minutes (if it feels fine), made up of sitting, slow walking and sitting. Each day, practise letting go of one small thing.

**Measurable goal:** practise for at least 40 minutes in total each day (you may split it into two sessions), on at least 5 of 7 days; each day, write down one small thing you let go of, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Near the end of the simile of the cobra (MN 22), after the raft passage read in Week 3, the Buddha returns to the five aggregates with a short piece of advice.

::: kinh mn22-sujato nguyen-van | MN 22, sections 40.1–41.3
So, mendicants, give up what isn’t yours. Giving it up will be for your lasting welfare and happiness. And what isn’t yours? Form isn’t yours: give it up. Giving it up will be for your lasting welfare and happiness.
:::

The discourse says the same of feeling, perception, choices and consciousness, then asks:

::: kinh mn22-sujato nguyen-van | MN 22, sections 41.12–41.18
What do you think, mendicants? Suppose a person was to carry off the grass, sticks, branches, and leaves in this Jeta’s Grove, or burn them, or do what they want with them. Would you think, ‘This person is carrying us off, burning us, or doing what they want with us’?”

“No, sir. Why is that? Because to us that’s neither self nor belonging to self.”
:::

### Reading it in context

- Jeta’s Grove is where the Buddha is staying as he speaks. The example is ordinary: no one feels hurt when someone gathers dry grass in the garden, because no one takes the grass to be "me".
- "Giving up" here means giving up the clinging "this is me, this is mine" towards the five aggregates. The discourse does not tell you to neglect your body, your work or the people close to you. Eat, sleep, see a doctor and care for your family as usual.
- This course applies the advice to small everyday things: an urge to win, a criticism, something you want to buy. Do not use the exercise to force yourself to "let go" of a great pain or of someone you love.
- In the tradition, longer practice usually happens with a teacher. This week is only a slightly longer session at home, not a meditation retreat.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A 2020 systematic review of 83 studies found that about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies), most often anxiety and depression; there were cases of depersonalisation and psychosis; it can happen to people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Verified] The interview study of Western Buddhist meditators identified many factors that influence whether a difficult experience becomes severe or mild, grouped into four domains: the practitioner, the practice, relationships, and health behaviours. (Lindahl et al. 2017)
- [Expert judgement] This course has not found any synthesis showing that sitting for 45 minutes is more beneficial than 30 minutes for people practising at home. That is why this week allows splitting the session in two, and keeping to 30 minutes if you need to.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 40–45 minutes a day. You may split it in two: morning (steps 1–3) and evening (steps 4–6).

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the two passages.
2. **Sit with the breath (10 minutes).**
3. **Slow walking (10 minutes).** As in Buddhism 2, Week 2: walk slowly in a room or outside, knowing the foot lifting, moving and placing.
4. **Sitting and letting go (15–20 minutes).** 15 minutes for the first three days, 20 minutes from the fourth day if it feels fine. Notice in turn the body, feeling tone, naming, wanting, and knowing. With each, breathe out and say very softly in your mind: "let it come, let it go". If a thought keeps clinging, do not push it away; just know "clinging", then breathe out.
5. **Back to the ground (5 minutes).** Open your eyes, feel your feet, name three things you see, have a sip of water, stand up and stretch.
6. **Write one line.** One small thing you let go of that day. Example: "Wanted to win the argument about who does the dishes. Let it be; we’ll talk tomorrow when we’re both calm."

Do not sit through panic or strange feelings. A longer session is not an endurance test.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "The melting ice cube"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Put a small ice cube in a bowl on the table.
2. Sit and watch it with the child. Every minute ask: "How is the ice cube now? Bigger or smaller? Is there more water in the bowl?"
3. Let the child touch the ice lightly with one finger and pull away: "Cold?"
4. At the end, ask: "Where did the ice cube go?" Then water a plant together with the water from the bowl.

Do not let the child suck or hold the ice for long. Talk only about ice turning into water, not about death.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- During longer sitting, you feel detached from your body, as if watching yourself from outside, or everything becomes empty and strange, and the feeling stays after you stand up. If so, go back to 20–30 minute sessions or stop altogether, do the "back to the ground" step, and talk to someone you trust. If the feeling lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- You start skipping meals, sleep or time with people close to you to sit longer.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before practices longer than 20 minutes.
- If there are serious warning signs, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
