**This week:** read a few passages from the opening discourse of the Middle Discourses, a famously difficult text. While sitting, practise separating three steps: seeing or hearing, naming, and what the mind adds ("mine", "I like it", "I hate it").

**Measurable goal:** sit for 35 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; each day, write down one time you noticed you had "added" something to what you saw or heard, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The discourse on the root of all things (Mūlapariyāya Sutta) is the first discourse of the Middle Discourses. It goes through a long list: earth, water, fire, air, creatures, gods, the meditative dimensions, the seen, the heard, the thought, the known, and more. For each, it describes three kinds of people: the ordinary person, the one still in training, and the one who is freed. This lesson quotes only the parts on "earth" and on "the seen".

::: kinh mn1-sujato nguyen-van | MN 1, sections 3.1–3.5
Take an unlearned ordinary person who has not seen the noble ones, and is neither skilled nor trained in the teaching of the noble ones. They’ve not seen true persons, and are neither skilled nor trained in the teaching of the true persons. They perceive earth as earth. Having perceived earth as earth, they conceive it to be earth, they conceive it in earth, they conceive it as earth, they conceive that ‘earth is mine’, they approve earth. Why is that? Because they haven’t completely understood it, I say.
:::

::: kinh mn1-sujato nguyen-van | MN 1, sections 19.1–19.4
They perceive the seen as the seen. Having perceived the seen as the seen, they conceive it to be the seen … Why is that? Because they haven’t completely understood it, I say.
:::

::: kinh mn1-sujato nguyen-van | MN 1, sections 27.1–27.4
A mendicant who is a trainee, who hasn’t achieved their heart’s desire, but lives aspiring to the supreme sanctuary from the yoke, directly knows earth as earth. Having directly known earth as earth, let them not conceive it to be earth, let them not conceive it in earth, let them not conceive it as earth, let them not conceive that ‘earth is mine’, let them not approve earth. Why is that? So that they may completely understand it, I say.
:::

### Reading it in context

- "Conceiving" (maññati) here means the mind building on top of what it has just perceived, above all building "I" and "mine". The discourse does not fault perceiving "earth as earth"; its point is the building that comes after.
- The trainee is told "let them not conceive": this is something being practised, not something finished. You will notice yourself "adding" many times; noticing is the exercise.
- The discourse ends by saying that the monks did not approve what the Buddha said (section 194.9), a rare ending. If you find this discourse hard, you are not the only one. This course does not try to explain the whole text; anyone who wants to go deeper should study with a teacher in the tradition.
- The list in the discourse mentions gods and realms in Buddhist cosmology. This course does not quote or discuss that part.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] The critical review by Bernstein and colleagues (2015) describes "decentering" as three processes: awareness of what one is experiencing, disidentification from internal experience, and reduced reactivity to the content of thoughts. This is a conceptual analysis, not a trial of effectiveness. (Bernstein et al. 2015)
- [Inference] The step of "seeing that I am adding" is close to the first and third processes above. No study has measured an exercise based on this discourse.
- [Verified] The interview study of Western Buddhist meditators recorded experiences involving the "sense of self" and perception among 59 kinds of meditation-related experience; some found them positive, others very distressing. (Lindahl et al. 2017)

## 3. Practice for adults

About 35 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the three passages.
2. **Sit with the breath (10 minutes).**
3. **Seeing, naming, adding (17 minutes).** With eyes half open, look down at a simple object in front of you (a cup, a leaf). Notice three steps:
   - *Seeing:* just colour, shape, light and shade.
   - *Naming:* the mind says "cup".
   - *Adding:* "my cup", "pretty", "that was expensive", "needs washing"… Each time you notice an "addition", say silently "adding", then come back to colour and shape.

   After about 8 minutes, close your eyes and do the same with sounds: *hearing*, *naming* ("a car"), *adding* ("too noisy", "must be the neighbours").
4. **Back to the ground (5 minutes).** Open your eyes, feel your feet, name three things you see, then stand up and stretch.
5. **Write one line each day.** One time during the day you noticed you had "added". Example: "A message from my manager: just 'call me please'. I added: 'I must have done something wrong.'"

There is no need to stop naming or stop thinking. Naming helps us live day to day; the exercise is only to see clearly where we add.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Feel before you name"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The adult puts a familiar object in a cloth bag (an orange, a spoon, a Lego brick).
2. The child reaches in and feels it, but may not say its name yet. They may only describe it: "round", "cold", "rough", "heavy".
3. After describing three things, the child guesses the name. Then take it out and look.
4. Swap roles: the child chooses, the adult describes.

Objects in the bag must be safe: no sharp edges, nothing small enough to put in the mouth.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- While looking or listening, things suddenly seem strange, as if behind glass, or you feel detached from your body, and the feeling stays after you stand up. If so, stop the contemplation, do the "back to the ground" step, and talk to someone you trust. If the feeling lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- Thoughts like "nothing means anything" or "I don’t matter" keep coming back for several days.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before practices longer than 20 minutes.
- If there are serious warning signs, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
