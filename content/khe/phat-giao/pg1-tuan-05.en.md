**This week:** read the opening and the closing of the Discourse on Love (Snp 1.8) slowly, then spend 10 minutes a day on loving-kindness meditation (mettā): wishing well to yourself, to someone dear, to someone you don’t know, and then to all beings.

**Measurable goal:** loving-kindness meditation for 10 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The Mettā Sutta in the *Suttanipāta* is often chanted in Theravada Buddhist countries. Convergence 1 – Week 7 read the middle of the discourse, where the good wishes go out to all beings. This week reads the other two parts: the opening says how someone practising loving-kindness should live, and the closing says the heart of love is kept in every posture.

::: kinh snp-sujato nguyen-van | Snp 1.8, lines 1.1–3.2 (opening)
Those who are skilled in the meaning of scripture
should practice as follows to realize the state of peace.
Let them be capable and upright, very upright,
easy to speak to, gentle and humble;

content and unburdensome,
unbusied, living lightly,
alert, with senses calmed,
courteous, not fawning on families.

Let them not do the slightest thing
that others who are wise would blame.
:::

::: kinh snp-sujato nguyen-van | Snp 1.8, lines 7.1–9.4
Even as a mother would protect with her life
her child, her only child,
so too for all creatures
unfold a boundless heart.

With love for the whole world,
unfold a boundless heart:
above, below, all round,
unconstricted, without enmity or foe.

When standing, walking, sitting,
or lying down while yet unweary,
keep this ever in mind;
for this, they say, is a divine meditation in this life.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Traditional teaching] Loving-kindness (mettā) is the first of the four immeasurables (brahmavihāras): love, compassion, rejoicing and equanimity. "Divine meditation" in the last line translates *brahmavihāra*.
- The opening shows that loving-kindness does not stand alone: it goes with an upright, gentle, contented way of life. "Not fawning on families" is advice for monastics who live on alms.
- The discourse ends (not quoted here) with the path of insight and "never return to a womb again", that is, no more rebirth. [Inference] In this tradition, loving-kindness is part of a path leading to liberation, not just a pleasant feeling.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A 2024 meta-analysis of 21 trials of loving-kindness and compassion meditation among working adults found lower burnout (g = 0.40; 10 trials) and stress (g = 0.54; 10 trials), and higher self-compassion (g = 0.65; 12 trials), compared with control groups. Results varied by type of work and method. (Wang et al. 2024)
- [Verified] A review of 83 studies on unwanted effects of meditation in general found that about 8% of meditators reported an adverse experience (about 4% in experimental studies). (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Inference] These studies measured programmes lasting several weeks, often with a teacher. A week of 10 minutes a day on your own is much lighter, so do not expect the same results.

Limits: these figures are averages across many different studies and do not predict what will happen for any one person.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10–15 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read one of the two passages, the second time softly aloud.
2. **Settle (1 minute).** Know a few in-breaths and out-breaths.
3. **Send good wishes (8 minutes).** Repeat silently, slowly, in time with the breath: "May ___ be at peace. May ___ be well. May ___ live with ease." Move through widening circles, about 1–2 minutes each:
   - yourself;
   - someone you find easy to love: family, a friend, a child, a pet;
   - someone you see but do not know: a shopkeeper, a driver;
   - everyone in your neighbourhood, then all beings in every direction, as the discourse says: "above, below, all round".
4. **Optional, from day 5.** Add someone who is a little difficult for you, for example a grumpy colleague. Choose only small matters; do not choose someone who has seriously hurt you.
5. **Note it (1 minute).** One line: "Who was it easy to wish well today, and who was hard?" Tick the days with 10 minutes; the goal is at least 5 of 7.

If wishing yourself well feels forced or uncomfortable, start with someone easy to love. You do not need to feel warm; repeating the wishes is enough.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Circles of good wishes"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Draw three circles, one inside another, on a large sheet of paper.
2. Inner circle: the child draws themselves and their family. Middle circle: classmates, the teacher, the school guard. Outer circle: animals, trees, people far away.
3. For each circle, the child says one wish: "May my family be well", "May my friends be happy", "May the birds have cosy nests".
4. Ask: "Which circle was easiest to wish well?"

Do not make the child wish well to someone they are angry with. Daynentang has a lesson for families with children aged 2–8: [Kind to myself](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-11/g11-tu-te-voi-chinh-minh/) (in Vietnamese).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Wishing yourself or a difficult person well brings a stream of sadness or self-blame that does not ease after a few minutes. Then go back to someone easy to love, or stop.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.

While waiting or walking today, silently wish three people you see: "May they be at peace."
