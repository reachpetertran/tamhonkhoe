**This week:** read chapter 44 and paragraph 2 of chapter 46 of the *Tao Te Ching* slowly. Each day, at one moment of wanting "more", stop and ask whether it is enough. In the evening, note one thing that was enough today.

**Measurable goal:** one "that's enough" stop a day, on at least 5 of 7 days; note one thing that was enough each evening, on at least 5 of 7 evenings.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 44 asks three questions: fame or life, life or wealth, gaining or losing, which is closer to us. The text gives its answer at the end of the chapter: whoever knows contentment and knows when to stop avoids shame, blame and danger. Chapter 46 continues: there is no greater calamity than being discontented with one’s lot.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 44
Or fame or life,  
Which do you hold more dear?  
Or life or wealth,  
To which would you adhere?  
Keep life and lose those other things;  
Keep them and lose your life:—which brings  
Sorrow and pain more near?

Thus we may see,  
Who cleaves to fame  
Rejects what is more great;  
Who loves large stores  
Gives up the richer state.

Who is content  
Needs fear no shame.  
Who knows to stop  
Incurs no blame.  
From danger free  
Long live shall he.
:::

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 46, paragraph 2
2\. There is no guilt greater than to sanction ambition; no calamity greater than to be discontented with one's lot; no fault greater than the wish to be getting. Therefore the sufficiency of contentment is an enduring and unchanging sufficiency.
:::

### Reading it in context

- Legge translates chapter 44 as rhymed verse; its three stanzas are the chapter’s three sections. His "--" is written here as "—". The last stanza was also used in [Body 3 – Week 4](/en/body/body-3-week-04/).
- "Life" here means one’s own person: health, time, peace of mind. "Long live shall he" is the text’s own phrase; the course does not promise it.
- Paragraph 1 of chapter 46 is about war (war-horses bred in the border lands); the course does not quote it.
- "Knowing enough" in this course means no longer chasing more once what you have is enough for what you need. It does not mean accepting hardship or not asking for fair pay or rights, and it is not financial advice.
- The exercise is not for eating: no eating less, no fasting, no dieting.
- [Inference] [Taoism 1 – Week 4](/en/traditions/taoism/taoism-1-week-04/) read chapter 33’s "he who is satisfied with his lot is rich". This week goes one step further: "knowing when to stop".

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 753 effect sizes from 259 independent samples: people who put more weight on money and possessions reported slightly lower well-being (r = −0.19 for the most widely used measures of materialist values and beliefs). This is a correlation: it does not show which leads to which. (Dittmar et al. 2014)
- [Inference] This points the same way as chapter 44, but it does not prove that practising "that's enough" makes people happier. We have not found any trial that evaluates this exercise.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus one pause during the day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read both passages. The second time, pause at "Who knows to stop / Incurs no blame".
2. **One "that's enough" (1 minute, during the day).** When you notice yourself wanting more (one more item in the shopping basket, ten more minutes of scrolling, one more task after the main work is done, one more comparison with someone else), pause for one breath and ask: "Is what I have already enough for this?" If it is, stop there.
3. **Fame or life (once in the week, 10 minutes).** Write down three things you are doing for reputation or money that cost you health, sleep or time with your family. Choose one to ease off a little in the coming week. Do not cut back on work that supports your family or on caring for people who depend on you.
4. **Note one thing that was enough (2 minutes, evening).** For example: "Dinner was enough for everyone", "My old jacket is still warm enough".

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Just enough water"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Take a clear plastic cup and a small jug of water, on a tray.
2. The adult sticks a piece of tape a little below the rim of the cup: this is the "just enough" line.
3. The child pours up to the line, then carries the cup a few steps. Then try once filling it to the brim and carrying it a few steps.
4. Ask: "Which time was easier to carry?" The adult says: "Just enough is the easiest to carry."

Use cool water and a plastic cup. It does not matter if some water spills on the tray.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The exercise makes you feel guilty whenever you spend on real needs, or you start cutting back on eating. Then stop the exercise and talk to someone you trust.
- If the signs are serious, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
