**This week:** read the first two paragraphs of chapter 64 of the *Tao Te Ching* slowly. Each day, find one thing that is still small and deal with it straight away. At the start of the week, choose one big task that is waiting and write an "if… then…" sentence for its first step.

**Measurable goal:** deal with one thing while it is still small each day, on at least 5 of 7 days; write one "if… then…" sentence and take the first step of a big task at least once in the week.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 64 of the *Tao Te Ching* contains a line known far beyond Taoism: "the journey of a thousand li commenced with a single step". It follows a less quoted passage: anything is easy to handle while it is still small, still at rest, not yet taking shape.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 64, paragraphs 1–2
1\. That which is at rest is easily kept hold of; before a thing has given indications of its presence, it is easy to take measures against it; that which is brittle is easily broken; that which is very small is easily dispersed. Action should be taken before a thing has made its appearance; order should be secured before disorder has begun.

2\. The tree which fills the arms grew from the tiniest sprout; the tower of nine storeys rose from a (small) heap of earth; the journey of a thousand li commenced with a single step.
:::

### Reading it in context

- Words in round brackets are Legge’s additions to make the English clear; they are kept as he wrote them.
- A "li" is a traditional Chinese measure of distance, much shorter than a mile. "A thousand li" simply means a very long way.
- Chapter 64 joins two ideas: deal with things while they are small (paragraph 1), and every big thing begins from something small (paragraph 2). Paragraph 3, about spoiling things when they are nearly done, is kept for Week 4.
- [Inference] [Taoism 2 – Week 6](/en/traditions/taoism/taoism-2-week-06/) practised "one small step for a task you are putting off". This week looks from the other side: catching a thing while it is small, before it grows.
- "Acting before a thing takes shape" does not mean worrying in advance about everything that might happen. The text is about *doing* something while it is small, not about thinking about it endlessly.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests with more than 8,000 people: making plans of the form "if situation X arises, then I will do Y" helped people reach their goals more than intentions alone, by a medium-to-large amount (d = 0.65). The studies differ widely in their goals and measures, so this figure is an average. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006)
- [Inference] An "if… then…" sentence is one way to make "the single step" concrete. The result above is about a planning technique; it does not evaluate the teaching of the *Tao Te Ching*.
- We have not found any study that evaluates "dealing with things while they are small" as a daily exercise.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day, plus one small action during the day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the two paragraphs. You can reread them every day.
2. **Catch it while it is small (5 minutes).** Each day, find one thing showing signs of growing: a small leak, a bill about to fall due, an unanswered message, a small misunderstanding at home, a pile of papers starting to rise. Deal with it now, or put it in your calendar at a specific time.
3. **One "if… then…" sentence (once, at the start of the week, 5 minutes).** Choose a big task that is waiting. Write its smallest first step in the form: "If it is [time, place], then I will [specific action]." For example: "If it is 7 a.m. on Monday and I sit down at my desk, then I will open the document and write one sentence."
4. **One line in the evening (1 minute).** What did you catch today while it was still small?
5. **End of the week.** Look back: which things would have grown if left another month? Has the first step of the big task been taken?

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "From a little bean"** (5–10 minutes a day, with an adult)

1. Soak a few mung beans in water overnight.
2. Put them on damp cotton wool or kitchen paper in a jar lid, somewhere with light.
3. Each day, the child adds a few drops of water and draws what the beans look like today.
4. The adult says: "Every big tree starts from a little sprout like this."

If a younger child joins in, the adult keeps hold of the beans so they do not go into mouths or noses. Daynentang has a lesson for families with children aged 2–8: [Caring for my plant](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-9/t9-cham-cay-cua-minh/) (in Vietnamese).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- "Looking for small things" turns into worrying about everything that could go wrong, or checking again and again. Then keep only one task a day and stop there.
- If the signs are serious, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
