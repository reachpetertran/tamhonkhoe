**This week:** read the whole of chapter 33 of the *Tao Te Ching* slowly, choose one small task and keep it every day, and each evening note one thing you already have enough of.

**Measurable goal:** keep the chosen small task on at least 6 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 33 sets things side by side in pairs: knowing others and knowing oneself, overcoming others and overcoming oneself, wealth and enough. The second half of each pair is valued more. The word "enough" in the course title comes from this chapter.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 33
1\. He who knows other men is discerning; he who knows himself is intelligent. He who overcomes others is strong; he who overcomes himself is mighty. He who is satisfied with his lot is rich; he who goes on acting with energy has a (firm) will.

2\. He who does not fail in the requirements of his position, continues long; he who dies and yet does not perish, has longevity.
:::

### Reading it in context

- "Overcoming oneself" here is close to self-mastery: doing what you have decided, not being led by habit or anger. [Inference] It is not driving yourself to exhaustion; chapters 15 and 16 have already spoken of stillness and returning to the root.
- "Satisfied with his lot" means recognising what you already have. [Expert judgement] The line does not tell anyone to accept deprivation or injustice; it speaks against bottomless craving.
- The last line ("dies and yet does not perish") is read in many ways in the commentary tradition. This course does not go into that question.
- Chapter 33 was read in [Convergence 0 – Week 4 "Discipline"](/en/convergence/week-04/), alongside Islamic and Stoic teaching. This week reads it among the chapters on water and stillness.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A 2017 meta-analysis of 33 studies of self-control training programmes found a small effect: g = 0.30. After correcting for publication bias (studies with good results are more likely to be published), the effect was smaller still, about 0.13–0.24. (Friese et al. 2017)
- [Inference] So this week sets only a modest goal: one small task, kept steadily, with no expectation of big change after a week.
- We have not found any study that evaluates practising "being satisfied with one’s lot" as taught in the *Tao Te Ching*.

Limits: this exercise is self-observation, not therapy.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 12 minutes a day, plus one small task.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read chapter 33. Pause on "he who overcomes himself is mighty".
2. **Choose one small task (first day, 2 minutes).** Something that takes under 5 minutes, for example: clearing your desk before bed, drinking a glass of water when you wake, not opening your phone for the first 15 minutes of the day. Keep the same task all week.
3. **Sit still (5 minutes)** as in Week 2, before or after the small task.
4. **Note one thing that was enough (2 minutes in the evening).** One line: "Today I had enough…" (a meal, someone asking how I was, an hour of rest).
5. **Tick the day.** The goal is to keep the small task on at least 6 of 7 days. If you miss a day, carry on the next day; do not do double to make up.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"A pebble a day"**: the child chooses one small daily task, and each time it is done drops a pebble in a jar. See the full activity at [Kids’ corner – Activity 4](/en/kids-corner/activity-04/).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Keeping "discipline" makes you blame yourself harshly when you miss a day, or makes you want to add task after task. Then keep just one task, or rest this week.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

Before you buy or want one more thing, ask yourself silently: "Do I have enough already?"
