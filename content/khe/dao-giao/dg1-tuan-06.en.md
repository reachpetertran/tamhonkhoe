**This week:** read the story of the cook in chapter III of the *Zhuangzi* slowly, each day do one familiar task very slowly and attentively, and at the end of the week review the whole course.

**Measurable goal:** do one familiar task slowly each day, on at least 5 of 7 days; at the end of the week, rate the 6 habits and choose 2 to keep.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] The *Zhuangzi* is the second great text of Taoism, made more of parables than of direct teaching. Chapter III, whose Chinese title means "the principle of nourishing life", tells of a cook cutting up an ox for his prince. He does not force the blade; he follows the openings that are already there, and slows down where it is hard.

::: kinh zhuangzi-giles nguyen-van | chapter III
Prince Hui's cook was cutting up a bullock. Every blow of his hand, every heave of his shoulders, every tread of his foot, every thrust of his knee, every *whshh* of rent flesh, every *chhk* of the chopper, was in perfect harmony,—rhythmical like the dance of the Mulberry Grove, simultaneous like the chords of the Ching Shou.

"Well done!" cried the Prince. "Yours is skill indeed."

"Sire," replied the cook; "I have always devoted myself to TAO. It is better than skill. When I first began to cut up bullocks, I saw before me simply *whole* bullocks. After three years' practice, I saw no more whole animals.

And now I work with my mind and not with my eye. When my senses bid me stop, but my mind urges me on, I fall back upon eternal principles. I follow such openings or cavities as there may be, according to the natural constitution of the animal. I do not attempt to cut through joints: still less through large bones.

[…]

"Nevertheless, when I come upon a hard part where the blade meets with a difficulty, I am all caution. I fix my eye on it. I stay my hand, and gently apply my blade, until with a *hwah* the part yields like earth crumbling to the ground. Then I take out my chopper, and stand up, and look around, and pause, until with an air of triumph I wipe my chopper and put it carefully away."

"Bravo!" cried the Prince. "From the words of this cook I have learnt how to take care of my life."
:::

Giles’s "--" is written here as "—"; his footnotes are left out.

### Reading it in context

- [Expert judgement] In the Chinese original the cook is named Ding (庖丁, Pao Ding), so the story is usually known as "Cook Ding cuts up an ox". Giles calls him only "Prince Hui’s cook".
- The omitted part ("[…]") is where the cook compares: a good cook changes his chopper once a year because he cuts, an ordinary cook once a month because he hacks, while his own chopper is nineteen years old and still as sharp as if fresh from the whetstone, because its thin edge goes where there is space.
- Giles titles the chapter "Nourishment of the Soul" and adds notes reflecting his own reading of "soul". The course does not follow that interpretation; it quotes only the story and leaves out the notes.
- The story describes butchering an ox, the cook’s trade at the time. The course does not discuss eating meat or vegetarianism; this week’s exercise needs no knife.
- [Inference] "Following the openings already there" links to the image of water in Week 1: not confronting the hard part head-on, but finding its way. This does not mean avoiding everything difficult: the cook still finishes the job, only more slowly and carefully.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any systematic review that evaluates doing everyday tasks slowly and attentively.
- [Verified] Better evidence exists for formal meditation programmes: small-to-moderate reductions in anxiety and depression (47 trials, 3,515 people), no more than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Inference] This week’s exercise is the "bringing it into daily life" part of the sitting in Weeks 2–5; the result above is only background and does not prove anything about this exercise.

Limits: this exercise is self-observation, not therapy.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the story once. The second time, pause at "when I come upon a hard part… I stay my hand".
2. **Choose one familiar task (first day).** Something safe that you do every day, about 5–10 minutes: washing up, folding clothes, watering plants, making tea, sweeping. Do not choose anything that needs speed or carries danger (driving, sharp knives, machinery).
3. **Do it very slowly (5–10 minutes).** Do it as if for the first time: where your hands touch, how heavy or light things are, where there is "an opening", where it snags. At a snag, slow down, look closely, do not force.
4. **Stand for a moment (1 minute).** Like the cook: when done, stand up, look around, put things away carefully.
5. **End-of-week review (15 minutes, once).** Rate the course’s six habits from 0 (not done) to 3 (become a habit): yielding instead of contending; sitting still to let the water settle; noticing a return to the root; one small task and having enough; one treasure; doing one familiar task slowly. Choose 2 habits to keep for the next 4 weeks, and write down when and where you will do them.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Slow hands"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Choose something the child can do: fold a handkerchief, pair up sandals, pour water from a small jug into a cup, tidy a pencil case.
2. Do it once at normal speed. Then do it again very slowly, "like a tortoise".
3. While going slowly, the adult asks quietly: "What are your hands touching? Where is it tricky?"
4. At a tricky part (a crooked corner, water about to spill), stop together, take one breath, then carry on more gently.
5. To finish: "When we go slowly, our hands find the way."

No knives, sharp scissors, hot water or breakable things. No racing to see who is faster. Daynentang has a lesson for families with children aged 2–8: [Walking slowly, eating slowly](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-9/g9-di-cham-an-cham/) (in Vietnamese).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Going slowly leads you to repeat a task again and again for fear it is not "right", or to check over and over. Then do the task at normal speed and keep only one attentive minute at the end.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

When you meet a "hard part", ask: "Where is the opening in this?" and slow your hand.
