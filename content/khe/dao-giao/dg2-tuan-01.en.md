**Before you start this course:** this is a Level 2 course, with 20–30 minutes of sitting a day for 8 weeks. It is best taken after [Taoism 1 "Water, stillness and enough"](/en/courses/taoism-1/). If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes. "Non-action" in this course means no more forcing and no more doing what is not needed; it does not mean giving up responsibilities, accepting harm or keeping quiet in the face of injustice.

**This week:** read chapter 2 of the *Tao Te Ching* slowly, sit still for 20 minutes each day, and notice the pairs of labels you put on things (pleasant/unpleasant, fast/slow, right/wrong).

**Measurable goal:** sit still for 20 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; each day write down one pair of opposites you have just used as a label, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 2 opens with an observation: beauty and ugliness, difficulty and ease, long and short arise together, each giving rise to the idea of the other. From there the chapter turns to the way of the sage: managing affairs without doing anything (non-action), teaching without words, and not resting in the work once it is done.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 2, paragraphs 1–4
1\. All in the world know the beauty of the beautiful, and in doing this they have (the idea of) what ugliness is; they all know the skill of the skilful, and in doing this they have (the idea of) what the want of skill is.

2\. So it is that existence and non-existence give birth the one to (the idea of) the other; that difficulty and ease produce the one (the idea of) the other; that length and shortness fashion out the one the figure of the other; that (the ideas of) height and lowness arise from the contrast of the one with the other; that the musical notes and tones become harmonious through the relation of one with another; and that being before and behind give the idea of one following another.

3\. Therefore the sage manages affairs without doing anything, and conveys his instructions without the use of speech.

4\. All things spring up, and there is not one which declines to show itself; they grow, and there is no claim made for their ownership; they go through their processes, and there is no expectation (of a reward for the results). The work is accomplished, and there is no resting in it (as an achievement).
:::

### Reading it in context

- Words in round brackets are Legge's additions to make the English clear; they are kept as he wrote them.
- [Expert judgement] "Manages affairs without doing anything" is Legge's rendering of the Chinese phrase 處無為之事. This is the first time the word "non-action" (*wu wei*) appears in the course. Commentators understand it in many ways; this course reads it in a narrow, practical sense: not doing what is not needed, not forcing.
- [Inference] The text does not say that beautiful and ugly are the same, nor that we should stop making distinctions. It points out that the two ends of a pair come together. This week's exercise is to notice the moment of labelling, not to stop telling right from wrong in daily life.
- Legge adds two rhyming lines at the end of the chapter; they are the translator's paraphrase and are not quoted here.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates practice based on chapter 2 of the *Tao Te Ching* as such.
- [Verified] The closest idea is "decentering". A 2015 critical review describes it as three processes: being aware of what one is experiencing, not identifying oneself with inner experience, and being less caught up in the content of thoughts. It is a conceptual analysis, not a trial measuring effects. (Bernstein et al. 2015)
- [Verified] A 2014 meta-analysis of 47 randomised trials with 3,515 people found that mindfulness meditation programmes reduced anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount; they were no better than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Inference] "Noticing the pleasant/unpleasant label" is close to the first process in Bernstein's model. That is a conceptual link, not evidence for this exercise.

Limits: sitting still for 20 minutes a day is practice, not treatment.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 25 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the four paragraphs. On the second reading, stop at paragraph 2 and count the pairs.
2. **Sit still (20 minutes).** Sit upright but at ease, and let the breath come and go by itself without steering it. When a label pops up ("too noisy", "nice", "I'm sitting wrong"), just note silently: "label". There is no need to swap it for its opposite or to push it away. For the first two days you can sit for 15 minutes.
3. **One pair during the day.** Catch one moment when you split things into two sides (fast people/slow people, a good day/a bad day). Ask: "What is the other end of this pair? Without that other end, what would this label mean?"
4. **Write one line (2 minutes).** Write down that pair. Do not score yourself as right or wrong.
5. **Tick the day.** The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Find the pair"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Set out a few things from around the house: a big cup and a small cup, a long spoon and a short one, a thick book and a thin one.
2. With the child, arrange them in pairs and name them: big – small, long – short, thick – thin.
3. Put the small cup away and ask: "Is this cup still the big cup now?" Let the child think; there is no single right answer.
4. To finish: "Big or small is when we compare it with something else."

No breakable things. Do not compare the child with brothers, sisters or friends (tall/short, good/bad at something).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Noticing labels turns into blaming yourself ("I'm judging again"). Then note that label too, and shorten the sitting.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
