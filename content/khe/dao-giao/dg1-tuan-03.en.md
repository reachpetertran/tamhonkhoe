**This week:** read chapter 16 of the *Tao Te Ching* slowly, sit still for 10 minutes, and each day note one moment when you "return to the root" and settle.

**Measurable goal:** note one moment of settling each day, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 16 looks at the rhythm of plants: they grow, flourish, then return to their root. That return is called stillness. The text teaches that whoever understands this rhythm of going out and coming back is intelligent, broad-minded and enduring; whoever does not is prone to wild movements.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 16
1\. The (state of) vacancy should be brought to the utmost degree, and that of stillness guarded with unwearying vigour. All things alike go through their processes of activity, and (then) we see them return (to their original state). When things (in the vegetable world) have displayed their luxuriant growth, we see each of them return to its root. This returning to their root is what we call the state of stillness; and that stillness may be called a reporting that they have fulfilled their appointed end.

2\. The report of that fulfilment is the regular, unchanging rule. To know that unchanging rule is to be intelligent; not to know it leads to wild movements and evil issues. The knowledge of that unchanging rule produces a (grand) capacity and forbearance, and that capacity and forbearance lead to a community (of feeling with all things). From this community of feeling comes a kingliness of character; and he who is king-like goes on to be heaven-like. In that likeness to heaven he possesses the Tao. Possessed of the Tao, he endures long; and to the end of his bodily life, is exempt from all danger of decay.
:::

### Reading it in context

- "Returning to the root" speaks of a natural rhythm: leaves turn from green to yellow, trees rest through the cold season. [Inference] The course uses this image for small moments in the day when we stop running and stop straining.
- The last sentence of paragraph 2 ("endures long", "exempt from all danger of decay") has been read in many ways by commentators. This course does not promise a longer or healthier life.
- "Heaven" and "the Tao" here are Taoist concepts, not a deity to pray to. People from other traditions can read this chapter as an observation of nature.
- Chapter 16 does not teach withdrawing from life. This week you work and care for your family as usual; you only add a few moments of "returning to the root".

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates practising "returning to stillness" as in chapter 16.
- [Verified] Mindfulness meditation programmes in general reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount (47 trials, 3,515 people), no more than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Verified] A 2018 critical review points out that many mindfulness studies rely on participants’ self-reports, use differing definitions and rarely track harms. (Van Dam et al. 2018) [Inference] The sense of "settling" you note this week is also a self-report: useful for self-observation, not evidence.

Limits: this exercise is self-observation, not therapy.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read paragraph 1 twice and paragraph 2 once. Pause on "This returning to their root is what we call the state of stillness."
2. **Look at a plant (2 minutes, optional).** Look at a tree, a potted plant or a branch near your home: where is it growing, where is it wilting, where is its root?
3. **Sit still (8 minutes).** As in Week 2. Towards the end, notice the out-breath: each out-breath is a small "return".
4. **Catch a moment of settling (during the day).** For example: switching off after work, sitting down after tidying up, the moment the children are asleep. When you notice it, pause for three breaths.
5. **Write one line in the evening (2 minutes).** When, where, what you felt in your body. The goal is at least 5 of 7 days.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Trees go back to their roots"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. The adult and the child stand like two trees: feet are roots, arms are branches.
2. "The wind is blowing!": sway your arms together, lean, and go "whoosh" (1 minute).
3. "The wind has stopped.": let your arms drop, stand still, feel your feet touching the ground. Count five breaths.
4. Do it two or three more times. Ask: "Which did you like better? When you stood still, how did your feet feel?"
5. To finish: "After playing with the wind, the tree comes back to its roots. So do we."

Play on a flat floor, away from anything breakable; a child who does not want to stand still can sit.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- "Settling" brings sadness or emptiness that lasts for several days. Then switch to something with movement (walking, housework) and talk to someone you trust.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
