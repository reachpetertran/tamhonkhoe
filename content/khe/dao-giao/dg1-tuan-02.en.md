**This week:** read chapter 15 of the *Tao Te Ching* (paragraphs 2–4) slowly, then each day sit still for 10 minutes in the manner of "letting the water settle".

**Measurable goal:** sit still for 10 minutes, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 15 describes the masters of old who lived by the Tao: cautious, unpretentious, empty like a valley. In the middle of the chapter comes a question: who can make muddy water clear? The answer is not to stir more, but to let it be.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 15, paragraphs 2–4
2\. Shrinking looked they like those who wade through a stream in winter; irresolute like those who are afraid of all around them; grave like a guest (in awe of his host); evanescent like ice that is melting away; unpretentious like wood that has not been fashioned into anything; vacant like a valley, and dull like muddy water.

3\. Who can (make) the muddy water (clear)? Let it be still, and it will gradually become clear. Who can secure the condition of rest? Let movement go on, and the condition of rest will gradually arise.

4\. They who preserve this method of the Tao do not wish to be full (of themselves). It is through their not being full of themselves that they can afford to seem worn and not appear new and complete.
:::

### Reading it in context

- Paragraph 3 has two halves. First: stillness, so the water clears. Second: movement, out of which rest arises. [Inference] The text does not say to sit still forever; stillness and movement go together.
- The comparisons in paragraph 2 (like unworked wood, like a valley) describe how a person living by the Tao appears. This course does not teach how to "become" like that; it only reads and practises letting be.
- Paragraph 3 was read in [Convergence 0 – Week 5 "Rest"](/en/convergence/week-05/). This week reads all three paragraphs to see where that sentence sits.
- [Expert judgement] "Letting the water settle" is different from "clearing away thoughts". You do not chase thoughts away; you simply stop stirring.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates sitting still in the manner of the *Tao Te Ching* as such.
- [Verified] The closest evidence is on meditation programmes in general. A 2014 meta-analysis of 47 randomised trials with 3,515 people found that mindfulness meditation programmes reduced anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount after about 8 weeks; they were no better than other active treatments such as exercise or psychotherapy. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Verified] Meditation is not always pleasant. A 2020 review of 83 studies found that about 8% of practitioners had adverse experiences such as increased anxiety or depression (about 4% in experimental studies), including people with no history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020)

Limits: 10 minutes of sitting a day is practice, not treatment.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read the three paragraphs. Pause on "Let it be still, and it will gradually become clear."
2. **Shake a jar (optional, 1 minute).** If you have one, put a pinch of soil or sand in a lidded jar of water, shake it, and set it in front of you.
3. **Sit still (10 minutes).** Sit upright but at ease, eyes on the jar or closed. When a thought arises, do not push it away or follow it: just notice "stirring", then let it be and come back to the breath. For the first two days you can start with 5 minutes.
4. **Write one line (2 minutes).** At the end, how far has the water in the jar (or in you) settled? No score, just a note.
5. **Tick the day.** The goal is at least 5 of 7 days.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"The muddy jar"**: shake a jar of water with a little soil until it clouds, then sit still together and watch it clear. See the full activity at [Kids’ corner – Activity 5](/en/kids-corner/activity-05/).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- Sitting still makes worries or painful memories crowd in more. Then open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, name three things around you, and shorten the sitting or switch to slow walking.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).

## 7. Practice for today

When you notice yourself "stirring" something (going over it again and again), say silently: "Let it be. The water will settle."
