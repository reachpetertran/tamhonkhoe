**This week:** read chapter 43 of the *Tao Te Ching* slowly, sit for 30 minutes each day in whichever way from the course suits you best, and at the end of the week rate the seven habits and choose two to keep.

**Measurable goal:** sit for 30 minutes (or 20 minutes sitting and 10 minutes slow walking) on at least 5 of 7 days; at the end of the week, rate the 7 habits, choose 2 and write a 4-week plan.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 43 gathers up the course's images: the soft overcoming the hard (like water in Taoism 1), what has no substance entering where there is no crevice (like the empty space in Week 2). From there the chapter speaks of the advantage of non-action and the teaching without words (as in chapter 2 in Week 1), and observes that few attain them.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 43, paragraphs 1–2
1\. The softest thing in the world dashes against and overcomes the hardest; that which has no (substantial) existence enters where there is no crevice. I know hereby what advantage belongs to doing nothing (with a purpose).

2\. There are few in the world who attain to the teaching without words, and the advantage arising from non-action.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Inference] "Few attain" is not a grade for the learner. After 8 weeks, what is worth noting is that you have noticed when you are forcing and when you could do less; not that you have "attained non-action".
- [Expert judgement] The course has read "non-action" in a narrow sense: not doing what is not needed, not forcing. Many commentators understand it more broadly, linking it to government and to the Tao as the source of all things. Those who want to go deeper should read further with someone from the tradition.
- The course keeps one point from Week 1: non-action does not mean giving up responsibilities, accepting harm or keeping quiet in the face of injustice.

## 2. What science finds

- No study has evaluated this 8-week programme directly.
- [Verified] The closest evidence: mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount (47 trials, 3,515 people), no more than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)
- [Verified] A 2018 critical review points out that "mindfulness" has no agreed definition, many studies are methodologically weak and rely heavily on self-report, and adverse effects are not monitored enough. (Van Dam et al. 2018)
- [Inference] So your own self-rating is the most useful information for you, even though it too is self-report.

Limits: this course is self-study practice, not treatment.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 35 minutes a day, plus 15 minutes of review once.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read the two paragraphs. Then reread the passage from the course you remember best.
2. **A 30-minute session.** Choose the way of sitting that felt steadiest over the seven weeks (labels, quiet gaps, softening the body, few instructions, listening, easing off 10%), or 20 minutes sitting and 10 minutes slow walking. At the end, come back to the ground: open your eyes, feel your feet, name three things.
3. **Review (15 minutes, once this week).** Rate the seven habits from 0 (not done) to 3 (a settled habit):
    - noticing labels in pairs (Week 1);
    - one 10-minute gap (Week 2);
    - not contending over small things (Week 3);
    - dropping one unnecessary thing (Week 4);
    - hearing someone out (Week 5);
    - the smallest step of a task you are putting off (Week 6);
    - ten minutes of not being useful (Week 7).
4. **Choose two and write a 4-week plan.** Choose 2 habits to keep over the next 4 weeks. For each, write: when, where, and after which familiar activity you will do it. Keep a 20–30 minute sitting if it suits you.
5. **Tick the day.** The goal is sitting on at least 5 of 7 days and finishing the rating.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Which game do you like best?"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Recall some of the games from the course: finding big – small pairs, finding things with empty spaces, the straw and the noodle, putting one thing away, listening to the bell, water flowing low, sitting with a tree.
2. Let the child choose one game to play again today.
3. Ask: "Why do you like this game?" Listen to the end without correcting.
4. To finish: choose together one game to play again next week.

Do not score the child or compare whether they have "got better".

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- The self-rating makes you blame yourself or feel you have "failed". Then just choose the easiest habit to keep, and drop the rating.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
