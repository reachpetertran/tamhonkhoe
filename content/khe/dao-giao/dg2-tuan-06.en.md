**This week:** read chapter 37 (paragraph 1) and chapter 63 (paragraph 2) of the *Tao Te Ching* slowly, sit for 25 minutes each day easing off the effort, then take the smallest step of a task you have been putting off.

**Measurable goal:** sit for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; each day take one small step (5 minutes or less) of a task you have been putting off, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] This week's two passages show that "non-action" is not doing nothing at all. Chapter 37 says the Tao does nothing for the sake of doing it, and yet nothing is left undone. Chapter 63 shows how: deal with the difficult while it is still easy, and with the great while it is still small.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 37, paragraph 1
1\. The Tao in its regular course does nothing (for the sake of doing it), and so there is nothing which it does not do.
:::

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 63, paragraph 2
2\. (The master of it) anticipates things that are difficult while they are easy, and does things that would become great while they are small. All difficult things in the world are sure to arise from a previous state in which they were easy, and all great things from one in which they were small. Therefore the sage, while he never does what is great, is able on that account to accomplish the greatest things.
:::

### Reading it in context

- Chapter 37 has two more paragraphs about princes and kings and "nameless simplicity"; the course quotes only paragraph 1.
- Paragraph 1 of chapter 63 contains the line "recompense injury with kindness". The course does not quote it this week because the exercise is not about how to respond to someone who does harm; that line does not mean one must put up with a person who is harming you.
- [Inference] "Not forcing" here does not mean waiting for inspiration or avoiding hard things. The text says the master still acts, only at the moment and at the size when the task is still easy.
- [Expert judgement] The line in chapter 37 (道常無為而無不為) is close to the line in chapter 48 read in Week 4.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates working in the way chapter 63 describes.
- [Verified] On "forcing": a 2001 meta-analysis of controlled studies found that trying to suppress a thought makes it come back more often, by a small-to-moderate amount; the effect varied with the kind of thought and how it was measured. (Abramowitz, Tolin & Street 2001)
- [Verified] A 2017 meta-analysis of 33 studies of self-control training found a small effect, smaller still after correcting for publication bias. (Friese et al. 2017)
- [Inference] Neither result is evidence for this week's exercise. They only suggest that pushing yourself by willpower does not always work, so breaking a task into small steps is worth trying.

Limits: the exercise is not a substitute for treatment when putting things off comes with depression, anxiety or burnout.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read the two passages. Pause at "does things that would become great while they are small".
2. **Sit, ease off by 10% (25 minutes).** Sit as in Week 4, with the fewest instructions that still feel steady. Whenever you notice yourself "trying" (trying to focus, to sit correctly, to be calm), ease off by about a tenth: soften the jaw, the shoulders, the breath, and carry on sitting. There is no need to drop effort completely; just loosen it.
3. **The smallest step of something you are putting off.** On the first day, choose a task you tend to let pile up (a phone call, some paperwork, a corner of the house). Each day take its smallest step, 5 minutes or less: open the folder, write the first line, clear one drawer. That step is enough for today; you may do more, but you don't have to.
4. **Write one line (2 minutes).** What was today's step? When you started, was the task heavier or lighter than you expected?
5. **Tick the day.** The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

This week uses the activity **"Water flows low"**: drip a few drops of water onto a slightly tilted tray and watch the water find its own way down without anyone pushing it. See the full activity at [Kids’ corner – Activity 9](/en/kids-corner/activity-09/).

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- "Not forcing" becomes a reason to drop tasks you need to do, or the pile of tasks leaves you anxious or sleepless. Then ask someone you trust to help you sort the tasks, and keep only the sitting.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
