**This week:** read chapter 22 of the *Tao Te Ching* slowly, sit still for 20 minutes each day letting the body soften, and once a day let go of showing off or arguing to win over something small.

**Measurable goal:** sit still for 20 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; once a day, "not contend" over something small and write it down, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 22 opens with paradoxes: the partial becomes complete, the crooked straight, the empty full. Then it describes the sage: free from self-display, self-assertion, self-boasting and self-complacency, and because he does not strive, no one is able to strive with him.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 22, paragraphs 1–3
1\. The partial becomes complete; the crooked, straight; the empty, full; the worn out, new. He whose (desires) are few gets them; he whose (desires) are many goes astray.

2\. Therefore the sage holds in his embrace the one thing (of humility), and manifests it to all the world. He is free from self-display, and therefore he shines; from self-assertion, and therefore he is distinguished; from self-boasting, and therefore his merit is acknowledged; from self-complacency, and therefore he acquires superiority. It is because he is thus free from striving that therefore no one in the world is able to strive with him.

3\. That saying of the ancients that 'the partial becomes complete' was not vainly spoken:—all real completion is comprehended under it.
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Expert judgement] The week's title follows the familiar reading of the chapter's opening Chinese phrase (曲則全). Legge translates the first character as "partial"; other translators give "bent" or "crooked". Both point to the same idea: what is not yet whole has room to become whole.
- The words "(of humility)" in brackets are Legge's interpretation, not a literal part of the text; many translations simply say "embraces the One".
- [Inference] "Not contending" here means not competing to be seen or praised. It does not mean keeping quiet when treated unjustly, not asking for what one is rightly owed, or hiding information that others need for their work or safety.
- [Inference] Paragraph 2 speaks of results ("and therefore he shines"), but the exercise does not use it as a trick for getting noticed. Holding back from boasting in order to be praised is still boasting, in another form.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates practising "not contending" as taught in the *Tao Te Ching*.
- [Verified] The closest research is on humility. A 2025 meta-analysis of 65 studies found that more humble people had slightly better mental health (small associations). This is a **correlation** and does not show that humility is the cause; there is no meta-analysis of humility training programmes yet. (Lee et al. 2025)
- [Verified] Mindfulness meditation programmes reduce anxiety and depression by a small-to-moderate amount (47 trials, 3,515 people), no more than other active treatments. (Goyal et al. 2014)

Limits: the exercise is self-observation, not training in communication or conflict resolution.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 25 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the three paragraphs. On the second reading, pause on the four phrases "free from self-display… self-complacency".
2. **Sit still (20 minutes).** Sit upright without straining. Every few minutes, scan gently from the forehead, jaw, shoulders and arms down to the belly: wherever you are trying hard to stay straight or look right, let it soften a little while still sitting steadily. The posture does not need to be perfect; a slightly curved back can still sit.
3. **One "not contending" a day.** Choose something small and safe: not cutting in with your own achievements while someone else is talking, not arguing to the end about something unimportant, not correcting a harmless slip in what someone said. Do not use this exercise for matters of rights, health, safety or injustice.
4. **Write one line (2 minutes).** What did you want to win at that moment? What happened when you did not contend?
5. **Tick the day.** The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "The straw and the noodle"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Get a bendy plastic straw (or a rubber band) and a strand of dry spaghetti.
2. Let the child gently bend the straw: it bends and springs back. Then together bend the dry spaghetti very gently: it snaps.
3. Ask: "Which one bends and doesn't break?"
4. To finish: "Sometimes being a little soft keeps us from breaking."

Do not use wooden or bamboo sticks or anything that could snap into sharp pieces. Do not use this image to tell a child to put up with being bullied.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- "Not contending" leaves you swallowing anger, feeling looked down on, or starting to give way on things you should not give way on. Then drop the daytime exercise and keep only the sitting.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
