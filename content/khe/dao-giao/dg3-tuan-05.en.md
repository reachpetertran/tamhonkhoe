**Before you start:** this Level 3 course deals with death and loss. If you are newly bereaved, seriously ill, or having thoughts of dying or harming yourself, please save the course for another time and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/) straight away.

**This week:** subjects like life and death are better not thought about alone. This week you read a short passage from chapter VI of the *Zhuangzi* with someone you trust (a partner, a sibling, a close friend, or a guide) and talk for 15 minutes. Each day you still sit for 40 minutes, and once a day you stop demanding that you be different.

**Measurable goal:** sit for 40 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days (30 minutes is fine); one 15-minute reading and conversation with someone during the week; one moment of "not demanding" each day, on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] In Week 3 you read the story of the four friends. The third of them, Tzu Lai, falls seriously ill, and his wife and children stand weeping around him. His friend Tzu Li comes to visit, leans against the door and wonders what the Creator will turn him into. Tzu Lai answers with an image:

::: kinh zhuangzi-giles nguyen-van | chapter VI, the great furnace
"Suppose that the boiling metal in a smelting-pot were to bubble up and say, 'Make of me an Excalibur;' I think the caster would reject that metal as uncanny. And if a sinner like myself were to say to God, 'Make of me a man, make of me a man;' I think he too would reject me as uncanny. The universe is the smelting-pot, and God is the caster. I shall go whithersoever I am sent, to wake unconscious of the past, as a man wakes from a dreamless sleep."
:::

### Reading it in context

- [Expert judgement] Giles writes "God" and "Excalibur". The Chinese uses 造化 (*zaohua*, the making and transforming of all things, in Vietnamese "Tạo Hóa") and the name of the sword Moye (鏌鋣). The Vietnamese renders "God" as "Tạo Hóa", following the Chinese, and "Excalibur" as "a precious sword".
- [Expert judgement] "A sinner like myself" is Giles's wording. The Chinese 今一犯人之形 is usually understood as "having once taken on human form", with no sense of "sin". The Vietnamese keeps the English wording and says so here; a reviewer should check it.
- [Inference] The point of the passage: metal does not choose to become a sword or a hoe; nor can a person demand to remain a person forever. The story is about letting go of demands, not about anyone dying early or refusing treatment. Just before this passage Tzu Lai has a line about "dying quickly"; the course does not quote it, to avoid misreading.
- "Not demanding" in the exercise applies to small things that cannot be changed (rain, a deep voice, being shorter than others). Do not use it for illness that needs treatment, for your rights, or for injustice.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates reading the *Zhuangzi* with another person.
- [Verified] Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), which includes practising acceptance of what cannot be changed so as to live by what one values, works for anxiety, depression and chronic pain, according to a review of 20 meta-analyses. (Gloster et al. 2020)
- [Inference] ACT is guided therapy. Its results do not show that the "not demanding" exercise here works as well.
- [Verified] About 8% of meditators had an adverse experience; relationships are among the factors that influence whether a difficult experience becomes more or less severe. (Farias et al. 2020; Lindahl et al. 2017)

## 3. Practice for adults

About 40 minutes a day, plus one 15-minute session with someone during the week.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the passage once. The second time, pause at "I shall go whithersoever I am sent".
2. **Sit for 35 minutes** (or 30): 10 minutes of breath; 15 minutes of body scan; 10 minutes just sitting. When you notice yourself wanting the sitting to "have to" be different (calmer, deeper), note "furnace", then return to the breath.
3. **Back to the ground (2 minutes).** Open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, name three things before you stand up.
4. **One moment of "not demanding" a day.** When you meet a small thing that cannot be changed, instead of grumbling, take one breath and say silently: "All right, that's how it is." Write one line.
5. **Reading with someone (once in the week, 15 minutes).**
    - Invite the person to read the passage with you. Say beforehand that it is a reading from a course, not anything urgent.
    - Each of you picks one question to answer: "Which sentence in the passage feels comforting, and which feels uncomfortable?" or "Is there something I keep demanding to be different that in fact cannot change?"
    - Just listen; do not argue about who is right. Do not discuss the death of a particular person.
6. **Tick it off.** Goal: sitting and "not demanding" on at least 5 of 7 days; one reading with someone during the week.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**"Baby me"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Find a photo of the child as a baby (or a set of newborn clothes).
2. Put it next to the child as they are now. Compare together: hands, feet, hair, teeth.
3. Ask: "Is the baby in the photo you? What has changed? What is still the same?"
4. Finish with: "You grow a little every day. You are still you."

Choose a happy photo. If the photo shows a relative who has died and whom the child often asks about, choose another photo for this game.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, a feeling that you or the world are "not real" that lasts, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself or of dying.
- The conversation leaves you or the other person sad for a long time. Then stop, say thank you, and do something light together (a walk, a cup of tea).
- The thought "I should let go of everything, there's no point trying" keeps coming back for several days. This is the time to talk to someone you trust or get help straight away, not to sit longer.
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for more than 20 minutes.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
