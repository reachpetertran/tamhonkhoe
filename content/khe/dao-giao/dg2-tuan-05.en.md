**This week:** read the "fasting of the heart" passage in chapter IV of the *Zhuangzi* slowly, sit and listen for 25 minutes each day, and once a day hear someone out completely without preparing your reply.

**Measurable goal:** sit and listen for 25 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; once a day, hear one person out without interrupting, on at least 5 of 7 days.

**This lesson involves no fasting from food.** "Fasting of the mind" is a fast of the mind. Eat and drink as usual this week.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Chapter IV of the *Zhuangzi* opens with the story of Yen Hui, a disciple of Confucius, who plans to go to the State of Wei to remonstrate with its ruler, a man who pays no heed to his people and will not see his own faults. Confucius sees that Yen Hui is still carrying too many plans, and teaches him to "fast": not from food, but by emptying the mind so that he can hear what is really there.

::: kinh zhuangzi-giles nguyen-van | chapter IV
"Then," said Yen Hui, "I am without resource, and venture to ask for a method."

Confucius said, "*FAST*.... Let me explain. You have a method, but it is difficult to practise. […]

"Well," replied Yen Hui, "my family is poor, and for many months we have tasted neither wine nor flesh. Is not that fasting?"

"The fasting of religious observance it is," answered Confucius, "but not the fasting of the heart."

"And may I ask," said Yen Hui, "in what consists the fasting of the heart?"

"Cultivate unity," replied Confucius.

"You hear not with the ears, but with the mind; not with the mind, but with your soul.

But let hearing stop with the ears. Let the working of the mind stop with itself. Then the soul will be a negative existence, passively responsive to externals. In such a negative existence, only TAO can abide. And that negative state is the fasting of the heart."
:::

### Reading it in context

- In this story Confucius is a character Zhuangzi uses to voice his own ideas; this is not a teaching from the Confucian books.
- [Expert judgement] "Fasting" here translates the Chinese 齋, the word for abstaining before a sacrifice, including from wine and meat. The phrase "fasting of the heart" (心齋) applies that same word to the mind. Yen Hui thinks of going without food; Confucius replies that this is the fasting of ritual, not of the heart. The course practises only the fasting of the heart.
- The cut "[…]" is one short sentence: for whoever takes it as easy, Heaven does not approve. [Expert judgement] Giles translates "Heaven" (天) here as "God"; the course leaves the sentence out so as not to put a theological concept into the character's mouth, and says so here.
- [Expert judgement] Where Giles writes "soul", the Chinese has *qi* (氣); where he writes "negative", the Chinese has 虛, "empty", the same word as the empty space in Week 2. The course does not follow Giles's "soul" interpretation; the English is quoted exactly as Giles wrote it.
- [Inference] "Let hearing stop with the ears" does not mean stop thinking. It means hearing what is there first, and only then letting the mind add comments, plans and replies.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any study that evaluates the practice of "fasting of the mind" as such.
- [Verified] The closest idea is "decentering": being aware of what one is experiencing, not identifying oneself with inner experience, and being less caught up in the content of thoughts. It is a conceptual analysis, not a trial measuring effects. (Bernstein et al. 2015)
- [Verified] About 8% of meditators have adverse experiences such as increased anxiety or depression (a review of 83 studies), including people with no prior history of mental illness. (Farias et al. 2020)
- [Verified] An interview study of Western meditators recorded many kinds of difficult experience in practice, including a group related to the "sense of self"; their impact ranged from minimal and passing to severe and lasting. (Lindahl et al. 2017)

Limits: sitting and listening is practice, not treatment; a sense of "emptiness" while sitting is not a sign of progress to chase.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 30 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes).** Read the passage. On the second reading, pause at "let hearing stop with the ears".
2. **Sit and listen (25 minutes).** Sit still, eyes closed or lowered. Widen your attention to sounds: near, far, loud, soft. Hear sounds as sounds, without needing to know what they are. When the mind starts telling a story ("whose noisy car is that", "I must call this afternoon"), notice it, then come back to hearing. In the last few minutes, open your eyes and take in the whole room. For the first two days you can sit for 20 minutes.
3. **Hear someone out once a day.** When someone speaks to you (partner, child, colleague), listen to the very last sentence without cutting in and without preparing your reply. If you need to answer, pause for one breath before speaking.
4. **Write one line (2 minutes).** What did I hear today that I would normally have missed?
5. **Tick the day.** The goal is at least 5 of 7 days for both.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel panic, have flashbacks of trauma, have a lasting sense that you or the world are "not real", lose sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself.
- A feeling of "emptiness", numbness or being a stranger to yourself stays after you get up. Then open your eyes, put your feet on the floor, name three things around you, and switch to slow walking. If it lasts more than a day, see a doctor.
- You hear voices or sounds that other people do not hear.
- If so, stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
- If you have a history of psychosis, bipolar disorder or severe PTSD, ask a doctor before sitting for longer than 20 minutes.
