**This week:** read three passages about giving thanks when eating. Each day, before one meal, pause for one minute: look at the food, breathe slowly, then say or think a thank-you in your own words.

**Measurable goal:** pause for one minute before at least one meal a day, on at least 5 of 7 days. Put a mark on a calendar each time.

**Before you start this course:** this course involves no calorie counting, no weighing, no dieting, and it is not aimed at weight loss. Anyone with a current or past eating disorder should talk to a doctor before taking it.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] Many traditions have words of thanks tied to meals. This week we read three passages: one from the Torah, one from the Gospels, one from the Qur'an.

### Judaism: eat, be satisfied, and bless

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Deuteronomy 8:10
And thou shalt eat and be satisfied, and bless the LORD thy God for the good land which He hath given thee.
:::

A note on context: [Expert judgement] in Jewish tradition this verse is usually cited as the basis for the blessing after meals (Birkat Hamazon). The thanks here comes after eating one's fill. The Jewish reviewer will say more.

### Christianity: looking up to heaven and blessing

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Matthew 14:19
He commanded the multitudes to sit down on the grass; and he took the five loaves and the two fish, and looking up to heaven, he blessed, broke and gave the loaves to the disciples; and the disciples gave to the multitudes.
:::

A note on context: this verse is part of the account of Jesus feeding the crowd with five loaves and two fish (14:13–21). This lesson only looks at the gesture of blessing before sharing the bread, not at the miracle. Many Christian families pray before meals.

### Islam: eat the good things and give thanks

::: kinh quran-pickthall nguyen-van | Qur'an 2:172
O ye who believe! Eat of the good things wherewith We have provided you, and render thanks to Allah if it is (indeed) He Whom ye worship.
:::

A note on context: this is a translation of the meaning; Muslims generally hold that only the Arabic text is the Qur'an. The next verse (2:173) is about forbidden foods; this lesson does not discuss dietary law.

### Where the traditions differ

- To whom the thanks is directed: in the three passages above, to God, to Allah. [Expert judgement] In Buddhism, the monastic reflection before meals recalls the purpose of eating, to sustain the body for practice, and is not a petition; Stoicism speaks of gratitude to nature and to other people.
- When it is said: the Deuteronomy passage ties the blessing to having eaten one's fill; the Matthew passage places it before the bread is shared.
- Each tradition has its own prayers, dietary laws and customs (kosher, halal, seasonal fasting from meat…). This lesson gives no ritual instruction for any tradition. If your family already has its own prayer, keep it as it is.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any review that evaluates the habit of giving thanks before meals as such.
- [Verified] The closest is research on gratitude exercises in general: a 2021 meta-analysis of 27 studies with 3,675 people found that gratitude exercises reduced symptoms of depression and anxiety by a small amount, and by less when compared with control groups that also did an activity. (Cregg & Cheavens 2021)
- [Inference] Any benefit of a one-minute pause before a meal is therefore inferred from gratitude research and has not been measured directly.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 5 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (3 minutes, one passage a day).** Days 1–2 read Deuteronomy, days 3–4 Matthew, days 5–6 the Qur'an, day 7 all three again.
2. **Choose one meal.** Pick the meal that is easiest to remember, for example dinner.
3. **Pause for one minute before eating.**
   - Put down your chopsticks or spoon. Look at the food: its colours, its warmth, its smell.
   - Take three slow breaths.
   - Say or think one sentence of thanks in your own words. For example: "Thank you to everyone who made this meal." People of faith may use a prayer from their own tradition.
4. **Put a mark** on the calendar when you have done it.

If you eat with others: you do not need anyone to join in. You can pause for the minute quietly.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "One thank-you"** (5 minutes, with an adult)

1. Before dinner, the whole family looks at the meal and takes three slow breaths together.
2. Everyone says one short thank-you: to whom, for what. For example: "Thank you, Mum, for making the soup."
3. The adult goes first so the child has an example. A child who does not want to speak can just nod.

Do not make the child finish the plate, do not call foods "good" or "bad", and do not mention weight.

## 5. Stop practising if

- The exercise makes you worry a lot about eating, makes you want to eat less or skip meals, or makes you feel guilty about eating. Stop this course and talk to a doctor or a qualified professional.
- Saying thanks before meals causes tension at home because of differences in belief. Then keep the quiet minute just for yourself.
- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop practising and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
