**This week:** if you wish, keep your chosen level of plant-based eating and your eating window, or a lighter one. Each day, spend two minutes listening to your body. At the end of the week, rate yourself and choose: keep, reduce or stop. Stopping altogether is also a good outcome.

**Measurable goal:** listen to your body for 2 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days; at the end of the week, write down your decision for the next 4 weeks.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Taoism: one who knows when to stop is free from danger

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 44 of the Tao Te Ching asks which is dearer, fame, wealth or life, and ends with a few lines on contentment and knowing when to stop.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 44, section 3
Who is content  
Needs fear no shame.  
Who knows to stop  
Incurs no blame.  
From danger free  
Long live shall he.
:::

A note on context: Legge translates this chapter as rhymed verse. [Inference] In this chapter, what one must know to stop is the craving for fame and wealth, not eating. This lesson borrows only the idea that a good discipline has its stopping point.

### Islam: the body has its rights

[Traditional teaching] Hadith 1975 in *Sahih al-Bukhari*, narrated by Abdullah bin Amr, sits in a chapter on the right of the body when fasting. The Prophet advised Abdullah not to fast every day and pray all night, but to fast on some days and not on others, to pray and to sleep, because

::: kinh sunnah-com trich | Bukhari 1975 (part)
your body has a right on you
:::

and so do his eyes, his wife and his guests. The English translation on sunnah.com is in copyright, so this page quotes only a short phrase. Read the full hadith at [sunnah.com, Bukhari 1975](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1975). This hadith was also read in [Body 2, Week 4](/en/body/body-2-week-04/).

A note on context: [Inference] The hadith is about fasting as worship, which has religious value in Islam; the 16:8 eating window of this course does not. This lesson borrows only the idea that even a good practice needs moderation, and that family and guests also have a share in one's daily rhythm. The Muslim reviewer will say how this hadith is usually taught.

### Where the traditions differ

- Laozi speaks of contentment and knowing when to stop as a way of guarding oneself against craving. The hadith places moderation within the worship of God and one's duties to family and guests.
- This lesson does not merge the two teachings into one shared idea. Each answers a different question within its own tradition.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] The 2025 meta-analysis (see Week 1): in trials of 24 weeks or longer, intermittent fasting was no longer better than daily calorie restriction; the authors say longer trials are needed. (Semnani-Azad et al. 2025)
- [Verified] The 12-month trial (see Week 3): 118 of 139 participants (84.9%) completed it. (Liu et al. 2022)
- [Inference] So there is no scientific reason to keep 16:8 for the long term if it makes you tired or tense. Stopping is not failure.
- Limits: there is no research on "listening to the body" while practising an eating window; this week's practice rests on traditional teaching and caution, not on evidence.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read the Tao Te Ching on odd days and the summary of the hadith on even days.
2. **Listen to the body (2 minutes, in the evening).** Sit still and breathe normally. Ask each question and answer in one word: hungry? tired? dizzy? irritable? able to focus? slept well last night? Write it down.
3. **Keep it or make it lighter.** If on any day you feel dizzy, very irritable or sleep badly, widen your window by 2 hours the next day or take a day off.
4. **Rate yourself (day 7).** Score 0–3 for four questions: (a) have I kept my health; (b) does this rhythm fit my family and work; (c) am I more preoccupied with food than before (scored in reverse: the more preoccupied, the lower); (d) am I more self-possessed and calm.
5. **Choose for the next 4 weeks (day 7).** One of three: keep the current level; reduce to a lighter level (for example a 12-hour window, one plant-based meal); or stop. If (a) or (c) scores below 2, choose to reduce or stop. Write one sentence giving your reason.

## 4. Children's corner (6+)

Your child eats as usual and does not follow this course.

**Game: "Thank you, body"** (5 minutes, with an adult)

1. Each person touches a part of the body and says what it helps with: "Thank you, legs, for letting me run", "Thank you, tummy, for giving me energy to play".
2. Ask your child: "What does your body want today? A drink of water, a rest, running around, or a good meal?"

Do not talk about weight or body shape, your child's or anyone's.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel dizzy, shaky, sweaty, have a racing heart or nearly faint. Eat straight away, stop the course and ask a doctor.
- You are more preoccupied with food than before, eat a lot without control, feel guilty about eating, or want to fast longer or eat less. Stop the course and talk to a doctor.
- You lose sleep, your periods change, you lose weight without meaning to, or you feel tired for a long time.
- You feel panic, have trauma flashbacks, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
