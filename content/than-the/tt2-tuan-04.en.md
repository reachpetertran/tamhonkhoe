**This week:** read three teachings about the place of the body in spiritual life. Review the three weeks, rate yourself, and choose a manageable rhythm of movement for the next four weeks.

**Measurable goal:** practise at least 4 sessions this week (walking, stretching or the continuous session); at the end of the week, write down your rhythm for next month.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Stoicism: matters of the body come second

[Traditional teaching] Epictetus teaches that what most deserves our care is the mind, that is, how we judge and choose.

::: kinh epictetus-long nguyen-van | Encheiridion XLI
It is a mark of a mean capacity to spend much time on the things which concern the body, such as much exercise, much eating, much drinking, much easing of the body, much copulation. But these things should be done as subordinate things; and let all your care be directed to the mind.
:::

A note on context: Epictetus does not say to give up exercise; he says not to let it take the place of what matters most.

### Christianity: bodily exercise and godliness

[Traditional teaching] In his letter to Timothy, Saint Paul compares training the body with training in godliness.

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | 1 Timothy 4:8
For bodily exercise has some value, but godliness has value in all things, having the promise of the life which is now and of that which is to come.
:::

A note on context: the verse before (4:7) urges "Exercise yourself toward godliness." The World English Bible says bodily exercise "has some value"; the 1925 Vietnamese Bible says it is of "little profit". Both put bodily exercise after godliness, with a slightly different emphasis. The Christian reviewer will say how this verse is usually taught.

### Islam: the body has its rights

[Traditional teaching] Hadith 1975 in *Sahih al-Bukhari*, narrated by Abdullah bin Amr, sits in a chapter on the right of the body when fasting. The Prophet advised Abdullah not to fast every day and pray all night, but to fast on some days and not on others, to pray and to sleep, because

::: kinh sunnah-com trich | Bukhari 1975 (part)
your body has a right on you
:::

and so do his eyes, his wife and his guests. The English translation on sunnah.com is in copyright, so this page quotes only a short phrase. Read the full hadith at [sunnah.com, Bukhari 1975](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1975).

A note on context: [Inference] The hadith is about moderation in worship, not about exercise. This lesson borrows only the idea that caring for the body, including rest and sleep, is a duty rather than a lapse. The Muslim reviewer will say how this hadith is usually taught.

### Where the traditions differ

- Epictetus and the letter to Timothy put bodily exercise in second place, after the mind or godliness. The hadith reminds us that the body has rights and must not be neglected out of religious zeal.
- This is not a contradiction to be resolved: each teaching answers a different concern, one about caring for the body too much, the other about pushing it too hard. This lesson does not rank one teaching above another.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] The 2023 overview of 97 systematic reviews (see Week 2): physical activity reduced symptoms of depression and anxiety by a medium amount. One detail worth noting: the effect was smaller in longer programmes. The authors do not give a reason; part of it may be people dropping out. [Inference] (Singh et al. 2023)
- [Verified] The meta-analysis of 15 cohorts (see Week 3): more daily walking went with a lower risk of death, levelling off beyond a certain point. This is CORRELATIONAL. (Paluch et al. 2022)
- A thought for you: a small rhythm you can keep for a long time is probably worth more than a big plan abandoned after a few weeks. This is [Expert judgement], not the result of a particular study.

## 3. Practice for adults

**Each day (about 15 minutes):** choose one of the three practices: slow walking (Week 1), stretching (Week 2) or the continuous session (Week 3).

**At the end of the week (10 minutes), rate yourself from 0 to 3** (0 = did not do it, 3 = did it regularly and found it pleasant):

| Habit | Score |
|---|---|
| Slow walking, 10 minutes | |
| Slow stretching, 10 minutes | |
| Continuous session, 15 minutes | |
| Walking during the day (to the market, up the stairs, to work) | |

Then write down **your rhythm for the next four weeks**, for example: "Monday, Wednesday and Friday, walk 15 minutes after dinner; Tuesday and Thursday, stretch 10 minutes before bed." Keep the one or two habits with the highest scores. You do not need to keep all four.

If you want to move more vigorously (running, cycling, swimming), build up gradually and ask a doctor if you have a health condition.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "Our family movement box"** (10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Everyone draws or writes on slips of paper a movement played this month: crossing the lotus pond, cat stretch, giraffe, little river, still lake.
2. Put them all in a box.
3. Each evening, the child draws a slip and the family does that movement for a minute or two.

If the child does not want to play, leave it. Do not turn the game into a compulsory exercise, a reward or a punishment.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel chest pain, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, or nearly fall. Sit down at once; if it does not pass, call emergency services.
- Movement becomes something you force yourself to overdo, do while ill or injured, or feel heavy guilt about whenever you miss a session. Stop and talk to a doctor.
- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting feeling that you or the world are "not real", cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop practising and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
