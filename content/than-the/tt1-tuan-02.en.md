**This week:** read two passages about attention and taste. Each day, eat one meal slowly, over about 15 minutes, noticing colour, smell, taste and the feeling of fullness.

**Measurable goal:** eat one meal slowly each day (about 15 minutes), on at least 5 of 7 days.

**A reminder:** this course involves no calorie counting or weighing and is not aimed at eating less. Eating slowly here is about knowing that you are eating, not about smaller portions.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Buddhism: awareness when eating, drinking, chewing and tasting

[Traditional teaching] In the Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation, after the sections on breathing and the postures, the Buddha teaches awareness in all everyday activities, eating included.

::: kinh mn10-sujato nguyen-van | MN 10, section 8.1 (situational awareness)
Furthermore, a mendicant acts with situational awareness when going out and coming back; when looking ahead and aside; when bending and extending the limbs; when bearing the outer robe, bowl and robes; when eating, drinking, chewing, and tasting; when urinating and defecating; when walking, standing, sitting, sleeping, waking, speaking, and keeping silent.
:::

A note on context: the teaching is addressed to monastics, but awareness while eating can be practised at any meal. This passage was also read in [Buddhism 2, Week 2](/en/traditions/buddhism/buddhism-2-week-02/).

### Taoism: the five flavours take away the taste

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 12 of the Tao Te Ching says that too much stimulation dulls the senses.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 12
Colour's five hues from th' eyes their sight will take;  
Music's five notes the ears as deaf can make;  
The flavours five deprive the mouth of taste;  
The chariot course, and the wild hunting waste  
Make mad the mind; and objects rare and strange,  
Sought for, men's conduct will to evil change.
:::

A note on context: Legge translates this chapter as rhymed verse. The next paragraph says the sage cares for the belly and not the eyes. [Inference] Here "belly" is usually understood as real need and "eyes" as endless craving; it is not dietary advice. The reviewer will say more.

### Where the traditions differ

- The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation teaches awareness as part of the path to liberation. Chapter 12 of the Tao Te Ching is an observation about desire and the senses; it does not describe a practice.
- This lesson borrows only the shared idea of eating with attention. It does not replace the practice of any tradition.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A 2013 meta-analysis of 24 experimental studies: eating while distracted led people to eat moderately more in that meal (SMD 0.39) and more again at a later meal (SMD 0.76). Remembering the meal just eaten reduced later intake. (Robinson et al. 2013)
- [Verified] A 2014 meta-analysis of 22 studies: eating slowly went with eating less in the meal (SMD 0.45), but hunger at the end of the meal and up to 3.5 hours later did not differ. The studies varied a lot. (Robinson et al. 2014)
- Limits: both are laboratory studies of one or two meals and did not measure well-being. The authors discuss uses for weight loss; this course does not have that aim.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes for one meal a day.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** On odd days read the Mindfulness Meditation passage, on even days the Tao Te Ching.
2. **Pause for one minute** as in Week 1.
3. **The first bite.** Look, smell, then put it in your mouth. Chew slowly and notice how the taste changes.
4. **Put down your chopsticks between bites.** Take the next bite only after swallowing.
5. **Ask yourself halfway through:** am I hungry, comfortable, or full? There is no right or wrong answer; it is only to notice.
6. **Eat until it feels enough for you.** You do not need to eat less, and you do not need to finish.

If lunch at work is too rushed, choose breakfast or dinner. On busy days, eating the first few bites slowly is fine.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

daynentang already has the activity [Walking slowly, eating slowly](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-9/g9-di-cham-an-cham/) (in Vietnamese). This week, do that activity and add a small game:

**Game: "Taste detective"** (5 minutes, with an adult)

1. Everyone has one piece of a familiar fruit.
2. Smell it first and guess: sweet or sour? Then take a small bite and chew very slowly.
3. Everyone says one word for the taste or feeling: "crunchy", "cool", "a bit sour".

Use only foods the child already knows and is not allergic to. Do not make the child eat something they dislike, and do not turn it into a race to eat the slowest.

## 5. Stop practising if

- Eating slowly makes you preoccupied with how much you eat, makes you want to eat less, or to skip meals. Stop this course and talk to a doctor.
- You find it hard to swallow, or you often choke. Eating slowly will not fix this; see a doctor.
- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop practising and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
