**This week:** read three passages about softness, endurance and simple exercise. Each day, join stretching (Week 2) and walking (Week 1) into one session of about 15 minutes, moving from one part to the next without a break, like flowing water.

**Measurable goal:** one continuous movement session of about 15 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Taoism: nothing is softer than water

[Traditional teaching] The Tao Te Ching often uses water as an image of what is soft and yet lasting.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 78, paragraphs 1–2
1\. There is nothing in the world more soft and weak than water, and yet for attacking things that are firm and strong there is nothing that can take precedence of it;--for there is nothing (so effectual) for which it can be changed.

2\. Every one in the world knows that the soft overcomes the hard, and the weak the strong, but no one is able to carry it out in practice.
:::

A note on context: the words in brackets were added by Legge. [Inference] The chapter is about how to live and how to govern, not about movement. This lesson only borrows the image of water: soft, continuous movement without straining.

### Judaism and Christianity: running without weariness

[Traditional teaching] The book of Isaiah is read in both traditions. This passage is a word of comfort to a people worn out.

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Isaiah 40:29–31
He giveth power to the faint;  
And to him that hath no might He increaseth strength.  
Even the youths shall faint and be weary,  
And the young men shall utterly fall;  
But they that wait for the LORD shall renew their strength;  
They shall mount up with wings as eagles;  
They shall run, and not be weary;  
They shall walk, and not faint.
:::

A note on context: in Isaiah, new strength comes from waiting for the LORD, not from training; the verse is not advice about exercise. This lesson borrows only the idea that even youthful strength runs out, and that steady walking needs something to sustain it. Each tradition understands that source in its own way.

### Stoicism: short exercises, then back to the mind

[Traditional teaching] In his fifteenth letter to Lucilius, Seneca mocks spending the whole day building muscle, but he does not give up exercise.

::: kinh seneca-gummere nguyen-van | Letter 15 (the passage on short exercises)
Now there are short and simple exercises which tire the body rapidly, and so save our time; and time is something of which we ought to keep strict account. These exercises are running, brandishing weights, and jumping,—high-jumping or broad-jumping, or the kind which I may call, “the Priest’s dance,” or, in slighting terms, “the clothes-cleaner’s jump.” Select for practice any one of these, and you will find it plain and easy. But whatever you do, come back soon from body to mind.
:::

A note on context: the last two kinds of jumping were Roman exercises; Gummere translates their names. Just after this passage, Seneca says that walking does not prevent reading, conversation or thought. For him, the body is cared for in the service of the mind.

### Where the traditions differ

- The Tao Te Ching values softness and not contending. Seneca values the mind and reason; exercise is a secondary matter. Isaiah places human strength within a relationship with God.
- This lesson does not merge these three views into one. It borrows only three images: soft movement, steady walking, and short, simple exercise.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A 2015 meta-analysis of 42 studies with 1,843 adults in outdoor walking groups: by the end of the programmes, systolic blood pressure was about 3.7 mmHg lower, resting heart rate was lower, and depression scores fell (SMD −0.67); no notable side effects were reported. Limit: most studies compared before and after, with no control group. (Hanson & Jones 2015)
- [Verified] A 2022 meta-analysis of 15 cohorts with 47,471 adults: people who took more steps a day had a lower risk of death; the benefit levelled off at about 6,000–8,000 steps a day in people aged 60 and over, and 8,000–10,000 in people under 60. This is a CORRELATIONAL study; it does not prove that walking is the cause. (Paluch et al. 2022)
- This course does not count steps. The figures above only show that regular walking goes with better health.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 15 minutes a day, continuous, without stopping between the parts.

1. **Stand still (1 minute).** Feel your weight on both feet.
2. **Stretch (4 minutes).** Choose three movements from Week 2. Move from one to the next very slowly, like flowing water, without jerking.
3. **Walk slowly (3 minutes).** As in Week 1: lifting, moving, placing.
4. **Walk at a normal pace (6 minutes).** Build up gradually to your usual walking pace. Keep part of your attention on your feet and breath. You can walk indoors, in a yard or around the block.
5. **Stand still (1 minute).** Notice your breathing and heartbeat slowing.

You can walk with a family member, but agree first: six minutes of normal-pace walking without talking, then you can talk.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

**Game: "The little river"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Put a few cushions on the floor as "rocks".
2. The family becomes a "river": walk slowly in a line, winding around the rocks, arms moving like waves. Nobody touches a rock.
3. The leader changes the pace: "slow river" (very slow walking), "steady river" (normal walking). Then change leaders.
4. At the end, everyone becomes a "still lake": stand very still, breathe slowly, count to five.

Clear a wide space and do not run. If the child gets very excited, let them wind around once more before the "still lake".

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel chest pain, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, or a very fast heartbeat that does not settle with rest. Sit down at once; if it does not pass, call emergency services.
- You feel sharp pain or numbness in an arm, leg or your back while stretching or walking. Stop that movement and ask a doctor.
- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting feeling that you or the world are "not real", cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop practising and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
