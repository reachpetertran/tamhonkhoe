**A reminder:** this course only describes; it does not teach fasting. Each week's practice has nothing to do with food.

**This week:** read the discourse AN 8.41 on the sabbath with eight factors, known in Vietnam as *bát quan trai* (the eight precepts). Practice: one evening without entertainment, then a review of the whole course with a comparison table.

**Measurable goal:** read slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; keep one evening without entertainment this week; complete the comparison table of the four traditions; rate the four practices of the course and choose at least one to keep.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] In the Pāli discourses, the *uposatha* day is a day on which lay followers keep extra precepts. In AN 8.41 of the Numbered Discourses, the Buddha teaches the mendicants about the sabbath with eight factors. Each factor follows the same pattern: the perfected ones keep this for life; "I, too, for this day and night" will keep it.

### Buddhism: living for a day and a night as the perfected ones do

::: kinh an8-41-sujato nguyen-van | AN 8.41, sections 2.1–2.3
“Mendicants, the observance of the sabbath with its eight factors is very fruitful and beneficial and brilliant and bountiful.

And how should it be observed?

It’s when a noble disciple reflects:
:::

::: kinh an8-41-sujato nguyen-van | AN 8.41, sections 7.1–7.4, 8.1–8.2
‘As long as they live, the perfected ones eat in one part of the day, abstaining from eating at night and from food at the wrong time.

I, too, for this day and night will eat in one part of the day, abstaining from eating at night and food at the wrong time.

I will observe the sabbath by doing as the perfected ones do in this respect.’

This is its sixth factor.

‘As long as they live, the perfected ones give up seeing shows of dancing, singing, and music; and attiring and adorning themselves with garlands, fragrance, and makeup.

I, too, for this day and night will give up seeing shows of dancing, singing, and music; and attiring and adorning myself with garlands, fragrance, and makeup.
:::

::: kinh an8-41-sujato nguyen-van | AN 8.41, section 10.1
The observance of the sabbath with its eight factors in this way is very fruitful and beneficial and brilliant and bountiful.”
:::

A note on context: the eight factors are: not killing; not stealing; celibacy; not lying; no alcohol; not eating at the wrong time; no shows of dancing, singing and music, no adornment; no high and luxurious beds. This lesson quotes only the sixth factor (food) and the seventh (entertainment). [Expert judgement] In the usual understanding, "the wrong time" runs from noon until dawn the next day, and those keeping the precepts still drink water. In Vietnam, lay Buddhists often keep the eight precepts for a day and a night at a temple, on the full moon, the new moon, or a day of their choosing. The Buddhist reviewer will confirm.

What the text says, read closely:

- **Time:** a day and a night, chosen by the lay follower.
- **Food:** one of eight factors, alongside precepts on speech, entertainment and sleep.
- **Purpose:** to do as the perfected ones do for one day; the discourse says this is "very fruitful".

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any review on the uposatha day or the eight precepts.
- [Verified] Not eating after noon resembles "time-restricted eating", which has been studied. A 2025 network meta-analysis of 99 trials: time-restricted eating did not lead to more weight loss than eating less every day. (Semnani-Azad et al. 2025)
- Limits: those trials were in people who were overweight or ill, eating within a time window for many weeks, not keeping precepts for one day; they say nothing about the religious meaning of the sixth precept. Optional eating disciplines for healthy adults are in [Body 3](/en/courses/body-3/).

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day. No fasting.

1. **Slow reading (5 minutes, days 1–5).** Read the passages and the list of eight factors.
2. **One evening without entertainment (once this week).** Taking the idea from the seventh factor: from after dinner until bed, no films, videos, social media or music for entertainment. Instead: read, walk, sit quietly, talk with your family. Eat as usual.
3. **Review (day 6).** Copy the table above into your notebook in your own words. Add one line: what did I not know about the four traditions before this course?
4. **Rate yourself (day 7).** Score 0–3 for the four practices of the course: one act of giving (Week 1), putting right a small wrong (Week 2), a good deed in secret (Week 3), an evening without entertainment (Week 4). Choose at least one to keep for the next 4 weeks and write a concrete schedule (for example: no entertainment on the first Sunday evening of the month).

## 4. Children's corner (6+)

Children do not fast in this course. Your child eats a full dinner as on any other day.

**Game: "A quiet evening"** (10 minutes, the whole family)

1. Choose an evening, a full-moon night if you can. The whole family turns off the TV and puts phones away for 10 minutes.
2. Look at the moon together or sit by a lamp; each person tells one happy thing from the day.
3. Tell your child: "Some Buddhists choose one day to live very gently: they do not hurt any animal, do not tell lies, and that evening they do not watch TV."

Do not make your child skip a meal, and do not link the quiet evening with punishment.

## 5. Stop practising if

- Reading about not eating after noon makes you want to skip dinner, eat less, or feel guilty about eating. Stop the course and talk to a doctor.
- You want to keep the eight precepts as part of your faith and have diabetes treated with medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have had an eating disorder. Ask your doctor, and ask a teacher at your temple how to keep them in a suitable way.
- The evening without entertainment makes you restless or clearly more anxious. Shorten it to 30 minutes, or skip it.
- You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
