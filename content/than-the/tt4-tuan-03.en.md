**A reminder:** this course only describes; it does not teach fasting. Each week's practice has nothing to do with food.

**This week:** read two passages from the Gospel of Matthew about fasting, and learn why Lent in the Christian churches lasts about forty days. Practice: a good deed done in secret, told to no one.

**Measurable goal:** read slowly on at least 5 of 7 days; write two things Matthew 6:16–18 teaches about how to fast; do at least 2 good deeds in secret during the week.

## 1. Traditional teaching

[Traditional teaching] In the Gospel of Matthew, right after his baptism, Jesus goes into the wilderness. In chapter 6, in the Sermon on the Mount, he teaches about giving, prayer and fasting.

### Christianity: forty days in the wilderness

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Matthew 4:1–2
Then Jesus was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil.

When he had fasted forty days and forty nights, he was hungry afterward.
:::

A note on context: the following verses (4:3–11) tell of three temptations; to the first, he answers that "Man shall not live by bread alone". [Expert judgement] The forty days of Lent are usually explained as recalling these forty days. This lesson does not treat a forty-day fast as something the reader should do.

### Christianity: fasting in secret

::: kinh web-bible nguyen-van | Matthew 6:16–18
“Moreover when you fast, don’t be like the hypocrites, with sad faces. For they disfigure their faces that they may be seen by men to be fasting. Most certainly I tell you, they have received their reward.

But you, when you fast, anoint your head and wash your face,

so that you are not seen by men to be fasting, but by your Father who is in secret; and your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you.
:::

A note on context: this passage sits beside two passages with the same structure, on giving (6:2–4) and on prayer (6:5–6): all three are done "in secret".

What the text says, read closely:

- **Matthew 4:** fasting is tied to a time in a lonely place and a time of testing, before the start of his public ministry.
- **Matthew 6:** "when you fast" assumes the listeners will fast, but says nothing about how long or when. What is taught is an attitude: not to put it on show.

[Expert judgement] Lent is a season of about forty days of preparation for Easter. Fasting rules differ between churches: the Roman Catholic Church has two fast days (Ash Wednesday and Good Friday) and abstinence from meat on Fridays, with exemptions by age and health; the Orthodox churches have fasting seasons that set aside more kinds of food; many Protestant churches leave it to each person. Many people today "give something up" for Lent, not necessarily food. The Christian reviewer will confirm these details.

### Where the traditions differ

- Of the three weeks so far, only the Qur'an passage names exemptions in the text itself. The Torah passage and the two Gospel passages do not; exemptions are found in later law and interpretation.
- Matthew 6 stresses each person's privacy. Ramadan and Yom Kippur are times the whole community keeps together.
- Lent prepares for a feast (Easter); Yom Kippur is itself the holy day; Ramadan is a whole month.

## 2. What science finds

- We have not found any review on Lent and mental health.
- [Verified] The closest to this week's practice: a meta-analysis of 27 studies with 4,045 people found that doing kind acts for others increased the well-being of the person doing them by a small amount; the statistical power of the studies pooled was still low. (Curry et al. 2018)
- [Inference] No study we have read compares good deeds done in secret with good deeds done in public.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day. No fasting.

1. **Slow reading (5 minutes).** Days 1–3 read Matthew 4:1–2; days 4–6 Matthew 6:16–18; day 7 both.
2. **A good deed in secret (at least twice this week).** Wash up a stack of dishes, pick up litter in a shared walkway, pay the parking fee for the person behind you. Tell no one, post nothing online.
3. **Put a mark** on a private calendar when you have done it. Only you will know.
4. **Write one line (day 7).** How is doing good that no one knows about different from when someone knows?

## 4. Children's corner (6+)

Children do not fast in this course.

**Game: "Secret good deeds"** (5–10 minutes, with an adult): do the activity [Secret good deeds](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-3/g3-viec-tot-bi-mat/) on daynentang (in Vietnamese). You can tell your child: "In the Bible, Jesus teaches us to do good without needing anyone to know."

Do not trade good deeds for rewards, and do not make your child give up food or toys.

## 5. Stop practising if

- Reading about a forty-day fast makes you want to try a long fast, eat less, or skip meals. Stop the course and talk to a doctor.
- You keep Lent as part of your faith and have a chronic illness, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have had an eating disorder. Ask your doctor, and your priest or pastor about exemptions.
- You feel panic, have traumatic flashbacks, cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
