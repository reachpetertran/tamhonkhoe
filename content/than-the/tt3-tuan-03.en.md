**An important reminder:** do not do this lesson if you are pregnant or breastfeeding; under 18; have diabetes and take medication; or have or have had an eating disorder. If you are in the "ask a doctor first" group in [Week 1](/en/body/body-3-week-01/), go ahead only once a doctor has agreed.

**This week:** try eating within a fixed window each day. Start with 12 hours, and narrow to 8 hours (16:8) only if your body feels fine. Within the window, eat full meals as usual.

**Measurable goal:** keep your chosen window (12, 10 or 8 hours) on at least 4 of 7 days. Stopping at 12 or 10 hours also counts as completing the week.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Stoicism: train for yourself, show no one

[Traditional teaching] In the Encheiridion, Epictetus teaches that simple living is not something to be proud of or to be seen by others.

::: kinh epictetus-long nguyen-van | Encheiridion XLVII
When at a small cost you are supplied with everything for the body, do not be proud of this; nor, if you drink water, say on every occasion, I drink water. But consider first how much more frugal the poor are than we, and how much more enduring of labor. And if you ever wish to exercise yourself in labor and endurance, do it for yourself, and not for others. Do not embrace statues; but if you are ever very thirsty, take a draught of cold water and spit it out, and tell no man.
:::

A note on context: "embrace statues" refers to ascetics of the time who hugged cold stone statues in winter so that others would see. Chapter numbers follow George Long's edition. [Inference] This lesson borrows two ideas: discipline is your own private matter; and people who go hungry from real need are not "training" at all; they are enduring hardship.

### Judaism and Christianity: everything has its time

[Traditional teaching] The Book of Ecclesiastes opens chapter 3 with a well-known line about time.

::: kinh jps1917 nguyen-van | Ecclesiastes 3:1
To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven:
:::

A note on context: the following verses list pairs of opposites in life: birth and death, planting and uprooting, weeping and laughing. [Inference] The verse speaks of the rhythm of human life under God's ordering, not of meal times. This lesson borrows only the image: there is a time to eat and a time to rest.

### Where the traditions differ

- Epictetus speaks of training as a work of reason, done for oneself. Ecclesiastes speaks of times as part of an order set by God.
- Traditions with religious fasting, such as Ramadan or Yom Kippur, have their own hours, laws and exemptions. The 16:8 window in this lesson is not a religious practice and does not replace any.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] The TREAT randomised trial (2020), 116 adults with overweight or obesity, 12 weeks: the group eating only from 12 noon to 8 pm (16:8) did not lose more weight than the group eating three regular meals (difference −0.26 kg, not statistically significant). In the subgroup measured in person, the 16:8 group lost more lean mass in the arms and legs. (Lowe et al. 2020)
- [Verified] A 12-month randomised trial (2022), 139 people with obesity, both groups on reduced calories: adding an eating window of 8 am to 4 pm did not reduce weight, fat or metabolic markers more than calorie reduction alone. The number of adverse events did not differ much between the groups. (Liu et al. 2022)
- [Verified] The 2025 meta-analysis (see Week 1): time-restricted eating was no better than daily calorie restriction; compared with whole-day fasting, it slightly raised total and LDL cholesterol. (Semnani-Azad et al. 2025)
- Limits: the trials were in people with overweight or obesity and measured weight and metabolic markers. There is no good evidence that 16:8 helps inner life. If you choose to practise it, choose it to train self-command, not for a health promise.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day for reading and notes; the eating window fits into daily life.

[Expert judgement] The steps below are a cautious approach suggested by the writer, not yet reviewed by a dietitian or doctor.

1. **Slow reading (2 minutes).** Read Epictetus on odd days and Ecclesiastes on even days.
2. **Choose a window that suits your household.** Use the meal times you noted in Week 1. It is good to keep the family dinner, for example a window of 10:00–18:00 or 11:00–19:00.
3. **Narrow step by step; do not jump.** Days 1–2: a 12-hour window (for example 7:00–19:00). Days 3–4: 10 hours. Days 5–7: 8 hours (16:8), only if the earlier days felt fine. If it is hard, stay at the previous level; do not force it.
4. **Eat enough within the window.** Eat full meals as usual, do not cut portions, do not count calories. Let the first meal include protein and vegetables.
5. **Outside the window:** drink plain water freely. Avoid heavy exercise or long drives after a long time without food.
6. **Take special days off.** On days when you are ill, doing heavy work, travelling far, or at a memorial meal, wedding or birthday, eat as usual. A day off is not a failure.
7. **Do not show off and do not push anyone** (as Epictetus says). Do not make your family eat on your schedule.
8. **Write one line each evening:** today's window; how you felt: hunger, dizziness, irritability, focus, sleep.

## 4. Children's corner (6+)

Do not apply eating windows to children. Your child eats breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks as usual, even while you practise.

**Game: "The clock of a day"** (10 minutes, with an adult)

1. Draw a big circle like a clock face.
2. Your child draws small pictures at the times of the day: waking up, breakfast, school, lunch, play, dinner, bedtime.
3. Talk: "Our bodies have a rhythm too: a time to eat, a time to play, a time to sleep."

Do not talk to your child about fasting, and do not praise skipping meals.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel dizzy, shaky, sweaty, have a racing heart, blurred vision or nearly faint. Eat straight away, stop this lesson and ask a doctor.
- You think about food almost all the time, eat a lot without control when the window opens, or feel guilty about eating outside the window. Stop the course and talk to a doctor.
- You want to fast for longer than 16 hours, or start cutting meals inside the window. This lesson does not teach that; stop.
- You lose sleep or stay irritable in ways that affect home or work; your periods change; or you lose weight without meaning to.
- You feel panic, have trauma flashbacks, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
