**This week:** read two passages, one about walking and one about small beginnings. Each day, walk very slowly for 10 minutes along a short stretch of ground, paying attention to the feeling in your feet.

**Measurable goal:** walk slowly for 10 minutes on at least 5 of 7 days. Put a mark on your calendar each time.

**Before you start the course:** this is gentle movement, not aimed at weight loss or performance. People with heart disease, uncontrolled blood pressure, joint or bone problems, recent surgery, pregnancy, or frequent dizziness and falls should first ask a doctor how much movement suits them. This course does not replace an exercise programme from a doctor or physiotherapist.

## 1. Traditional teaching

### Buddhism: knowing that you are walking

[Traditional teaching] In the Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation, after the section on breathing, the Buddha teaches knowing the body's posture: walking, standing, sitting, lying down.

::: kinh mn10-sujato nguyen-van | MN 10, sections 6.1–6.2 (the four postures)
Furthermore, when a mendicant is walking they know: ‘I am walking.’ When standing they know: ‘I am standing.’ When sitting they know: ‘I am sitting.’ And when lying down they know: ‘I am lying down.’ Whatever posture their body is in, they know it.
:::

A note on context: the teaching is addressed to monastics and is part of a path of practice, not an exercise routine. This passage was also read in [Buddhism 2, Week 2](/en/traditions/buddhism/buddhism-2-week-02/). The discourse does not describe how to walk slowly; the method in section 3 is a common present-day practice, not taken from this text.

### Taoism: a journey of a thousand li

[Traditional teaching] Chapter 64 of the Tao Te Ching says that every great thing begins from something very small.

::: kinh ddk-legge nguyen-van | chapter 64, paragraph 2
The tree which fills the arms grew from the tiniest sprout; the tower of nine storeys rose from a (small) heap of earth; the journey of a thousand li commenced with a single step.
:::

A note on context: the word "(small)" in brackets was added by Legge. A "li" is an old Chinese measure of distance. [Inference] The chapter is about dealing with things early, while they are still small and easy to manage; it is not about walking. This lesson only borrows the image of the first step.

### Where the traditions differ

- The Discourse on Mindfulness Meditation teaches knowing each posture as part of the path to liberation. Chapter 64 of the Tao Te Ching is advice on how to act and govern; it does not describe a practice.
- This lesson borrows only the shared idea of attending to each step and starting small. It does not replace the practice of any tradition.

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A 2022 randomised trial in Thailand with 68 older adults who had fallen before (average age 69): walking meditation for 20–30 minutes a day, 5–7 days a week for 24 weeks improved balance about as much as standard balance training; no serious adverse events were reported. The two groups did not differ, so the study shows walking meditation was "comparable", not "better". (Phoobangkerdphol et al. 2022)
- [Verified] A review of 83 studies of meditation in general: about 8% of people who meditate report an adverse experience, most often anxiety or depression. Walking meditation keeps the eyes open and the attention on the feet, but the "Stop practising if" box still applies. (Farias et al. 2020)
- Limits: there is no meta-analysis of walking meditation and mental health in healthy adults. Most studies of walking meditation are small trials in people with an illness.

## 3. Practice for adults

About 10 minutes a day.

1. **Choose a place (1 minute).** A flat stretch 5–10 steps long: a corridor, a yard, a quiet pavement. If your balance is not good, choose a spot with a wall or handrail beside you.
2. **Stand still (1 minute).** Eyes open, looking at the ground about two metres ahead. Feel your weight resting on both feet.
3. **Walk very slowly (7 minutes).** With each step, notice three things: lifting the foot, moving it forward, placing it down. You can silently name them: "lifting, moving, placing". At the end of the path, stop, turn slowly, and walk on.
4. **When the mind wanders:** notice it, then bring attention back to the feet. No need to blame yourself.
5. **Stand still (1 minute).** Finish as you began.

If walking very slowly makes you unsteady, walk a little faster so you feel stable. On a busy day, walk slowly from where you park to your workplace.

## 4. Kids' corner 6+

daynentang already has the activity [Walking slowly, eating slowly](https://daynentang.com/khoa-hoc/gia-dinh-2-8/tuan-9/g9-di-cham-an-cham/) (in Vietnamese). This week, do the walking part of that activity and add a small game:

**Game: "Crossing the lotus pond"** (5 minutes, with an adult)

1. Lay 5–6 sheets of paper on the floor as "lotus leaves", one short step apart.
2. The child steps very slowly from leaf to leaf, whispering "lift, place" at each step, so as "not to wake the frog".
3. Afterwards, the child says what their feet felt: cool, hard, ticklish.

Tape the paper down so it does not slip. Do not race to see who is slowest, and do not play on stairs.

## 5. Stop practising if

- You feel chest pain, unusual breathlessness, dizziness or light-headedness, or nearly fall. Sit down at once; if it does not pass, call emergency services.
- Pain in a joint or your back gets worse after walking. Rest and ask a doctor.
- You feel panic, have flashbacks to trauma, a lasting feeling that you or the world are "not real", cannot sleep for several nights, or have thoughts of harming yourself. Stop practising and see [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
