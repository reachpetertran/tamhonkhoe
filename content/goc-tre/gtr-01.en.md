**Goes with:** [Convergence 0 – Week 1: Pause](/en/convergence/week-01/)

**Length:** 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. **For:** children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

**Small goal:** play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

## 1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions teach pausing for a moment before reacting. For a child, "pausing" is a movement game: in the middle of moving, stand still and notice the body.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson [Convergence 0 – Week 1: Pause](/en/convergence/week-01/).

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] Meta-analysis of 33 randomised trials, 3,666 children and adolescents: mindfulness programmes improved attention, anxiety/stress and depression by a small amount (d from 0.16 to 0.30). Against active control groups, benefits remained only for mindfulness, depression and anxiety/stress. These were multi-session school programmes, not a 5-minute game at home. (Dunning et al. 2019)
- Limits: no study has measured this particular game. The research shows a general direction only; it does not promise a child will change after a few rounds.

## 3. What you need

A clear space in the room, with nothing fragile or sharp table corners nearby.

## 4. How to play

1. Adult and child walk or bounce around the room together. Some cheerful music can help.
2. When the adult calls "Stop!" (or pauses the music), everyone freezes like a statue.
3. While frozen, each person silently counts 3 breaths, then says one thing they notice: feet on the floor, a sound, warm or cold hands.
4. Play 3–4 rounds. Swap roles: the child gets to call "Stop!".
5. Finish with one question: "When at school or at home could I be a statue for a moment too?"

Things the adult might say:

- "My statue can hear the fan. What can your statue hear?"
- "There is no wrong answer. Whatever you notice is fine."

## 5. Adjusting for age

- Age 6–7: count just 1–2 breaths; the adult gives a hint first ("what do your feet feel?").
- Age 8–10: add a "tricky statue" round (on one foot, holding a support) and count 5 breaths.
- A sibling under 6 joins in: they only need to freeze, not count or tell.

## 6. Cautions and when to stop

- Do not make the child close their eyes. Do not use the game to make a child keep still as a punishment.
- If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
- If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
