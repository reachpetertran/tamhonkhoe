**Goes with:** [Convergence 0 – Week 3: The Golden Rule](/en/convergence/week-03/)

**Length:** 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. **For:** children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

**Small goal:** play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

## 1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions teach treating others as you would want to be treated. For a child, the first step is imagining: "If I were them, how would I feel?"

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson [Convergence 0 – Week 3: The Golden Rule](/en/convergence/week-03/).

## 2. What science finds

- [Expert judgement] There is no research synthesis on this game for children aged 6–10. It rests on teaching experience, not on specific scientific evidence.

## 3. What you need

Paper and coloured pens or crayons.

## 4. How to play

1. Adult and child each draw a shoe on paper, then swap. "Now I’m wearing the other person’s shoes."
2. The adult tells a small, real situation from the day: "The baby was building a tower and it fell", "Mum came home tired".
3. Ask: "If you were wearing the baby’s (Mum’s) shoes, how would you feel? What would you want someone to do for you?"
4. Together pick one small thing the child can do now: help rebuild the tower, bring a glass of water, say "I’ll help you".

Things the adult might say:

- "What do you think the baby wanted when the tower fell?"
- "If it were you, what would you want someone to do?"

## 5. Adjusting for age

- Age 6–7: use situations with family members, happening right in front of them.
- Age 8–10: add a school situation (a friend forgot a pen, a new classmate) and think of two ways to help.
- A sibling under 6: they can be the "person being helped" in the game.

## 6. Cautions and when to stop

- Do not mark the child’s answers right or wrong. Do not use the game to blame the child ("see, you made your sister sad"). Choose gentle situations, not bullying or frightening events.
- If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
- If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
