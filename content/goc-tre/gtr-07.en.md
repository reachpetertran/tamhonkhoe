**Goes with:** [Convergence 1 – Week 7: Loving-kindness](/en/convergence/week-07/)

**Length:** 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. **For:** children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

**Small goal:** play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

## 1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions teach wishing others well, including people we do not know. For a child, it is one specific wish for one specific person.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson [Convergence 1 – Week 7: Loving-kindness](/en/convergence/week-07/).

## 2. What science finds

- [Expert judgement] There is no research synthesis on this game for children aged 6–10. It rests on teaching experience, not on specific scientific evidence.

## 3. What you need

A few small pieces of paper, coloured pens.

## 4. How to play

1. Adult and child sit side by side, each holding a small piece of paper.
2. The child chooses three people or creatures to wish well: grandparents, a classmate, the cat, the tree outside.
3. For each one, the child says or draws one wish: "I hope Grandma is well", "I hope the cat has enough to eat".
4. The adult asks: "How did it feel inside when you wished someone well?"

Things the adult might say:

- "I wish the vegetable seller sells everything today. Who would you like to wish well?"
- "Would you like to wish something for yourself too?"

## 5. Adjusting for age

- Age 6–7: just one or two people; drawing instead of writing.
- Age 8–10: add someone the child knows less well (the school cleaner, a new classmate) if they want to.
- A sibling under 6: they choose an animal or a toy to wish well.

## 6. Cautions and when to stop

- Do not ask the child to wish well someone they are angry with or someone who has frightened them.
- If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
- If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
