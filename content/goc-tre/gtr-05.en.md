**Goes with:** [Convergence 0 – Week 5: Rest](/en/convergence/week-05/)

**Length:** 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. **For:** children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

**Small goal:** play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

## 1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions make room for rest and stillness. For a child, the jar is an easy picture: when we stop stirring, things settle by themselves.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson [Convergence 0 – Week 5: Rest](/en/convergence/week-05/).

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] Meta-analysis of 33 randomised trials, 3,666 children and adolescents: mindfulness programmes improved attention, anxiety/stress and depression by a small amount (d from 0.16 to 0.30). Against active control groups, benefits remained only for mindfulness, depression and anxiety/stress. These were multi-session school programmes, not a 5-minute game at home. (Dunning et al. 2019)
- Limits: no study has measured this particular game. The research shows a general direction only; it does not promise a child will change after a few rounds.

## 3. What you need

A clear plastic jar with a tight screw lid, water, a spoonful of soil or sand.

## 4. How to play

1. Pour water into the jar and add a spoonful of soil or sand. The adult screws the lid on tightly.
2. The child shakes the jar: the water turns cloudy. "Like when we’re rushed, noisy or angry."
3. Put the jar on the table. Adult and child sit still and watch, breathing slowly, without talking for a minute or two.
4. Ask: "What does the water look like now? We didn’t do anything, so how did it clear?"

Things the adult might say:

- "We don’t need to scoop the soil out. We just leave it still."
- "When might you like to look at the jar for a bit?"

## 5. Adjusting for age

- Age 6–7: watch for about one minute; you can count slowly together.
- Age 8–10: try a hard shake and a gentle shake and see which clears more slowly; let the child keep the jar in their own "calm corner".
- A sibling under 6: they just watch while the adult holds the jar.

## 6. Cautions and when to stop

- Use a plastic jar, never glass. The adult keeps hold of the lid. Do not use the game to tell a very angry child to "sit still"; at that moment the child first needs an adult close by.
- If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
- If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
