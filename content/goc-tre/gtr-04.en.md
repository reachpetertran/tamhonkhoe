**Goes with:** [Convergence 0 – Week 4: Discipline](/en/convergence/week-04/)

**Length:** 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. **For:** children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

**Small goal:** play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

## 1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions value small things done steadily over big things done once. For a child, discipline means being able to see the times they did it.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson [Convergence 0 – Week 4: Discipline](/en/convergence/week-04/).

## 2. What science finds

- [Expert judgement] There is no research synthesis on this game for children aged 6–10. It rests on teaching experience, not on specific scientific evidence.

## 3. What you need

A clear plastic jar; large pebbles (too big to fit in a small child’s mouth) or stickers.

## 4. How to play

1. Adult and child together choose a small task the child can do every day: put shoes on the rack, water a plant, pack books into the school bag.
2. Put the jar where the child can see it. Each time the task is done, the child drops in a pebble (or adds a sticker).
3. At the end of the week, count the pebbles together and ask: "What was it like doing a little every day?"
4. The child decides: keep this task next week or choose another.

Things the adult might say:

- "Look at the jar: how many times have you done it?"
- "Forgetting today is fine; we carry on tomorrow."

## 5. Adjusting for age

- Age 6–7: one very easy task, at the same time each day (for example right after school).
- Age 8–10: the child picks the task and remembers it; a 7-box chart can replace the jar.
- A child under 3 in the house: use stickers, not pebbles.

## 6. Cautions and when to stop

- No food rewards, no punishment for forgetting, no comparing the child’s jar with a sibling’s.
- If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
- If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
