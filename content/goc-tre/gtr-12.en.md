**Goes with:** [Convergence 1 – Week 12: Giving and review](/en/convergence/week-12/)

**Length:** 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. **For:** children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

**Small goal:** play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

## 1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions teach giving, each with its own ways and reasons. For a child, giving is a small gift they choose themselves.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson [Convergence 1 – Week 12: Giving and review](/en/convergence/week-12/).

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] An experiment with children under 2: they looked happier giving treats to others than receiving them, and happier still when giving away their own. It is one small study of very young children, measured once; it says nothing about the long-term effect of a helping habit. (Aknin, Hamlin & Dunn 2012)
- Limits: no study has measured this particular game. The research shows a general direction only; it does not promise a child will change after a few rounds.

## 3. What you need

Paper and coloured pens; or a toy in good condition that the child wants to give.

## 4. How to play

1. Adult and child choose someone to give a gift to: a grandparent, a teacher, the building guard, a younger child.
2. The child chooses how: draw a card, or pick a toy in good condition that they **want** to give.
3. The child and an adult deliver the gift together.
4. In the evening the adult asks: "How did it feel to give the gift?" You can look back at the notebook, the pebble jar and the cards from the 11 earlier activities.

Things the adult might say:

- "Who would you like to give to? Why?"
- "A card you drew yourself is a complete gift."

## 5. Adjusting for age

- Age 6–7: give to a family member or someone the child sees every day.
- Age 8–10: the child can write the card, or choose items with the family to donate through a trusted place.
- A sibling under 6: they help draw the card or come along to give it.

## 6. Cautions and when to stop

- Do not make the child give away a toy they still treasure. A drawn card is a complete gift. No food or money rewards. Do not let the child take gifts to strangers alone.
- If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
- If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
