**Goes with:** [Convergence 0 – Week 2: Gratitude](/en/convergence/week-02/)

**Length:** 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. **For:** children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

**Small goal:** play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

## 1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions teach noticing the good one has received. For a child, gratitude starts with naming it: who helped, and with what.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson [Convergence 0 – Week 2: Gratitude](/en/convergence/week-02/).

## 2. What science finds

- [Verified] A trial with 221 early adolescents, 11 classes randomly assigned: writing down things to be grateful for went with more gratitude, optimism and life satisfaction and less negative feeling; the clearest effect was on satisfaction with school. These pupils were older than 6–10, so results may differ for young children. (Froh et al. 2008)
- Limits: no study has measured this particular game. The research shows a general direction only; it does not promise a child will change after a few rounds.

## 3. What you need

Paper and coloured pens or crayons.

## 4. How to play

1. Adult and child sit together. The adult goes first and is very specific, so the child has an example.
2. Each person names three things in order: someone who helped them today, something in nature they liked (sunshine, a tree, rain, the neighbour’s cat), something their body can do (run, laugh, hug).
3. The child picks one person from the first part and draws a small thank-you card.
4. The next day the child gives the card or says thank you to that person, with an adult if needed.

Things the adult might say:

- "Today the guard held the gate for me when my hands were full. Who helped you today?"
- "Who would you most like to thank?"

## 5. Adjusting for age

- Age 6–7: only the first part; the card can be just a drawing.
- Age 8–10: add one written line to the card: "Thank you … for …".
- A sibling under 6: they draw on the same card as the older child.

## 6. Cautions and when to stop

- Do not judge what the child says as "right" or "not enough". Do not compare with siblings or friends, and do not say things like "you should be grateful because you have more than others".
- If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
- If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
