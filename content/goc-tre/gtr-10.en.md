**Goes with:** [Convergence 1 – Week 10: Accepting change](/en/convergence/week-10/)

**Length:** 5–10 minutes, always with an adult playing too. **For:** children aged 6–10; younger siblings can join using the tips in section 5.

**Small goal:** play at least twice this week. A child who enjoys it is enough; they do not need to "learn the lesson".

## 1. The idea, for adults

Many traditions teach that everything changes. For a child, this activity stays with changes that are easy to see and not frightening: leaves change colour, seasons turn, children grow.

The game does not teach any religion’s doctrine. If your family has its own faith, you can add to it in your own way. What the traditions teach is in the lesson [Convergence 1 – Week 10: Accepting change](/en/convergence/week-10/).

## 2. What science finds

- [Expert judgement] There is no research synthesis on this game for children aged 6–10. It rests on teaching experience, not on specific scientific evidence.

## 3. What you need

A few leaves from the yard or park, a notebook, sticky tape.

## 4. How to play

1. Adult and child collect a few leaves: green, yellow, brown.
2. Line them up by colour, from green to brown.
3. The adult asks: "Do you know how leaves change colour?" Together name other things that change: the sky, the seasons, the child’s height, the tree outside.
4. The child picks the prettiest leaf to tape into the notebook, with the date.

Things the adult might say:

- "How tall were you last year? And now?"
- "What has changed at home since you were little?"

## 5. Adjusting for age

- Age 6–7: just sort leaves by colour and name one other thing that changes.
- Age 8–10: photograph or draw the same tree each month and compare after a few months.
- A sibling under 6: they collect leaves and pick a favourite colour; do not let them put leaves in their mouth.

## 6. Cautions and when to stop

- Talk only about changes in trees, seasons, objects and the child growing up. Do not bring death into the game. If the child asks, answer briefly, gently and truthfully. Do not use this activity when the family has recently lost someone. Wash hands after collecting; avoid unfamiliar plants with sap.
- If the child is bored, tired or uncomfortable: stop, and try again another time or skip this one.
- If during the game the child tells you something worrying (being hit, bullied or frightened by someone): stop the game, listen, do not blame the child, and get professional help. See [Get help now](https://daynentang.com/en/get-help/).
